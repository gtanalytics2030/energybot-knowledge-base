## Chunk 7: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023 (Section 1.5)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 02 of 2025 – Determination of Tariff for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Biomass based Power Projects and Bagasse based Cogeneration Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 1 / Introduction / Section 1.5

**PROVISION:** Section 1.5 - Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023

**TITLE:** Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023 - State Framework for RE Development and Project Setup

**SUMMARY:**
The Government of Gujarat notified the Renewable Energy Policy 2023 on 4 October 2023, which shall remain in operation until 30 September 2028. This policy establishes the framework for setting up renewable energy projects in Gujarat for both captive use and third-party sales, whether registered under the REC mechanism or direct sales to distribution licensees. Key provisions include: (i) no capacity restrictions for RE projects used for captive consumption or third-party sales with respect to the consumer's contracted demand or sanctioned load; (ii) AC capacity of RE projects is considered as installed capacity; (iii) energy accounting and banking for all renewable projects, including rooftop systems, shall be as per GERC regulations in accordance with Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022, with banking charges payable on billing cycle basis for non-banking consumers but no banking charges for residential solar consumers; (iv) for non-banking consumers in REC-registered projects, settlement is on 15-minute time block basis with no banking charges; (v) for transmission/distribution network utilization, transmission and wheeling charges and losses as determined by GERC apply as for normal open access consumers; and (vi) GUVNL is the implementing and facilitating agency while GEDA acts as State Nodal Agency. This policy creates the enabling environment for biomass and bagasse projects to operate under open access provisions.

**KEY TOPICS:** Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023, Captive Use, Third-Party Sales, REC Mechanism, Energy Banking, Banking Charges, AC Capacity, GERC Regulations, Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022, GUVNL, GEDA, Wheeling Charges, Transmission Charges, Residential Consumers, Settlement Mechanism

**TEXT:**

The Government of Gujarat notified the 'Renewable Energy Policy-2023' on 4th October 2023 for development of Renewable Energy projects in the State. Some important provisions of this Policy are listed below:

• This Policy came into force with effect from the date of notification and shall be remained in operation till 30th September 2028.

• The Policy states that the RE projects can be set up under this policy for captive use and/or for selling electricity to any other third party whether registered under the REC mechanism or not, or selling electricity to distribution licensees, subject to the provisions of this Policy and in accordance with the provisions of the Electricity Act 2003, as amended from time to time.

• There shall be no capacity restriction for setting up of RE projects for captive use or for selling electricity to third party consumer with respect to the consumer's contracted demand! sanctioned load (kW/kVA/MVA) with DlSCOMs. The AC capacity of the RE project shall be considered as the project installed capacity.

• Energy accounting and banking for all renewable energy projects, including rooftop projects, shall be as per the regulations framed by GERC from time to time in accordance with the Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 notified by the Ministry of Power, Govt of India. In case of the consumers availing energy banking facility, the settlement of renewable energy against consumer's consumption shall be carried out on billing cycle basis upon payment of applicable banking charges as determined by GERC from time to time. No banking charges shall be applicable on solar power consumed by Residential consumers.

• In case the consumer is not availing energy banking facility and consumption is from RE projects registered under REC mechanism, the energy settlement shall be carried out on 15-minute time block basis and no banking charges will be applicable.

• For utilization of State transmission/distribution network for wheeling of power from RE projects located within the state or from outside the state to consumer end, transmission and wheeling charges and losses as determined by GERC shall be levied as applicable to normal open access consumer depending on the location of the RE plant and the point of consumption.

• GUVNL shall be implementing, facilitating and monitoring agency for this policy while GEDA shall act as the State Nodal Agency (SNA) for implementation of Policy.

**PROVISION:** Section 1.6 - GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations 2024

**TITLE:** GERC Green Energy Open Access Regulations 2024 - Framework for Green Energy Trading and Banking

**SUMMARY:**
The GERC has notified the (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations 2024, which provide the comprehensive operational and regulatory framework for green energy open access in Gujarat. These regulations stipulate various provisions governing grant of open access for green energy generating projects facilitating consumption of green energy for both captive use and purchase from third-party generators. Key areas covered include: energy banking facility with applicable banking charges; energy settlement mechanism including banking charges; applicability of various open access charges (transmission charges, wheeling charges, distribution charges, cross-subsidy surcharge); metering and interconnection requirements; technical connection specifications; deviation settlement; and administrative procedures. The regulations align with the Ministry of Power's Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 and provide the practical mechanisms for implementing the tariff orders issued by GERC for renewable energy projects. These regulations are considered alongside this tariff order to ensure comprehensive regulatory coverage of biomass and bagasse project operations.

## Chunk 9: Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO) in Gujarat - RPO Targets FY 2017-18 to 2024-25 (Section 1.7)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 02 of 2025 – Determination of Tariff for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Biomass based Power Projects and Bagasse based Cogeneration Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 1 / Introduction / Section 1.7

**PROVISION:** Section 1.7 - Renewable Purchase Obligation in Gujarat

**TITLE:** Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO) Targets and Obligated Entities Framework

**SUMMARY:**
The GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) (Third Amendment) Regulations 2022 dated 08 April 2022 specify mandatory Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO) targets for obligated entities in Gujarat. The RPO framework establishes minimum percentage requirements for renewable electricity procurement that must be met by distribution licensees and other eligible captive/open-access consumers. For FY 2017-18 to FY 2024-25, the total RPO ranges from 10% to 20.70%, with differentiation between non-solar RPO (wind, biomass, bagasse 0.50% to 0.80%) and solar RPO (1.75% to 11.25%), plus hydro component (0% to 0.10% for later years). Beyond FY 2024-25, RPO targets of 20.70% shall continue unless specified otherwise by the Commission. RPO applies to: (i) distribution licensees; and (ii) captive generators ≥5 MW capacity and open-access consumers procuring from conventional generation. The framework recognizes Renewable Energy Certificates (REC) issued by CERC as valid instruments for discharging RPO obligations. Biomass and bagasse-based projects contribute to non-solar RPO and must be procured by obligated entities to achieve these targets.

**KEY TOPICS:** Renewable Purchase Obligation, RPO, Obligated Entities, Distribution Licensees, Captive Generators, Open Access, Biomass RPO, Solar RPO, Wind RPO, Hydro, REC Mechanism, Renewable Energy Certificates, CERC REC, Compliance, Targets FY 2017-25, Procurement Obligation, Percentage Requirement, GERC Regulations

**TEXT:**

The GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources), (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2022 dated 08.04.2022, specify the Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO) targets till FY 2024-25 and beyond as below:

(Source: Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) (Third Second Amendment) Regulations, 2022.)

As per the RPO Regulation, the obligated entities have the obligation to purchase electricity (in kWh) from specified RE sources. The said purchase shall be at a defined minimum percentage of the total consumption of its consumers including T&D losses during a year.

This renewable purchase obligation applies to:

• distribution licensees; and

• any other captive and open-access users consuming electricity (i) generated from conventional captive generating plant having capacity of 5 MW and above for their own use and/or (ii) procured from conventional generation through open access and third-party sale.

The aforesaid Regulations also provides that the targets specified for Obligated Entities for FY 2024-25 shall be continued beyond for FY 2025-26 and onwards unless specified by the Commission separately.

Further, this Regulation recognizes the certificates issued within the scope of Central Electricity Regulatory Commission's (CERC) Renewable Energy Certificate (REC) as the valid instruments for the discharge of the mandatory obligations set out in these Regulations for the obligated entities to purchase electricity from renewable energy sources termed as Renewable Energy Certificates (REC).

**PROVISION:** Section 1.9 - Public Hearing (Methodological Statement)

**TITLE:** Commission's Methodology for Integrating Stakeholder Feedback into Tariff Order

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission clarifies its structured approach to stakeholder engagement and tariff development. Rather than addressing every single suggestion separately (which would be impractical given the volume), the Commission integrated substantive suggestions into the clause-by-clause analysis sections of the order. Each substantive section (Sections 2.1 through 3.11) follows a consistent format: (i) Proposed in Discussion Paper (outlining the Commission's preliminary view); (ii) Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders (summarizing material comments received); and (iii) Analysis and Commission's Ruling (providing the Commission's reasoned response and final decision). This methodology ensures that the Commission has transparently considered all suggestions while making the order concise and usable. The absence of specific elaboration on a suggestion does not imply it was not considered—it may have been addressed in the overarching analysis or deemed not to change the Commission's preliminary position. This approach maintains the balance between transparency (showing how comments were addressed) and practicality (avoiding repetition of identical or minor variations of the same comment across multiple versions).
