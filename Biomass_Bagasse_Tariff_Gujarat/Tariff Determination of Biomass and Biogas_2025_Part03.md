## Chunk 13: Tariff Framework - Cost-Plus Methodology (Section 2.1)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 02 of 2025 – Determination of Tariff for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Biomass based Power Projects and Bagasse based Cogeneration Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Tariff for Biomass and Bagasse Based Cogeneration / Section 2.1

**PROVISION:** Section 2.1 - Tariff Framework

**TITLE:** Tariff Framework - Cost-Plus Tariff Methodology and Distribution Licensee Procurement Authority

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission in previous Orders No. 1 of 2018 and No. 3 of 2022 mandated distribution licensees to procure power from biomass and bagasse-based cogeneration projects at tariffs determined by the Commission under Section 62 of Electricity Act 2003. The Tariff Policy 2016 stipulates that the Appropriate Commission may determine preferential (differential) tariff for procurement of power by distribution licensees from non-conventional energy sources until central government notifies mandatory competitive bidding requirements. The Commission decides to continue with the cost-plus tariff methodology as adopted in previous tariff orders for procurement of power from biomass and bagasse-based cogeneration projects by distribution licensees during this control period. Distribution licensees are directed to procure electricity from prospective biomass and bagasse-based cogeneration projects as per tariffs determined by the Commission under Section 62 of Electricity Act 2003. The Commission shall consider any incentive or subsidy offered by Central and State Governments, including accelerated depreciation (AD) benefits if availed by the generating company, when computing tariff for biomass and bagasse-based cogeneration plants. This approach ensures transparency, cost-reflectiveness, and promotional support for emerging biomass technology while maintaining consumer protection through regulatory oversight.

**KEY TOPICS:** Tariff Framework, Cost-Plus Tariff, Preferential Tariff, Section 62 Tariff Determination, Distribution Licensee Procurement, Biomass Projects, Bagasse Cogeneration, Government Incentives, Accelerated Depreciation, Subsidy Treatment, Tariff Methodology, Control Period Approach

**TEXT:**

### 2.1.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Commission in Order No. 1 of 2018 and Order No. 3 of 2022 mandated the distribution licensees for procurement of power from Biomass and Bagasse based Cogeneration projects at the tariff determined by the Commission in the said Orders under Section 62 of the Act.

The Commission notes that the Tariff Policy 2016 notified by the Central Government on 28.01.2016 in pursuance of the Section 3 of the Act stipulates that the Appropriate Commission may determine preferential tariff for procurement of power by distribution licensees from non-conventional sources of energy till issue of notification of procure power from renewable energy sources through competitive bidding by Central Government. The relevant extract of para 6.4 of the Tariff Policy is given below.

".........(2) States shall endeavour to procure power from renewable energy sources through competitive bidding to keep the tariff low, except from the waste to energy plants. Procurement of power by Distribution Licensee from renewable energy sources from projects above the notified capacity, shall be done through competitive bidding process, from the date to be notified by the Central Government.

However, till such notification, any such procurement of power from renewable energy sources projects, may be done under Section 62 of the Electricity Act, 2003. While determining the tariff from such sources, the Appropriate Commission shall take into account the solar radiation and wind intensity which may differ from area to area to ensure that the benefits are passed on to the consumers.

(3) The Central Commission should lay down guidelines for pricing intermittent power, especially from renewable energy sources, where such procurement is not through competitive bidding. The tariff stipulated by CERC shall act as a ceiling for that category."

### 2.1.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

The Commission has not received any comments/suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

### 2.1.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

As proposed in the discussion paper the Commission decides to continue with the cost-plus tariff methodology as adopted by the Commission in the previous Tariff Orders for procurement of power from biomass and bagasse-based co-generation projects by the distribution licensees in the state during the control period of this tariff order. Accordingly, the Commission directs that the distribution licensees may procure electricity from the prospective Biomass and Bagasse based Cogeneration Projects as per the tariff determined by the Commission under Section 62 of the Act. Further the Commission while calculating the tariff shall take into account any incentive or subsidy offered by the Central and State Government, including the AD benefit if availed by the generating company, for the Biomass and Bagasse based Cogeneration plant.

**PROVISION:** Section 2.2 (a) - Control Period

**TITLE:** Control Period Definition and Effective Dates

**SUMMARY:**
The control period of this tariff order is effective from 01 April 2023 to 31 March 2027, representing a four-year regulatory period. This control period commences immediately after expiry of the previous Order No. 03 of 2022, which ended on 31 March 2023. The Commission adopted this control period after noting that the Ministry of Power notified the Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy Through Green Energy Open Access) Rules 2022 on 6 June 2022, and no extension was granted to the previous order's control period. To provide regulatory clarity and implement the Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 under Electricity Act 2003, the Commission defined this new control period. The control period applies to all projects to be commissioned under power purchase agreements signed during this period, establishing when the tariff framework and methodology specified in this order become operative and applicable.

**KEY TOPICS:** Control Period, Effective Date, 01 April 2023, 31 March 2027, Four-Year Period, Regulatory Period, Previous Order Expiry, Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022, Project Commissioning, PPA Execution, Tariff Applicability

### 2.2.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission noted that the Ministry of Power notified the Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy Through Green Energy Open Access) Rules, 2022 on 6th June 2022 (MoP Rules) outlining the policy and regulatory provisions for promotion of renewable sources of energy through Open Access. Further, the MoP Rules are effective from the date of its Notification. The previous Order No. 03 of 2022 dated 27.06.2022 was having control period up to 31.03.2023 and thereafter no extension in control period of said order is granted. Therefore, in order to give effect to the provision of Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 notified by Ministry of Power under the Electricity Act, 2003 and specifically when there was no tariff order of the Commission after 31.03.2023 with respect to Biomass and Bagasse based Co-generation Power Projects, the Commission feal it more appropriate to adopt the regulatory provisions outlined in MoP Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022 and proposed in the discussion paper to define the Control Period of new tariff order effective from 01.04.2023 i.e. after expiry of Control Period of previous Order No. 03 of 2022.

Accordingly, the Commission with an intention to provide clarity on the tariff framework, decides to define the Control Period of the Order effective from 01.04.2023 to 31.03.2027, for procurement of power generated from Biomass and Bagasse based Cogeneration Projects in the State of Gujarat by Distribution Licensees, under the powers conferred to it under Sections 61(h), 62(1) (a), and 86(1) (b) & (e) of the Electricity Act, 2003, and National Electricity Policy, 2005, and Tariff Policy, 2016.

## Chunk 16: Tariff Period and Tariff Structure - Single-Part Tariff with Two Components (Section 2.2(d) and 2.4)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 02 of 2025 – Determination of Tariff for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Biomass based Power Projects and Bagasse based Cogeneration Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Tariff for Biomass and Bagasse Based Cogeneration / Section 2.2 / Clause 2.2(d) and Section 2.4

**PROVISION:** Section 2.2(d) and Section 2.4 - Tariff Period and Tariff Structure

**TITLE:** Single-Part Tariff Structure with Fixed and Variable Cost Components

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission determines a single-part tariff comprising two components for procurement of electricity from biomass-based power projects and bagasse-based cogeneration projects to be commissioned under PPAs signed during the control period: (i) **Fixed Component**: Levelized over the useful life of the plant (25 years), representing the depreciation benefit for those availing accelerated depreciation (AD) benefit to be internalized. The fixed component is constant across all years and allows the project to recover capital costs and appropriate returns on equity; (ii) **Variable Cost Component**: Specified for each year of the control period, considering annual escalation of fuel cost at 5% per annum. Variable cost recovers fuel and O&M operating expenses that vary with generation. Projects commissioned under PPAs during the new control period receive the fixed component over 25 years of useful life plus yearly variable component as specified in the tariff order. **For Earlier Projects**: Biomass and bagasse cogeneration projects commissioned during the control period of previous tariff orders and executed PPAs with GUVNL/DISCOM shall be eligible for yearly variable cost component determined in this new order while continuing to receive the levelized fixed cost component as per previous tariff orders of the Commission, as applicable. At the end of the control period (31 March 2027), the Commission shall re-assess fuel price market and re-determine the variable cost component for subsequent tariff orders.

**KEY TOPICS:** Single-Part Tariff, Fixed Component, Variable Component, Levelized Fixed Cost, Fuel Escalation, Two-Component Tariff, Cost Structure, Accelerated Depreciation, Useful Life, Annual Escalation, Fuel Cost, O&M Cost, PPA Execution, Control Period, Tariff Revision

**TEXT:**

### 2.4.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Commission in the discussion paper proposed to determine a single-part tariff with two components for procurement of electricity from biomass-based power projects and bagasse-based cogeneration projects by the distribution licensee to be commissioned under PPAs signed during the control period of the Order.

Fixed component of tariff shall be levelized over the useful life of the plant and the depreciation benefit shall be internalised for those availing the accelerated depreciation benefit. Variable cost component of tariff shall be specified for each year of the control period by considering annual escalation of fuel cost as proposed in the discussion paper. The biomass power and bagasse-based co-generation project to be commissioned under the PPA signed during the control period of new tariff order shall be eligible to get the fixed component of tariff over the useful life of 25 years of the plant and yearly variable component of tariff as specified in the tariff order. The Biomass and Bagasse Cogeneration Projects Commissioned during the control period of previous tariff Orders of the Commission shall be eligible for yearly variable cost component of tariff determined by the Commission in this new tariff order while they shall continue to get levelized fixed cost component as per the previous tariff orders of the Commission as applicable. At the end of the control period, the Commission shall re-assess the fuel price market and re-determine the variable cost component of tariff for Biomass and Bagasse based Cogeneration projects, which will be specified in the subsequent tariff orders to be issued by the Commission.

## Chunk 19: Applicability of Merit Order Dispatch Principle (Section 2.2(g) and Section 2.7)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 02 of 2025 – Determination of Tariff for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Biomass based Power Projects and Bagasse based Cogeneration Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Tariff for Biomass and Bagasse Based Cogeneration / Section 2.2 / Clause 2.2(g) and Section 2.7

**PROVISION:** Section 2.2(g) and Section 2.7 - Applicability of Merit Order Dispatch Principle

**TITLE:** Exemption from Merit Order Dispatch - Must-Run Status for Biomass and Bagasse Projects

**SUMMARY:**
Biomass and bagasse-based cogeneration plants, irrespective of their plant capacity, shall be considered as 'MUST RUN' power plants and shall not be subjected to 'Merit Order Dispatch' (MOD) principles. Merit Order Dispatch, typically applied to thermal power stations, prioritizes power dispatch based on cost and operational efficiency, resulting in units with lower short-run marginal costs (fuel + variable O&M) being dispatched first during surplus generation scenarios. Subjecting biomass and bagasse plants to MOD would introduce dispatch uncertainties and create operational constraints given the fixed fuel cost structure and seasonal fuel availability (particularly for bagasse in sugar season). By classifying biomass and bagasse projects as Must-Run plants, the Commission ensures: (i) operational predictability and certainty for project developers, supporting long-term financial viability; (ii) promotion of renewable energy as mandated by Electricity Act 2003 Section 86(1)(e); (iii) protection of fuel-dependent operations where fuel supply chains must be maintained; and (iv) cost-plus tariff functionality which assumes dispatch at available capacity. This must-run status aligns with national policy promoting non-conventional renewable energy sources.

**KEY TOPICS:** Merit Order Dispatch, Must-Run Plants, Dispatch Principle, Power Plant Classification, Fuel Certainty, Cost Structure, Capacity-Based Operation, Operational Certainty, Renewable Energy Promotion, Dispatch Exemption

**TEXT:**

### 2.7.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Biomass and Bagasse based Cogeneration plants irrespective of plant capacity shall be considered as 'MUST RUN' power plants and shall not be subjected to 'Merit Order Dispatch' principles.

### 2.7.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

### 2.7.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission decides that the Biomass and Bagasse based Cogeneration plants irrespective of plant capacity shall be considered as 'MUST RUN' power plants and shall not be subjected to 'Merit Order Dispatch' principles.

## Chunk 20: Metering Point and Interconnection Point Definition (Section 2.2(h) and Section 2.8)

**PROVISION:** Section 2.2(h) and Section 2.8 - Metering Point and Interconnection Point

**TITLE:** Technical Definition of Interconnection and Metering Points for Biomass and Bagasse Plants

**SUMMARY:**
For biomass and bagasse-based cogeneration plants: **(i) Interconnection Point** is defined as the line isolator on the outgoing feeder on the high voltage (HV) side of the generator transformer. This is the boundary point where the generating facility's electrical system interfaces with either the transmission or distribution system. The interconnection point serves as the legal and technical demarcation for responsibility transfer between the generator and the transmission/distribution operator. **(ii) Metering Point** is defined as the interconnection point of the generator bus-bar with the transmission or distribution system concerned, as the case may be. At this metering point, energy measurement instruments are installed to record electricity generation for commercial purposes (billing, settlement, and compliance verification). This metering point location ensures that all output from the generator is accurately measured and accounted for in power purchase agreements, regulatory compliance, and financial settlement with distribution licensees. These definitions align with standard grid code requirements and ensure clear responsibility allocation, accurate measurement, and proper financial settlement between generating units and grid operators.

**KEY TOPICS:** Metering Point, Interconnection Point, Line Isolator, HV Side, Generator Transformer, Generator Bus-bar, Transmission System, Distribution System, Energy Measurement, Billing Point, Settlement, Responsibility Demarcation

**TEXT:**

The Commission in the discussion paper defined that the interconnection point shall be at the line isolator on outgoing feeder on HV side of generator transformer and the metering point shall be at the interconnection point of the generator bus-bar with the transmission or distribution system concerned, as the case may be.

## Chunk 21: Treatment of Subsidy or Incentive by Central/State Government (Section 2.2(i) and Section 2.9)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 02 of 2025 – Determination of Tariff for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Biomass based Power Projects and Bagasse based Cogeneration Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Tariff for Biomass and Bagasse Based Cogeneration / Section 2.2 / Clause 2.2(i) and Section 2.9

**PROVISION:** Section 2.2(i) and Section 2.9 - Treatment of Subsidy or Incentive by Central/State Government

**TITLE:** Incorporation of Government Incentives and Subsidies in Tariff Computation

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission, when calculating tariff for biomass and bagasse-based cogeneration projects, shall take into account any incentive or subsidy offered by the Central Government and/or the State Government of Gujarat. This includes, but is not limited to: (i) capital subsidies or grants for project development; (ii) interest rate subsidies or concessional financing schemes; (iii) tax incentives such as Accelerated Depreciation (AD) benefits if availed by the generating company; (iv) renewable energy certificates (RECs); and (v) any other benefits or support programs notified by central or state authorities. The purpose of this provision is to ensure that tariff determination is fair and cost-reflective, and that consumers benefit from government promotional schemes without overpaying for power. When such benefits are available to the project developer, they must be factored into the tariff computation to reduce the tariff burden on distribution licensees and ultimately consumers. This approach aligns with the regulatory principle of cost-plus pricing and ensures that government support mechanisms designed to promote renewable energy achieve their intended objective of cost reduction. The project developer has the responsibility to disclose all government support received or available to it during the tariff determination process.
