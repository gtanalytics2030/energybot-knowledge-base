**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Policy Guideline / Regulatory Procedure

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Policy Foundation

**PROVISION:** Policy Framework & Legislative Basis

**TITLE:** Regulatory Basis for DREBP - Gujarat RE Policy 2023 and GERC Tariff Orders

**SUMMARY:**
The Government of Gujarat notified the "Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023" enabling eligible entities to set up renewable energy projects for sale to distribution licensees without competitive bidding. The GERC issued tariff orders (Notification No. 06 of 2024 for solar and No. 05 of 2024 for wind) establishing generic tariffs for RE projects below 5 MW (solar) and 10 MW (wind). To fulfill distributed RE Renewable Purchase Obligations (RPO) and promote small-scale RE projects, GUVNL prepared these guidelines for bilateral procurement of solar projects less than 5 MW and wind projects less than 10 MW under feed-in tariff arrangement.

**KEY TOPICS:**

- DREBP (Feed-in tariff) category

**TEXT:**

Government of Gujarat has notified "Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy- 2023"- any individual, company, body corporate, association or body of individuals, whether incorporated or not, or artificial juridical person will be eligible for setting up the RE projects under the policy. Under this policy, the solar up to 4 MW capacity and Wind power projects less than 10 MW capacity can be set up for selling of electricity to distribution licensees without competitive bidding process. 

Further, The GERC vide notification No. 06 of 2024 dated 31.08.2024 has issued tariff order for procurement of power from solar energy project by Distribution licensees and vide notification No. 05 of 2024 for Wind project which provide that commission has determined generic tariff for procurement of power from project below threshold limit of 5 MW for solar and 10 MW for wind. 

In view of above, to fulfil distributed RE RPO and to promote small scale RE projects, GUVNL has prepared guideline for procurement of RE from solar project less than 5 MW and wind project less than 10 MW.

## CHUNK 2: DREBP SOP - Registration & Application Process (Part A: Portal Registration & Documentation)

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Procedure / Standard Operating Procedure

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 1 → Registration Process

**PROVISION:** Section 1 - Standard Operating Procedure for Distributed RE Bilateral Purchase projects (Registration Phase)

**TITLE:** Registration Process on AkshayUrja Setu Portal and Required Documentation

**SUMMARY:**
Eligible applicants must register on the "Single Window Portal – AkshayUrja Setu" under the "Distributed Renewable Energy Bilateral Purchase (DREBP) Feed-in tariff - Power sale to DISCOM" category. Registration operates on a first-come-first-serve basis. Applicants must submit company identification documents (ROC certificate, board resolution, or PAN card) and a notarized undertaking on Rs 300 stamp paper. A non-refundable registration fee of Rs. 10,000 per MW (pro-rata basis) must be paid at registration. Upon successful DREBP application submission, applicants must register with GEDA and subsequently apply for grid connectivity on the AkshayUrja Setu portal. Failure to pay the registration fee within 7 days or obtain connectivity within two months results in automatic cancellation and fee forfeiture.

## CHUNK 5: DREBP Eligibility Conditions - Aggregation, Splitting Prohibition, and Multi-Parcel Rules

**DOCUMENT:** Amended Guideline for Procurement of RE from Project below threshold limit by DISCOMs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Guideline / Eligibility Conditions

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 2 → Section 2.2

**PROVISION:** Section 2.2 - Eligibility of the Projects

**TITLE:** Project Eligibility Conditions - Aggregation Limits, Splitting Prohibition, and Multi-Facility Rules

**SUMMARY:**
DREBP projects must be new or in-progress construction with untied capacity not registered under REC mechanism. A single eligible RE power producer (including group companies, associates, SPVs, partnership firms, LLPs) can interconnect a maximum of less than 5 MW solar or less than 10 MW wind at a single GETCO receiving end sub-station. Solar projects may be installed on separate smaller land parcels (minimum 500 KW each) aggregated through internal lines, but not more than one solar/wind project per company per substation is permitted to prevent project splitting. Solar and wind projects from the same land parcel terminating at the same GETCO substation qualify as individual projects (subject to capacity limits). Projects of a single producer with interconnection at two different GETCO substations from the same land parcel are treated as single project with aggregated capacity limits. These provisions prevent artificial project fragmentation while permitting efficient land utilization through multiple small parcels.

**PROVISION:** Section 4.2 - Interconnection voltage with grid

**TITLE:** Interconnection Voltage Requirements by Project Capacity

**SUMMARY:**
Interconnection voltage with the grid is capacity-dependent. Solar and wind projects with capacity greater than 0.5 MW up to 4.0 MW must interconnect at 11 KV, 3-phase. Solar and wind projects with capacity greater than 4.0 MW and up to less than 10 MW must interconnect at 66 KV, 3-phase. This bifurcated voltage classification reflects grid planning principles: smaller distributed projects use lower voltage distribution infrastructure while larger projects require higher voltage transmission infrastructure to minimize losses and optimize grid stability.

## CHUNK 13: Grid Integration - Common Evacuation Lines & Pooling Arrangements (Section 4.6)

**DOCUMENT:** Amended Guideline for Procurement of RE from Project below threshold limit by DISCOMs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Guideline / Infrastructure Sharing Mechanism

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 4 → Section 4.6

**PROVISION:** Section 4.6 - Common dedicated evacuation/transmission line with pooling stations

**TITLE:** Common Evacuation Infrastructure Development, Lead Generator Framework, and Cost Sharing Provisions

**SUMMARY:**
Adjoining RE power producers may jointly develop common dedicated evacuation/transmission line infrastructure with pooling stations as per applicable CEA technical standards and best practices. Each producer must install appropriate metering at their respective project end and a common meter at the pooling station sending end and interconnection point. New RE power producers can connect to existing common infrastructure only with consent of already-connected producers and on mutually agreed terms. The first applicant (by timestamp) becomes the "Lead Generator" responsible for all operational and commercial responsibilities including Grid Code compliance, scheduling and dispatch, transmission charge collection, and deviation settlement. All connected producers share the development, infrastructure, and regulatory compliance costs based on mutually decided modalities. Lead generators cannot deny subsequent producers from using common infrastructure; however, all producers must execute a common agreement. The structure permits efficient land use and infrastructure optimization while protecting earlier investors through lead generator designation.

If both main and check meters are defective, loss-adjusted generation meter data is the source of truth for billing and deviation settlement.

## CHUNK 26: DREBP T&C - Delay Penalties & Default Remedies (Section 8.9)

**DOCUMENT:** Amended Guideline for Procurement of RE from Project below threshold limit by DISCOMs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Guideline / Delay Penalties & Default

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 8 → Clause 8.9

**PROVISION:** Section 8.9 - Delay penalties, capacity reduction, and PBG encashment

**TITLE:** Delay in Commissioning - Graduated Penalties, PBG Encashment, and Capacity Reduction Beyond 180 Days

**SUMMARY:**
Graduated delay penalties apply to projects failing to commission by the SCSD, with increasing severity based on delay duration and reason. For delays up to 180 days from SCSD, PBG is encashed on a per-day basis at Rs. 5,555 per MW per day, pro-rata to the contracted capacity not yet commissioned. These daily penalties represent liquidated damages (pre-estimated loss amount) for DISCOM's inability to procure contracted power. If commissioning is delayed beyond 180 days from SCSD for reasons other than force majeure, DISCOM/GETCO transmission delays, or RE power producer contract compliance issues, the generator's "Event of Default" under the PPA (Article 9.2.1(a)) is triggered, and contracted capacity is permanently reduced to the level actually commissioned within 180 days. The remaining uncommissioned capacity PBG is encashed as additional liquidated damages. A minimum 0.5 MW must be commissioned; projects commissioning less than 0.5 MW within 180 days trigger PPA termination and full PBG encashment. This mechanism incentivizes on-time commissioning (avoid penalties) while protecting DISCOMs from extended uncertainty regarding procurement adequacy.

## CHUNK 27: DREBP T&C - Tariff Determination & AD Benefit Framework (Section 8.10-8.11 Part A)

**PROVISION:** Section 8.10-8.11 - Firm tariff and AD benefit options

**TITLE:** Firm Tariff Guarantee and Accelerated Depreciation (AD) Benefit Election

**SUMMARY:**
The tariff payable to the RE power producer is firm (fixed and unchanging) for the entire PPA term, providing revenue certainty essential for long-term project financing. For solar projects, the tariff is per GERC Tariff Order No. 06 of 2024 Clause 3.2(b) and (e): Net Tariff of Rs. 2.48/unit (calculated as Levelized Tariff Rs. 2.76 minus AD benefit Rs. 0.28). For wind projects, the tariff is per GERC Tariff Order No. 05 of 2024 Clause 3.2(a) and (d): Net Tariff of Rs. 2.84/unit (Levelized Tariff Rs. 3.17 minus AD benefit Rs. 0.33). RE power producers have an election: (1) avail Accelerated Depreciation (AD) benefit of 40% (with/without additional depreciation under Income Tax Act) and pay the lower net tariff (Rs. 2.48 solar, Rs. 2.84 wind), or (2) not avail AD benefits and receive the higher Levelized Tariff (Rs. 2.76 solar, Rs. 3.17 wind). This choice reflects the economic trade-off: producers accepting AD taxation benefits (higher depreciation deduction reducing taxable income) receive proportionally lower tariff from DISCOM; producers declining AD benefits receive higher tariff but cannot claim accelerated tax deductions. The choice is made at PPA signing time and is binding for the entire 25-year term. Notably, if a producer opts for "no AD benefits" (higher tariff) but later claims AD benefits in income tax filings, the tariff is retroactively amended to the lower "AD benefit availed" rate effective from initial payment date, with DISCOM recovery of overpayment plus applicable adjustments.

## CHUNK 28: DREBP T&C - AD Benefit Documentation & Verification (Section 8.11 Part B)

**PROVISION:** Section 8.11 (Continuation) - AD benefit documentation, verification, and tariff amendment for misrepresentation

**TITLE:** AD Benefit Declaration, Annual Verification through ITR, and Tariff Retroactive Amendment for Misrepresentation

**SUMMARY:**
RE power producers must submit a Notarised Undertaking (Annexure-D) on Rs 300 stamp paper with the PPA, declaring whether they will avail or not avail AD benefits. This undertaking is legally binding and constitutes the basis for tariff calculation. Annually thereafter, producers must furnish income tax returns (ITRs) along with a Chartered Accountant (CA) certificate to DISCOM/GUVNL verifying whether Accelerated Depreciation benefits were actually availed in that financial year. If the producer fails to submit required ITRs/CA certificates, the default assumption is that AD benefits were availed (conservative approach protecting DISCOM finances), and the lower tariff (Rs. 2.48 solar, Rs. 2.84 wind) automatically applies regardless of the initial declaration. If a producer initially opted for "not avail AD benefits" (higher tariff) but later documentation reveals AD benefits were actually claimed in tax filings, the tariff is retroactively amended to "AD benefits availed" status effective from the date payment commenced. The DISCOM recovers the tariff differential from payment date forward plus applicable interest and adjustment mechanisms per PPA. This verification framework prevents producers from obtaining higher tariffs while simultaneously claiming tax depreciation benefits—they must choose one. The annual ITR requirement ensures continuous verification throughout the PPA term.
