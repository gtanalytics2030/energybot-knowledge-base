PROVISION: Article 2.I

TITLE: RE Power Producer's Obligation to Acquire and Maintain Regulatory Clearances

SUMMARY:
The RE power producer bears sole responsibility and cost for acquiring and maintaining all regulatory clearances, consents, permits, licenses, and approvals required from competent statutory authorities to enable performance of its obligations under the PPA. These approvals are necessary at various stages: project design, land acquisition, environmental clearance, grid connectivity, and equipment installation. Non-rendering or partial rendering of assistance by authorities does not absolve the RE producer of this obligation or waive the requirement to obtain such clearances. The producer must pursue all necessary approvals proactively without relying on the DISCOM or GETCO to facilitate approval processes.

KEY TOPICS: Regulatory Clearances, Permits, Licenses, Consents, Approvals, Statutory Authority, RE Producer Responsibility, Sole Cost, Obligation to Obtain

TEXT:

I. The RE power producer, at its sole cost and expense, shall acquire and maintain in effect all clearances, consents, permits, licences and approval required from time to time by all regulatory / statutory competent authority(ies) in order to enable it to perform its obligations under the Agreement.

II. Provided, however, non-rendering or partial rendering of assistance shall not in any way absolve the RE power producer of its obligations to obtain such clearances. Nor shall it mean to confer any right of or indicate any intentions to waive the need to obtain such clearances.

PROVISION: Article 2.III

TITLE: Land Acquisition on Sale or Lease Basis for RE Project Development

SUMMARY:
The RE power producer must acquire or possess the land required for the project on either a sale basis or a lease basis from private parties or the Government of Gujarat. The RE producer retains the burden of negotiating, executing, and maintaining all land agreements independently of the DISCOM/GUVNL.

HIERARCHY: Article 2 – Licences & Permits / Section IV

PROVISION: Article 2.IV – Project Splitting Restrictions (Part A)

TITLE: Single Project Limit per Receiving Substation and Aggregation Rules

SUMMARY:
Splitting of RE projects is strictly prohibited to prevent fragmentation of capacity and circumvention of regulatory capacity caps. A single eligible RE power producer (including group companies, associates, SPVs, partnerships, or LLPs) may interconnect only one solar project of less than 5 MW total capacity to a single receiving-end GETCO substation. However, the project can be physically installed on separate land parcels of minimum 500 KW each, aggregated via internal electrical lines, provided the total aggregated capacity does not exceed the specified limit. Further, no more than one solar project per single company (or companies with common directors, promoters, or related entities) is allowed at the same GETCO substation. This prevents artificial project splitting by the same entity or common ownership structures to exceed capacity thresholds.

KEY TOPICS: Project Splitting Prohibition, Single Project Limit, Receiving Substation, Aggregated Capacity, Land Parcels, 500 KW Minimum, Single Company Restriction, Common Director/Promoter Rule, Dedicated Evacuation Line, Internal Electrical Lines

TEXT:

IV. Splitting of the RE projects shall not be allowed. In this context, 

a) For any solar project, single eligible RE power producer (including group companies, associates, SPV, partnership firm, LLP) can interconnect less than 5 MW solar project at single receiving end sub-station of GETCO. However solar projects can be installed on separate smaller land parcels of minimum 500 KW capacity subject to total aggregated project capacity shall not be more than above specified limit and same shall be aggregated through internal electrical lines while dedicated evacuation line from project shall be installed up to GETCO sub-station. Further, it is to clarify that not more than one solar project shall be allowed to interconnect to the same substation of the same company or company having common director or the same promoter company or any eligible RE power producer including partnership firm, LLP to avoid splitting of project.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Article 2.IV(b) and (c) (Solar/wind projects on same land parcel; interconnection at two different substations), Annexure-B Undertaking clause 2–5 (Applicant agrees not to exceed capacity limits)

CROSS-REFERENCES: DREBP Guidelines clause 2.2 (capacity restriction rules), Annexure-B undertaking requirement

PROVISION: Article 2.IV(b) – Solar and Wind Projects on Same Land Parcel

TITLE: Separate Individual Project Treatment for Solar and Wind from Same Land Parcel

SUMMARY:
If both solar and wind projects are installed on the same land parcel and connected to the same receiving-end GETCO substation, each shall be treated as an individual solar project and an individual wind project respectively, not as a single hybrid or combined project. Each individual project is subject to the maximum capacity limits specified in Article 2.IV(a). This clarification ensures that developers cannot circumvent project capacity restrictions by co-locating different renewable technology types on the same land.

KEY TOPICS: Solar and Wind Projects, Same Land Parcel, Individual Projects, Separate Treatment, Receiving Substation, Capacity Limits, Hybrid Projects

TEXT:

b) It is to clarify that solar and wind project from same land parcel terminating at same receiving end sub-station of GETCO will be eligible as individual solar/wind project subject to maximum capacity as per above (a).

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Article 2.IV(a) (Capacity limits), Article 2.IV(c) (Two different receiving substations from same land parcel)

HIERARCHY: Article 2 – Licences & Permits / Section IV (c)

PROVISION: Article 2.IV(c) – Multiple Interconnections from Same Land Parcel at Different Substations

TITLE: Single Project Treatment for Multi-Substation Interconnection from Same Land Parcel

SUMMARY:
If a single eligible RE power producer operates multiple interconnections from the same land parcel to two or more different receiving substations of GETCO, all such interconnections shall be treated collectively as a single solar project for the purpose of capacity restrictions. The maximum capacity limits applicable to individual projects per Article 2.IV(a) shall apply to the combined/aggregated capacity across all substations. This provision prevents developers from multiplying the effective capacity by artificially splitting generation across multiple grid connection points from a unified land asset.

KEY TOPICS: Same Land Parcel, Multiple Substations, Two Different Receiving Substations, Single Project Treatment, Aggregated Capacity, Capacity Limits, Interconnection Limit

TEXT:

c) Any solar project of single eligible RE power producer (including group companies, associates, SPV, partnership firm, LLP) having interconnection at two different receiving sub-station of GETCO from same land parcel will be treated as single solar project. Maximum capacities as per above (a) will be applicable for individual solar projects.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Article 2.IV(a) (Individual project capacity limits)

HIERARCHY: Article 3 – Obligations / Section 3.1

PROVISION: Article 3.1(I) – (XXI) – RE Power Producer Obligations (Comprehensive Bundle)

TITLE: RE Power Producer's Comprehensive Development, Operation, and Compliance Obligations

SUMMARY:
Article 3.1 imposes comprehensive obligations on the RE power producer across the project lifecycle: (I) obtaining all statutory approvals at producer's cost beyond those in Schedule-2; (II) constructing, operating, and maintaining the project at producer's cost and risk, with required interconnection facilities; (III) selling all generated power exclusively to DISCOM/GUVNL without third-party sales or captive use (penalty 1.5× tariff or actual third-party sale revenue, whichever is higher); (IV) not connecting any load at the project (unauthorized use penalties apply); (V) seeking GETCO/DISCOM approval for interconnection facilities; (VI) undertaking construction/upgrading of interconnection facilities and transmission lines at producer's cost per GETCO/DISCOM specifications; (VII) operating and maintaining the project per Prudent Utility Practices and evacuation infrastructure; (VIII) bearing all taxes, cesses, duties, and levies on land, equipment, and project assets; (IX) allowing GETCO/DISCOM to perform evacuation facility maintenance on mutual consent with applicable charges (separate agreement required); (X) procuring start-up power from DISCOM at applicable tariff; (XI) fulfilling all other PPA obligations. These obligations ensure the RE project is professionally developed, reliably operated, legally compliant, and functionally integrated into the distribution grid.

KEY TOPICS: RE Producer Obligations, Statutory Approvals, Construction and Maintenance, Exclusive Sale to DISCOM, No Captive Use, Interconnection Facilities, Evacuation Infrastructure, Prudent Utility Practices, Taxes and Levies, Start-up Power, GETCO/DISCOM Coordination, Grid Integration

TEXT:

3.1 Obligations of the RE power producer:

I. The RE power producer shall obtain all statutory approvals, clearances and permits necessary for the Project at his cost in addition to those approvals as listed in Schedule 2.

II. The RE power producer shall construct, operate and maintain the project during the term of PPA at his cost and risk including the required interconnection facilities and in close coordination with GETCO / DISCOM's feasibility.

III. The RE power producer shall sell all the power generated from identified Solar Power Plants to DISCOM/GUVNL and shall not sell to any third party nor consume for captive use. Otherwise penalty as per, clause No 5.1 (VII) shall be applicable.

IV. The RE power producer shall not connect any load at the RE project. Penalty will be imposed as per applicable rules and regulations for unauthorized use of energy.

V. The RE power producer shall seek approval of GETCO / DISCOMs in respect of interconnection facilities.

VI. The RE power producer shall undertake at its own cost construction / up-gradation of (i) interconnection facilities, (ii) the transmission lines and as per specifications and requirements of GETCO / DISCOMs, as notified to the RE power producer at clause 5 of schedule-1.

VII. The RE power producer shall operate and maintain the project in accordance with Prudent Utility Practices and evacuation infrastructure up to interconnection point.

VIII. The RE power producer shall be responsible for all payments on account of any taxes, cesses, duties or levies imposed by the GoG or its competent statutory authority on the land, equipment, material or works of the project or assets owned by RE power producer.

IX. The evacuation facility and maintenance of the transmission up to interconnection point may be performed by GETCO / DISCOM on mutual consent basis along with applicable charges for which, the RE power producer shall enter into separate agreement with GETCO / DISCOMs.

X. To procure start up power required for the plant from respective DISCOM at applicable tariff.

XI. Fulfilling all other obligations undertaken by him under this Agreement.

HIERARCHY: Article 3 – Obligations / Section 3.1(XII)

PROVISION: Article 3.1(XII) – Shareholding and Control Maintenance Requirements

TITLE: Promoter Shareholding Stability and Control Lock-In for One Year from COD

SUMMARY:
The RE power producer must maintain minimum shareholding control until one year after COD. For single-company producers, shareholding must not fall below 51% before the one-year milestone from SCSD, except with prior DISCOM/GUVNL approval. For consortiums, combined consortium member shareholding must not fall below 51% before one year from SCSD, except with prior approval. The producer (or consortium) must not allow its promoters to cede control (defined as ownership of more than 50% voting shares or right to appoint majority Directors) before one year from SCSD, except with prior procurer approval. Producer must disclose promoter information and shareholding details to procurer before PPA signing. After one year from SCSD, shareholding changes can occur with only intimation (not approval) to procurer. If the RE producer defaults to lenders, lenders may seek substitution of promoter in concurrence with procurer.

KEY TOPICS: Shareholding Requirement, 51% Threshold, Promoter Control, One Year from SCSD, Control Definition, Voting Shares, Director Appointment, Lender Substitution, Consortium Members, Ownership Stability

TEXT:

XII. The RE power producer, if being a single company, shall ensure that its shareholding in the SPV/project company executing the PPA shall not fall below 51% (fifty-one per cent) at any time prior to 1 (one) year from the SCSD except with the prior approval of the Procurer. In the event the RE power producer is a consortium, then the combined shareholding of the consortium members in the SPV/project company executing the PPA, shall not fall below 51% at any time prior to 1 (one) year from the SCSD, except with the prior approval of the Procurer. Further, the RE power producer shall ensure that its promoters shall not cede control of the bidding company/ consortium till 1 (one) year from the SCSD, except with the prior approval of the Procurer. In this case it shall also be essential that the RE power producer shall provide the information about its promoters and their shareholding to the Procurer before signing of the PPA with Procurer. Any change in the shareholding after the expiry of 1 (one) year from the SCSD can be undertaken under intimation to Procurer. In the event the RE power producer is in default to the lender(s), lenders shall be entitled to undertake "Substitution of Promoter" in concurrence with the Procurers. The expression 'control' shall mean the ownership, directly or indirectly, of more than 50% (fifty per cent) of the voting shares of such Company or right to appoint majority Directors.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Article 9.2.1(i) (Failure to maintain shareholding as event of default)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Financing arrangements and lender rights, PPA amendment for substitution per Article 9.3.1(c)

PROVISION: Article 3.1(XXII) – DC Capacity Installation and AC Output Capping

TITLE: Unrestricted DC Installation with AC Output Capped at Contracted Capacity

SUMMARY:
The RE producer may install solar DC capacity without restriction of contracted capacity, subject only to grid constraints and land availability. However, the final AC output (after conversion via inverter) in no case shall exceed the AC contracted capacity specified in the PPA. In any 15-minute time block during the entire PPA term, injected power must not exceed contracted AC capacity. If supply at the delivery point exceeds contracted capacity in any time block, such excess is treated as inadvertent energy and no payment is made; applicable charges are recovered per regulation. This provision allows producers to "over-size" the DC array (e.g., 1.2x contracted AC) to account for equipment losses and weather variability, while protecting the grid from over-injection.

KEY TOPICS: DC Capacity, AC Output, Contracted Capacity, Inverter, Oversizing, 15-Minute Time Block, Delivery Point, Inadvertent Energy, Grid Constraint, Final AC Output

TEXT:

XXII. The RE power producer can install DC capacity without restriction of contracted capacity subject to grid constraint. The final AC output in no instance shall be more than the AC contracted capacity as specified in PPA. It is to clarify that in any 15 min time block during entire term of PPA, the injected power shall not exceed contracted AC capacity. In case supply of power at delivery point exceed the contracted capacity in any time block, such excess energy shall be considered as inadvertent energy and applicable charges will be recovered as per regulation and no payment shall be made for such excess injection of energy.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Definition of "Contracted Capacity", Article 3.1(XIII) (Capacity injection limits), Article 5.1(VI) (Excess CUF not paid for)

HIERARCHY: Article 3 – Obligations / Section 3.3

PROVISION: Article 3.3 – Liquidated Damages for Delay in Commissioning

TITLE: Penalty Schedule and Capacity Reduction for Failure to Achieve SCSD

SUMMARY:
The project must be fully commissioned on or before the Scheduled Commencement of Supply Date (SCSD), as physically inspected and certified by GEDA. Delays trigger liquidated damages: (1) Delay of 0–180 days from SCSD: PBG is encashed on a per-day basis at Rs 5,555 per MW per day, prorated to the contracted capacity not yet operational. (2) Delay beyond 180 days (unless caused by Force Majeure, material DISCOM/GETCO default, or transmission facility delays attributable to STU/GETCO/DISCOM): The RE producer is deemed in default per Article 9.2.1(a); contracted capacity is reduced to only the capacity commissioned within the 180-day window; PBG for the uncommissioned balance is encashed as liquidated damages. Minimum commissioned capacity must be ≥0.5 MW, or total capacity is cancelled. (3) The STU cancels the unachieved capacity allotment; the producer has no claim on cancelled capacity and must pay relinquishment charges determined by GERC. Cancelled capacity is added to spare capacity lists published by STU/GUVNL on the AkshayUrja Setu portal for prospective consumers.

KEY TOPICS: Liquidated Damages, SCSD, Delay, Performance Bank Guarantee, Rs 5,555/MW/day, 180 Days, Capacity Reduction, Minimum 0.5 MW, Force Majeure Exception, DISCOM Default Exception, Transmission Delay Exception, STU Cancellation, Relinquishment Charges

TEXT:

3.3 Liquidated Damages for Delay in Commissioning the Small Solar Project.

I. The Project shall be fully commissioned on or before the Scheduled Commercial Operation Date (SCSD). In this regard, GEDA-State Nodal Agency will physically inspect and certify successful commissioning of the Project.

II. Delay in commissioning beyond SCSD up to 180 days from SCSD shall lead encashment of Performance Bank Guarantee (PBG) on per-day basis at the rate Rs 5555 per MW per day and proportionate to the contracted capacity that has not commenced supply of power. In case the commissioning of the project is delayed beyond 180 days from SCSD, due to reasons other than mentioned below, the RE power producers' Event of Default as per Article No 9.2.1 (a) of PPA shall be considered to have occurred and the contracted capacity shall stand reduced to the project capacity commissioned up to 180 days from SCSD and bank guarantee proportionate to the balanced capacity not commissioned shall be encashed as liquidated damages. In any case, project commissioned capacity shall not be less than 0.5 MW. 

The project cannot commissioned by SCSD because of Force Majeure event. 
The RE power producer is prevented from performing its obligations because of material default on part of DISCOM/GETCO. 
RE power producer is unable to achieve commercial operation on SCSD because of delay in transmission facilities / evacuation systems for reasons solely attributable to the GETCO / DISCOM. 

III. The STU shall cancel the capacity allotment to the extent of capacity not commissioned and the RE power producer shall have no claim on such capacity and pay relinquishment charges as determined by GERC. Further, STU shall include such cancelled capacity in the list of spare available capacity for RE integration to be published on their website/ "AkshayUrja Setu" for prospective consumers. In case of commissioned capacity less than 0.5 MW upto 180 days from SCSD, total project capacity shall be cancelled with levy of applicable charges.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Article 9.2.1(a) (Default if SCSD missed beyond 180 days), Article 4.6 (PBG amount, validity, and return upon commissioning), Article 8.1(a)(xi) (Connectivity delays as Force Majeure)

CROSS-REFERENCES: GERC relinquishment charges determination, AkshayUrja Setu capacity publishing procedures
