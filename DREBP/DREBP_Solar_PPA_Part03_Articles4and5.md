HIERARCHY: Article 4 – Synchronization, Commissioning & Commercial Operation / Section 4.1

PROVISION: Article 4.1 – Delay in Commencement of Supply Due to Transmission System Delays

TITLE: SCSD Extension and Penalty Pass-Through for Transmission Infrastructure Delays

SUMMARY:
The RE power producer bears primary responsibility for obtaining grid connectivity up to the Delivery Point prior to commencing power supply. If, after connectivity is granted, the STU/GETCO/DISCOM delays the operationalization of connectivity, the readiness of the GETCO substation at the Delivery Point, or the power evacuation/transmission infrastructure until the SCSD, and three conditions are met – (1) RE producer fully complied with application procedures as per detailed STU/GETCO/DISCOM procedures; (2) RE producer adhered to applicable CEA/GERC regulations/procedures; (3) the delay is attributable to the STU/transmission licensee/DISCOM and beyond producer control – then GUVNL/DISCOM may extend the SCSD after case-by-case examination. Conversely, if delay in power supply commencement is attributable to the RE producer, resulting in liquidated damages/penalties levied on GUVNL/DISCOM (including transmission charges under GERC regulations), such damages shall be passed on to the RE producer for reimbursement.

KEY TOPICS: Transmission System Delay, SCSD Extension, Connectivity Operationalization, Substation Readiness, Evacuation Infrastructure, STU/GETCO Responsibility, RE Producer Compliance, Case-by-Case Extension, Penalty Pass-Through, Producer Attributable Delay

TEXT:

4.1 Delay in Commencement of supply on account of delay in transmission system 

For sale of power to DISCOM/GUVNL from SCSD/COD, the responsibility of obtaining Connectivity up to the delivery point shall be of the RE power producer prior to commencement of supply of power from the Project. Subsequent to grant of connectivity, in case there is a delay in grant/operationalization of connectivity by the STU/GETCO/DISCOM and/or there is a delay in readiness of the STU/GETCO/DISCOM substation Delivery Point, including readiness of the power evacuation and transmission infrastructure of the STU/GETCO/DISCOM network until SCSD of the Project, and it is established that: 

I. The RE power producer has complied with the complete application formalities as per the Detailed Procedure as issued by the STU/GETCO/DISCOM, 

II. The RE power producer has adhered to the applicable regulations/ procedures in this regard as notified by the CEA/GERC, and 

III. The delay in grant of connectivity by the STU/GETCO/DISCOM and/or delay in readiness of the STU/GETCO/DISCOM substation at the Delivery Point, including readiness of the power evacuation and transmission infrastructure of the STU/GETCO/DISCOM network, is a factor attributable to the STU/transmission licensee/DISCOM and is beyond the control of the RE power producer; 

In case of delay in commencement of power supply from the Project due to reasons beyond the reasonable control of the RE power producer, GUVNL/DISCOM may extend the SCSD after examining the issue on a case-to-case basis. Further, in case of delay in commencement of power supply on account of reasons solely attributable to the RE power producer, resulting in any liquidated damages/penalty levied on the GUVNL/DISCOM including Transmission charges under the applicable regulation as notified by GERC, such damages/penalty shall be passed on to the RE power producer.

HIERARCHY: Article 4 – Synchronization, Commissioning & Commercial Operation / Section 4.2

PROVISION: Article 4.2 – Term of Agreement and Extension Provisions

TITLE: PPA Effective Date, 25-Year Term, and Mutual Extension Mechanism

SUMMARY:
The PPA becomes effective upon execution and delivery by all parties (Effective Date). The agreement remains valid from the Effective Date until the Expiry Date (25 years from COD) or the lifespan of the solar project as defined by GERC/Ministry of Power/MNRE, whichever is earlier. The parties may extend the PPA for an additional period on mutually agreed terms and conditions, provided notice of intent to extend is given at least 180 days before the Expiry Date. After expiry, the RE producer is free to continue operating the plant if other conditions (land lease rights, permits, approvals, clearances) remain valid, but DISCOM/GUVNL is not obligated to procure power beyond the Expiry Date unless a new agreement is signed. After PPA signing, the RE producer may change the project location (land parcel) subject to Article 2(IV) constraints up to the SCSD, but the SCSD date remains fixed. Location change requires a supplementary PPA (SPPA) amendment; however, the interconnection point (grid connection location) cannot be changed once specified in the connectivity grant.

KEY TOPICS: Effective Date, PPA Term, 25 Years, Expiry Date, COD, Project Lifespan, GERC/MoP/MNRE Definition, Mutual Extension, 180 Days Notice, Post-Expiry Operation, Procurer Obligation Limit, Location Change, SCSD Fixed, Interconnection Point, Supplementary PPA

TEXT:

4.2 Term of Agreement 

I. This Agreement shall come into effect from the date of its execution by all the parties and such date shall be referred to as the Effective Date. 

II. Agreement shall be valid for a term from the Effective Date until the Expiry Date or the lifespan of solar project as defined by GERC/MoP/MNRE whichever is earlier. This Agreement may be extended for a further period at least one hundred eighty (180) days prior to the Expiry Date, on mutually agreed terms and conditions. 

III. The RE power producer is free to operate their plants beyond the Expiry Date if other conditions like land lease / Right to Use of Land (as applicable), permits, approvals and clearances etc. allow. In such case unless otherwise agreed by DISCOM/GUVNL, DISCOM/GUVNL shall not be obligated to procure power beyond the Expiry Date. 

IV. After signing of PPA, RE power producer can change project location subject to Article 2 (IV) of this PPA up to SCSD. The project SCSD shall remain unchanged. SPPA will be executed for change in location. The change in interconnection point shall not be permitted.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Definition of "Effective Date" (not explicitly defined but referenced as execution date), Definition of "Expiry Date" (25 years from COD), Definition of "COD", Article 2.IV (Project splitting constraints on location change), Article 12.VIII (Amendments and SPPA procedure)

HIERARCHY: Article 4 – Synchronization, Commissioning & Commercial Operation / Section 4.3

PROVISION: Article 4.3(I) through (VI) – Synchronization and Commissioning Process

TITLE: Project Synchronization Procedures, Approvals, and Grid Connection Testing

SUMMARY:
The RE producer must commission the project by the SCSD. The producer must obtain Developer and/or Transfer Permission and Key Plan Drawing from GEDA if required. The producer must give SLDC/ALDC/DISCOM/GUVNL at least 15 days' advance written notice of the intended synchronization date. Synchronization may proceed once the project meets all Grid Code conditions and regulatory/legal requirements. Synchronization equipment, Remote Terminal Unit (RTU) for power scheduling/data transmission per applicable regulations, must be installed by the producer at its cost at the generation facility; synchronization may occur only after DISCOM/GETCO/SLDC/ALDC and GEDA approval. After each synchronization or generator trip, the producer must immediately inform the grid substation per Grid Code. The producer will inject in-firm (test) power from time to time for operational/functional testing prior to commercial operation; this test power is not paid for (inadvertent). Importantly, synchronization is explicitly not deemed commissioning; full commissioning requires energy flow witnessing and GEDA certification.

KEY TOPICS: Synchronization, Commissioning, SCSD, GEDA Permission, Key Plan Drawing, 15 Days Notice, SLDC/ALDC Notice, Grid Code Compliance, Synchronization Equipment, RTU, Remote Terminal Unit, Scheduling Data Transmission, Approval, Test Power, Inadvertent Power, Grid Substation Notification, GEDA Certification, Commissioning vs. Synchronization Distinction

TEXT:

4.3 Synchronization & Commissioning 

I. The RE power producer shall commission the project within SCSD. 

II. The RE power producer shall be required to obtain Developer and/or transfer permission, Key plan drawing etc from GEDA, if required. 

III. The RE power producer shall give at least fifteen (15) days written notice in advance to the SLDC / ALDC / DISCOM/GUVNL as the case may be, of the date on which it intends to synchronize the power project to the Grid system. 

IV. The Power Project may be synchronized by the RE power producer to the Grid System when it meets all the conditions prescribed in the Grid Code along with all other regulatory and Indian legal requirements for synchronization to the Grid system. 

V. The synchronization equipment and all necessary arrangements / equipment including Remote Terminal Unit (RTU) for scheduling of power generated from the Project and transmission of data to the concerned authority as per applicable regulation shall be installed by the RE power producer at its generation facility of the Power Project at its own cost. The RE power producer shall synchronize its system with the Grid System only after the approval of DISCOM/ GETCO / SLDC / ALDC and GEDA. 

VI. The RE power producer shall immediately after each synchronization/tripping of generator, inform the sub-station of the Grid System to which the Power Project is electrically connected in accordance with applicable Grid Code. In addition, the RE power producer will inject in-firm power to grid from time to time to carry out operational/ functional test prior to commercial operation which will be considered as inadvertent and not paid . For avoidance of doubt, it is clarified that Synchronization / Connectivity of the Project with the grid shall not to be considered as Commissioning of the Project.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Definition of "Commissioning" (equipment installed, energy flowed, witnessing), Definition of "COD", Definition of "SCSD", Definition of "SLDC", Definition of "ALDC"

PROVISION: Article 4.4 – Forecasting and Scheduling Requirements

TITLE: Compliance with GERC Forecasting and Scheduling Regulations for Solar Generation

SUMMARY:
The RE power producer must comply with Forecasting and Scheduling procedures as specified in GERC regulations from time to time. Per GERC's Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources Regulations 2019, forecasting, scheduling, and deviation settlement procedures apply to all solar RE producers with combined capacity exceeding 1 MW connected to the State Grid or sub-station, including those connected via pooling stations. Any deviation from the agreed schedule attracts financial consequences per applicable regulations/guidelines/directions; such financial implications (deviation charges, imbalance charges) are borne entirely by the RE power producer. This ensures predictable grid operations and penalizes unpredictable generation patterns.

KEY TOPICS: Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement, GERC Regulations, Solar Generation, Combined Capacity >1 MW, SLDC Procedures, Pooling Stations, Deviation Charges, Imbalance Charges, Grid Predictability, Applicable Regulations

TEXT:

4.4 Forecasting and Scheduling 

I. The RE power producer shall be required to follow the Forecasting and Scheduling procedures as per the Regulations issued by GERC from time to time. It is to clarify that in terms of GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019 the procedures for Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation settlement are applicable to all solar RE power producer having combined capacity above 1 MW connected to the State Grid / Sub-station including those connected via pooling stations. 

II. Any deviation from the schedule will attract the provisions of applicable regulation / guidelines / directions and any financial implication on account of this shall be to the account of the RE power producer.

HIERARCHY: Article 4 – Synchronization, Commissioning & Commercial Operation / Section 4.5

PROVISION: Article 4.5 – Early Commissioning and Part Commissioning in Phases

TITLE: Phased Project Commissioning with Minimum 0.5 MW Tranches and Same Tariff

SUMMARY:
Early commissioning of projects is permitted if connectivity and land use rights are available for the early-commissioned capacity. Projects may be commissioned in phased tranches until the SCSD, with a minimum capacity of 0.5 MW per tranche. Tariff for early-commissioned capacity and part-commissioned capacity remains the same as the PPA tariff (no discount for early commissioning, no premium for phased approach). This flexibility allows producers to monetize portions of their project before full capacity is ready, while the DISCOM benefit from earlier power supply at the same agreed tariff. Each commissioned tranche's energy is metered and billed separately.

KEY TOPICS: Early Commissioning, Part Commissioning, Phased Commissioning, 0.5 MW Minimum, Connectivity Available, Land Use Rights, SCSD, Same Tariff, Early Operation Benefit, Flexible Commissioning

TEXT:

4.5 Early Commissioning & Part Commissioning 

I. Early commissioning of the projects are allowed subjected to availability of establishment of connectivity and land use rights for the early commissioned capacity of the project. Tariff for early commissioned project shall be same as PPA tariff. 

II. Part commissioning of the RE projects in phased manner till the date of SCSD will be permitted subject to minimum capacity 0.5 MW. Tariff for part commissioned project shall be same as PPA tariff.

PROVISION: Article 4.7 – Restrictions on Use of Fossil Fuels

TITLE: Prohibition on Fossil Fuel-Based Power Generation and Grid Injection

SUMMARY:
Post-commissioning, during the entire PPA term, the RE power producer shall not use fossil fuels for power generation and injection into the grid. This provision ensures that the solar project remains a pure renewable energy source without hybrid fossil fuel backup or supplementary generation that would compromise the renewable energy character of the facility and violate the intent of the DREBP scheme and Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023.

HIERARCHY: Article 5 – CUF, Tariff, Charges and Compensation / Section 5.1

PROVISION: Article 5.1(I) through (VIII) – Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF) Calculation, Targets, and Penalties

TITLE: CUF Declaration, Maintenance Requirements, Minimum/Maximum Bands, Shortfall Penalty, and Excess Generation Exclusion

SUMMARY:
The contracted CUF of the project is declared upfront in the PPA (blank to be filled). CUF is calculated annually from April 1 to March 31 and must be declared upfront, with a minimum of 19% for solar projects (Article 5.1(II)). The producer must maintain annual supply corresponding to CUF within 90%–120% of the declared CUF value during the 25-year PPA term. The lower limit (90% of declared) is relaxable by GUVNL/DISCOM to the extent that grid non-availability is beyond the producer's control. CUF criteria are not applied in the year of COD or year of PPA termination/expiry. The producer may revise the CUF once within the first year after COD, but the revised CUF cannot be less than 19%. If actual CUF falls below the minimum (90% of declared), a penalty is levied at 1.5× the PPA tariff on the shortfall generation (equivalent to declared CUF minus actual CUF, applied to contracted capacity). This penalty does not apply during Force Majeure events affecting power supply. Excess generation beyond the maximum CUF (120% of declared) is treated as inadvertent energy with no payment. DISCOM/GUVNL is not obligated to purchase generation beyond the CUF limit in any contract year.

KEY TOPICS: CUF, Capacity Utilization Factor, Annual Calculation, April–March Fiscal Year, Minimum 19%, Declared CUF, 90% Minimum Band, 120% Maximum Band, Grid Non-Availability Relaxation, Revision Once/Year 1, 1.5× Tariff Penalty, Shortfall Penalty, Inadvertent Excess Energy, Force Majeure Exception, DISCOM Obligation Limit

TEXT:

5.1 CUF 

I. The contracted CUF of this project is ____%. 

II. CUF of the project shall be declared upfront in the PPA which shall in no case be less than 19% for the solar project. Calculation of CUF will be on yearly basis from 1st April of the year to 31st March of next year. RE power producer shall maintain energy supply so as to achieve annual supply corresponding to CUF not less than 90% of the declared value (i.e., Minimum CUF) and not more than 120% of the declared CUF value (i.e., Maximum CUF), during the PPA duration of 25 years. The lower limit will, however, be relax-able by GUVNL/DISCOM to the extent of non-availability of grid for evacuation which is beyond the control of the RE power producer. 

III. CUF criteria will not be applicable to year of COD and year of termination/expiry of PPA. 

IV. RE power producer will be allowed to revise the CUF once within first year after COD. However, revised CUF shall not be less than 19% as mentioned in 5.1 (II). 

V. In case of shortfall on CUF beyond minimum CUF specified in 5.1 (II) above, penalty shall be levied at the rate of 1.5 Times of PPA tariff for the shortfall generation corresponding to PPA specified CUF for the contracted capacity of the project. However, this penalty shall not be applicable in events of Force Majeure identified under Article – 8 of PPA with DISCOM/GUVNL affecting supply of power by RE power producer. 

VI. Excess injection of energy generation beyond maximum stipulated CUF specified in above 5.1 (II) corresponding to the PPA contracted capacity shall be considered as inadvertent flow of energy and no payment shall be made towards such excess energy. 

VII. The RE power producer shall not sell the power from Contracted Capacity to any other third party including Power Exchanges. In case the RE power producer fails to offer the contracted power as per the agreement to DISCOM/GUVNL and sells this power, the damages shall be recovered from RE power producer for an amount equal to higher of : 

(a) 1.5 times the tariff as per the PPA for the corresponding contracted power; and 

(b) The entire sale revenue accrued from Third parties on account of sale of the contracted power. 

These damages shall be in addition to other penalties / liquidated damages specified in the PPA. 

VIII. DISCOM/GUVNL shall not be obliged to purchase power generated from the project beyond CUF limit as mentioned in clause No (II) above in a contract year.

PROVISION: Article 5.2(I) through (VIII) – Tariff for Delivered Energy and Accelerated Depreciation (AD) Options

TITLE: Firm Tariff per GERC Order 06 of 2024, AD Benefit Selection, Tax Return Verification, and Tariff Control Period

SUMMARY:
DISCOM/GUVNL shall pay the RE producer monthly for Delivered Energy as certified in the SEA by Gujarat SLDC from COD onwards, for the full PPA term. The tariff is firm throughout the PPA period (no escalation). Per GERC Tariff Order No. 6 of 2024 (read with clauses 3.2(b) and (e)), the applicable tariff for solar projects <5 MW is: Levelized Tariff Rs 2.76/Unit, AD Benefit Rs 0.28/Unit, Net Tariff Rs 2.48/Unit. The producer may elect to avail or not avail Accelerated Depreciation (AD) benefits. If availing AD at 40% (with or without additional depreciation), GUVNL will consider the AD benefit of Rs 0.28/Unit (net tariff Rs 2.48/Unit). If not availing AD, the producer receives the higher levelized tariff of Rs 2.76/Unit. The producer must submit an Undertaking (Annexure-D) on Rs 300 stamp paper selecting the option. The producer must furnish income tax returns with CA certificate annually to verify whether AD benefits have been availed. If the producer fails to submit IT returns, it is presumed AD benefits were availed from that year onwards. If the producer elected "does not desire AD benefits" (higher tariff) but later avails AD benefits, the tariff is retroactively amended to the lower rate from the initial date, effective for the entire PPA term, with recovery from the producer. The tariff control period under GERC Order 06 of 2024 ends March 31, 2027. Projects commissioned by March 31, 2027 receive the specified tariff. Projects commissioned after March 31, 2027 receive the lower of: (i) new GERC tariff order (post-March 2027) or (ii) GERC Order 06 of 2024 tariff (Rs 2.48 or 2.76, whichever applies).

Energy certified in the SEA by SLDC constitutes the official energy record for billing. All other ABT provisions, Open Access regulations, and GERC/CERC amendments apply. Additionally, GERC Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources Regulations 2019 (as amended) are applicable, defining forecasting, scheduling, and deviation charge procedures for projects >1 MW.

PROVISION: Article 5.4 – Compensation for Transmission/Evacuation Unavailability

TITLE: No Compensation Payable for Transmission Infrastructure Unavailability During Operation

SUMMARY:
In the event of unavailability of transmission or evacuation infrastructure during the project's operation period (post-COD), no compensation shall be payable by the power procurer (DISCOM/GUVNL) to the RE producer. This means that if GETCO's transmission system or DISCOM's evacuation lines are unavailable due to maintenance, failure, or grid constraints, preventing the producer from injecting power or reducing the amount of power that can be injected, the producer bears the commercial consequence and receives no compensation for lost revenue. This is a risk allocation favoring the procurer and reflects the assumption that transmission availability is the responsibility of the STU/GETCO and is not within the producer's control.
