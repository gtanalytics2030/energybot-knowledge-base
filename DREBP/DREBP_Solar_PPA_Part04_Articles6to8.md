HIERARCHY: Article 6 – Billing and Payment / Section 6.1

PROVISION: Article 6.1 – Monthly Billing Based on SEA Energy Certification

TITLE: Monthly Billing Schedule and Three-Year Limitation Period for Bill Raising

SUMMARY:
Billing is conducted on a monthly basis. DISCOM/GUVNL is billed by the RE power producer based on energy certified in the SEA (State Energy Account) of Gujarat by SLDC at the end of each month for the energy supplied. The billing follows the energy certification issued by SLDC in the SEA document. Any bill raised after a period of three years from the date of SEA certification is barred by limitation and shall not be payable. This three-year bar prevents the producer from claiming unpaid energy from the distant past, encouraging timely billing and settlement.

HIERARCHY: Article 6 – Billing and Payment / Section 6.3

PROVISION: Article 6.3 – Late Payment Charges for Overdue Invoices

TITLE: Penalty Interest on DISCOM's Late Tariff Payments

SUMMARY:
If DISCOM/GUVNL fails to pay the monthly bill by the Due Date of Payment (30 days from invoice receipt), a late payment charge is levied on the outstanding amount. The rate is calculated as: SBI 1-Year Marginal Cost of Funds Based Lending Rate (MCLR) + 7% per annum, computed on a week-or-part-thereof basis (i.e., divided by 52). Example: if SBI 1-Year MCLR is 6.5%, the rate becomes 13.5% per annum, or approximately 0.26% per week. This incentivizes timely payment and compensates the producer for time-value-of-money costs and financing charges during the delay period. The rate adjusts automatically if SBI replaces the MCLR benchmark with a different rate.

KEY TOPICS: Late Payment Charge, Overdue Invoice, SBI MCLR, Marginal Cost of Funds, 7% Excess, Per Annum, Weekly Calculation, 52 Weeks, Outstanding Amount, Penalty Interest, Incentive for Timely Payment

TEXT:

6.3 Late Payment 

For payment of Monthly bill by DISCOM/GUVNL, if paid after Due Date of Payment, a late payment charge shall be payable by DISCOM to the RE power producer at the rate of seven (7) percent in excess of SBI 1 year Marginal Cost of Funds Based Lending Rate (MCLR) per annum / any replacement thereof by SBI, on the amount of outstanding payment, calculated on a week or part thereof basis viz. 

(SBI MCLR rate + 7%) 
= 52 per week or part thereof.

PROVISION: Article 6.4 – Early Payment Rebate for Advance DISCOM Payments

TITLE: Interest Rebate for Early/Advance Payment by Distribution Company

SUMMARY:
Conversely, if DISCOM/GUVNL pays the monthly bill before the Due Date of Payment, a rebate (discount) is deducted by DISCOM from the invoice amount. This rebate compensates the producer for early payment with a financial benefit equivalent to the time-value advantage gained by DISCOM from the early cash inflow. The symmetry in the late payment and early payment rates encourages efficient cash flow management on both sides.

PROVISION: Article 6.5(I) through (III) – Supplementary Bills and Adjustment Mechanisms

TITLE: Additional Billing for Energy Account Adjustments and Same Payment Terms as Monthly Bills

SUMMARY:
Either party may raise a Supplementary Bill for adjustments required due to corrections in the Energy Accounts. Such supplementary bills are paid by the other party. Both parties must remit amounts due under supplementary bills to the designated account by the Due Date (30 days from receipt). Rebate and Late Payment Surcharge provisions applicable to monthly bills (Articles 6.3 and 6.4) apply identically to supplementary bills: late payment attracts SBI MCLR + 7% per annum, early payment qualifies for the same rate rebate. Supplementary bills typically arise from meter re-reading, energy account corrections, or retroactive adjustments identified after the original month-end billing.

HIERARCHY: Article 6 – Billing and Payment / Section 6.6

PROVISION: Article 6.6(I) through (VII) – Bill Dispute Procedures and Interim Payment Obligation

TITLE: Structured Dispute Resolution for Monthly and Supplementary Bills with 100% Undisputed Amount Payment

SUMMARY:
Bills not disputed by DISCOM/GUVNL within 30 days of receipt are conclusively deemed valid and accepted. If DISCOM/GUVNL disputes a bill, it must pay 100% of the undisputed portion and, within 15 days, issue a Bill Dispute Notice specifying the disputed amount, its estimate of the correct amount, and supporting materials. If the producer agrees, it revises the bill and presents the corrected version with the next bill; excess paid is refunded with interest at the late-payment-charge rate (SBI MCLR + 7%) from the date of overpayment to refund date. If the producer disagrees, it issues a Bill Disagreement Notice within 15 days, providing reasons, its estimate of the correct amount, and supporting materials. Authorized representatives of DISCOM and the producer then meet for 15 days to amicably resolve the dispute. If unresolved, the matter proceeds to Dispute Resolution per Article 10 (GERC adjudication if unresolved within 60 days of initial dispute). Throughout this process, DISCOM must pay the undisputed 100% of the bill amount without prejudice to its dispute rights.

KEY TOPICS: Bill Dispute, 30-Day No-Dispute Period, Conclusive Validity, Undisputed Amount, 100% Payment, Bill Dispute Notice, 15 Days, Estimate Correct Amount, Producer Agreement, Bill Revision, Excess Refund, Interest, SBI MCLR + 7%, Bill Disagreement Notice, Amicable Resolution, 15-Day Negotiation, Dispute Resolution Article 10, GERC Adjudication

TEXT:

6.6 Disputed Bills 

I. If DISCOM/GUVNL does not dispute a Monthly Bill or a Supplementary Bill raised by the RE power producer within thirty (30) days of receiving such Bill shall be taken as conclusive. 

II. If DISCOM/GUVNL disputes the amount payable under a Monthly Bill or a Supplementary Bill, as the case may be, it shall pay 100% undisputed amount of the invoice amount and it shall within fifteen days of receiving such Bill, issue a notice (the "Bill Dispute Notice") to the invoicing Party setting out: 

 The details of the disputed amount; 
 Its estimate of what the correct amount should be; and 
 All written material in support of its claim. 

III. If the RE power producer agrees to the claim raised in the Bill Dispute Notice, the RE power producer shall revise such Bill and present along with the next Monthly Bill. In such a case excess amount shall be refunded along with interest at the same rate as Late Payment Surcharge, which shall be applied from the date on which such excess payment was made by the disputing Party to the invoicing Party and up to and including the date on which such payment has been received as refund. 

IV. If the RE power producer does not agree to the claim raised in the Bill Dispute Notice, it shall, within fifteen (15) days of receiving the Bill Dispute Notice, furnish a notice (Bill Disagreement Notice) to DISCOM/GUVNL providing: 

 Reasons for its disagreement; 
 Its estimate of what the correct amount should be; and 
 All written material in support of its counter claim. 

V. Upon receipt of the Bill Disagreement Notice by DISCOM/GUVNL, authorized representative(s) of DISCOM/GUVNL and RE power producer shall meet and make best endeavours to amicably resolve such dispute within fifteen (15) days of receipt of the Bill Disagreement Notice. 

VI. If the Parties do not amicably resolve the Dispute within fifteen (15) days of receipt of Bill Disagreement Notice, the matter shall be referred to Dispute resolution in accordance with Article 10 of PPA. 

VII. For the avoidance of doubt, it is clarified the despite a Dispute regarding an invoice, DISCOM/GUVNL shall, without prejudice to its right to Dispute, be under an obligation to make payment of undisputed amount of the invoice amount in the Monthly Bill.

TITLE: ABT-Compliant Metering, Automatic Meter Reading, and Shared Evacuation Line Governance

SUMMARY:
(I) The Delivery Point is at the receiving end of the GETCO substation. For energy accounting, the RE producer must install ABT-compliant meters equipped with Automatic Meter Reading (AMR) at all metering points at the producer's cost. (II) Common dedicated evacuation/transmission lines with pooling stations may be set up by adjoining RE producers per applicable CEA technical standards and best practices, with metering at each producer's end and a common meter at the pooling station sending end and interconnection point. After commissioning, new producers can connect to such shared infrastructure with consent of all connected producers and mutually agreed terms. Energy injection is calculated proportionally using common metering data apportioned per meter readings at each producer's sending end meter (done by DISCOM/SLDC/ALDC). Key sharing conditions include: (i) DREBP applicant can share common lines with existing/new captive or third-party users; (ii) applicant must build evacuation system matching project capacity but develop 4 MW capacity between GETCO substation first gentry and 11 KV panel; (iii) in simultaneous applications, applicants mutually designate or first applicant (per timestamp) becomes "lead generator"; (iv) lead generator may share infrastructure with other renewables if not fully utilizing dedicated transmission/bay capacity; (v) lead generator acts on behalf of all connected generators for operational/commercial responsibilities per Gujarat Grid Code, including dispatch, transmission charges, deviation charges, congestion charges; (vi) all connectivity grantees share costs of developing common network, infrastructure, connectivity/LTA fees, and statutory requirements (generators decide sharing modalities); (vii) lead generator cannot deny subsequent generators access to common infrastructure; all applicants sign common agreement/consent; (viii) RE generators connect via lead generator's pooling station or tap the lead generator's dedicated line between pooling station and GETCO substation; (ix) all RE generators must install appropriate ABT meters enabling SLDC energy accounting per prevailing regulations/orders.

HIERARCHY: Article 8 – Force Majeure / Section 8.1

PROVISION: Article 8.1(a) and (b) – Force Majeure Events and Exclusions

TITLE: Comprehensive Force Majeure Clause with Enumerated Events and Specific Exclusions

SUMMARY:
Force Majeure events are circumstances beyond the reasonable control of an affected party that excuse performance of non-monetary obligations and delay milestones, but do NOT suspend payment obligations incurred before the force majeure event. Enumerated force majeure events include: (i) acts of God; (ii) typhoons, floods, lightning, cyclone, hurricane, drought, famine, epidemic, plague, other natural calamities; (iii) acts of war (declared/undeclared), invasion, civil unrest; (iv) court/judicial orders (provided not caused by party's breach/default); (v) inability to obtain/renew/maintain required licenses/approvals despite legal compliance; (vi) earthquakes, explosions, accidents, landslides, fire; (vii) expropriation/compulsory acquisition by government; (viii) chemical/radioactive contamination; (ix) damage to/breakdown of transmission facilities of GETCO/DISCOMs; (x) exceptionally adverse weather exceeding 100-year statistical measure; (xi) delay in STU connectivity/LTA grant or InSTS (Interstate Transmission System) substation/evacuation infrastructure readiness until SCSD, contingent on: (a) InSTS substation and evacuation infrastructure scheduled for completion by SCSD; (b) RE producer applied for connectivity at facility scheduled for that SCSD; (c) RE producer complied with connectivity application formalities; (d) RE producer adhered to GERC/STU procedures. Force Majeure explicitly excludes (unless consequences of an enumerated FM event): (1) unavailability/late delivery/cost increases of plants, machinery, equipment, materials, spares, consumables; (2) contractor/subcontractor performance delays; (3) normal wear/tear; (4) labor strikes/disturbances; (5) insufficient finances/agreement becoming onerous; (6) non-performance by party's negligent/intentional acts, errors, omissions; failure to comply with Indian law; or breach/default under this or other agreements.

PROVISION: Article 8.1(c) and (d) – FM Notification Requirements and Ongoing Mitigation

TITLE: Notice Obligations and Continuous Remediation During Force Majeure Events

SUMMARY:
The affected party must notify the other party of any force majeure event as soon as reasonably practicable, but not later than 15 days after the party knew or should have reasonably known of its commencement. If force majeure breaks down communication, notice is given as soon as practicable after communication reinstatement, but not later than one day thereafter. The FM notice must include full particulars of the event, its effects on the affected party, and proposed remedial measures. The affected party must provide regular (at least monthly) progress reports on remedial measures and any information reasonably requested by the other party. FM notice is a precondition for claiming FM relief under the PPA. After FM ends, the affected party must notify cessation of the FM event and cessation of its effects on performance under the agreement. To the extent not prevented by FM, the affected party continues performing obligations; it shall use reasonable efforts to mitigate FM effects as soon as practicable. These provisions prevent parties from claiming indefinite relief and encourage proactive resolution.

PROVISION: Article 8.2 – Available Relief and Limitations for Force Majeure Events

TITLE: Performance Excuse Without Tariff Adjustment; Pre-FM Payment Obligations Continue

SUMMARY:
Neither party is in breach of its PPA obligations to the extent that performance was prevented, hindered, or delayed by a force majeure event. However, no tariff adjustment is allowed on account of force majeure. Neither party's obligation to pay money due or payable prior to the FM event is suspended or excused by FM. The event specified in Article 8.1(a)(xi) – transmission system/connectivity/evacuation infrastructure delay – and consequent delay in SCSD shall be treated as delay beyond the parties' control; both parties are eligible for suitable time extension in the SCSD. This means: (1) performance excuse applies only to non-monetary obligations (commissioning, operation); (2) financial obligations (tariff, fees) continue regardless of FM; (3) the procurer cannot claim tariff reduction due to FM; (4) connectivity delays attributable to STU trigger SCSD extension, not liquidated damages or default.
