PROVISION: Article 9.1 – PPA Term (25 Years) and Survival Clause

TITLE: Agreement Effective Date, 25-Year Term from COD, and Post-Expiry Obligation Survival

SUMMARY:
The PPA becomes effective upon execution and delivery by all parties and shall continue for 25 years from the Commercial Operation Date of the Project, unless terminated earlier per the agreement's termination provisions. The parties may extend the agreement for a further period on mutually agreed terms and conditions at least 180 days before the Expiry Date. Upon expiry or termination, accrued rights, obligations, and liabilities continue; the right to receive penalties per PPA terms survives. Continuing obligations that expressly survive expiry/termination include: Article 8 (Force Majeure), Article 9 (Term, Termination, Default), Article 10 (Dispute Resolution), Article 11 (Indemnity), Article 12 (Miscellaneous Provisions), and any other articles/schedules that expressly or necessarily survive. This ensures that claims for damages, dispute procedures, and indemnification obligations persist beyond the formal PPA term.

9.1 Term of the Agreement: 

This Agreement shall become effective upon the execution and delivery thereof by the Parties hereto and unless terminated pursuant to other provisions of the Agreement, shall continue to be in force for such time until the completion of a period of 25 years (Twenty Five) from the Commercial Operation Date of the Project. This Agreement may be extended for a further period on mutually agreed terms and conditions at least one hundred eighty (180) days prior to the Expiry Date.

PROVISION: Article 9.2.1(a) through (j) – RE Power Producer Default Events

TITLE: Enumerated Default Events Triggering DISCOM Right to Terminate PPA

SUMMARY:
RE power producer default events include: (a) Failure to commission project by SCSD beyond the period in Article 3.3 (180 days + capacity reduction); (b) Failure to supply power per PPA terms; (c) O&M Default (90+ continuous days of non-operation/non-maintenance per Prudent Utility Practice); (d) Failure/refusal to perform any material PPA obligation; (e) Failure to pay any amount due to DISCOM within 3 months after invoice due date; (f) Assignment/transfer of rights or novation of obligations in violation of PPA; (g) Bankruptcy, liquidation, dissolution, receiver/liquidator appointment, unless for merger/consolidation/reorganization where successor has equivalent financial standing and expressly assumes obligations; (h) Repudiation of agreement; (i) Failure to maintain 51% shareholding before one year from SCSD (single company) or 51% combined shareholding (consortium) before one year from SCSD; any change in ownership allowed only after one year from commissioning with prior DISCOM consent; (j) Any other material breach specified in PPA, if unable to cure within the cure period (if any). These defaults empower DISCOM to initiate termination procedures and claim damages.

HIERARCHY: Article 9 – Term, Termination and Default / Section 9.3.1

PROVISION: Article 9.3.1(a) through (c) – Termination for RE Producer Default and Lender Substitution Rights

TITLE: Default Notice, Cure Period, Compensation Calculation, and Lender Substitution Mechanism

SUMMARY:
Upon RE producer default per Article 9.2.1 (except 9.2.1(a) which has Article 3.3 consequences), DISCOM/GUVNL delivers a "Default Notice" specifying the default and calling for remedy. Where the default requires cooperation from both parties, DISCOM shall render all reasonable cooperation to enable remedy without legal obligation. Thirty days from default notice delivery, if the default is not remedied and parties haven't agreed otherwise, the RE producer becomes liable to pay compensation to DISCOM/GUVNL of 6 months' billing at the declared contracted CUF percentage for the contracted capacity. This compensation applies only to termination after project commissioning. DISCOM may recover damages via forfeiture of the performance bank guarantee without prejudice to other legal remedies. If the RE producer defaults, the project lender is entitled to exercise substitution of the producer (with a selectee) per a substitution agreement and in concurrence with DISCOM. If lenders cannot substitute within DISCOM's stipulated period, DISCOM may terminate via a "Termination Notice." Lenders may amend/novate the PPA in favor of a selectee; the defaulting producer must cooperate with substitution and continue operating per the PPA until substitution is finalized. Substitution or fresh PPA with a new entity triggers a Rs 1 lakh per project per transaction facilitation fee (plus 18% GST) payable to DISCOM.

PROVISION: Article 9.3.2 – Termination for DISCOM Default and Producer Exit Rights

TITLE: DISCOM Default Notice, Novation Option, and Producer Termination with Damages Claim

SUMMARY:

DISCOM/GUVNL may, with prior consent of the RE producer, novate its part of the PPA to any third party (including its affiliates) within 7 days following expiry of the notice period. This provision protects RE producers from being stuck in PPAs with insolvent or non-performing DISCOMs.

HIERARCHY: Article 10 – Dispute Resolution

PROVISION: Article 10 – Multi-Stage Dispute Resolution (Negotiation then GERC Adjudication)

TITLE: Bilateral Negotiation Precedent to GERC Regulatory Adjudication

SUMMARY:
All disputes or differences arising out of or in connection with the PPA shall first be settled through mutual negotiation. Both parties agree to attempt prompt, equitable, good-faith resolution. Each party designates in writing an authorized representative to resolve disputes equitably and make binding decisions by mutual agreement. If disputes remain unresolved after 60 days of mutual negotiation, the matter is adjudicated by GERC (Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission) in accordance with law. This two-stage approach encourages commercial settlement while preserving access to independent regulatory adjudication if necessary. GERC has jurisdiction to interpret PPA terms, determine breach, award damages, and direct remedies per its regulations.

KEY TOPICS: Dispute Resolution, Mutual Negotiation, Good Faith, 60 Days, Authorized Representative, GERC Adjudication, Regulatory Jurisdiction, Binding Decisions, Equitable Resolution, Party Designated Representative

HIERARCHY: Schedule-1 – Parameters and Technical Limits of Supply

PROVISION: Schedule-1 Sections 1–5 – Electrical Characteristics, Quality of Service, Reactive Power, Voltage Limits, and Delivery Specifications

TITLE: Electrical and Technical Interface Requirements for Solar Project

SUMMARY:
Schedule-1 specifies the technical parameters and limits for the solar project's interface with the GETCO grid: (1) Electrical Characteristics: Three-phase alternating current, nominal frequency 50.0 Hz, final voltage at delivery point (blank – to be specified per project), short circuit rating calculated by producer and supplied to DISCOM/GETCO (tolerances and insulation levels per relevant grid code/CEA standards). (2) Quality of Service: Producer responsible for delivering energy conforming to GERC-approved Performance Standards for Transmission and Bulk Supply; maximum current/voltage waveform distortion per CEA (Grid Standards) Regulations 2010 (as amended). (3) Power Factor/Reactive Power: Producer capable of supplying dynamically varying reactive power per GERC regulations and GETCO/DISCOM specifications; suitable protection devices to isolate generators on grid failure. Connectivity criteria (short circuit level, neutral grounding, fault clearance, current unbalance, harmonics) per Grid Code. Dynamically varying reactive support via inverters or apparatus (STATCOM, SVC, synchronous condenser) essential during LVRT (Low Voltage Ride Through) or HVRT (High Voltage Ride Through) transients. (4) Technical Limits – Voltage: Nominal steady-state: three-phase AC at 50 Hz ±0.5 Hz, nominal voltage 11 KV or 66 KV.
