**SUMMARY**:

## CHUNK 2: Provisional Registration and Connectivity Portal Application

**DOCUMENT**: Distributed Renewable Energy Procurement (DREBP) – GERC Guidelines (Amended Guideline under GERC Tariff Orders No. 5 & 6 of 2024)

**DOCUMENT TYPE**: Procedural Guidance / Flowchart / Annexure

**HIERARCHY**: Annexure-A / Procedure Step 2 – Provisional Registration and Connectivity Application

**PROVISION**: Annexure-A – Provisional Registration and Portal Application Step

**TITLE**: Application for Provisional Registration with GEDA and Connectivity Request on AkshayUrja Setu Portal

**SUMMARY**:
Following acceptance of the Single Window Portal application, project proponents must apply to GEDA (Gujarat Energy Development Agency) for provisional registration and simultaneously submit a connectivity request through the AkshayUrja Setu portal (the state's renewable energy portal). This dual application establishes the project in GEDA's records and reserves connectivity capacity within the available quantum on a priority basis. Provisional registration precedes final development permission and must be completed before obtaining full grid connectivity, project charging approval, and commissioning authorization. The process ensures that renewable capacity is tracked through GEDA's system and that grid availability constraints are identified early in the project lifecycle.

**KEY TOPICS**: Provisional Registration, GEDA, AkshayUrja Setu Portal, Connectivity Request, Available Quantum, Priority Allocation, Grid Availability, Capacity Reservation

## CHUNK 3: Connectivity Decision Trigger Point and Priority-Based Allocation

**DOCUMENT**: Distributed Renewable Energy Procurement (DREBP) – GERC Guidelines (Amended Guideline under GERC Tariff Orders No. 5 & 6 of 2024)

**DOCUMENT TYPE**: Procedural Guidance / Flowchart / Annexure

**HIERARCHY**: Annexure-A / Procedure Step 3 – Connectivity Confirmation and Agreement Execution

**PROVISION**: Annexure-A – Connectivity Issuance Step

**TITLE**: Issuance of Connectivity by DISCOM/GUVNL in Priority Order

**SUMMARY**:
When grid capacity becomes available, DISCOM (Distribution Company) or GUVNL (Gujarat Urja Vikas Nigam Limited) issues connectivity to eligible projects on a priority basis. The connectivity is issued in the first week of the ensuing month, establishing the formal technical connection point. Upon issuance of connectivity, the project must sign the grid interconnection agreement within 5 days and execute the Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) within 15 days. These tight timelines ensure that allocated capacity is not unnecessarily reserved and that projects with confirmed connectivity proceed rapidly to contractual commitment. Failure to execute within the specified periods may result in loss of allocated capacity or reduction of project scope.

**KEY TOPICS**: Connectivity Issuance, DISCOM, GUVNL, Priority Allocation, Grid Connection, Interconnection Agreement (5-day execution deadline), Power Purchase Agreement (15-day execution deadline), Connectivity Agreement, Capacity Allocation

## CHUNK 4: System-Generated Letter of Award (LoA) Issuance

**DOCUMENT**: Distributed Renewable Energy Procurement (DREBP) – GERC Guidelines (Amended Guideline under GERC Tariff Orders No. 5 & 6 of 2024)

**DOCUMENT TYPE**: Procedural Guidance / Flowchart / Annexure

**HIERARCHY**: Annexure-A / Procedure Step 4 – Letter of Award Issuance

**PROVISION**: Annexure-A – System-Generated LoA Step

**TITLE**: Auto-Generated Letter of Award (LoA) by DISCOM/GUVNL for Grid Interconnection and PPA Execution

**SUMMARY**:
Upon successful validation of the application and confirmation of available grid capacity, DISCOM or GUVNL generates and issues a system-automated Letter of Award (LoA) to the project proponent. The LoA serves as formal notification of project acceptance, allocation of generation capacity, and timeline for completion of key contractual and technical milestones. The LoA explicitly instructs the project proponent to: (1) sign the grid interconnection agreement within 15 days; (2) execute the PPA within a specified period; (3) submit Performance Bank Guarantee (PBG) along with these documents. The LoA acts as the binding commitment from the distribution company and establishes the legal basis for all subsequent contractual and technical obligations, including project commissioning deadlines.

**KEY TOPICS**: Letter of Award (LoA), System-Generated Notification, DISCOM/GUVNL, Grid Interconnection Agreement, Power Purchase Agreement Execution, Performance Bank Guarantee (PBG), Project Capacity Allocation, Contractual Commitment Timeline

**DOCUMENT TYPE**: Procedural Guidance / Flowchart / Annexure

**HIERARCHY**: Annexure-A / Procedure Step 5 – Final Development Permission and GEDA Registration

**PROVISION**: Annexure-A – Final Development Permission Step

**TITLE**: Final Development Permission and Final Registration with GEDA

**SUMMARY**:
After obtaining the LoA, grid interconnection agreement, and PPA, the project proponent must obtain final development permission from relevant authorities (state nodal agency, municipal corporation, forest/revenue departments as applicable) and submit evidence of this permission to GEDA along with the LoA and executed PPA. This step ensures compliance with all land, environmental, and administrative requirements before project commissioning. The final registration with GEDA consolidates the project's formal status in the state's renewable energy database and authorizes GEDA to issue the commissioning certificate and project charging approval from CEI (Central Electricity Authority's designated technical entity). Final development permission is a critical gate that prevents projects lacking proper land tenure, environmental clearance, or administrative authorization from proceeding to the construction and commissioning phases.

**PROVISION**: Annexure-A – CEI Charging Approval and GEDA Commissioning Certificate Step

**TITLE**: Obtaining Project Charging Approval from CEI and Commissioning Certificate from GEDA

**SUMMARY**:
Upon final registration with GEDA, the project must obtain project charging approval from CEI (Central Electricity Authority or designated representative body responsible for technical grid standards) and a commissioning certificate from GEDA confirming that the project has been constructed in compliance with technical specifications and grid connection standards. These dual certifications validate that: (1) the project meets all technical requirements under the GERC Grid Code and GERC Power System Management Standards; (2) the project has been physically constructed according to approved design specifications; (3) the project is ready for commercial operation and power injection into the grid. The commissioning certificate and CEI approval are mandatory prerequisites for beginning commercial operation and claiming renewable purchase obligation (RPO) compliance or REC (Renewable Energy Certificate) eligibility.

## CHUNK 7: Commissioning Timeline Requirements – Solar and Wind Projects

**DOCUMENT**: Distributed Renewable Energy Procurement (DREBP) – GERC Guidelines (Amended Guideline under GERC Tariff Orders No. 5 & 6 of 2024)

**DOCUMENT TYPE**: Procedural Guidance / Flowchart / Annexure

**HIERARCHY**: Annexure-A / Commissioning Period Specification

**PROVISION**: Annexure-A – Commissioning Timeline Requirement

**TITLE**: Project Commissioning Timeline – 18-Month Deadline for Solar and Wind Projects

**SUMMARY**:
Both solar and wind energy projects developed under the DREBP framework must achieve commercial operation and full commissioning within 18 months from the date of the Power Purchase Agreement (PPA). This 18-month commissioning period is a critical regulatory timeline that enforces project execution discipline and prevents indefinite land reservation or capacity hoarding. The clock begins on the date the PPA is executed; projects failing to achieve commissioning within this period face penalties including encashment of the Performance Bank Guarantee and cancellation of allocated capacity. This timeline is non-negotiable and applies uniformly to both solar (18 months) and wind (18 months) technologies under DREBP. Any project seeking extension beyond 18 months requires formal regulatory approval from GERC and justification of delay circumstances.

**KEY TOPICS**: Commissioning Timeline, 18-Month Deadline, PPA Execution Date (Reference Point), Solar Projects, Wind Projects, DREBP, Project Development Timeline, Regulatory Compliance, Performance Bank Guarantee Encashment, Capacity Cancellation

## CHUNK 8: Cancellation Pathway – Application/LoA Rejection and Capacity Return

**DOCUMENT**: Distributed Renewable Energy Procurement (DREBP) – GERC Guidelines (Amended Guideline under GERC Tariff Orders No. 5 & 6 of 2024)

**DOCUMENT TYPE**: Procedural Guidance / Flowchart / Annexure

**HIERARCHY**: Annexure-A / Procedure – Cancellation Pathway (Early-Stage Rejection)

**PROVISION**: Annexure-A – Application and LoA Cancellation Step

**TITLE**: Cancellation of Application, LoA, and Connectivity – Return of Capacity to Available Pool

**SUMMARY**:
If a project application does not meet preliminary eligibility criteria or if a project proponent fails to proceed with LoA conditions and timelines, DISCOM/GUVNL and GETCO (Gujarat Energy Transmission and Dispatch Company) may cancel the application, LoA, and any issued connectivity. Upon cancellation at this early stage (before PPA execution or significant project development), the allocated capacity is immediately returned to the list of spare available capacity, making it available for allocation to other qualifying projects. This mechanism ensures that capacity is not indefinitely reserved for projects that cannot or do not proceed, and maintains the integrity of the DREBP capacity allocation system. Early-stage cancellations do not trigger Performance Bank Guarantee encashment (as no PBG would have been collected at that point) but do result in permanent loss of registration and forfeiture of the non-refundable Rs. 10,000/MW application fee.

**KEY TOPICS**: Application Cancellation, LoA Cancellation, Connectivity Cancellation, Capacity Return, Spare Available Capacity, GETCO, Capacity Pool Restoration, Early-Stage Rejection, Registration Forfeiture, Non-refundable Registration Fee

## CHUNK 9: Cancellation Pathway – PPA Non-Execution and Conditional Cancellation

**DOCUMENT**: Distributed Renewable Energy Procurement (DREBP) – GERC Guidelines (Amended Guideline under GERC Tariff Orders No. 5 & 6 of 2024)

**DOCUMENT TYPE**: Procedural Guidance / Flowchart / Annexure

**HIERARCHY**: Annexure-A / Procedure – Conditional Cancellation (PPA Timeline Breach)

**PROVISION**: Annexure-A – Conditional PPA Cancellation and Capacity Reduction Step

**TITLE**: Cancellation of PPA or Project Capacity Reduction Upon Timeline Breach – Performance Bank Guarantee Encashment

**SUMMARY**:
If a project fails to execute the PPA within the 15-day window specified in the LoA, or if the project fails to achieve commissioning within the 18-month deadline from PPA date, the distribution company may cancel the PPA and reduce the project's allocated capacity to the installed (as-built) capacity, or cancel the project entirely. Upon such cancellation, the Performance Bank Guarantee (PBG) is encashed by DISCOM/GUVNL to partially recover the cost of capacity reservation, failed procurement planning, and administrative overhead. The cancelled or reduced capacity is immediately returned to GETCO's list of spare available capacity for re-allocation to other projects. This penalty mechanism enforces strict contractual discipline and protects the state's renewable energy procurement objectives from project abandonment or extended delays. Partial capacity reduction (keeping only installed capacity) may be permitted if the proponent has constructed a portion of the planned project and demonstrates technical feasibility of completion at the reduced scale.

**KEY TOPICS**: PPA Cancellation, Capacity Reduction, Installed Capacity, Performance Bank Guarantee Encashment, Timeline Breach, 15-Day PPA Execution Deadline, 18-Month Commissioning Deadline, Penalty Mechanism, Capacity Return to Pool, GETCO

## CHUNK 11: Power Purchase Obligation and Grid Commitment

**DOCUMENT**: Distributed Renewable Energy Procurement (DREBP) – GERC Guidelines (Amended Guideline under GERC Tariff Orders No. 5 & 6 of 2024)

**DOCUMENT TYPE**: Procedural Guidance / Flowchart / Annexure

**HIERARCHY**: Annexure-A / Procedure Step – Post-Commissioning Obligations

**PROVISION**: Annexure-A – DISCOM/GUVNL Power Purchase Commitment Step

**TITLE**: DISCOM/GUVNL Power Purchase Obligation and Grid Commitment at Commercial Operation

**SUMMARY**:
Upon successful commissioning and receipt of the GEDA commissioning certificate and CEI charging approval, the distribution company (DISCOM) or GUVNL assumes the obligation to purchase all power generated by the project at the terms and conditions agreed in the executed PPA. This power purchase obligation is non-cancellable and continues for the duration specified in the PPA (typically 25 years for solar, 25 years for wind under DREBP). The distribution company's commitment is guaranteed and enforceable; power generated and injected into the grid must be purchased at the tariff specified in the PPA, with costs recovered through the distribution company's revenue requirement and passed on to consumers through retail tariffs. This commitment provides long-term revenue certainty to project developers, enabling them to secure financing for project construction. The power purchase obligation is a critical downstream element that validates the entire DREBP framework and ensures that grid capacity allocated through this procurement mechanism is utilized for actual renewable energy procurement, not speculative capacity reservation.
