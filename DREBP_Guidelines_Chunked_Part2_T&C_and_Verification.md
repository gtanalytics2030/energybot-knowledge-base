## CHUNK 29: DREBP T&C - Early Commissioning & Part Commissioning (Section 8.12-8.13)

**DOCUMENT:** Amended Guideline for Procurement of RE from Project below threshold limit by DISCOMs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Guideline / Commissioning Flexibility

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 8 → Clauses 8.12-8.13

**PROVISION:** Section 8.12-8.13 - Early and phased commissioning provisions

**TITLE:** Early Commissioning and Phased Part Commissioning Permissions

**SUMMARY:**
Early commissioning of DREBP projects is permitted provided that: (1) grid connectivity is established and operationalized for the early-commissioned capacity, and (2) land use rights are secured for the early-commissioned portion. Tariff for early-commissioned capacity is identical to the full PPA tariff—no premium is offered for ahead-of-schedule commissioning. This flexibility allows developers to achieve partial revenue generation before full project completion, reducing pre-COD financing burden. Similarly, part commissioning (phased deployment) is permitted in multiple tranches until the SCSD, provided each phase is at least 0.5 MW (minimum viable unit). Tariff for phased capacity is the same PPA rate applied to each phase as commissioned. This enables developers to commission solar via separate land parcels (Section 2.2) sequentially or deploy wind turbines in multiple installations. The uniform tariff treatment (no differentiation by commissioning timing) removes complexity while the mandatory 0.5 MW minimum per phase prevents excessive fragmentation and administrative burden. These provisions significantly increase deployment flexibility compared to single-date all-or-nothing commissioning.

- Multi-parcel solar deployment
- Multi-turbine wind deployment

**TEXT:**

8.12. Early commissioning of the projects are allowed subjected to availability of establishment of connectivity and land use rights for the early commissioned capacity of the project. Tariff for early commissioned project shall be same as PPA tariff.

## CHUNK 30: DREBP T&C - AC Capacity Limit & Injection Control (Section 8.14)

**DOCUMENT:** Amended Guideline for Procurement of RE from Project below threshold limit by DISCOMs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Guideline / Technical Capacity Control

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 8 → Clause 8.14

**PROVISION:** Section 8.14 - AC output limit and excess injection penalty

**TITLE:** Final AC Output Limitation and Inadvertent Energy Penalties for Overcapacity Injection

**SUMMARY:**
While solar DC (Direct Current) installed capacity may exceed AC (Alternating Current) contracted capacity (reflecting typical inverter efficiency and power electronics conversion losses), the final AC output from the project must never exceed the AC-contracted capacity specified in the PPA. This ensures DISCOM receives maximum contracted power but prevents over-injection that would destabilize grid voltage/frequency or exceed bilateral contractual limits. Specifically, in any 15-minute time block during the entire PPA term, the injected AC power must not exceed the contracted AC capacity. If injection exceeds contracted capacity in any time block, the excess energy is classified as "inadvertent energy" and: (1) no payment is made for the excess (producer bears loss), and (2) applicable regulatory charges are recovered from the producer. This creates a hard ceiling on producer generation rights and prevents attempts to "overproduce" beyond contractual limits. The 15-minute granularity matches SLDC's energy accounting time blocks and ALDs scheduling intervals. The "inadvertent energy" classification aligns with GERC deviation settlement terminology.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- AC output limit

- DC to AC conversion
- Inverter efficiency losses
- 15-minute time block (settlement period)
- Excess injection
- Inadvertent energy
- No payment for excess
- Regulatory charges recovery
- Hard capacity ceiling
- Energy cannot be sold beyond contracted amount
- Penalizing overcapacity generation
- Grid stability protection

**TEXT:**

8.14. There is no restriction in the installed DC capacity of the solar RE project subject to grid constraint. However, the Final AC output in no instance shall be more than the AC Contracted capacity as specified in PPA. It is to clarify that in any 15 min time block during entire term of PPA, the injected power shall not exceed contracted AC capacity. In case supply of power at delivery point exceed the contracted capacity in any time block, such excess energy shall be considered as inadvertent energy and applicable charges will be recovered as per regulation and no payment shall be made for such excess injection of energy.

**PROVISION:** Section 8.15 - Repowering of RE projects during PPA term

**TITLE:** Project Repowering and Equipment Upgrade Provisions During 25-Year PPA Period

**SUMMARY:**
RE developers may upgrade and repower their plants (replace aging or underperforming equipment, retrofit turbines/panels, or increase component capacity) during the PPA period entirely at their own risk and cost. Repowering is permitted to maintain declared Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF) performance parameters—particularly important as solar PV panels degrade ~0.7% annually and wind turbines may degrade similarly, threatening the minimum CUF commitment. However, GUVNL/DISCOM has no obligation to purchase power exceeding the CUF range (minimum 90% of declared CUF, maximum 120% of declared CUF) or beyond the contracted capacity specified in the original PPA, regardless of enhanced equipment capabilities post-repowering. This provision allows operational optimization while protecting DISCOM from unexpected additional deliveries beyond PPA terms. For example, a developer might replace lower-efficiency solar modules with higher-efficiency modern modules; the upgraded system can still only sell within the original CUF band and contracted capacity. The repowering framework balances developer flexibility (technical improvements permitted) with DISCOM certainty (no obligation to buy beyond contractual limits).

## CHUNK 33: DREBP T&C - CUF Declaration & Performance Range (Section 8.17)

**DOCUMENT:** Amended Guideline for Procurement of RE from Project below threshold limit by DISCOMs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Guideline / Capacity Utilization Factor

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 8 → Clause 8.17

**PROVISION:** Section 8.17 - CUF declaration, minimum/maximum bounds, and revision

**TITLE:** Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF) Declaration and Operational Performance Boundaries

**SUMMARY:**
The Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF)—the ratio of actual annual energy delivered to maximum potential energy from the contracted capacity—must be declared upfront in the PPA and may not be less than 19% for solar projects or 38% for wind projects. These minimums reflect conservative technical standards (solar PV performance, wind resource availability) and are prudent from a DISCOM perspective (ensure contracted capacity will actually deliver meaningful energy). CUF is calculated on a yearly basis from April 1 to March 31 (Indian financial year). During the 25-year PPA, the RE power producer must maintain annual supply corresponding to CUF within a band: minimum 90% of declared CUF (lower bound), maximum 120% of declared CUF (upper bound). For example, if declared CUF is 25% for solar, acceptable range is 22.5%-30% annually. The lower bound (90% of declared CUF) is relaxable by GUVNL/DISCOM only to the extent that grid non-availability for evacuation is beyond the producer's control (force majeure)—transmission line outages, substation faults, etc. The declared CUF may be revised once during the PPA term, but revised CUF must still meet the 19% (solar) or 38% (wind) minimum. Revision is permitted to reflect more accurate resource assessment after initial operational year(s). This framework balances: (1) DISCOM certainty (minimum guaranteed energy with CUF floor), (2) developer flexibility (revision if initial estimates proved conservative), and (3) grid operational reality (exemptions for transmission unavailability).

**TEXT:**

8.17. CUF of the project shall be declared upfront in the PPA which shall in no case be less than 19% for the solar project and 38% for the Wind projects. Calculation of CUF will be on yearly basis from 1st April of the year to 31st March of next year. RE power producer shall maintain energy supply so as to achieve annual supply corresponding to CUF not less than 90% of the declared value (i.e., Minimum CUF) and not more than 120% of the declared CUF value (i.e., Maximum CUF), during the PPA duration of 25 years. The lower limit will, however, be relaxable by GUVNL/DISCOM to the extent of non-availability of grid for evacuation which is beyond the control of the RE Developer. The declared CUF can be revised once as per PPA terms.

## CHUNK 34: DREBP T&C - Excess CUF Injection - No Payment (Section 8.18)

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 8 → Clause 8.18

**PROVISION:** Section 8.18 - Excess CUF injection penalty

**TITLE:** Excess Injection Beyond Maximum CUF - Energy Classification and No-Payment Rule

**SUMMARY:**
Energy generation exceeding the maximum stipulated CUF (120% of declared CUF) corresponding to the contracted capacity in any contract year is classified as "inadvertent flow of energy" and receives no payment. This provision prevents over-generation above the annual commitment and protects DISCOM from unexpected excess supply. The inadvertent energy classification aligns with GERC deviation settlement terminology—energy outside scheduled/contracted parameters. Unlike CUF shortfall (which triggers penalties to the producer), CUF excess results in producer loss (no revenue for surplus generation). This creates an incentive for accurate CUF forecasting: conservative estimates result in penalties if actual falls below minimum; aggressive estimates result in uncompensated excess if actual exceeds maximum. The interplay between Clauses 8.17 (CUF band), 8.18 (excess no-payment), and 8.19 (shortfall penalty) creates a symmetric economic framework encouraging accurate mid-range CUF declaration.

## CHUNK 35: DREBP T&C - CUF Shortfall Penalty (Section 8.19)

**DOCUMENT:** Amended Guideline for Procurement of RE from Project below threshold limit by DISCOMs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Guideline / CUF Shortfall Penalty

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 8 → Clause 8.19

**PROVISION:** Section 8.19 - CUF shortfall penalty and exemptions

**TITLE:** CUF Shortfall Beyond Minimum - Penalty Calculation and Force Majeure Exemption

**SUMMARY:**
If annual energy generation falls short of the minimum CUF (90% of declared CUF) specified in the PPA, the RE power producer incurs a penalty at 1.5 times the PPA tariff for the shortfall generation (i.e., the energy that should have been generated at the minimum CUF but was not). Penalty calculation: if declared CUF is 25% and actual is 20% (below 90% of 25% = 22.5%), the shortfall is (25% - 20%) × contracted capacity × 8760 hours = shortfall MWh. Penalty = shortfall MWh × PPA tariff × 1.5. The 1.5× multiplier represents liquidated damages greater than the lost revenue, incentivizing generators to maintain performance. However, the penalty does not apply in years where Force Majeure events (as defined in Draft PPA Article 8) prevent power supply. CUF penalties are inapplicable for the year of Commercial Operation Date (COD) and the year of PPA termination/expiry, recognizing that these partial-year periods distort annualized CUF calculation. This framework balances: (1) DISCOM certainty (penalties ensure productive effort), (2) force majeure relief (uncontrollable events exempt), and (3) fairness (startup and terminal years exempt).

In case of shortfall of CUF beyond minimum CUF on annual basis specified in PPA, penalty shall be levied at the rate of 1.5 Times of PPA tariff for the shortfall generation corresponding to PPA specified CUF for the contracted capacity of the project. **HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 8 → Clause 8.21

**PROVISION:** Section 8.21 - No government financial support provision

**TITLE:** Prohibition on Government Financial Support and Subsidy

**SUMMARY:**
The Energy & Petrochemicals Department, GEDA (Gujarat Energy Development Agency), GUVNL, and DISCOMs will not provide any financial support toward the setting up of solar/wind power plants. This explicitly states that DREBP is a commercial arrangement with no government subsidy, concessional financing, or capital grants. Developers must self-fund or arrange commercial financing (bank loans, equity, etc.) entirely through market mechanisms. This positions DREBP as a feed-in tariff regime (government guarantees purchase through fixed tariff) rather than a subsidy regime (government provides capital support). The provision clarifies that the tariff (Rs. 2.48 solar, Rs. 2.84 wind) is not supplemented by capital subsidy. Developers rely on project economics (tariff × expected energy generation) vs. capex and opex to evaluate investment returns. This approach is consistent with the market-oriented renewable energy development policy and distinguishes DREBP from subsidy-dependent schemes.

## CHUNK 39: DREBP T&C - Renewable Energy Attributes & RPO Claim (Section 8.23)

**DOCUMENT:** Amended Guideline for Procurement of RE from Project below threshold limit by DISCOMs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Guideline / RE Attributes and RPO Framework

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section 8 → Clause 8.23

**PROVISION:** Section 8.23 - RE attributes ownership and RPO claims

**TITLE:** Renewable Energy Attribute Ownership - DISCOM RPO Claim and Producer Waiver

**SUMMARY:**
Renewable Energy attributes (RECs, Green Energy Certificates, or equivalent regulatory recognition) generated from DREBP projects are claimed by DISCOMs/GUVNL for their Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO) fulfillment. The RE power producer explicitly waives any claim on RPO obligations or Renewable Energy attribute generation from the bilateral power sale. This critical provision clarifies ownership: the DISCOM who purchases the power owns the associated RE attributes for regulatory compliance. This is the economic quid pro quo for the feed-in tariff: the producer receives guaranteed tariff revenue, while the DISCOM retains the RE attribute value (regulatory compliance credit or potential certificate trading value). If the producer separately claimed RPO or REC benefits, they would receive double compensation (tariff + REC trading value), which the guideline prohibits. Carbon credits under any existing or future mechanism are allocated per GERC tariff order (not automatic to producer). This provision prevents double-compensation while clarifying the DISCOM's ability to meet statutory RPO targets through DREBP power procurement.

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Guideline / ALMM Equipment Standards - Transition Rules

**PROVISION:** Section 8.24 - ALMM List applicability by PPA signing date

**TITLE:** Temporal ALMM List Applicability Rules - Cutoff Dates and Exemptions for Earlier Signatories

**SUMMARY:**
ALMM equipment standards are applied with transition provisions based on PPA signing date, not commissioning date. Solar PPAs signed after August 31, 2025 must use solar PV modules from ALMM List I and solar PV cells from ALMM List II, regardless of commissioning date. However, solar PPAs signed before August 31, 2025 are mandated to use modules from ALMM List I but are exempted from the ALMM List II cell requirement for solar PV cells, even if commissioning extends beyond June 1, 2026. This temporal exemption reflects a transition policy: earlier signatories (who made investment commitments before new standards were enacted) receive grandfathering protection, while new signatories (post-August 31, 2025) must comply with all updated ALMM requirements. The provision accommodates:

(1) Investment certainty for developers who signed PPAs before ALMM List II became mandatory;
(2) Supplier chain lead times (sourcing List II-compliant cells may require longer procurement);
(3) Regulatory flexibility (future MNRE amendments to ALMM applicability automatically apply).

This represents GERC's policy of protecting earlier stakeholders while tightening standards for new market entrants.

**TITLE:** Project Location Modification - Permitted Until SCSD with Conditions

**SUMMARY:**

The location flexibility enables developers to respond to land acquisition challenges, environmental findings, or grid capacity updates, while the interconnection point rigidity ensures grid planners can rely on confirmed connection points.

**TITLE:** Wind Project Capacity Change Range - ±5% Tolerance at Commissioning

**SUMMARY:**
For wind project commissioning, a capacity change (variance) in the range of +5% to -5% (approximately) of the contracted capacity is permitted, subject to the maximum capacity limits specified in Section 2.2(I) (less than 10 MW per single eligible RE power producer at single substation). The symmetrical ±5% variance around contracted capacity prevents disputes over minor equipment specification variations while maintaining the upper 10 MW single-point limit (overage beyond +5% is not permitted). Example: a 5 MW wind project can commission between 4.75 MW and 5.25 MW; if it attempts 5.5 MW, the excess 0.5 MW violates the +5% tolerance and triggers capacity reduction to 5 MW (Section 8.9 non-compliance cascade). This tolerance differs from solar projects (which have no explicit tolerance mentioned, suggesting strict AC capacity matching), reflecting wind turbine manufacturing practice and industry standards.

## CHUNK 44: DREBP T&C - No Capacity Enhancement (Section 8.28)

**PROVISION:** Section 8.28 - Enhancement of contracted capacity prohibition

**TITLE:** Contracted Capacity - No Enhancement Permitted

**SUMMARY:**
Enhancement of contracted capacity is not permitted during the PPA term. Once a capacity is contracted in the PPA (e.g., 3 MW wind), it cannot be increased to 4 MW even with DISCOM consent. This hard limit protects DISCOM's supply planning assumptions and prevents producers from incrementally increasing procurement commitments through informal requests. Contrast with Section 8.15 (repowering permitted for performance maintenance) and Section 8.22 (repowering excess capacity eligible under capacity limits): repowering may improve equipment efficiency but cannot increase contracted sales obligation. If a developer wants to add capacity beyond contracted amount, they must sign a separate PPA for the incremental capacity (subject to aggregation rules in Section 2.2). This provision enforces contractual finality: once signed, the capacity term is fixed. The prohibition also prevents capacity "creep" that would gradually push a 4 MW project to 5 MW+ over time, breaching the 5 MW (solar) or 10 MW (wind) single-substation thresholds.

This enables commercial rooftop solar business models (developer owns system, sells power to DISCOM, building reduces grid purchase) alongside traditional self-consumption models.
