PROVISION: Regulation 33

TITLE: Co-operation with State Transmission Utility, State Load Despatch Centre and Other Licensees

SUMMARY:
Regulation 33 establishes three cooperation obligations for Distribution Licensees: (i) provide reasonably required information to State Load Despatch Centre, State Transmission Utility, and other licensees for them to perform their functions; (ii) comply with directions of State Load Despatch Centre and State Transmission Utility issued under the Electricity Act or State Act; (iii) cooperate with generating companies, other licensees, and State Load Despatch Centre for efficient and coordinated operation of the power system.

KEY TOPICS: Cooperation, Information Sharing, SLDC, Transmission Utility, Coordination

TEXT:
33. Co-operation with State Transmission Utility, State Load Despatch Centre and other Licensees

(i) The Distribution Licensee shall provide to the State Load Despatch Centre and State Transmission Utility and/or other Licensees, such information as may reasonably be required by them to perform their functions and exercise their powers under the Act and/or the State Act.

(ii) The Distribution Licensee shall always comply with the directions of State Load Despatch Centre and State Transmission Utility issued under the Act and/or the State Act.

(iii) The Distribution Licensee shall co-operate with generating companies, other Licensees and State Load Despatch Centre for the efficient and co-ordinated operation of the power system.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Electricity Act 2003, State Act, Regulation 31 (Compliance)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 38 (Information to Commission), GERC State Grid Code, GERC Distribution Code

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Distribution Licence

PROVISION: Regulation 34

TITLE: Obligation to Install Meter

SUMMARY:
Regulation 34 establishes that no Distribution Licensee shall supply electricity after two years from the "Appointed Date" (the date of commencement of the Electricity Act, 2003) except through a correct meter installed per Central Electricity Authority regulations. A proviso permits licensees to require consumers to provide security for meter cost or enter hire agreements, unless consumers elect to purchase. The Commission may extend the two-year period for specified classes or areas. The Central Electricity Authority may direct additional meter installations at stages and locations of distribution or trading as deemed necessary for proper accounting and audit.

KEY TOPICS: Metering, Electricity Measurement, Meter Installation, Meter Hire, Accountability

TEXT:
34. Obligation to install meter

(i) No Distribution Licensee shall supply electricity, after the expiry of Two years from the Appointed Date, except through installation of a correct meter in accordance with regulations made in this behalf by the Central Electricity Authority:

Provided that the Distribution Licensee may require the consumer to give it security for the price of a meter and enter into an agreement for the hire thereof, unless the consumer elects to purchase a meter:

Provided further that the Commission may, by notification extend the said period of two years for a class or classes of persons or for such area as may be specified in that notification.

(ii) For proper accounting and audit in the generation, transmission and distribution or trading of electricity, the Central Electricity Authority may direct the installation of meters by a Distribution Licensee at such stages of distribution or trading of electricity and at such location of distribution or trading of electricity, as it may deem necessary.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Electricity Act 2003, Central Electricity Authority Regulations on Metering, Regulation 23 (Power to require security)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 35 (Power to disconnect supply), Regulation 37 (Power to enter premises and remove fittings)

DOCUMENT: GERC (Distribution Licence) Regulations, 2005 – Notification No 4 of 2005

DOCUMENT TYPE: Regulation

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Distribution Licence

PROVISION: Regulation 35

TITLE: Power to Disconnect Supply in Case of Default of Payment

SUMMARY:
Regulation 35 establishes the Distribution Licensee's (or generating company's) power to disconnect electricity supply for non-payment of electricity charges or other sums due. After giving not less than 15 clear days' written notice, the licensee may cut off supply and disconnect supply lines without prejudice to recovery by suit. Supply remains cut until charges and reconnection expenses are paid. However, supply cannot be cut if the consumer deposits under protest either the claimed amount or average monthly charges for preceding 6 months (whichever is less), pending dispute resolution. Further, no sum recoverable is enforceable after two years unless continuously shown as arrear, and supply cannot be cut. For sums due from Government companies or corporations, recovery is as arrears of land revenue.

KEY TOPICS: Disconnection, Non-Payment, Consumer Default, Recovery, Dispute Resolution, Two-Year Limitation

TEXT:
35. Power to disconnect supply in case of default of payment

(i) Where any person neglects to pay any charge for electricity or any sum other than a charge for electricity due from him to a Distribution Licensee (or to the generating company) in respect of supply, transmission or distribution or wheeling of electricity to him, the Distribution Licensee (or the generating company) may, after giving not less than fifteen (15) clear days notice in writing, to such person and without prejudice to its rights to recover such charge or other sum by suit, cut off the supply of electricity, and for that purpose, cut or disconnect any electric supply line or other works, being the property of such Distribution Licensee (or the generating company), through which electricity may have been supplied, transmitted, distributed or wheeled, and may discontinue the supply until such charge or other sum, together with any expenses incurred by it in cutting off and reconnecting the supply, are paid, but no longer.

(ii) Provided that the supply of electricity shall not be cut off if such person deposits, under protest, -

(a) an amount equal to the sum claimed from him, or

(b) the electricity charges due from him for each month calculated on the basis of average charge for electricity paid by him during the preceding six months,

whichever is less, pending disposal of any dispute between him and the Distribution Licensee.

(iii) Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force, no sum due from any consumer, as referred to in this Regulation 34 shall be recoverable after the period of two years from the date when such sum became first due, unless such sum has been shown continuously as recoverable as arrear of charges for electricity supplied, and the Distribution Licensee shall not cut off the supply of the electricity.

(iv) Where –

(a) any person neglects to pay any charge for electricity or any sum other than a charge for electricity due from him to the Board, or a Licensee in respect of the supply of electricity to him, and

(b) the Board or the Licensee to whom such charge or other sum is due is a Government company or a corporation owned or controlled by the State Government.

Such charge or other sum or the aggregate of such charge and other sum due to the Board or the Licensee shall be recoverable as arrears of land revenue.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 47 of Electricity Act 2003, Limitation period principles

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 21 (Obligation to supply on demand), Regulation 23 (Power to require security), Regulation 37 (Power to enter premises)

DOCUMENT TYPE: Regulation

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Distribution Licence

PROVISION: Regulation 36

TITLE: Protection of Railways, Highways, Telegraphic Lines etc.

SUMMARY:
Regulation 36 establishes the Distribution Licensee's obligation to protect critical infrastructure including railways, highways, airports, tramways, canals, water-ways, docks, wharves, and piers vested in or controlled by local authorities. The licensee must take all reasonable precautions in constructing, laying, and placing electric lines and works to avoid injuring such infrastructure or obstructing traffic. The licensee must take precautions against injurious effects on telegraph, telephone, or electric signaling communication through induction or otherwise. If disputes arise as to whether the licensee has breached these obligations, matters are referred to the Central Government. The Central Government may direct alterations needed to comply (unless the wire/line was placed unreasonably close after licensee's construction). Default in compliance requires the licensee to pay full compensation for losses, with disputes settled by arbitration per Section 158 of the Act.

KEY TOPICS: Protection of Infrastructure, Railways, Highways, Telegraph Lines, Compensation

TEXT:
36. Protection of Railways, Highways, Telegraphic Lines etc.

(i) The Distribution Licensee shall not, in the course of distribution, supply or use of electricity, in any way injure any railway, highway, airport, tramway, canal or water-way or any dock, wharf or pier vested in or controlled by a local authority, or obstruct or interfere with the traffic on any railway, airway, tramway, canal or water-way.

(ii) The Distribution Licensee shall take all reasonable precautions in constructing, laying down and placing its electric lines, electrical plant and other works and in working its system, so as not to injuriously affect, whether by induction or otherwise, the working of any wire or line used for the purpose of telegraphic, telephone or electric signaling communication, or the currents in such wire or line.

(iii) Where any difference or dispute arises between the Distribution Licensee and the telegraph authority as to whether the Distribution Licensee has constructed, laid down or placed its electric lines, electrical plant or other works, or worked its system, in contravention of clause (ii) above or as to whether the working of any wire, line or current is or is not injuriously affected thereby, the matter shall be referred to the Central Government.

(iv) The Central Government, unless it is of the opinion that the wire or line has been placed in unreasonable proximity to the electric lines, electrical plant or works of the Distribution Licensee after the construction of such lines, plant or works, may direct the Distribution Licensee to make such alterations in, or additions to, its system as may be necessary in order to comply with the provisions of this Regulation 35, and the Distribution Licensee shall make such alterations or additions accordingly:

Provided that nothing in clause (iii) above and this clause (iv) shall apply to the repair, renewal or amendment of any electric line or electrical plant so long as the course of the electric line or electrical plant and the amount and nature of the electricity transmitted thereby are not altered.

(v) Where the Distribution Licensee makes default in complying with the requirements of this section, it shall pay full compensation for any loss or damage incurred by reason thereof, and, where any difference or dispute arises as to the amount of such compensation, the matter shall be determined by arbitration conducted in accordance with section 158 of the Act.

Explanation - For the purposes of this Regulation 35, a telegraph line shall be deemed to be injuriously affected if telegraphic, telephonic or electric signaling communication by means of such line is, whether through induction or otherwise, prejudicially interfered with by an electric line, electrical plant or other work or by any use made thereof.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 158 of Electricity Act 2003

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 20 (Development and Maintenance), Regulation 37 (Power to enter premises)

PROVISION: Regulation 37

TITLE: Power to Enter Premises and Remove Fittings

SUMMARY:
Regulation 37 establishes the Distribution Licensee's authority to enter premises for inspection, testing, repair, or alteration of distribution equipment, or to remove equipment when supply is no longer required. Entry must be at reasonable time with notice to occupier. Entry is permitted under three circumstances: (1) routine maintenance/inspection of equipment without special order; (2) special order from Executive Magistrate after 24 hours' notice for inspection/testing/removal; (3) testing of consumer wiring. If consumers refuse entry or refuse to give reasonable facilities, the licensee may discontinue supply after 24 hours' written notice.

KEY TOPICS: Right of Access, Inspection, Maintenance, Consumer Cooperation, Supply Discontinuance

TEXT:
37. Power to enter premises and remove fittings

(i) The Distribution Licensee or any person duly authorised by a Distribution Licensee may, at any reasonable time, and on informing the occupier of its intention, enter any premises to which electricity is, or has been, supplied by it, or any premises or land, under, over, along, across, in or upon which the electric supply-lines or other works have been lawfully placed by it for the purpose of –

(a) inspecting, testing, repairing or altering the electric supply lines, meters, fittings, works and apparatus for the supply of electricity belonging to the Distribution Licensee; or

(b) ascertaining the amount of electricity supplied or the electrical quantity contained in the supply; or

(c) removing, where a supply of electricity is no longer required, or where the Distribution Licensee is authorised to take away and cut off such supply, any electric supply-lines, meters, fittings, works or apparatus belonging to the Distribution Licensee.

(ii) The Distribution Licensee or any person authorised as aforesaid may also, in pursuance of a special order in this behalf made by an Executive Magistrate and after giving not less than twenty-four hours notice in writing to the occupier, -

(a) enter any premises or land referred to in clause (i) above for any of the purposes mentioned therein;

(b) enter any premises to which electricity is to be supplied by it, for the purpose of examining and testing the electric wires fittings, works and apparatus for the use of electricity belonging to the consumer.

(iii) Where a consumer refuses to allow the Distribution Licensee or any person authorised as aforesaid to enter his premises or land in pursuance of the provisions of clauses (i) or (ii) above or when such Distribution Licensee or person has so entered, refuses to allow it to perform any act which it is authorised by those clauses to perform, or fails to give reasonable facilities for such entry or performance, the Distribution Licensee may, after the expiry of twenty-four hours from the service of a notice in writing on the consumer, cut off the supply to the consumer for so long as such refusal or failure continues, but for no longer.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Regulation 34 (Obligation to install meter), Regulation 35 (Power to disconnect)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 21 (Obligation to supply on demand), Regulation 23 (Power to require security)

PROVISION: Regulation 38

TITLE: Information to the Commission

SUMMARY:
Regulation 38 establishes comprehensive information reporting obligations for Distribution Licensees. Without delay, licensees must furnish information, documents, and details related to Licensed Business or Other Business as required by the Commission for Commission purposes or Central Government, State Government, CERC, Central Electricity Authority, RLDC, SLDC, CTU, STU purposes, in Commission-specified form. Within one week of any Major Incident (as defined in Distribution Code), licensees must notify the Commission and submit detailed report within one month. The Commission may order compensation for persons suffering substantial injury or heirs of those killed where a Major Incident resulted from employee/agent act, omission, or negligence. The Commission's determination of what constitutes a Major Incident is final. Licensees must report significant changes affecting ability to meet obligations, material breaches of Act/State Act/rules/regulations/codes/agreements, and major changes in shareholding/ownership/management.

KEY TOPICS: Reporting, Information Disclosure, Incident Reporting, Major Incident, Transparency, Shareholder Changes

TEXT:
38. Information to the Commission

(i) The Distribution Licensee shall furnish to the Commission, without delay, such information, documents and details related to the Licensed Business or any Other Business of the Distribution Licensee, as the Commission may require from time to time, for its own purposes or for the purposes of the Central Government, State Government, the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission, the Central Electricity Authority, the Regional Load Dispatch Centre, State Load Dispatch Centre, the Central Transmission Utility and the State Transmission Utility. The information shall be furnished in such form as may be specified by the Commission.

(ii) The Distribution Licensee shall notify the Commission as soon as possible and in no case later than one week after the occurrence of any Major Incident (as defined under the 'Distribution Code') affecting any part of the distribution system and shall, within one month of the date of such Major Incident:

(a) submit a report giving full details of the facts within the knowledge of the Distribution Licensee regarding the Major Incident and its cause. The Commission in its own discretion may require the submission of a report to be prepared by an independent person at the expense of the Distribution Licensee; and

(b) give copies of the report to the Commission and to all parties involved in the Major Incident as the Commission may direct.

(iii) The Commission may by order direct the Distribution Licensee to provide such amount of compensation as the Commission may specify to any person who suffers substantial injury or to the heirs of those who have lost their lives, where a Major Incident has been caused by any act of commission, omission or negligence on the part of any of the employees or agents of the Distribution Licensee.

(iv) The decision of the Commission as to what constitutes a Major Incident shall be final.

(v) The Distribution Licensee shall, as soon as practicable, report the following to the Commission:

a. Any significant change in circumstances which may affect the Distribution Licensee's ability to meet its obligations;

b. Any material breach of the provisions of the Act, the State Act, the rules, regulations and codes made thereunder, directives and orders issued by the Commission and the terms of agreements/arrangements entered into by the Distribution Licensee with other Licensees or generating companies or consumers; and/or

c. Any major change in the shareholding pattern or in the ownership, control or management of the Distribution Licensee.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Regulation 31 (Compliance), GERC Distribution Code, Regulation 2(h) (Licensed Business), Regulation 2(i) (Other Business)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 32 (Standards of Performance), Regulation 40 (Accounts)

PROVISION: Regulation 39

TITLE: Other Obligations of the Distribution Licensee

SUMMARY:
Regulation 39 establishes that the Commission may additionally require Distribution Licensees to: (a) enter into agreements permitting others to use electric lines, plants, and equipment; (b) furnish information and documents as Commission requires; (c) inform Commission of proposed schemes; (d) undertake functions and obligations of the Board under the Indian Electricity Act 1910 and Electricity (Supply) Act 1948; (e) refer disputes under the licence for Commission determination or arbitration; (f) supply electricity in bulk to other licensees, exemption holders, sanction holders in-state or out-of-state; (g) comply with Commission directions; (h) act in accordance with licence terms and conditions.

KEY TOPICS: Additional Obligations, Dispute Resolution, Bulk Supply, Scheme Notification, Cooperation

TEXT:
39. Other obligations of the Distribution Licensee

(i) The Commission may additionally require the Distribution Licensee to:

(a) enter into an agreement with another person permitting such person to use electric lines, electrical plants and associated equipment operated by the Distribution Licensee;

(b) furnish information and documents which the Commission may require for its purpose;

(c) inform the Commission of any scheme permitted by the Distribution Licence which the Distribution Licensee proposes to undertake;

(d) undertake such functions and obligation of the Board under the Indian Electricity Act, 1910 and the Electricity (Supply) Act, 1948 as the Commission may, through a separate order, specify;

(e) refer a dispute arising under the Distribution Licence for determination by the Commission or by an arbitrator appointed by the Commission;

(f) supply electricity in bulk to other Licensees or exemption holder or sanction holder in the State or to any person outside the State;

(g) comply with any direction given by the Commission; and

(h) act in accordance with the terms and conditions of the Distribution Licence.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Indian Electricity Act 1910, Electricity (Supply) Act 1948, Regulation 2(f) (Distribution Licence)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 31 (Compliance), Regulation 38 (Information to Commission)
