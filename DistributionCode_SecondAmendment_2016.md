## CHUNK B.3: AMENDMENT 1 - SECTION 4.5 (12) (DISTRIBUTION SYSTEM PLANNING)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Distribution Code (Second Amendment), 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Notification / Substantive Amendment

**HIERARCHY:** Amendment Document / Clause 2 / Section 4.5 (12) Amendment

**PROVISION:** Clause 2 - Amendment to Section 4.5 (12) of the Principal Code

**TITLE:** Distribution System Planning - Cost Effectiveness and Energy Loss Reduction Standards

**SUMMARY:** Amendment 1 modifies Section 4.5 (12) of the principal Distribution Code regarding planning philosophy for distribution systems. The amended provision requires that "The planning of the Distribution System shall always keep in view the cost effectiveness and reduction in energy losses without sacrificing the requirements of Security Standards, Safety Standards and Power Quality Standards for the Distribution System." This amendment emphasizes dual objectives: (1) cost-effective planning and energy loss reduction, and (2) maintaining security, safety, and power quality standards. The amendment reflects GERC's policy requiring distribution licensees to balance financial efficiency (through loss reduction) with reliability and safety obligations. The original Section 4.5 (12) contained similar language but with different emphasis; this amendment reinforces the primacy of cost-effectiveness and loss reduction as explicit planning objectives.

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
The Second Amendment (2016) elevates cost-effectiveness and energy loss reduction to explicit primary objectives of distribution system planning. This reflects policy evolution recognizing that financial sustainability (through technical loss reduction and efficient capital deployment) is essential for long-term service quality. The amendment maintains three-pillar constraint framework: cost-effectiveness PLUS security/safety/power quality. This is not a relaxation of standards but a reframing to incentivize distribution licensees to optimize both financial and technical performance simultaneously. Section 4.5 (12) becomes the overarching principle for all specific planning criteria in Section 4.5 (1)-(11).

## CHUNK B.4: AMENDMENT 2 - SECTION 6.7 (1) (COMMUNICATION LINKS)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Distribution Code (Second Amendment), 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Notification / Substantive Amendment

**HIERARCHY:** Amendment Document / Clause 3 / Section 6.7 (1) Amendment

**PROVISION:** Clause 3 - Amendment to Section 6.7 (1) of the Principal Code

**TITLE:** Operational Communication Links - Large Consumer E-mail and Telephone Requirements

**SUMMARY:** Amendment 2 modifies Section 6.7 (1) of the principal Distribution Code (Operation Code) to strengthen communication infrastructure requirements for large consumers (contract demand ≥ 1 MW). The amended provision requires "Reliable and redundant communication links viz e-mail id (available round the clock), telephone, fax, etc. shall be made available by the consumer whose contract demand is 1 MW and above for data exchange and sharing information and operating instruction between the Licensee, Consumer, LMU, Sub-SLDC and SLDC. Each distribution utility shall ensure installation and maintenance of proper communication infrastructure at consumer end." The amendment introduces three key requirements: (1) E-mail availability 24/7 (round-the-clock), (2) Multi-party communication links (Licensee, Consumer, LMU, Sub-SLDC, SLDC), (3) Distribution utility responsibility for consumer-end infrastructure maintenance. This reflects modernization of operational procedures following digital communication standards and the need for real-time coordination with large consumers, load management units (LMU), and state load dispatch centers (SLDC).

## CHUNK B.5: AMENDMENT 3 - SECTION 6.9 (1) (CONTROL PERSONS FOR SAFETY COORDINATION)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Distribution Code (Second Amendment), 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Notification / Substantive Amendment

**HIERARCHY:** Amendment Document / Clause 4 / Section 6.9 (1) Amendment

**PROVISION:** Clause 4 - Amendment to Section 6.9 (1) of the Principal Code

**TITLE:** Control Persons for Safety Coordination - Designation, E-mail, and Telephone Contact Exchange

**SUMMARY:** Amendment 3 modifies Section 6.9 (1) of the principal Distribution Code (Operation Code) to enhance safety coordination procedures through formalized contact information exchange. The amended provision requires "The Distribution Licensee and the Users (comprising Generation Companies, Transmission Licensee and Consumers having load above 5 MW or dedicated lines) and any other Distribution Licensee having common electrical interface with the Licensee shall designate suitable persons to be responsible for safety co-ordination. These persons shall be referred to as Control Persons. Their designation, e-mail id and telephone numbers shall be exchanged between all the concerned persons. Any change in the list shall be notified promptly to all the concerned." The amendment adds two substantive changes to the original Section 6.9 (1): (1) addition of "e-mail id and telephone numbers" (original had only designations), and (2) requirement for "prompt notification" of changes. This reflects modernization of inter-organizational communication and formalization of emergency contact protocols. The stakeholder base remains unchanged (Distribution Licensee, Generation Companies, Transmission Licensees, large Consumers ≥5 MW with dedicated lines, neighboring Distribution Licensees).

This amendment parallels Section 6.7 (1) communication infrastructure requirements and signals GERC's systematic move toward formalized emergency communication protocols.

## CHUNK B.6: AMENDMENT 4 - ATTACHMENT 1, CLAUSE 12 (1) (METER TAMPER-PROOFING)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Distribution Code (Second Amendment), 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Notification / Substantive Amendment

**HIERARCHY:** Amendment Document / Clause 5 / Attachment 1, Clause 12 (1) Amendment

**PROVISION:** Clause 5 - Amendment to Attachment 1 of the Principal Code (Clause 12 (1) - Metering Standards)

**TITLE:** Meter Installation and Protection Standards - Tamper-Proof Box Design Requirements for Single-Phase and Three-Phase Supply

**SUMMARY:** Amendment 4 modifies Clause 12 (1) of Attachment 1 (Distribution System Planning and Security Standard) regarding meter installation and tamper-proofing requirements. The amendment significantly expands the specification for both single-phase (230V) and three-phase (400V) meter enclosures. For 230V single-phase supply, the amended provision requires metering "on a suitable board, located in such a place protected from sun and rain and shall be in a convenient position for taking readings enclosed in a suitable tamper-proof box. The tamper-proof box shall be of sufficient strength and design with locking and sealing devices and shall have adequate provision for heat dissipation with the required electrical clearances. The design shall permit readings to be taken without access to the meter or its connections. The terminals of the meter and box shall be made tamper-proof and sealed." For 400V three-phase supply, "the meters and associated metering equipment including connections shall be enclosed in a suitable tamper-proof box. The tamper-proof box shall be of sufficient strength and design with locking and sealing devices and shall have adequate provision for heat dissipation with the required electrical clearances. The design shall permit readings to be taken without access to the meter or its connections." The amendment introduces detailed specifications for box strength, locking/sealing mechanisms, heat dissipation, terminal protection, and remote readability—all addressing meter security and fraud prevention.

- Meter installation standards

- Box strength and material specifications

The design shall permit readings to be taken without access to the meter or its connections. The terminals of the meter and box shall be made tamper - proof and sealed. The design shall permit readings to be taken without access to the meter or its connections."**

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
The Second Amendment (2016) reflects GERC's focus on meter security and fraud prevention, particularly addressing theft and tampering risks at consumer premises. The specifications for locking/sealing devices, tamper-proofing of terminals, and heat dissipation indicate targeted design standards responding to field experience with meter manipulation. The remote readability requirement (readings without meter access) suggests anticipation of smart meter deployment and SCADA integration. Separate specifications for single-phase and three-phase emphasize different risk profiles: single-phase (predominantly residential/small commercial) faces direct consumer tampering, while three-phase (large industrial) requires coordinated protection of multiple terminals and associated equipment. The amendment predates smart meter mandates but establishes protection principles enabling future digital metering.
