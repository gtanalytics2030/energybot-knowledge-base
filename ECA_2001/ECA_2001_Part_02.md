## CHUNK 5: SECTION 3 - ESTABLISHMENT AND INCORPORATION OF BUREAU OF ENERGY EFFICIENCY

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter II / Section 3

**PROVISION:** Section 3

**TITLE:** Establishment and Incorporation of Bureau of Energy Efficiency

**SUMMARY:** Section 3 establishes the Bureau of Energy Efficiency as a body corporate under the Act with perpetual succession and common seal. The Bureau is empowered to acquire, hold, and dispose of movable and immovable property, contract, and litigate (sue or be sued) under the name "Bureau of Energy Efficiency." The Bureau's head office is mandatorily located at Delhi, with authority to establish additional offices at other places in India.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Bureau corporatization
- Legal capacity and perpetual succession
- Property rights
- Contracting authority
- Litigation capacity
- Head office location (Delhi)
- Regional office establishment

**TEXT:**

"3. Establishment and incorporation of Bureau of Energy Efficiency.—(1) With effect from such date as the Central Government may, by notification, appoint, there shall be established, for the purposes of this Act, a Bureau to be called the Bureau of Energy Efficiency.

(2) The Bureau shall be a body corporate by the name aforesaid having perpetual succession and a common seal, with power subject to the provisions of this Act, to acquire, hold and dispose of property, both movable and immovable, and to contract, and shall, by the said name, sue or be sued.

(3) The head office of the Bureau shall be at Delhi.

(4) The Bureau may establish offices at other places in India."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** 
- Section 2(d) - Bureau definition
- Related to Section 12 - Transfer of assets from Energy Management Centre

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** 
- Central Government notification (commencement authority)
- Section 1(3) - Commencement mechanism

## CHUNK 6: SECTION 4 - MANAGEMENT OF BUREAU

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter II / Section 4

**PROVISION:** Section 4

**TITLE:** Management of Bureau - Governing Council Composition and Powers

**SUMMARY:** General superintendence, direction, and management of the Bureau vests in a Governing Council of 20-26 members appointed by the Central Government. The Council comprises: (a) Minister in charge of Power (ex officio Chairperson); (b)-(f) five Secretaries to Government of India for Power, Petroleum & Natural Gas, Coal, Non-conventional Energy Sources, and Atomic Energy (ex officio members); (g) Secretary for Consumer Affairs; (h)-(n) Heads of CEA, CPRI, PCRA, CMPDI, BIS, NTH, IREDA (ex officio members); (o) one representative each from five power regions; (p) up to four industry/equipment/architect/consumer representatives; (q) up to two members nominated by Governing Council; (r) Director-General as ex officio member-secretary. Appointed members (o, p, q) serve 3-year terms with prescribed fees and procedures for filling vacancies. The Governing Council may exercise all Bureau powers.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Governing Council composition
- Ex officio membership (13 positions)
- Regional representation (5 members)
- Industry and consumer representation (up to 6 members)
- Member tenure (3 years)
- Chairperson role and selection
- Committee procedures and powers
- Delegated executive authority

**TEXT:**

"4. Management of Bureau.—(1) The general superintendence, direction and management of the affairs of the Bureau shall vest in the Governing Council which shall consist of not less than twenty, but not exceeding twenty-six, members to be appointed by the Central Government.

(2) The Governing Council shall consist of the following members, namely:—

(a) the Minister in charge of the Ministry or Department of the Central Government dealing with the Power — ex officio Chairperson;

(b) the Secretary to the Government of India, in charge of the Ministry or Department of the Central Government dealing with the Power — ex officio member;

(c) the Secretary to the Government of India, in charge of the Ministry or Department of the Central Government dealing with the Petroleum and Natural Gas — ex officio member;

(d) the Secretary to the Government of India, in charge of the Ministry or Department of the Central Government dealing with the Coal — ex officio member;

(e) the Secretary to the Government of India, in charge of the Ministry or Department of the Central Government dealing with the Non-conventional Energy Sources — ex officio member;

(f) the Secretary to the Government of India, in charge of the Ministry or Department of the Central Government dealing with the Atomic Energy — ex officio member;

(g) the Secretary to the Government of India, in charge of the Ministry or Department of the Central Government dealing with the Consumer Affairs — ex officio member;

(h) Chairman of the Central Electricity Authority established under the Electricity (Supply) Act, 1948 (54 of 1948) — ex officio member;

(i) Director-General of the Central Power Research Institute registered under the Karnataka Societies Act, 1960 (Karnataka Act 17 of 1960) — ex officio member;

(j) Executive Director of the Petroleum Conservation Research Association, a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 (XXI of 1860) — ex officio member;

(k) Chairman-cum-Managing Director of the Central Mine Planning and Design Institute Limited, a company incorporated under the Companies Act, 1956 (1 of 1956) — ex officio member;

(l) Director-General of the Bureau of Indian Standards established under the Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 1986 (63 of 1986) — ex officio member;

(m) Director-General of the National Test House, Department of Supply, Ministry of Commerce and Industry, Kolkata — ex officio member;

(n) Managing Director of the Indian Renewable Energy Development Agency Limited, a company incorporated under the Companies Act, 1956 (1 of 1956) — ex officio member;

(o) one member each from the five power regions representing the States of the region to be appointed by the Central Government — member;

(p) such number of persons, not exceeding four as may be prescribed, to be appointed by the Central Government as members from amongst persons who are in the opinion of the Central Government capable of representing industry, equipment and appliance manufacturers, architects and consumers — member;

(q) such number of persons, not exceeding two as may be nominated by the Governing Council as members — member;

(r) Director-General of Bureau ex officio member-secretary.

(3) The Governing Council may exercise all powers and do all acts and things which may be exercised or done by the Bureau.

(4) Every member referred to in clauses (o), (p) and (q) of sub-section (2) shall hold office for a term of three years from the date on which he enters upon his office.

(5) The fee and allowances to be paid to the members referred to in clauses (o), (p) and (q) of sub-section (2) and the manner of filling up of vacancies and the procedure to be followed in the discharge of their functions shall be such as may be prescribed."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 9 - Director-General appointment
- Section 56(2)(a) - Governing Council member appointment rules
- Section 56(2)(b) - Fee and allowances rules
- Electricity (Supply) Act, 1948 (54 of 1948) - CEA establishment

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Electricity (Supply) Act, 1948
- Karnataka Societies Act, 1960
- Societies Registration Act, 1860
- Companies Act, 1956
- Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 1986
- Five Power Regions of India

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** The Governing Council integrates 13 ex officio federal heads plus 5 regional representatives plus up to 6 appointed representatives from industry/consumers. This creates a multi-stakeholder governance structure spanning energy, minerals, standards, and consumer sectors.

## CHUNK 7: SECTION 5 - MEETINGS OF GOVERNING COUNCIL

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter II / Section 5

**PROVISION:** Section 5

**TITLE:** Meetings of Governing Council - Procedures and Voting

**SUMMARY:** The Governing Council meets at times and places prescribed by regulations, following procedure rules that include quorum requirements. The Chairperson presides, or if unable to attend, another member chosen by members present. Decisions are by majority vote of members present and voting. In event of vote equality, the Chairperson (or presiding member) has a casting/second vote.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Meeting frequency and scheduling
- Procedural regulations
- Quorum requirements
- Chairperson role
- Voting mechanism
- Tie-breaking procedures
- Regulatory discretion

**TEXT:**

"5. Meetings of Governing Council.—(1) The Governing Council shall meet at such times and places, and shall observe such rules of procedure in regard to the transaction of business at its meetings (including quorum at such meetings) as may be provided by regulations.

(2) The Chairperson or, if for any reason, he is unable to attend a meeting of the Governing Council, any other member chosen by the members present from amongst themselves at the meeting shall preside at the meeting.

(3) All questions which come up before any meeting of the Governing Council shall be decided by a majority vote of the members present and voting, and in the event of an equality of votes, the Chairperson or in his absence, the person presiding, shall have a second or casting vote."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 58 - Bureau regulations authority (includes meeting procedures)
- Section 58(2)(a) - Specific meeting procedures regulations

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Meeting procedures detailed in regulations made under Section 58.

## CHUNK 8: SECTION 6 - VACANCIES, ETC., NOT TO INVALIDATE PROCEEDINGS

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter II / Section 6

**PROVISION:** Section 6

**TITLE:** Vacancies and Procedural Defects - No Invalidation of Proceedings

**SUMMARY:** No act or proceeding of the Bureau, Governing Council, or Committee is invalid merely because of: (a) vacancy or defect in constitution of the Bureau, Council, or Committee; (b) defect in appointment of a Director-General, Secretary, or member; or (c) procedural irregularity not affecting the merits of the case. This provision insulates institutional actions from technical/procedural challenges.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Procedural validity
- Vacancy tolerance
- Constitutional defects
- Appointment defects
- Procedural irregularities
- Merits-based review standard

**TEXT:**

"6. Vacancies, etc., not to invalidate proceedings of Bureau, Governing Council or Committee.—No act or proceeding of the Bureau or the Governing Council or any Committee shall be invalid merely by reason of—

(a) any vacancy in, or any defect in the constitution of, the Bureau or the Governing Council or the Committee; or

(b) any defect in the appointment of a person acting as a Director-General or Secretary of the Bureau or a member of the Governing Council or the Committee; or

(c) any irregularity in the procedure of the Bureau or the Governing Council or the Committee not affecting the merits of the case."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** 
- Section 3 - Bureau establishment
- Section 4 - Governing Council composition
- Section 8 - Advisory Committees

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** This section protects institutional legitimacy during transitions, vacancies, or administrative irregularities.

## CHUNK 9: SECTION 7 - REMOVAL OF MEMBER FROM OFFICE

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter II / Section 7

**PROVISION:** Section 7

**TITLE:** Removal of Member from Office - Grounds and Procedures

**SUMMARY:** The Central Government may remove appointed members (under Section 4(2) clauses o, p, q) from office if they: (a) have been adjudicated insolvent; (b) are of unsound mind and declared so by a competent court; (c) have been convicted of an offence involving moral turpitude (in Central Government's opinion); or (d) have abused their position detrimentally to public interest. No removal under clause (d) is permitted without giving the member reasonable opportunity to be heard.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Member disqualification grounds
- Insolvency
- Mental capacity determination
- Moral turpitude
- Public interest standard
- Procedural fairness
- Audi alteram partem principle

**TEXT:**

"7. Removal of member from office.—The Central Government shall remove a member referred to in clauses (o), (p) and (q) of sub-section (2) of section 4 from office if he—

(a) is, or at any time has been, adjudicated as insolvent;

(b) is of unsound mind and stands so declared by a competent court;

(c) has been convicted of an offence which, in the opinion of the Central Government, involves a moral turpitude;

(d) has, in the opinion of the Central Government, so abused his position as to render his continuation in office detrimental to the public interest:

Provided that no member shall be removed under this clause unless he has been given a reasonable opportunity of being heard in the matter."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 4(2) clauses (o), (p), (q) - Appointed member categories

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Removal applies only to appointed members, not ex officio members; reasonable opportunity to be heard required under clause (d).

## CHUNK 10: SECTION 8 - CONSTITUTION OF ADVISORY COMMITTEES AND OTHER COMMITTEES

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter II / Section 8

**PROVISION:** Section 8

**TITLE:** Constitution of Advisory Committees and Technical Committees

**SUMMARY:** The Bureau must constitute Advisory Committees within six months of Act commencement to efficiently discharge functions. Each committee has a Chairperson and other members as determined by regulations. Without prejudice to this, the Bureau may constitute technical committees of experts for formulating energy consumption standards and norms for equipment or processes as deemed necessary.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Advisory Committee establishment (mandatory)
- Commencement deadline (6 months)
- Committee composition
- Regulatory determination
- Technical committees (discretionary)
- Standards formulation
- Expert involvement

**TEXT:**

"8. Constitution of Advisory Committees and other committees.—(1) Subject to any regulations made in this behalf, the Bureau shall, within six months from the date of commencement of this Act, constitute Advisory Committees for the efficient discharge of its functions.

(2) Each Advisory Committee shall consist of a Chairperson and such other members as may be determined by regulations.

(3) Without prejudice to the powers contained in sub-section (1), the Bureau may constitute, such number of technical committees of experts for the formulation of energy consumption standards or norms in respect of equipment or processes, as it considers necessary."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 1(3) - Commencement date determination
- Section 58 - Bureau regulation-making authority
- Section 58(2)(b) - Advisory Committee composition regulations

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Committee composition and procedures determined through regulations under Section 58.

## CHUNK 11: SECTION 9 - DIRECTOR-GENERAL OF BUREAU

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter II / Section 9

**PROVISION:** Section 9

**TITLE:** Director-General of Bureau - Appointment, Qualifications, Term, and Authority

**SUMMARY:** The Central Government appoints a Director-General from persons of ability and standing possessing knowledge and experience in energy production, supply, energy management, standardization, and conservation. The Government also appoints a Secretary (not below Deputy Secretary rank). The Director-General holds office for 5 years or until age 60, whichever is earlier (amended from 3 years in 2010). Salary, allowances, and service conditions are prescribed. Subject to Governing Council superintendence, the Director-General is the Bureau's Chief Executive Authority, exercising powers and duties as determined by regulations.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Director-General appointment authority
- Qualification requirements
- Secretary appointment
- Term of office (5 years / age 60)
- Remuneration authority
- Chief Executive Authority status
- Regulatory discretion
- 2010 Amendment (extended term)

**TEXT:**

"9. Director-General of Bureau.—(1) The Central Government shall, by notification, appoint a Director-General from amongst persons of ability and standing, having adequate knowledge and experience in dealing with the matters relating to energy production, supply and energy management, standardisation and efficient use of energy and its conservation.

(2) The Central Government shall, by notification, appoint any person not below the rank of Deputy Secretary to the Government of India as Secretary of the Bureau.

(3) The Director-General shall hold office for a term of five years from the date on which he enters upon his office or until he attains the age of sixty years, whichever is earlier.

(4) The salary and allowances payable to the Director-General and other terms and conditions of his service and other terms and conditions of service of the Secretary of the Bureau shall be such as may be prescribed.

(5) Subject to general superintendence, direction and management of the affairs by the Governing Council, the Director-General of the Bureau shall be the Chief Executive Authority of the Bureau.

(6) The Director-General of the Bureau shall exercise and discharge such powers and duties of the Bureau as may be determined by regulations."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 56(2)(c) - Director-General salary and service conditions rules
- Section 58 - Bureau regulation-making authority (for powers/duties)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** 
- 2010 Amendment changed Section 3 from 3 years to 5 years (Subs. by Act 28 of 2010, s. 3)
- Chief Executive Authority status subject to Governing Council oversight

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Director-General is chief executive but subordinate to Governing Council; requires Deputy Secretary rank minimum; 5-year tenure (extended from 3 years) creates continuity; mandatory retirement at 60.
