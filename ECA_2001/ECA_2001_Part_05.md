## CHUNK 30: SECTION 19 - GRANTS AND LOANS BY CENTRAL GOVERNMENT

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter VII / Section 19

**PROVISION:** Section 19

**TITLE:** Central Government Financing - Grants and Loans to Bureau and States

**SUMMARY:** The Central Government may, after Parliamentary appropriation, make grants and loans of such sums as it considers necessary to the Bureau or State Governments. This provides the financial basis for Bureau operations and state-level implementation.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Appropriation requirement
- Discretionary grants authority
- Discretionary loans authority
- Bureau and State beneficiaries
- Financial flexibility

**TEXT:**

"19. Grants and loans by Central Government.—The Central Government may, after due appropriation made by Parliament by law in this behalf, make to the Bureau or to the State Governments grants and loans of such sums of money as the Central Government may consider necessary."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 20 - Central Energy Conservation Fund (fund into which grants/loans are credited)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Parliamentary appropriation requirement; both grants (non-repayable) and loans (repayable) authorized.

## CHUNK 31: SECTION 20 - ESTABLISHMENT OF FUND BY CENTRAL GOVERNMENT

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter VII / Section 20

**PROVISION:** Section 20

**TITLE:** Central Energy Conservation Fund - Establishment and Application

**SUMMARY:** The Central Government establishes the Central Energy Conservation Fund. The Fund receives: (a) grants and loans made to the Bureau under Section 19; (b) all fees received by Bureau under Act; (c) all sums from other sources decided by Central Government. The Fund is applied for: (a) salary, allowances, remuneration of Director-General, Secretary, officers, employees; (b) Bureau expenses in discharging Section 13 functions; (c) Governing Council member fees/allowances under Section 4(5); (d) other authorized expenses.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Central fund establishment
- Revenue sources (grants, loans, fees, other)
- Discretionary revenue source incorporation
- Expenditure categories (personnel, operations, governance, other)
- Fund application limitation

**TEXT:**

"20. Establishment of Fund by Central Government.—(1) There shall be constituted a Fund to be called as the Central Energy Conservation Fund and there shall be credited thereto—

(a) any grants and loans made to the Bureau by the Central Government under section 19;

(b) all fees received by the Bureau under this Act;

(c) all sums received by the Bureau from such other sources as may be decided upon by the Central Government.

(2) The Fund shall be applied for meeting—

(a) the salary, allowances and other remuneration of Director-General, Secretary, officers and other employees of the Bureau;

(b) expenses of the Bureau in the discharge of its functions under section 13;

(c) fee and allowances to be paid to the members of the Governing Council under sub-section (5) of section 4;

(d) expenses on objects and for purposes authorised by this Act."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 19 - Grants and loans source
- Section 13 - Bureau functions (expense basis)
- Section 4(5) - Governing Council member fees
- Section 13(2)(n) - Fee collection authority

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Mirrored fund structure to Section 16 (State Energy Conservation Fund).

## CHUNK 32: SECTION 21 - BORROWING POWERS OF BUREAU

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter VII / Section 21

**PROVISION:** Section 21

**TITLE:** Bureau Borrowing Authority and Central Government Guarantee

**SUMMARY:** The Bureau may, with Central Government consent or under general/special authority from Central Government, borrow money from any source for discharging all or any Act functions. The Central Government may guarantee repayment of principal and interest on loans borrowed by Bureau.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Bureau borrowing authority (consent-based)
- Borrowing source flexibility
- Central Government guarantee authority
- Principal and interest coverage
- Function-tied borrowing limitation

**TEXT:**

"21. Borrowing powers of Bureau.—(1) The Bureau may, with the consent of the Central Government or in accordance with the terms of any general or special authority given to it by the Central Government, borrow money from any source as it may deem fit for discharging all or any of its functions under this Act.

(2) The Central Government may guarantee, in such manner as it thinks fit, the repayment of the principal and the payment of interest thereon with respect to the loans borrowed by the Bureau under sub-section (1)."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 13 - Bureau functions (borrowing purpose)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Borrowing authority is not unlimited; requires Central Government consent or standing authority.

## CHUNK 36: SECTION 25 - ACCOUNTS AND AUDIT

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter VII / Section 25

**PROVISION:** Section 25

**TITLE:** Bureau Accounts Maintenance and Audit by Comptroller and Auditor-General

**SUMMARY:** The Bureau maintains proper accounts and records and prepares annual statement of accounts in form prescribed by Central Government in consultation with Comptroller and Auditor-General (CAG). CAG audits Bureau accounts at specified intervals; Bureau pays audit expenses. CAG or appointed auditors have same rights/privileges/authority as CAG has for government accounts, including rights to demand books/documents and inspect Bureau offices. CAG-certified accounts and audit reports are forwarded annually to Central Government and laid before both Houses of Parliament.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Accounts maintenance requirement
- Annual statement of accounts (prescribed form)
- CAG consultation authority
- Audit interval specification
- Audit expense payment (by Bureau)
- CAG audit rights and privileges
- Document production authority
- Office inspection authority
- Parliamentary laying requirement

**TEXT:**

"25. Accounts and audit.—(1) The Bureau shall maintain proper accounts and other relevant records and prepare an annual statement of accounts in such form as may be prescribed by the Central Government in consultation with the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India.

(2) The accounts of the Bureau shall be audited by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India at such intervals as may be specified by him and any expenditure incurred in connection with such audit shall be payable by the Bureau to the Comptroller and Auditor-General.

(3) The Comptroller and Auditor-General of India and any other person appointed by him in connection with the audit of the accounts of the Bureau shall have the same rights and privileges and authority in connection with such audit as the Comptroller and Auditor-General generally has in connection with the audit of the Government accounts, and in particular, shall have the right to demand the production of books, accounts, connected vouchers and other documents and papers and to inspect any of the offices of the Bureau.

(4) The accounts of the Bureau as certified by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India or any other person appointed by him in this behalf together with the audit report thereon shall be forwarded annually to the Central Government and that Government shall cause the same to be laid before each House of Parliament."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 56(2)(p) - Rules for account form prescription

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** CAG audits under Article 149 of Indian Constitution; audit reports go to Parliament via Central Government.

## CHUNK 37: SECTION 26 - PENALTY (PART A: GENERAL PENALTIES FOR FAILURES)

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter VIII / Section 26

**PROVISION:** Section 26(1)

**TITLE:** Penalties for Non-Compliance - General Provisions and Penalty Ceiling

**SUMMARY:** If any person fails to comply with provisions of Section 14 clauses (c), (d), (h), (i), (k), (l), (r), (s) or Section 15 clauses (b), (c), (h), they are liable to penalty not exceeding 10 lakh rupees for each failure, plus additional penalty up to 10,000 rupees per day for continuing failure. Provided: no penalty is payable within 5 years of Act commencement. The 2010 Amendment increased penalty from 10,000 rupees to 10 lakh rupees and daily penalty from 1,000 rupees to 10,000 rupees.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Non-compliance penalty liability
- Specific provision violations (Section 14 and 15)
- Penalty amount ceiling (10 lakh rupees per failure)
- Daily continuing failure penalty (10,000 rupees)
- 5-year grace period from commencement
- 2010 Amendment increases

**TEXT:**

"26. Penalty.—(1) If any person fails to comply with the provisions of clause (c) or clause (d) or clause (h) or clause (i) or clause (k) or clause (l) or clause (r) or clause (s) of section 14 or clause (b) or clause (c) or clause (h) of section 15, he shall be liable to a penalty which shall not exceed ten lakh rupees for each such failure and, in the case of continuing failure, with an additional penalty which may extend to ten thousand rupees for every day during which such failure continues:

Provided that no person shall be liable to pay penalty within five years from the date of commencement of this Act."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 14 - Equipment prohibition, labeling, audits, information, energy manager, compliance, schemes
- Section 15 - Building compliance, audits, information
- Section 27 - Adjudication procedure
- Section 28 - Factors for penalty assessment

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Specific violations triggering penalties (not all Section 14/15 provisions); 5-year immunity expires at commencement date.

## CHUNK 38: SECTION 26 - PENALTY (PART B: COMPLIANCE NORM VIOLATION PENALTY)

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter VIII / Section 26

**PROVISION:** Section 26(1A)

**TITLE:** Penalties for Energy Consumption Norm Violations

**SUMMARY:** Section 26(1A) (inserted 2010) creates separate penalty for failure to comply with Section 14(n) (compliance with energy consumption norms). Penalty is not less than the value (at prescribed MTOE rate) of excess energy consumed over the prescribed norms. This incentivizes norm compliance; greater excess generates proportionately higher penalties. The provision links penalty to actual energy inefficiency as valued in MTOE terms.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Norm compliance violation penalty
- Excess consumption valuation (MTOE-based)
- Proportionate penalty mechanism
- Price-linked penalty
- 2010 Amendment addition

**TEXT:**

"(1A) If any person fails to comply with the provisions of clause (n) of section 14, he shall be liable to a penalty which shall not exceed ten lakh rupees and, in the case of continuing failure, with an additional penalty which shall not be less than the price of every metric ton of oil equivalent of energy, prescribed under this Act, that is in excess of the prescribed norms."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 14(n) - Compliance with energy consumption norms
- Section 14B - Energy value (MTOE) prescription authority
- Section 56(2)(laa) - Rules for MTOE value

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** 2010 Amendment (Act 28 of 2010, s. 8); penalty floor is MTOE-priced excess; ceiling is 10 lakh rupees (same as Section 26(1)).

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** This section creates economic disincentive proportional to excess consumption; penalty = (Excess MTOE units) × (Prescribed MTOE price), subject to 10 lakh rupee cap.

## CHUNK 40: SECTION 27 - POWER TO ADJUDICATE (PART A: ADJUDICATING OFFICER APPOINTMENT AND INQUIRY PROCEDURE)

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter VIII / Section 27

**PROVISION:** Section 27(1)-(2)

**TITLE:** Penalty Adjudication - Officer Appointment and Inquiry Procedure

**SUMMARY:** For adjudging penalties under Section 26, the State Commission appoints one of its members as adjudicating officer to hold inquiry (prescribed manner) after giving accused person reasonable opportunity to be heard. If no State Commission exists, State Government appoints an officer not below Secretary rank (legal affairs ministry) as adjudicating officer; such officer ceases on State Commission appointment. When adjudicating officer ceases, they transfer all pending matters to State Commission's adjudicating officer, who continues adjudication.

During inquiry, adjudicating officer may summon witnesses and demand documents. If satisfied that person violated provisions of Section 14/15 clauses specified in Section 26, officer may impose penalty under Section 26(1) or (1A).

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Adjudication officer appointment (State Commission member)
- Fallback appointment (Secretary-rank government officer)
- Inquiry procedure (prescribed manner)
- Reasonable opportunity to be heard (audi alteram partem)
- Witness summons authority
- Documentary evidence authority
- Penalty imposition authority
- Transition procedures

**TEXT:**

"27. Power to adjudicate.—(1) For the purpose of adjudging under section 26, the State Commission shall appoint any of its members to be an adjudicating officer for holding an inquiry in such manner as may be prescribed by the Central Government, after giving any person concerned a reasonable opportunity of being heard for the purpose of imposing any penalty.

(2) While holding an inquiry, the adjudicating officer shall have power to summon and enforce the attendance of any person acquainted with the facts and circumstances of the case to give evidence or produce any document which in the opinion of the adjudicating officer, may be useful for or relevant to the subject-matter of the inquiry, and if, on such inquiry, he is satisfied that the person has failed to comply with the provisions of any of the clauses of the sections specified in section 26, he may impose such penalty as he thinks fit in accordance with the provisions of any of those clauses of that section:

Provided that where a State Commission has not been established in a State, the Government of that State shall appoint any of its officer not below the rank equivalent to a Secretary dealing with legal affairs in that State to be an adjudicating officer for the purposes of this section and such officer shall cease to be an adjudicating officer immediately on the appointment of an adjudicating officer by the State Commission on its establishment in that State:

Provided further that where an adjudicating officer appointed by a State Government ceased to be an adjudicating officer, he shall transfer to the adjudicating officer appointed by the State Commission all matters being adjudicated by him and thereafter the adjudicating officer appointed by the State Commission shall adjudicate the penalties on such matters."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 26 - Penalty provisions (adjudication subject)
- Section 28 - Factors for penalty assessment
- Section 56(2)(q) - Rules for inquiry manner

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** 
- State Commission under Electricity Regulatory Commissions Act, 1998
- Procedural fairness required (reasonable opportunity to be heard)

## CHUNK 41: SECTION 28 - FACTORS TO BE TAKEN INTO ACCOUNT BY ADJUDICATING OFFICER

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter VIII / Section 28

**PROVISION:** Section 28

**TITLE:** Penalty Assessment Factors - Quantum Determination Principles

**SUMMARY:** While assessing penalty quantum under Section 26, adjudicating officer must consider: (a) disproportionate gain or unfair advantage made as a result of default (where quantifiable); and (b) repetitive nature of default. These factors enable graduated penalties reflecting culpability and pattern of violation.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Penalty quantum assessment authority
- Disproportionate gain/unfair advantage factor
- Quantification requirement
- Repetitive violation factor
- Graduated penalty principle

**TEXT:**

"28. Factors to be taken into account by adjudicating officer.—While adjudicating the quantum of penalty under section 26, the adjudicating officer shall have due regard to the following factors, namely:—

(a) the amount of disproportionate gain or unfair advantage, wherever quantifiable, made as a result of the default;

(b) the repetitive nature of the default."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 26 - Penalty ceiling and framework
- Section 27 - Adjudication procedure

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Provides guidance for penalty discretion within Section 26 ceilings; factors reflect both unjust enrichment and pattern violations.

## CHUNK 42: SECTION 29 - CIVIL COURT NOT TO HAVE JURISDICTION

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter VIII / Section 29

**PROVISION:** Section 29

**TITLE:** Adjudicative Exclusivity - Civil Court Ouster Clause

**SUMMARY:** No civil court has jurisdiction to entertain suit or proceeding in respect of any matter which an adjudicating officer or Appellate Tribunal is empowered to determine under the Act. No court may grant injunction for any action taken or to be taken in pursuance of Act powers. This ensures adjudicative exclusivity and protects enforcement actions from court interference.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Civil court jurisdiction ouster
- Adjudicating officer and Appellate Tribunal exclusivity
- Injunction prohibition
- Enforcement action protection
- Administrative law separation

**TEXT:**

"29. Civil court not to have jurisdiction.—No civil court shall have jurisdiction to entertain any suit or proceeding in respect of any matter which an adjudicating officer appointed under this Act or the Appellate Tribunal is empowered by or under this Act to determine and no injunction shall be granted by any court or other authority in respect of any action taken or to be taken in pursuance of any power conferred by or under this Act."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 26, 27 - Adjudication matters
- Section 30, 31 - Appellate Tribunal matters

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Creates exclusive administrative adjudication forum; prevents court interference.
