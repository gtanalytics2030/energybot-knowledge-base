# Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 - Batch 4: Bureau Name Protection and Central Government Powers

---

## CHUNK 4.1

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 5 - Insertion of New Section 13A

**PROVISION:** Section 13A - Prohibition of Use of Bureau Name

**TITLE:** Protection of Bureau Name and Prevention of Deceptive Naming

**SUMMARY:** New Section 13A prohibits any person from using the Bureau's name, or any name closely resembling it, without permission, if such use is likely to deceive the public. It further mandates that no registering authority shall register any company, firm, or entity bearing a name that resembles the Bureau's name. This provision protects the Bureau's institutional identity and prevents misrepresentation.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Bureau name protection
- Deceptive naming prohibition
- Public protection
- Registering authority obligations
- Company registration requirements
- Firm registration
- Body of persons registration

**TEXT:**

5. After section 13 of the principal Act, the following section shall be inserted, namely:—

"13A. (1) No person shall, without previous permission of the Bureau, use any name which so nearly resembles the name of the Bureau as to deceive or likely to deceive the public.

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force, no registering authority shall register any company, firm or other body of persons which bears any name or mark resembling the name of the Bureau."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, inserted after Section 13
- Registering authorities: Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA), Registrar of Companies (ROC), Partnership registries

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Related to intellectual property and organizational identity protection
- Applicable across all jurisdictions in India

## CHUNK 4.2

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 6 - Amendment of Section 14 (Powers of Central Government)

**PROVISION:** Section 6(i) and (ii) - Extension of Appliance Standards to Vehicles, Vessels, and Establishments

**TITLE:** Expansion of Energy Consumption Standards to Transportation and Other Sectors

**SUMMARY:** Section 14, clause (a) is amended to extend energy consumption standards applicability from "appliance" to include "appliance, vehicle, vessel, industrial unit, building or establishment." Clause (b) is similarly amended to enable the Central Government to specify energy consumption standards or norms for this expanded set of categories. This significantly broadens the Act's regulatory reach into transportation (vehicles and vessels) and establishes establishment-level efficiency requirements.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Energy consumption standards
- Appliance standards
- Vehicle fuel efficiency
- Vessel fuel efficiency
- Industrial unit standards
- Building efficiency
- Establishment norms
- Central Government authority
- Equipment classification

**TEXT:**

6. In section 14 of the principal Act,––

(i) in clause (a), for the word "appliance", the words "appliance, vehicle, vessel, industrial unit, building or establishment" shall be substituted;

(ii) in clause (b), after the words "class of equipment or appliances,", the words "or vehicle, vessel, industrial unit, building or establishment" shall be inserted;

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 14, clauses (a) and (b)
- Section 2 definitions: vehicle (ta), vessel (tb), building (c), energy (h)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Vehicle efficiency standards: Supported by Section 6(iii) - prohibition on non-compliant manufacture/import with penalty framework
- Vessel standards: Merchant marine fuel efficiency
- Industrial unit standards: Energy-intensive sector efficiency
- Building standards: Tied to energy conservation and sustainable building codes
- Section 12(2) provides penalty framework for non-compliance

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
This amendment expands the Act's scope from building and industrial facilities to the transportation sector (vehicles and vessels), reflecting India's commitment to comprehensive energy efficiency across all economic sectors.

## CHUNK 4.3

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 6 - Amendment of Section 14 (Powers of Central Government)

**PROVISION:** Section 6(iii) - Substitution of Clause (c) - Prohibition and Closure Mechanisms for Non-Compliant Equipment

**TITLE:** Enhanced Prohibition and Compliance Enforcement Mechanisms

**SUMMARY:** Clause (c) is comprehensively substituted to provide three mechanisms: (1) prohibition on manufacture or import of non-compliant equipment, appliances, vehicles, or vessels; (2) closure of operations for non-compliant industrial units; and (3) transition periods before enforcement—6 months for equipment/appliances/vehicles/vessels (extendable by up to 6 months) and 2 years for industrial unit closures. The Central Government may extend transition periods based on market share and technological development.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Prohibition of non-compliant equipment
- Import restrictions
- Manufacture bans
- Industrial unit closure
- Compliance enforcement
- Transition period
- Market share considerations
- Technological development
- Phase-in period
- Central Government discretion

**TEXT:**

(iii) for clause (c), the following clause shall be substituted, namely:––

"(c) prohibit manufacture or import of any equipment or appliance or vehicle or vessel specified under clause (b), unless it conforms to energy consumption standards specified under clause (a):

Provided that an industrial unit specified under clause (b) shall close its operations unless it conforms to the norms for processes or energy consumption standards specified under clause (a):

Provided further that from the date of notification of norms for processes and energy consumption standards under clause (a), no notification prohibiting such manufacture or import shall be issued––

(i) within a period of six months in the case of equipment or appliance or vehicle or vessel; and

(ii) within a period of two years for closure of industrial unit:

Provided also that the Central Government may, having regard to the market share and the technological development having impact on equipment or appliance or vehicle or vessel, and for reasons to be recorded in writing, extend the said period of six months referred to above, by a further period not exceeding six months;"

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 14, clause (c)
- Section 14, clauses (a) and (b) - Standards specification mechanism
- Section 14, clause (f) - Energy Intensive Industries definition

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Implementation period: 6 months for appliances/vehicles/vessels; 2 years for industrial units
- Market transition: Allows industry time to achieve compliance
- Technological development: Recognizes innovation curves in standards achievement
- Penalty provisions: Section 12(2) for non-compliance with manufacturer penalties

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
The prohibition mechanism is a critical enforcement tool, with built-in transition periods acknowledging business adjustment needs. The distinction between short-term (6 months + 6 months) for discrete products and longer-term (2 years) for industrial closures reflects sectoral realities. Written justification required for extensions ensures procedural fairness.

## CHUNK 4.7

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 6 - Amendment of Section 14 (Powers of Central Government)

**PROVISION:** Section 6(viii) - Insertion of New Clauses (w) and (x)

**TITLE:** Central Government Authority for Carbon Credit Scheme and Non-Fossil Energy Requirements

**SUMMARY:** Two new powers are added to the Central Government under Section 14: (w) authority to specify a carbon credit trading scheme for emissions reduction, and (x) authority to specify minimum shares of consumption of non-fossil energy sources by designated consumers, with discretion to specify different shares for different non-fossil sources and different designated consumer categories. These provisions establish the legal foundation for market-based carbon mechanisms and renewable energy mandates.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Carbon credit trading scheme
- Carbon emissions reduction
- Non-fossil energy sources
- Renewable energy mandates
- Designated consumers
- Energy and feedstock consumption
- Market-based mechanisms
- Central Government prescription
- Differentiated requirements

**TEXT:**

(viii) after clause (v), the following clauses shall be inserted, namely:––

"(w) specify the carbon credit trading scheme;

(x) specify minimum share of consumption of non-fossil sources by designated consumers as energy or feedstock, provided different share of consumption may be specified for different types of non-fossil sources for different designated consumers:"

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 14
- Section 2(da) - Definition of "carbon credit certificate"
- Section 2(db) - Definition of "carbon credit trading scheme"
- Section 8 - Insertion of Section 14AA (issuance of carbon credit certificates)
- Section 4(te) of Amendment Act - Bureau recommendation role

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Designated consumers: Energy-intensive industries, large commercial establishments with specified connected loads
- Non-fossil sources: Solar, wind, hydro, nuclear, biomass, tidal, geothermal, hydrogen
- Feedstock: Raw material for industrial processes using non-fossil energy
- Carbon credit trading: Mechanism for achieving energy conservation and emissions reduction targets
- India's climate commitments: 500 GW renewable energy capacity by 2030; carbon neutrality by 2070

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
These clauses operationalize India's commitment to climate action and renewable energy transition. The carbon credit scheme creates a market mechanism for voluntary or mandated emissions reductions, while non-fossil energy consumption mandates directly support renewable energy adoption targets. The flexibility to differentiate by source and consumer category enables tailored policy implementation.
