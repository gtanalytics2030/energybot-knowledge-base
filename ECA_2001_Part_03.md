## CHUNK 14: SECTION 12 - TRANSFER OF ASSETS, LIABILITIES AND EMPLOYEES OF ENERGY MANAGEMENT CENTRE

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter III / Section 12

**PROVISION:** Section 12

**TITLE:** Transfer of Assets, Liabilities, and Employees - Energy Management Centre to Bureau

**SUMMARY:** Upon Bureau establishment, all references to the Energy Management Centre in laws, contracts, or instruments are deemed references to the Bureau. All EMC properties, assets (movable/immovable), rights, and liabilities transfer to the Bureau. All debts, obligations, contracts, and legal proceedings of EMC become Bureau's debts, obligations, contracts, and proceedings. All sums due to EMC become due to Bureau. All suits by or against EMC may be continued or instituted by or against the Bureau. All EMC employees transfer to Bureau employment with same tenure, remuneration, leave, provident fund, retirement benefits, on same terms—or employee may opt out within six months. Absorption does not entitle compensation under Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Asset transfer
- Liability succession
- Contract novation (deemed assignment)
- Legal proceedings continuity
- Employee transition
- Remuneration preservation
- Benefit continuation
- Exclusion of severance compensation

**TEXT:**

"12. Transfer of assets, liabilities and employees of Energy Management Centre.—(1) On and from the date of establishment of the Bureau—

(a) any reference to the Energy Management Centre in any law other than this Act or in any contract or other instrument shall be deemed as a reference to the Bureau;

(b) all properties and assets, movable and immovable of, or belonging to, the Energy Management Centre shall vest in the Bureau;

(c) all the rights and liabilities of the Energy Management Centre shall be transferred to, and be the rights and liabilities of, the Bureau;

(d) without prejudice to the provisions of clause (c), all debts, obligations and liabilities incurred, all contracts entered into and all matters and things engaged to be done by, with or for the Energy Management Centre immediately before that date, for or in connection with the purposes of the said Centre shall be deemed to have been incurred, entered into, or engaged to be done by, with or for, the Bureau;

(e) all sums of money due to the Energy Management Centre immediately before that date shall be deemed to be due to the Bureau;

(f) all suits and other legal proceedings instituted or which could have been instituted by or against the Energy Management Centre immediately before that date may be continued or may be instituted by or against the Bureau; and

(g) every employee holding any office under the Energy Management Centre immediately before that date shall hold his office in the Bureau by the same tenure and upon the same terms and conditions of service as respects remuneration, leave, provident fund, retirement or other terminal benefits as he would have held such office if the Bureau had not been established and shall continue to do so as an employee of the Bureau or until the expiry of six months from that date if such employee opts not to be the employee of the Bureau within such period.

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (14 of 1947) or in any other law for the time being in force, the absorption of any employee by the Bureau in its regular service under this section shall not entitle such employee to any compensation under that Act or other law and no such claim shall be entertained by any court, tribunal or other authority."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2(l) - Energy Management Centre definition (1989 establishment)
- Section 3 - Bureau establishment date

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (14 of 1947) - Expressly excluded for compensation claims
- Government of India Resolution dated 5th July, 1989

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** This is a complete institutional succession/merger provision. Deemed novation of all contracts; all legal proceedings continue automatically; employees retain all accrued benefits but no additional severance is due. Opt-out window of 6 months provided.

## CHUNK 15: SECTION 13 - POWERS AND FUNCTIONS OF BUREAU (PART A: SUBSECTION 1-2 INTRODUCTION AND CLAUSES A-H)

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter IV / Section 13

**PROVISION:** Section 13(1)-(2) clauses (a) through (h)

**TITLE:** Powers and Functions of Bureau - Coordination, Standards, Labels, and Audits

**SUMMARY:** The Bureau effectively coordinates with designated consumers, agencies, and others, recognizing/utilizing existing resources and infrastructure in performing assigned functions. Section 13(2) grants the Bureau specific powers including: (a) recommending energy consumption standards for equipment/appliances; (aa) recommending energy savings certificates; (b) recommending label particulars for equipment; (c) recommending designated consumer notifications; (d) prescribing building energy code guidelines; (e) creating awareness and disseminating conservation information; (f) arranging training for personnel; (g) strengthening consultancy services; (h) promoting research and development in energy conservation.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Coordination mechanism
- Standards recommendation
- Labeling authority
- Awareness and training
- Research promotion
- Consultancy development
- Resource utilization

**TEXT:**

"13. Powers and functions of Bureau.—(1) The Bureau shall, effectively co-ordinate with designated consumers, designated agencies and other agencies, recognise and utilise the existing resources and infrastructure, in performing the functions assigned to it by or under this Act.

(2) The Bureau may perform such functions and exercise such powers as may be assigned to it by or under this Act and in particular, such functions and powers include the function and power to—

(a) recommend to the Central Government the norms for processes and energy consumption standards required to be notified under clause (a) of section 14;

(aa) recommend to the Central Government for issuing of the energy savings certificate under section 14A;

(b) recommend to the Central Government the particulars required to be displayed on label on equipment or on appliances and manner of their display under clause (d) of section 14;

(c) recommend to the Central Government for notifying any user or class of users of energy as a designated consumer under clause (e) of section 14;

(d) take suitable steps to prescribe guidelines for energy conservation building codes under clause (p) of section 14;

(e) take all measures necessary to create awareness and disseminate information for efficient use of energy and its conservation;

(f) arrange and organise training of personnel and specialists in the techniques for efficient use of energy and its conservation;

(g) strengthen consultancy services in the field of energy conservation;

(h) promote research and development in the field of energy conservation;"

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 14 - Central Government powers (implementing authority)
- Section 14A - Energy savings certificate power
- Section 2 - Designated consumer, designated agency definitions

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Bureau makes recommendations; Central Government issues binding notifications under Section 14.

## CHUNK 18: SECTION 14 - POWER OF CENTRAL GOVERNMENT TO ENFORCE EFFICIENT USE OF ENERGY AND ITS CONSERVATION (PART A: CLAUSES A-D)

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter V / Section 14

**PROVISION:** Section 14, clauses (a) through (d)

**TITLE:** Central Government Powers - Standards, Equipment Specification, Prohibition, and Labeling

**SUMMARY:** The Central Government may, by notification in consultation with the Bureau, specify: (a) norms for process and energy consumption standards for any equipment/appliance; (b) equipment or appliances to be covered; (c) prohibit manufacture/sale/purchase/import of specified equipment not conforming to standards. Prohibition notification cannot be issued within 6 months of standards notification; the Government may extend this period by further 6 months (maximum 12 months total) considering market share, technological development, industry capacity, and equipment availability. (d) Direct display of energy consumption particulars on equipment labels in specified manner.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Standards specification authority
- Equipment scope definition
- Prohibition mechanism
- Market protection period (6-12 months)
- Technological consideration
- Labeling directive
- Notification instrument

**TEXT:**

"14. Power of Central Government to enforce efficient use of energy and its conservation.—The Central Government may, by notification, in consultation with the Bureau,—

(a) specify the norms for processes and energy consumption standards for any equipment, appliance which consumes, generates, transmits or supplies energy;

(b) specify equipment or appliance or class of equipments or appliances, as the case may be, for the purposes of this Act;

(c) prohibit manufacture or sale or purchase or import of equipment or appliance specified under clause (b), unless such equipment or appliance conforms to energy consumption standards:

Provided that no notification prohibiting manufacture or sale or purchase or import of equipment or appliance shall be issued within a period of six months from the date of notification issued under clause (a) of this section:

Provided further that the Central Government may, having regard to the market share and the technological development having impact on equipment or appliance, and for reasons to be recorded in writing, extend the said period of six months referred to in the first proviso by a further period not exceeding six months;

(d) direct display of such particulars on label on equipment or on appliance specified under clause (b) and in such manner as may be specified by regulations;"

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 13(2)(a) - Bureau recommendation for standards
- Section 13(2)(b) - Bureau recommendation for label particulars
- Section 58(2)(i) - Regulations for label particulars

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Central Government acts on Bureau recommendations; 2010 Amendment added proviso for extended market protection period.

## CHUNK 22: SECTION 14A - POWER OF CENTRAL GOVERNMENT TO ISSUE ENERGY SAVINGS CERTIFICATE

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter V / Section 14A

**PROVISION:** Section 14A

**TITLE:** Energy Savings Certificate - Issuance and Trading Mechanism

**SUMMARY:** Section 14A (inserted by 2010 Amendment) permits the Central Government to issue energy savings certificates (ESCs) to designated consumers whose energy consumption is less than prescribed norms/standards, according to prescribed procedure. Designated consumers exceeding prescribed norms/standards are entitled to purchase ESCs to achieve compliance. This creates a market-based compliance mechanism allowing over-performers to trade savings with under-performers.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Energy savings certificate issuance
- Over-compliance reward mechanism
- Market-based compliance trading
- Procedure prescription authority
- Flexibility mechanism
- 2010 Amendment addition

**TEXT:**

"14A. Power of Central Government to issue energy savings certificate.—(1) The Central Government may issue the energy savings certificate to the designated consumer whose energy consumption is less than the prescribed norms and standards in accordance with the procedure as may be prescribed.

(2) The designated consumer whose energy consumption is more than the prescribed norms and standards shall be entitled to purchase the energy savings certificate to comply with the prescribed norms and standards."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2(ma) - Energy savings certificate definition
- Section 56(2)(la) - Rule-making for ESC procedure
- Section 13(2)(aa) - Bureau's ESC recommendation function

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Added by Act 28 of 2010, s. 7. Creates positive incentive structure for efficiency.

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** ESCs enable flexible compliance: high performers generate tradeable credits; under-performers can purchase to meet standards. This is a cap-and-trade mechanism for energy efficiency.

**KEY TOPICS:**

- Compliance calculation basis
- Trading mechanism foundation
- 2010 Amendment addition

**TEXT:**

"14B. Power of Central Government to specify value of energy.—The Central Government may, in consultation with the Bureau, prescribe the value of per metric ton of oil equivalent of energy consumed for the purposes of this Act."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 56(2)(laa) - Rule-making for MTOE value

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Added by Act 28 of 2010, s. 7. Complements Section 14A ESC mechanism by establishing energy value basis.

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** MTOE value is critical parameter for: (1) Penalty calculation under Section 26(1A); (2) ESC trading pricing; (3) Efficiency achievement assessment.
