## CHUNK 43: SECTION 30 - APPELLATE TRIBUNAL

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter IX / Section 30

**PROVISION:** Section 30

**TITLE:** Appellate Tribunal - Establishment and Function

**SUMMARY:** The Appellate Tribunal established under Section 110 of the Electricity Act, 2003, serves as the Appellate Tribunal for this Act without prejudice to Electricity Act provisions. It hears appeals against orders of adjudicating officers, Central Government, State Government, or other authorities under this Act. The 2010 Amendment transferred tribunal authority from a dedicated tribunal to the Electricity Act's multi-sector tribunal.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Appellate forum establishment
- Electricity Act 2003 integration
- Multi-sector tribunal (electricity and energy conservation)
- Appeal jurisdiction
- Authority orders appealable

**TEXT:**

"30. Appellate Tribunal.—The Appellate Tribunal established under section 110 of the Electricity Act, 2003 (36 of 2003) shall, without prejudice to the provisions of the Electricity Act, 2003, be the Appellate Tribunal for the purposes of this Act and hear appeals against the orders of the adjudicating officer or the Central Government or the State Government or any other authority under this Act."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 110 of Electricity Act, 2003 (36 of 2003)
- Section 31 - Appeal procedure
- Section 27 - Adjudicating officer orders (appealable)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** 
- 2010 Amendment (Act 28 of 2010, s. 9) completely substituted Section 30
- Previously had dedicated tribunal; now uses Electricity Act tribunal

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Section 30 was substantially changed in 2010, shifting from dedicated tribunal to shared Electricity Act tribunal infrastructure. This reflects convergence of electricity regulation and energy conservation policy.

## CHUNK 44: SECTION 31 - APPEAL TO APPELLATE TRIBUNAL (PART A: APPEAL FILING AND DEPOSIT REQUIREMENTS)

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter IX / Section 31

**PROVISION:** Section 31(1)-(2)

**TITLE:** Appeal to Tribunal - Filing Requirements and Penalty Deposit

**SUMMARY:** Any person aggrieved by order of adjudicating officer, Central Government, State Government, or other authority may appeal to Appellate Tribunal. Person appealing against adjudicating officer's penalty order must deposit the penalty amount while filing appeal. Provided: Tribunal may dispense with deposit if it considers payment would cause undue hardship, subject to conditions safeguarding penalty realization. Appeals must be filed within 45 days of receiving order copy in prescribed form, verified manner, and with prescribed fee. Tribunal may entertain late appeals if satisfied sufficient cause prevented timely filing.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Appeal right (aggrieved party)
- Penalty deposit requirement (mandatory)
- Hardship exemption (Tribunal discretion)
- Realization safeguard conditions
- 45-day filing deadline
- Prescribed form and verification
- Fee requirement
- Late filing exception

**TEXT:**

"31. Appeal to Appellate Tribunal.—(1) Any person aggrieved, by an order made by an adjudicating officer or the Central Government or the State Government or any other authority under this Act, may prefer an appeal to the Appellate Tribunal for Energy Conservation:

Provided that any person, appealing against the order of the adjudicating officer levying any penalty, shall, while filing the appeal, deposit the amount of such penalty:

Provided further that where in any particular case, the Appellate Tribunal is of the opinion that the deposit of such penalty would cause undue hardship to such person, the Appellate Tribunal may dispense with such deposit subject to such conditions as it may deem fit to impose so as to safeguard the realisation of penalty.

(2) Every appeal under sub-section (1) shall be filed within a period of forty-five days from the date on which a copy of the order made by the adjudicating officer or the Central Government or the State Government or any other authority is received by the aggrieved person and it shall be in such form, verified in such manner and be accompanied by such fee as may be prescribed:

Provided that the Appellate Tribunal may entertain an appeal after the expiry of the said period of forty-five days if it is satisfied that there was sufficient cause for not filing it within that period."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 26 - Penalty orders (appealable)
- Section 27 - Adjudicating officer orders (appealable)
- Section 56(2)(r) - Rules for appeal form/fee

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Penalty deposit creates finality incentive while allowing hardship relief; 45-day deadline with condonable delay; prescribed procedures.

## CHUNK 45: SECTION 31 - APPEAL TO APPELLATE TRIBUNAL (PART B: TRIBUNAL POWERS AND DISPOSAL)

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter IX / Section 31

**PROVISION:** Section 31(3)-(6)

**TITLE:** Appeal Tribunal Powers - Hearing, Decision, Timeline, and Record Examination

**SUMMARY:** On receipt of appeal, Tribunal may (after giving parties opportunity to be heard) pass orders confirming, modifying, or setting aside the appealed order. Tribunal sends copy of every order to parties, adjudicating officer, and relevant authority. Appeals should be dealt expeditiously with endeavor to dispose finally within 180 days of receipt; if not disposed, Tribunal must record written reasons. Tribunal may, on own motion or otherwise, call for proceeding records to examine legality, propriety, or correctness of any order made by adjudicating officer, Central Government, State Government, or other authority, and make such order as it thinks fit.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Tribunal hearing procedure
- Party participation right
- Decision options (confirm, modify, set aside)
- Order communication requirement
- 180-day disposal target
- Delay reasoning requirement
- Suo moto examination power
- Legality review authority
- Propriety review authority
- Correctness review authority
- Discretionary ordering authority

**TEXT:**

"(3) On receipt of an appeal under sub-section (1), the Appellate Tribunal may, after giving the parties to the appeal an opportunity of being heard, pass such orders thereon as it thinks fit, confirming, modifying or setting aside the order appealed against.

(4) The Appellate Tribunal shall send a copy of every order made by it to the parties to the appeal and to the concerned adjudicating officer or the Central Government or the State Government or any other authority.

(5) The appeal filed before the Appellate Tribunal under sub-section (1) shall be dealt with by it as expeditiously as possible and endeavour shall be made by it to dispose of the appeal finally within one hundred and eighty days from the date of receipt of the appeal:

Provided that where an appeal could not be disposed of within the said period of one hundred and eighty days, the Appellate Tribunal shall record its reasons in writing for not disposing of the appeal within the said period.

(6) The Appellate Tribunal may, for the purpose of examining the legality, propriety or correctness of any order made by the adjudicating officer or the Central Government or the State Government or any other authority under this Act, as the case may be, in relation to any proceeding, on its own motion or otherwise, call for the records of such proceedings and make such order in the case as it thinks fit."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 31(1) - Appeal filing
- Section 27 - Adjudicating officer orders

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Tribunal has full merits review authority; 180-day disposal target with flexibility for complex matters; suo moto power enables proactive review.

**KEY TOPICS:**

**TEXT:**

"45. Appeal to Supreme Court.—Any person aggrieved by any decision or order of the Appellate Tribunal, may, file an appeal to the Supreme Court within sixty days from the date of communication of the decision or order of the Appellate Tribunal to him, on any one or more of the grounds specified in section 100 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (5 of 1908):

Provided that the Supreme Court may, if it is satisfied that the appellant was prevented by sufficient cause from filing the appeal within the said period, allow it to be filed within a further period not exceeding sixty days."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 31 - Appeal to Tribunal
- Section 100, Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 - Limited appeal grounds

## CHUNK 50: SECTION 47 - POWER OF CENTRAL GOVERNMENT TO SUPERSEDE BUREAU

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter X / Section 47

**PROVISION:** Section 47

**TITLE:** Central Government Supersession - Grave Emergency, Default, or Public Interest

**SUMMARY:** Central Government may supersede the Bureau (notification, max 6 months) if: (a) grave emergency prevents function discharge; (b) persistent default in complying with Central Government directions and Act duties resulting in financial/administrative deterioration; or (c) public interest requires supersession. Upon supersession: (i) all appointed members (Section 4(2) clauses o, p, q) vacate office; (ii) all Bureau powers/functions assumed by person(s) directed by Central Government until reconstitution; (iii) all Bureau property vests in Central Government until reconstitution. On expiration of supersession period or earlier, Central Government may reconstitute Bureau by fresh appointment; previously vacated members are not disqualified for re-appointment. Central Government must lay supersession notification and full report before Parliament immediately.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Supersession authority
- Supersession grounds (emergency, default, public interest)
- Maximum supersession period (6 months)
- Appointed member removal effect
- Administrator power delegation
- Property vesting (temporary)
- Reconstitution authority
- Disqualification prevention
- Parliamentary reporting requirement

**TEXT:**

"47. Power of Central Government to supersede Bureau.—(1) If at any time the Central Government is of opinion—

(a) that on account of grave emergency, the Bureau is unable to discharge the functions and duties imposed on it by or under the provisions of this Act; or

(b) that the Bureau has persistently made default in complying with any direction issued by the Central Government under this Act or in discharge of the functions and duties imposed on it by or under the provisions of this Act and as a result of such default, the financial position of the Bureau had deteriorated or the administration of the Bureau had deteriorated; or

(c) that circumstances exist which render it necessary in the public interest so to do, the Central Government may, by notification, supersede the Bureau for such period, not exceeding six months, as may be specified in the notification.

(2) Upon the publication of a notification under sub-section (1) superseding the Bureau,—

(a) all the members referred to in clauses (o), (p) and (q) of sub-section (2) of section 4 shall, as from the date of supersession, vacate their offices as such;

(b) all the powers, functions and duties which may, by or under the provisions of this Act, be exercised or discharged by or on behalf of the Bureau, shall until the Bureau is reconstituted under sub-section (3), be exercised and discharged by such person or persons as the Central Government may direct; and

(c) all property owned or controlled by the Bureau shall, until the Bureau is reconstituted under sub-section (3), vest in the Central Government.

(3) On the expiration of the period of supersession specified in the notification issued under sub-section (1), the Central Government may reconstitute the Bureau by a fresh appointment and in such case any person or persons who vacated their offices under clause (a) of sub-section (2), shall not be deemed disqualified for appointment:

Provided that the Central Government may, at any time, before the expiration of the period of supersession, take action under this sub-section.

(4) The Central Government shall cause a notification issued under sub-section (1) and a full report of any action taken under this section and the circumstances leading to such action to be laid before each House of Parliament at the earliest."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 4 - Governing Council composition (appointed members)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Supersession is extraordinary remedy; parliamentary accountability required; appointed members protected from permanent disqualification.

## CHUNK 51: SECTION 48 - DEFAULT BY COMPANIES

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Central Legislation

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter X / Section 48

**PROVISION:** Section 48

**TITLE:** Corporate Liability - Officers and Company Default

**SUMMARY:** Where a company defaults in complying with Section 14 clauses (c), (d), (h), (i), (k), (l), (n), (r), (s) or Section 15 clauses (b), (c), (h), every person in charge of and responsible for company business conduct, as well as the company, are deemed to have contravened those provisions and liable to penalty under Section 26. Provided: person is not liable if they prove contravention was without their knowledge or they exercised due diligence to prevent it. Additionally, if any such contravention occurs with consent/connivance of or is attributable to neglect by any director, manager, secretary, or company officer, such officer is also deemed to have contravened and liable to penalty. "Company" means body corporate, including firms or associations of individuals.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Corporate liability doctrine
- Officer personal liability
- "Person in charge" liability
- Responsible conduct standard
- Knowledge defense
- Due diligence defense
- Director liability (consent/connivance/neglect)
- Manager, secretary, officer liability
- Partnership and association inclusion

**TEXT:**

"48. Default by companies.—(1) Where a company makes a default in complying with the provisions of clause (c) or clause (d) or clause (h) or clause (i) or clause (k) or clause (l) or clause (n) or clause (r) or clause (s) of section 14 or clause (b) or clause (c) or clause (h) of section 15, every person who at the time of such contravention was in charge of, and was responsible to the company for the conduct of the business of the company, as well as the company, shall be deemed to have acted in contravention of the said provisions and shall be liable to be proceeded against and imposed penalty under section 26 accordingly:

Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall render any such person liable for penalty provided in this Act if he proves that the contravention of the aforesaid provisions was committed without his knowledge or that he exercised all due diligence to prevent the contravention of the aforesaid provision.

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1), where any contravention of the provisions of clause (c) or clause (d) or clause (h) or clause (i) or clause (k) or clause (l) or clause (n) or clause (r) or clause (s) of section 14 or clause (b) or clause (c) or clause (h) of section 15 has been committed with the consent or connivance of, or is attributable to, any neglect on the part of, any director, manager, secretary or other officer of the company, such director, manager, secretary or other officer shall also be deemed to have contravened the said provisions and shall be liable to be proceeded for imposition of penalty accordingly.

Explanation.—For the purposes of this section, "company" means a body corporate and includes a firm or other association of individuals."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 14 - Central Government powers (violation clauses)
- Section 15 - State Government powers (violation clauses)
- Section 26 - Penalty framework

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Dual liability (company + officer); knowledge and due diligence defenses available; partnership/association expressly included.

- State Legislature laying requirement
- Democratic oversight

**TEXT:**

"59. Rules and regulations to be laid before Parliament and State Legislature.—(1) Every rule made by the Central Government and every regulation made under this Act shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament while it is in session, for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or regulation, or both Houses agree that the rule or regulation should not be made, the rule or regulation shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that rule or regulation.
