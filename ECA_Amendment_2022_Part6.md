# Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 - Batch 6: Penalty Amendments and Compliance Enforcement

---

## CHUNK 6.1

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 12 - Substitution of New Section 26

**PROVISION:** Section 26(1) - Penalty for Non-Compliance with Bureau Functions and State Functions

**TITLE:** Enhanced Penalty Framework for Non-Compliance with Energy Conservation Obligations

**SUMMARY:** Section 26(1) is amended to prescribe penalties (up to ₹10 lakh) for failure to comply with multiple Bureau and State Government functions, specifically: Section 14(h) or (i) or (k) or (l) [energy manager/auditor requirements, energy consumption standards], or Section 15(c) or (h) [State notification and implementation obligations]. A continuing failure proviso adds ₹10,000 per day penalty for ongoing non-compliance.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Penalty amount
- Compliance failure
- Energy manager/auditor
- Designated consumers
- Energy efficiency standards
- Continuing violation
- Daily penalty accrual
- State obligation enforcement

**TEXT:**

12. For section 26 of the principal Act, the following section shall be substituted, namely:––

"26. (1) If any person fails to comply with the provisions of clause (h) or clause (i) or clause (k) or clause (l) of section 14 or clause (c) or clause (h) of section 15, he shall be liable to a penalty which shall not exceed ten lakh rupees:

Provided that in the case of continuing failures, the person shall be liable to an additional penalty which may extend to ten thousand rupees for every day during which such failures continue."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 26
- Section 14(h) - Clause requiring energy manager/auditor for designated consumers
- Section 14(i) - Energy consumption standards
- Section 14(k) - Energy audit requirements
- Section 14(l) - Energy auditor/manager prescriptions
- Section 15(c) - State notification of codes
- Section 15(h) - State implementation requirements

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Designated consumers: Industrial and commercial entities with specified energy consumption thresholds
- Energy manager/auditor qualifications: Prescribed under section 14(m)
- Enforcement: By Central and State authorities
- Related penalties: Section 26(2)-(5) for specific violations

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
Tier 1 penalty covers foundational compliance with energy management requirements. The daily penalty for continuing failures creates strong incentive for immediate compliance correction. This penalty tier is least severe but applies to essential operational requirements.

## CHUNK 6.2

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 12 - Substitution of New Section 26

**PROVISION:** Section 26(2) - Penalty for Non-Compliance with Energy Consumption Standards (Equipment, Vehicles, Vessels, Industrial Units)

**TITLE:** Differentiated Penalty Framework for Non-Compliant Equipment, Vehicles, Vessels, and Industrial Units

**SUMMARY:** Section 26(2) creates a comprehensive penalty framework for manufacturer/importer non-compliance with energy consumption standards prescribed under Section 14(c) and (d). Base penalty: ₹10 lakh plus additional penalties varying by category: (a) ₹2,000-₹5,000 per appliance/equipment, (b) for industrial units/vessels: twice the oil equivalent price of excess consumption, (c) for vehicle non-compliance: ₹25,000 per vehicle (0.2 L/100 km deviation) or ₹50,000 per vehicle (>0.2 L/100 km deviation). This creates proportionate financial consequences for market violations.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Manufacturer penalty
- Importer penalty
- Equipment compliance
- Appliance compliance
- Vehicle fuel efficiency
- Industrial unit standards
- Vessel efficiency
- Oil equivalent metrics
- Fuel consumption deviation
- Per-unit penalties
- Market compliance enforcement

**TEXT:**

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or any other Act for the time being in force, if any person fails to comply with the provisions of clauses (c) and (d) of section 14, he shall in addition to the penalty of ten lakh rupees, be also liable to pay additional penalty which shall not exceed five thousand rupees per appliance or equipment in relation to which the non-compliance has occurred, but shall not be lower than two thousand rupees:

Provided that where such non-compliance relates to any industrial unit or vessel, he shall also be liable to an additional penalty which shall not exceed twice the price of every metric ton of oil equivalent consumed in excess of the prescribed norms:

Provided further that if the manufacturer of a vehicle fails to comply with the fuel consumption norms, he shall also be liable to pay an additional penalty per unit of vehicles sold in the corresponding year, as follows, namely:––

(i) twenty-five thousand rupees per vehicle for non-compliance of norms up to 0.2 litres per 100 kms;

(ii) fifty thousand rupees per vehicle for non-compliance of norms above 0.2 litres per 100 kms."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 26
- Section 14(c) - Prohibition on manufacture/import
- Section 14(d) - Standards specification
- Section 6(iii) of Amendment Act - Substitution of section 14(c)
- Section 6(i) and (ii) of Amendment Act - Extensions to vehicles, vessels, industrial units

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Equipment and appliances: Household and industrial appliances, lighting, motors, compressors
- Industrial units: Energy-intensive industries (steel, cement, chemicals, refineries, etc.)
- Vessels: Merchant marine, cargo ships, tankers, container vessels
- Vehicles: Automobiles, heavy commercial vehicles, two-wheelers (per Motor Vehicles Act definition)
- Oil equivalent metric: Standard energy measurement for comparing consumption across categories
- Metric ton of oil equivalent (MTOE): 41.868 GJ of energy

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
This is the most complex penalty tier, reflecting the diverse compliance obligations across sectors. The per-unit penalties (especially vehicle penalties) create direct financial accountability for manufacturers, while the oil equivalent formula addresses industrial efficiency deviations. The graduated vehicle penalties (₹25K vs ₹50K) provide proportionate enforcement based on deviation severity.

## CHUNK 6.3

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 12 - Substitution of New Section 26

**PROVISION:** Section 26(3) - Penalty for Non-Compliance with Directions on Energy Consumption and Non-Fossil Energy Mandates

**TITLE:** Penalty for Non-Compliance with Central Government Directions on Energy Reduction and Non-Fossil Sourcing

**SUMMARY:** Section 26(3) prescribes penalties for non-compliance with Central Government directions issued under Section 14(n) [energy consumption reduction directions] and Section 14(x) [non-fossil energy source consumption mandates]. Base penalty: up to ₹10 lakh per failure, with an additional penalty up to twice the price of every metric ton of oil equivalent exceeding prescribed norms. This enforces both absolute energy reduction targets and renewable energy sourcing requirements.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Central Government direction
- Energy consumption reduction
- Non-fossil energy mandate
- Compliance direction
- Penalty enforcement
- Oil equivalent pricing
- Excess consumption metrics
- Designated consumer obligation

**TEXT:**

(3) If any person fails to comply with the directions issued under clauses (n) and (x) of section 14, he shall be liable to a penalty which shall not exceed ten lakh rupees for each such failure:

Provided that he shall also be liable to an additional penalty which shall not exceed twice the price of every metric ton of oil equivalent prescribed under this Act, which is in excess of the prescribed norms."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 26
- Section 14(n) - Power to issue directions on energy consumption reduction
- Section 14(x) - Power to specify non-fossil energy source consumption shares
- Section 6(viii) of Amendment Act - Insertion of clause (x)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Designated consumers: Industrial and large commercial entities subject to mandated efficiency improvements
- Non-fossil energy sources: Renewable and nuclear energy sources required to meet mandated percentages
- Direction mechanism: Central Government prescriptive power over large energy consumers
- Compliance timeline: Specified in the Direction issued by Central Government

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
This penalty tier addresses substantive energy policy non-compliance—when entities fail to reduce energy consumption or meet non-fossil energy sourcing mandates despite government direction. It's distinct from equipment standards (Tier 2) because it applies to designated consumers' operational decisions. The MTOE-based additional penalty aligns with the actual energy policy cost of non-compliance.

## CHUNK 6.4

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 12 - Substitution of New Section 26

**PROVISION:** Section 26(4) - Penalty for Bureau Name Violation and Information Non-Furnishment

**TITLE:** Penalty for Deceptive Naming and Failure to Provide Information

**SUMMARY:** Section 26(4) prescribes penalties for two distinct violations: (a) contravention of Section 13A (using Bureau's name deceptively) or failure to provide information under Section 52: initial penalty up to ₹50,000, with additional penalty up to ₹10,000 per day for subsequent violations or continued failures. This enforces institutional identity protection and data transparency requirements.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Bureau name protection
- Deceptive naming violation
- Information furnishing obligation
- Non-compliance with Section 52
- Daily penalty accrual
- Information disclosure
- Transparency requirement

**TEXT:**

(4) If a person fails to comply with the provisions of sub-section (1) of section 13A or fails to provide any information under section 52, he shall be liable to a penalty which may extend to fifty thousand rupees on first such non-compliance or failure:

Provided that for every subsequent non-compliance or failure, he shall be liable to pay an additional penalty which shall not exceed ten thousand rupees per day of such non-compliance or failure."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 26
- Section 13A - Prohibition of use of deceptive name (newly inserted by Section 5)
- Section 52 - Information furnishing obligation
- Section 15 of Amendment Act - Amendment to section 52

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Bureau name protection: Prevents misuse of institutional brand
- Section 52 information: Energy consumption, manufacturing standards, testing data
- Sample supply obligation: Manufacturers and importers must supply test samples

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
This penalty tier addresses administrative compliance—respecting the Bureau's institutional identity and providing required information. Tier 4 penalties are lower (₹50K initial, ₹10K/day) because these are primarily administrative/procedural failures rather than substantive energy policy violations.

## CHUNK 6.5

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 12 - Substitution of New Section 26

**PROVISION:** Section 26(5) - Penalty Recovery Mechanism

**TITLE:** Recovery of Penalties as Arrear of Land Revenue

**SUMMARY:** Section 26(5) provides that any unpaid penalty amount under Section 26 may be recovered as if it were an arrear of land revenue. This invokes the powerful recovery mechanism available under Indian revenue law, allowing collection through land sales or other revenue recovery procedures.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Penalty recovery
- Arrear of land revenue
- Revenue recovery procedure
- Collection mechanism
- Enforcement power

**TEXT:**

(5) Any amount payable under this section, if not paid, may be recovered as if it were an arrear of land revenue."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 26
- Section 26(1)-(4) - All penalty provisions

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Land Revenue Recovery: Under State revenue laws and the Revenue Recovery Act framework
- Enforcement authority: Central and State Governments, revenue officers
- Collection mechanism: Available across Indian states through established revenue recovery procedures

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
The arrear of land revenue mechanism is the strongest enforcement tool in Indian administrative law. By equating unpaid penalties to revenue arrears, the Act ensures that non-payment does not escape consequences—penalties can be collected through property attachment and auction if necessary.

## CHUNK 6.6

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 13 - Insertion of New Section 27A

**PROVISION:** Section 27A - Power of State Commission to Make Regulations

**TITLE:** State Commission Regulatory Authority and Procedural Rules

**SUMMARY:** New Section 27A empowers State Commissions (State-level energy bodies or regulatory authorities) to make regulations for their functions under the Act, including: (a) procedures for applications before the State Commission and applicable fees, and (b) other matters as prescribed. All State Commission regulations must be laid before each House of the State Legislature (or the single House if unicameral) as soon as possible after making.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- State Commission authority
- Regulation-making power
- Application procedure
- State Commission fee
- Legislative oversight
- Procedural rules
- Transparent governance

**TEXT:**

13. After section 27 of the principal Act, the following section shall be inserted, namely:––

"27A. (1) The State Commission may, by notification, make regulations for discharging its functions under this Act.

(2) In particular, and without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing power, such regulations may provide for—

(a) the manner of making application before the State Commission and the fee payable;

(b) any other matter which is to be, or may be, provided by regulations by the State Commission for the purposes of its function:

Provided that every regulation made by the State Commission under this section shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of the State Legislature where it consists of two Houses, or where such Legislature consists of one House, before that House."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, inserted after Section 27
- Section 27 of principal Act - (likely regarding Commission authority)
- Section 15 and 15A - Designated agency budget and functions

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- State Commission: Energy conservation body at State level (different from State Electricity Regulatory Commission)
- Regulation-making: Under statutory authority
- Legislative oversight: Mandatory tabling before State Legislature ensuring democratic accountability
- Similar framework: Followed in many statutes for delegated legislation

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
Section 27A creates a regulatory framework for State-level implementation, ensuring that State Commissions have clear authority to make procedural rules while remaining accountable to State Legislatures. The mandatory laying requirement prevents regulations from operating in administrative darkness.

## CHUNK 6.7

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 14 - Amendment of Section 28

**PROVISION:** Section 28 - Amendment to Section 28 (Scope of Commission Authority)

**TITLE:** Addition of Consumer Compensation Authority to State Commission Functions

**SUMMARY:** Section 28 is amended by adding a new clause (c) recognizing that the State Commission has authority regarding "the loss caused to a consumer and amount of compensation thereof." This explicitly establishes that State Commissions have power to determine and award compensation to consumers harmed by energy conservation violations or non-compliance by entities subject to the Act.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Consumer loss
- Compensation authority
- State Commission jurisdiction
- Consumer protection
- Damage remedy
- State Commission power

**TEXT:**

14. In section 28 of the principal Act, after clause (b), the following clause shall be inserted, namely:––

"(c) the loss caused to a consumer and amount of compensation thereof."

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 28
- Section 27A - State Commission regulatory authority

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Consumer protection: Extends Act's consumer remedies framework
- State Commission: Authority to award compensation
- Related to building code violations, energy efficiency failures affecting consumer welfare

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
This amendment recognizes that violations of energy conservation requirements can cause consumer harm (e.g., non-compliant buildings with poor efficiency, devices failing to meet standards). It explicitly grants State Commissions authority to assess this harm and award compensation, providing a consumer remedy mechanism beyond just penalties against violators.

## CHUNK 6.8

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 15 - Amendment of Section 52

**PROVISION:** Section 52 - Amendment to Section 52 (Information and Sample Furnishing)

**TITLE:** Expansion of Information and Documentation Requirements

**SUMMARY:** Section 52 is amended by substituting the original reference to specified Section 14(b) entities with broader language: "or any other person or entity covered under this Act shall furnish to the Bureau such information, documents or records relating to energy consumption, and such samples." This expands the information-gathering power from specific designated categories to all persons/entities subject to the Act, including energy consumption documentation and all relevant records.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Information furnishing obligation
- Broader entity coverage
- Documents and records
- Energy consumption documentation
- Sample supply
- Transparency requirement
- Bureau authority

**TEXT:**

15. In section 52 of the principal Act, for the words, brackets, letter and figures "specified under clause (b) of section 14 shall supply the Bureau with such information, and with such samples", the words "or any other person or entity covered under this Act shall furnish to the Bureau such information, documents or records relating to energy consumption, and such samples" shall be substituted.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 52
- Section 14 - Scope of entities covered under the Act
- Section 6 of Amendment Act - Extension to vehicles, vessels, industrial units, buildings

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Information scope: Energy consumption, manufacturing specifications, testing records
- Sample requirement: For testing compliance with energy consumption standards
- Enforcement: Bureau authority to request and receive documents
- Penalty: Section 26(4) penalties for information non-furnishment

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
This amendment broadens the Bureau's information-gathering power to encompass all Act-covered entities (not just originally specified ones) and all energy consumption-related documentation (not just samples). This supports the Bureau's expanded mandate over vehicles, vessels, and other categories added by the Amendment.
