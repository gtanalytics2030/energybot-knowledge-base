## CHUNK 7.1

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act to the Energy Conservation Act, 2001

**HIERARCHY:** Section 16 - Amendment of Section 56

**PROVISION:** Section 56 - Amendment to Section 56(2)(l) (Rule-Making Authority—Building Codes)

**TITLE:** Rule-Making Authority for Energy Conservation and Sustainable Building Codes

**SUMMARY:** Section 56(2)(l) is amended to replace the reference to "energy conservation building codes" with "energy conservation and sustainable building codes" in the Central Government's rule-making authority. This harmonizes rule-making power with the expanded definition of building codes that now includes sustainability requirements and renewable energy integration.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Rule-making authority
- Building code standards
- Sustainable development
- Central Government rules
- Regulatory framework

**TEXT:**

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Principal Act: Energy Conservation Act, 2001, Section 56, sub-section (2), clause (l)
- Section 2(v) - Definition of "energy conservation and sustainable building code"
- Section 4(ii) - Similar substitution in section 13(2)(d)
- Section 6(vii) - Similar substitution in section 14 clauses (p), (q), (r)
- Section 9(i) - Similar substitution in section 15 clause (a)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:**
- Building code standards: Set by Central Government
- Implementation: By States through amendments to Section 56(2)(a)
- Regulatory harmonization: Ensures consistent terminology across Act

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
This is part of a systematic terminology update across the Act (appearing in Sections 4, 6, 9, 16, 17) replacing "energy conservation building codes" with "energy conservation and sustainable building codes." This demonstrates parliamentary intent to evolve the Act beyond narrow energy conservation to encompass holistic building sustainability.

## CHUNK 7.7 (ANCHOR CHUNK - SYNTHESIS)

**DOCUMENT:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, No. 19 of 2022

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Amendment Act (Sectoral Expansion Synthesis)

**HIERARCHY:** Meta-Level Cross-Reference

**PROVISION:** ⭐ Sectoral Expansion of Energy Conservation Scope

**TITLE:** Extension of Energy Conservation Standards from Buildings to Transportation and Vessels

**SUMMARY:** The Amendment systematically extends energy conservation requirements beyond buildings and industrial units (covered in original 2001 Act) to two new major sectors: (1) VEHICLES - all motor vehicles per Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 definition, with fuel consumption standards and manufacturer penalties, and (2) VESSELS - all water craft in inland and coastal waters, including merchant marine, tankers, container vessels, with energy efficiency standards. This represents a 50+ year energy policy expansion from static buildings to mobile sources of energy consumption.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Transportation energy efficiency
- Vehicle fuel consumption standards
- Maritime vessel efficiency
- Sectoral expansion
- Manufacturer accountability
- Fuel economy norms
- Merchant marine standards
- Consumer vehicle standards

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS (SECTORAL EXPANSION MAP):**

```
VEHICLES FRAMEWORK:
  Definition: Section 2(vii)(ta) - Reference to Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, Section 2(28)
  Standards Authority: Section 6(i) - Central Government power
  Prohibition Mechanism: Section 6(iii) - Ban on non-compliant manufacture/import
  Transition Period: 6 months + 6 months extension authority
  Penalties: Section 12(2) - ₹25,000 (0.2 L/100 km deviation) or ₹50,000 (>0.2 L/100 km)
  Enforcement: Vehicle manufacturers, importers

VESSELS FRAMEWORK:
  Definition: Section 2(vii)(tb) - Comprehensive water craft definition
  Standard Categories: Ships, boats, tugs, barges, ferries, tankers, container vessels, gas carriers
  Standards Authority: Section 6(i) - Central Government power
  Prohibition Mechanism: Section 6(iii) - Ban on non-compliant operation/import
  Penalty Framework: Section 12(2) - Twice the price of MTOE in excess of norms
  Enforcement: Vessel operators, shipping companies, port authorities

APPLICABILITY TO ENERGY SECTOR:
  ├─ Port operations: Vessel mooring, cargo handling energy efficiency
  ├─ Maritime logistics: Fuel-efficient shipping critical to power commodity transport
  ├─ DISCOM operations: Vehicle fleets for field operations, meter reading
  ├─ Power evacuation: Vessel support for offshore wind installations
  └─ Energy security: Reduced transportation energy consumption supports renewable targets
```

**CROSS-REFERENCES WITH ELECTRICITY SECTOR:**
- GERC Green Energy Open Access: Renewable energy exported via efficient transportation infrastructure
- DISCOM tariff: Vehicle efficiency costs passed through operating expenditure
- Offshore wind: Vessel standards support deep-water wind farm construction and operation
- Grid charging: EV standards (implicit in vehicle framework) support electric vehicle adoption for grid-connected renewable charging

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:**
This sectoral expansion makes the Energy Conservation Act comprehensive across all energy-consuming activities: buildings (static), industrial processes (static), vehicles (dynamic/land), and vessels (dynamic/marine). For energy law queries, this expansion matters because:
1. Vehicle/vessel efficiency standards affect energy cost structures across sectors
2. Carbon credit trading encompasses all four sectors
3. Non-fossil energy mandates now apply to vehicle fuels (biofuels, electric charging)
4. Penalties for non-compliance create financial accountability in transportation

**TITLE:** Market-Based and Mandated Mechanisms for Emissions Reduction

**SUMMARY:** The Amendment establishes two complementary mechanisms for emissions reductions and renewable energy integration: (1) CARBON CREDIT TRADING - a voluntary/mandatory market mechanism where registered entities earning carbon credits through efficiency improvements can trade these instruments; and (2) NON-FOSSIL ENERGY MANDATES - prescriptive requirements for designated consumers to source minimum percentages of energy/feedstock from non-fossil sources. Together, these represent India's market and regulatory pathways for achieving climate targets and renewable energy commitments (500 GW by 2030, carbon neutrality by 2070).
