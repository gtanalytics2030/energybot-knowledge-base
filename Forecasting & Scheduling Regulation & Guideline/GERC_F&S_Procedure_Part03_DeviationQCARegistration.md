## Chunk 21: Interstate RE Generator Deviation Settlement Procedures

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Notification No.1 2019 Forecasting, Scheduling, DSM & related matters Solar and Wind generation sources regulation 2019. Implementation Procedure

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation Implementation Procedure

**HIERARCHY:** Section 3 / Subsection 3.5 / Part 3.5.3

**PROVISION:** Section 3.5.3 – Interstate RE Generator Deviation Settlement

**TITLE:** Interstate Renewable Energy Generator Schedule and Deviation Accounting

**SUMMARY:**
Wind and solar generators connected to the State grid and selling power outside Gujarat must maintain a separate daily schedule for interstate transactions to enable accurate tracking of state-level injections versus interstate injections. Interstate schedule is submitted independently to WRLDC for inter-state boundary scheduling, while the intra-state schedule is submitted separately to SLDC-Gujarat. This separate accounting ensures that deviation charges for interstate transactions are calculated under CERC Deviation Settlement Mechanism regulations rather than intra-state GERC procedures, protecting interstate generators from double-charging and enabling accurate financial settlement for multi-state power flows.

## Chunk 22: Under-Injection Deviation Charges – Graduated Penalty Schedule

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Notification No.1 2019 Forecasting, Scheduling, DSM & related matters Solar and Wind generation sources regulation 2019. Implementation Procedure

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation Implementation Procedure

**HIERARCHY:** Section 3 / Subsection 3.5 / Part 3.5.4

**PROVISION:** Section 3.5.4 – Under-Injection Deviation Charges

**TITLE:** Deviation Settlement Charges for Under-Injection Against Committed Schedule

**SUMMARY:**
Wind and solar generators that inject less power than their committed daily schedules are liable to pay under-injection deviation charges according to a graduated penalty structure based on absolute percentage error. The charge structure specified in Table I of GERC Notification No.1 of 2019 provides escalating penalties: lower percentage errors incur charges closer to the PPA tariff rate, while higher deviations (beyond 35% under-injection) incur charges at 130% of the PPA tariff rate. This graduated structure incentivizes generators to forecast accurately and commit realistically to schedules while protecting system stability. The actual charge is calculated as: Absolute Error % × Applicable Multiplier × PPA Rate × Scheduled Energy (MWh).

**KEY TOPICS:** Under-Injection, Deviation Charge, Table I, Graduated Penalty, Absolute Error, PPA Rate, Multiplier, Scheduled Energy, Generator Liability, Financial Settlement

**TEXT:**
3.5.4 The RE generator under injection against the committed schedule shall be liable to pay deviation charges as per the Table - I of GERC Notification No.1 of 2019, which is graduated in nature based on absolute percentage error of the deviation.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Table I – Under-Injection Deviation Charges (GERC Notification No.1 of 2019), PPA Rate Schedule, Absolute Error Calculation Methodology

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Section 3.5.2 (DSM liability framework), Section 3.5.5 (Over-Injection charges – complementary), Section 3.5.6 (DSM pool account revenue neutrality), Section 3.5.7 (Additional metering requirements)

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation Implementation Procedure

**HIERARCHY:** Section 3 / Subsection 3.5 / Part 3.5.5

**PROVISION:** Section 3.5.5 – Over-Injection Deviation Settlement (Inadvertent Energy Treatment)

**TITLE:** Deviation Settlement Charges for Over-Injection Treated as Inadvertent Energy

**SUMMARY:**
Wind and solar generators that inject more power than their committed daily schedules (over-injection) are treated as inadvertent energy suppliers into the grid. Over-injection deviations are charged according to Table II of GERC Notification No.1 of 2019, which establishes a graduated charge schedule across all error bands (low to high percentage deviations). The charge mechanism recovers costs associated with managing unscheduled power flows while providing a price signal that discourages forecast inaccuracy. Over-injection charges apply across the entire spectrum of deviation percentages, incentivizing generators to maintain schedule discipline and preventing free-rider behavior where unscheduled supply could undermine market mechanisms.

## Chunk 24: DSM Pool Account Revenue Neutrality and Financial Settlement

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Notification No.1 2019 Forecasting, Scheduling, DSM & related matters Solar and Wind generation sources regulation 2019. Implementation Procedure

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation Implementation Procedure

**HIERARCHY:** Section 3 / Subsection 3.5 / Part 3.5.6

**PROVISION:** Section 3.5.6 – DSM Pool Account Revenue Neutrality

**TITLE:** Deviation Settlement Pool Account – Revenue Neutrality and Pool Accounting Mechanism

**SUMMARY:**
The Deviation Settlement Mechanism (DSM) pool operates on a revenue-neutral basis: all deviation charges collected from under-injecting generators are credited to over-injecting generators through the DSM pool account. No revenue accrues to SLDC or other parties; the pool functions as a zero-sum clearing house distributing collected penalties equitably among pool members. For QCA-managed pooling stations with multiple generators, the QCA collects DSM liability from all pool members, deposits collected amounts into the common pool account, and receives credits for over-injections. Individual generators' net DSM liability (charges minus credits) is settled through monthly accounting. This mechanism ensures that forecasting discipline is enforced system-wide while individual generators' actual performance determines their financial position without creating system-wide revenue leakage.

**KEY TOPICS:** DSM Pool Account, Revenue Neutral, Pool Clearing, Deviation Charges Collection, Over-Injection Credits, Zero-Sum Settlement, QCA Pool Management, Pool Member Accounting, Monthly Settlement, Financial Distribution

**TEXT:**
3.5.6 The DSM pool account is revenue neutral i.e., all RE generator under injection charges collected are credited back to over injection RE generators through DSM pool account.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** DSM Pool Account management procedures, Revenue neutrality principles, Pool clearing mechanisms per GERC Regulation 5.12

## Chunk 27: General Provisions – Default Payment Mechanism

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Notification No.1 2019 Forecasting, Scheduling, DSM & related matters Solar and Wind generation sources regulation 2019. Implementation Procedure

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation Implementation Procedure

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Subsection 4.1

**PROVISION:** Section 4 – General (Default Payment Procedures)

**TITLE:** Default Payment Procedures and Tariff Benefits Restrictions for RE Generators

**SUMMARY:**
In case of default by a RE generator (failure to meet payment obligations, DSM liability, or other financial commitments), payment from the power buyer to the generator is blocked at the monthly energy certification stage. The generator cannot claim preferential tariff benefits available to captive or self-consumption generators and cannot obtain any rebate or preferential rate from DISCOMs or RECs (Renewable Energy Certification bodies). Default status acts as a comprehensive bar to financial benefits, incentivizing timely payment and regulatory compliance. Once default is cured and past dues settled, normal tariff applicability is restored. This provision protects the financial integrity of the DSM mechanism and ensures generators prioritize their regulatory payment obligations.

**KEY TOPICS:** Default Payment, Blocked Payment, Monthly Certification, Preferential Tariff Denial, Captive Tariff, Self-Consumption Benefit, REC Benefit, DISCOM Tariff, Financial Incentive Restriction, Default Remediation

**TEXT:**
4. In case of default by the RE generator in payment, no benefit of captive generation / preferential tariff from DISCOM or REC shall be available to the generator. The payment from power buyer to generator shall be blocked/withheld at monthly energy certification stage.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Payment default standards, GERC Tariff Regulations (preferential tariffs), DISCOM and REC tariff schedules

## Chunk 37: QCA Clarifications – Financial Security and DSM Liability

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Notification No.1 2019 Forecasting, Scheduling, DSM & related matters Solar and Wind generation sources regulation 2019. Implementation Procedure

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation Implementation Procedure

**HIERARCHY:** Section 7 / Subsections 7.1-7.4

**PROVISION:** Section 7 – Clarification for QCA

**TITLE:** QCA Financial Responsibility: Pooling Station-Level Security and Individual Generator Default Liability

**SUMMARY:**
Financial security (Bank Guarantee or Letter of Credit) is organized at the pooling station level: one BG per pooling station, not per individual generator. SLDC issues DSM bills exclusively in QCA name; QCA is the sole contracting party with SLDC for DSM settlement. The QCA is individually liable for all DSM pool account bills for all generators at each pooling station, including default amounts from any individual pool member. If any individual RE generator defaults in payment to the QCA, the QCA's BG/LC is encashed to cover the full amount and satisfy SLDC's claims. Internal allocation of collected amounts among pool members (de-pooling) is the QCA's responsibility and does not affect SLDC's right to recover from the BG. This structure ensures SLDC has single-party accountability and reliable payment security without involvement in internal pool disputes. QCAs must establish internal billing/collection processes to prevent such defaults.

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation Implementation Procedure

**TITLE:** Force Majeure Handling and Binding Application of Implementation Procedure

**SUMMARY:**
This Implementation Procedure is binding on all RE generators (wind and solar), QCAs, and power purchase beneficiaries operating in Gujarat. Force majeure events (natural disasters, severe weather, grid emergencies, acts of government) are handled through appropriate operational workarounds coordinated between SLDC and affected generators; force majeure conditions do not excuse compliance with forecasting/scheduling submission requirements but may justify reasonable extension or temporary procedural modifications with SLDC approval. Generators experiencing force majeure must notify SLDC immediately and document circumstances. SLDC, in its discretion, may adjust DSM calculations, extend payment deadlines, or waive procedural penalties for force majeure-affected periods while maintaining overall system stability. The procedure applies uniformly to all RE generators regardless of capacity, technology, or PPA structure. No party can unilaterally suspend compliance; all procedural obligations remain in effect unless officially waived by SLDC.
