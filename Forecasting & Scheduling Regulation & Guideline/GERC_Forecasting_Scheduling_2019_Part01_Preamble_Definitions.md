**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 2 – Applicability

**PROVISION:** Regulation 2

**TITLE:** Applicability – Scope of Regulations

**SUMMARY:**
These regulations apply to all wind and solar generators with combined installed capacity exceeding 1 MW connected to the State grid or substation, whether directly or via pooling stations. Applicability extends to generators selling power within Gujarat, outside Gujarat, or consuming power for self-consumption. The 1 MW threshold ensures that small rooftop and distributed solar installations are excluded, while utility-scale and captive renewable energy plants fall within the regulatory scope. This establishes jurisdiction over all significant wind and solar generation facilities interfacing with the Gujarat electricity system.

## Chunk 4: Definition – Absolute Error

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 3 – Definitions and Interpretation / Definition 3.1(a)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 3.1(a)

**TITLE:** Definition – Absolute Error

**SUMMARY:**
Absolute Error is defined as the absolute (positive) value of the discrepancy between the actual electrical injection (generation supplied to the grid) and the scheduled generation in a 15-minute time block. It is calculated as a percentage using the formula: Absolute Error (%) = 100 × [Actual Injection − Scheduled Generation] / Available Capacity. This metric measures forecast accuracy and is used to determine whether deviation charges apply under Tables-I and II. The absolute value ensures that both under-injection and over-injection deviations are treated equally in magnitude.

**KEY TOPICS:** Absolute Error, Forecast Accuracy, Actual Injection, Scheduled Generation, Available Capacity, 15-Minute Time Block, Deviation Measurement, Calculation Formula

**TEXT:**
In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires:

(a) 'Absolute Error' means the absolute value of the error in the actual injection of wind or solar generators with reference to the 'Scheduled Generation' and the 'Actual Generation' as calculated using the following formula for each 15-minute time block:

Absolute Error (%) : 100 X [Actual Injection – Scheduled Generation]/Available Capacity;

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Definition of Scheduled Generation (3.1(s)), Definition of Actual Injection (3.1(d)), Definition of Available Capacity (3.1(e)), Deviation Charges in Regulation 8.5 and 8.6

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Time-block definition (3.1(x)), deviation accounting procedure (Regulation 12.1), tables of deviation charges (Regulation 8 Tables-I and II)

**SUMMARY:**
Actual Injection, measured in a time-block, is the amount of electricity generated or supplied by a seller (generator) as measured at its interface meter. ## Chunk 8: Definition – Available Capacity

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 3 – Definitions and Interpretation / Definition 3.1(e)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 3.1(e)

**TITLE:** Definition – Available Capacity

**SUMMARY:**
Available Capacity (AvC) for wind or solar generators means the cumulative nameplate capacity rating of all wind turbines or solar inverters at the facility that are capable of generating power during a given 15-minute time block. This is the denominator in Absolute Error percentage calculations. For wind generators, AvC is applicable for the entire 24 hours; for solar generators, AvC is applicable only between 05:30 and 19:00 hours due to solar irradiation availability (per Regulation 5.12). AvC may vary over time if turbines or inverters are temporarily unavailable due to maintenance or technical faults.

**KEY TOPICS:** Available Capacity, Installed Capacity, Wind Turbines, Solar Inverters, Cumulative Rating, Time-Block Basis, 24-Hour Availability, Solar Irradiation Window

**TEXT:**
(e) 'Available Capacity or 'AvC' for wind or solar generators means the cumulative capacity rating of the wind turbines or solar inverters that are capable of generating power in a given time-block;

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 5.12 (AvC applicability windows for wind and solar), Absolute Error definition (3.1(a)), time-block definition (3.1(x))

## Chunk 13: Definition – Deviation

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 3 – Definitions and Interpretation / Definition 3.1(j)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 3.1(j)

**TITLE:** Definition – Deviation

**SUMMARY:**
Deviation, measured in a time-block for a seller, is defined as the difference between total actual injection of energy and total scheduled generation. Deviation = Actual Injection − Scheduled Generation. A positive deviation indicates over-generation (surplus to schedule); negative deviation indicates under-generation (shortfall from schedule). This is the foundation metric for commercial settlement of deviation charges, which compensate the grid for the cost of balancing actual supply against scheduled supply.

**KEY TOPICS:** Deviation, Actual Injection, Scheduled Generation, Seller, Time-Block Basis, Over-Generation, Under-Generation, Energy Balance

**TEXT:**
(j) 'Deviation' in a time-block for a seller means its total actual injection of energy minus its total scheduled generation;

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Absolute Error calculation (3.1(a)), deviation settlement (Regulation 8), deviation accounting (Regulation 12), de-pooling of deviations (Regulation 16)

## Chunk 20: Definition – Pooling Station

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 3 – Definitions and Interpretation / Definition 3.1(q)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 3.1(q)

**TITLE:** Definition – Pooling Station

**SUMMARY:**
A Pooling Station is a substation (electrical facility) at which the electrical output of multiple individual wind or solar generators is combined (pooled) and interfaced with the state transmission utility (STU) or distribution licensee (DISCOM) substation. Pooling stations enable collective forecasting and scheduling via a single Qualified Coordinating Agency (QCA), reducing administrative burden. The regulation includes a proviso: where no separate pooling station exists and generators connect via common feeders or individual feeders terminated at a distribution/transmission substation, that substation is deemed the pooling station for those generators. This ensures all generators (whether at dedicated or ad-hoc pooling stations) have a defined interface point.

**KEY TOPICS:** Pooling Station, Substation, Multiple Generators, Pooling of Generation, STU, DISCOM, Interconnection, Common Feeder, Individual Feeder, Interface Point

**TEXT:**

**SUMMARY:**

## Chunk 27: Definition – Time-Block

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 3 – Definitions and Interpretation / Definition 3.1(x)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 3.1(x)

**TITLE:** Definition – Time-Block

**SUMMARY:**
A Time-Block is a 15-minute interval for which specified electrical parameters (voltage, frequency, power, energy) are recorded by the Special Energy Meter (SEM). The first time-block of each day starts at 00:00 hours (midnight), making 96 time-blocks in a 24-hour day (96 × 15 minutes = 1440 minutes). Every schedule submission, metering record, and deviation calculation is based on 15-minute time-blocks. This granularity balances operational precision (enabling frequent revisions) with administrative manageability (not requiring second-by-second metering).

**KEY TOPICS:** Time-Block, 15 Minutes, Special Energy Meter, SEM, Daily Time Blocks, 96 Time-Blocks, 00:00 Hours, Metering Intervals

## Chunk 29: Objective of the Regulations

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 4 – Objective

**PROVISION:** Regulation 4.1, 4.2

**TITLE:** Objective – Grid Integration of Solar and Wind Generation

**SUMMARY:**
The objective of these regulations is two-fold: (1) To facilitate large-scale grid integration of solar and wind generating stations while maintaining grid stability and security as envisaged under the Grid Code, through forecasting, scheduling, and commercial deviation settlement mechanisms. (2) To enable the grid operator to consider wind and solar power generation forecasts in mid-term, long-term, day-ahead, and intra-day planning and operations, making full use of conventional power plant flexibility and inter-grid tie-line capacity to accommodate maximum renewable power while maintaining system security and stability. This regulatory framework is thus fundamentally aligned with India's renewable energy integration strategy and grid modernization objectives.

**KEY TOPICS:** Objective, Grid Integration, Solar Generation, Wind Generation, Grid Stability, System Security, Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement, Grid Operator, Renewable Energy

**TEXT:**
4. Objective

4.1 The objective of these Regulations is to facilitate large-scale grid integration of solar and wind generating stations while maintaining grid stability and security as envisaged under the Grid Code, through forecasting, scheduling and commercial mechanism for deviation settlement of the generators.

4.2 In order to maintain system security, stability & reliability, the grid operator shall consider the Wind & Solar Power Generation forecast in the mid-term to long term, day ahead & intraday operation, for planning, and the process of scheduling. The grid operator shall make full use of the flexibility from conventional power plant as well as the capacity of inter grid tie lines to accommodate the maximum Wind & Solar Power while maintaining grid security.
