## Chunk 30: Forecasting and Scheduling Code – Overview

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5 – Forecasting and Scheduling Code / Regulation 5.1–5.2

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5.1–5.2

**TITLE:** Forecasting and Scheduling Code – Methodology and Metering Requirements

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5.1 establishes the regulatory framework for day-ahead and intra-day scheduling of wind and solar generators connected to the state grid. The regulation specifies that schedule revisions are permitted if the revision is greater than 2% of the previous schedule. Wind generators may make maximum 16 intra-day revisions per day; solar generators are limited to 9 revisions per day (starting 05:30 to 19:00 hours due to daylight availability). Regulation 5.2 requires appropriate metering for energy accounting and mandates that generators or their QCA install telemetry, data acquisition systems, and communication systems for real-time data transmission to SLDC. This ensures operational visibility and deviation accountability.

**KEY TOPICS:** Forecasting, Scheduling, Day-Ahead Schedule, Intra-Day Revision, Wind Generators, Solar Generators, Metering, Telemetry, Data Acquisition, SLDC Reporting

**TEXT:**
5. Forecasting and Scheduling Code

5.1 This code provides methodology for day-ahead/intra-day scheduling of wind and solar energy generator(s) which are connected to the State Grid/Sub-Station, and revision of schedule(s) and methodology of handling deviations of such wind and solar energy generator(s) and its deviation charges. Revision of schedule shall be allowed if the revision is more than 2% of the previous schedule. For wind energy based generation (maximum 16 intra-day revisions) and solar energy based generation (maximum 9 intra-day revisions) shall be allowed.

5.2 Appropriate meters shall be provided for energy accounting. Telemetry/communication system & Data Acquisition System shall also be provided for transfer of information to the Gujarat SLDC by the generator or QCA appointed by it. Generator(s)/QCA shall install the meter/communication/data acquisition system etc.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Revision percentage threshold (2%), revision limits (16 for wind, 9 for solar), Regulation 5.10–5.11 (specific revision procedures)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Metering standards (Regulation 9.1), telemetry procedures (Regulation 5.13), data communication (Regulation 10), QCA responsibilities (Regulation 6.2)

## Chunk 32: Forecasting Methodology – Generator-Centric Forecast

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5 – Forecasting and Scheduling Code / Regulation 5.4–5.6

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5.4–5.6

**TITLE:** Forecasting Methodology – Generator and QCA Forecasting, SLDC Forecasting, and Forecast Choice

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5.4 establishes that wind and solar generators, or their appointed QCAs, shall conduct forecasting and prepare generator-centric schedules indicating the availability of wind turbines/solar generators at each pooling station. Regulation 5.5 specifies that SLDC also undertakes independent forecasting of state-wide wind and solar injection to facilitate secure grid operation and resource planning. Regulation 5.6 provides generators a choice: they may either derive their schedule from SLDC's published forecast, or provide SLDC with their own forecast-based schedule. However, if a generator adopts SLDC's forecast, it waives the right to claim that SLDC's forecast was erroneous if deviations occur. This mechanism balances generator autonomy with risk accountability.

**KEY TOPICS:** Forecasting, Generator Forecast, QCA Forecast, SLDC Forecast, Schedule Derivation, Forecast Choice, Generator Autonomy, Risk Responsibility, Pooling Station Schedule

**TEXT:**
5.4 Forecasting shall be done by wind and solar generators connected to the State grid, or by QCAs on their behalf. The forecast by the QCA or wind and solar generator, as the case may be, shall be generator centric with intimation to SLDC containing the availability of WTGs/Solar generators based schedule for each pooling station separately.

5.5 SLDC shall also undertake forecasting of wind and solar power that is expected to be injected into the State grid. The forecast by the SLDC shall be with the objective of ensuring secure grid operation by planning for the requisite balancing resources and grid operation.

5.6 The QCA or wind and solar generator/generators may either derive its forecast based on the SLDC's forecast for preparing its schedule or provide the SLDC with a schedule based on their own forecast. However, if the generator(s)/QCA derive its schedule based on SLDC's forecast, they shall not take a plea that the error is due to erroneous forecast by SLDC.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 5.9 (day-ahead and week-ahead schedule submission), Regulation 5.4 (pooling station schedule format)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** SLDC forecasting role, grid operation planning (Regulation 4.2), deviation accountability (Regulation 8.5–8.6)

## Chunk 33: Aggregation Restrictions at Pooling Stations

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5 – Forecasting and Scheduling Code / Regulation 5.7–5.8

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5.7–5.8

**TITLE:** Aggregation Restrictions and Schedule Coordination via QCA

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5.7 prohibits any single QCA or individual generator (whether injecting wind, solar, or both) from aggregating and scheduling more than one pooling station (or multiple individual generating stations at the same substation). This prevents a single entity from excessively concentrating control over forecasting and scheduling, ensuring distributed decision-making. Regulation 5.8 specifies the practical flow: the QCA coordinates the aggregation of individual generator schedules at the pooling station and communicates the pooled schedule to SLDC. For generators directly connected to substations (not via pooling), the generator or its QCA communicates the schedule directly at the interface/interconnection point to SLDC.

**KEY TOPICS:** Aggregation Restriction, Pooling Station Limit, QCA Coordination, Schedule Aggregation, Individual Generator, Direct Connection, SLDC Communication

**TEXT:**
5.7 The QCA or individual generator, either injecting wind energy, solar energy or both, connected to a substation shall not be permitted aggregation of more than one pooling stations or individual generating station connected to a substation as the case may be.

5.8 The QCA shall co-ordinate the aggregation of schedules of all generators connected to a pooling station and communicate it to the SLDC. In case of the generators who are directly connected to the Sub-station, such generators or the QCA appointed/nominated by such generators shall communicate their schedule at interface / interconnection point to the SLDC.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Definition of Pooling Station (3.1(q)), definition of QCA (3.1(r)), interface point definition (3.1(n))

## Chunk 34: Day-Ahead and Week-Ahead Schedule Submission

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5 – Forecasting and Scheduling Code / Regulation 5.9

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5.9

**TITLE:** Day-Ahead and Week-Ahead Schedule Submission – 9 AM Deadline, 15-Minute Intervals

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5.9 establishes the core scheduling submission requirement. Every day by 9 AM, each QCA or generator must submit to SLDC: (1) a "Day-Ahead" schedule containing 96 time-block forecasts (one for each 15-minute interval from 00:00 to 23:45 hours) for the next calendar day, and (2) a "Week-Ahead" schedule containing the same information for the following seven calendar days. These schedules are used for grid planning, availability margin calculation, and as the baseline for deviation measurement. The 9 AM submission time ensures SLDC has time to coordinate all renewable schedules with conventional generation and inter-state tie-line availability before day-ahead operations.

**KEY TOPICS:** Day-Ahead Schedule, Week-Ahead Schedule, 9 AM Submission, 96 Time-Blocks, 15-Minute Intervals, Schedule Format, Grid Planning, Availability Margin, Energy Planning

**TEXT:**
5.9 The QCA or the wind and solar generator shall submit "Day-Ahead" and a "Week-Ahead" schedule by 9 AM everyday for each pooling station or each generating station, as the case may be, which shall be utilized for planning availability of energy and for calculating the margin available in the grid of the State. "Day-Ahead" schedule shall contain wind or solar energy generation schedule at intervals of 15 minutes (time-block) for the next day, starting from 00:00 hours of the day, and prepared for all 96 time-blocks. "Week-Ahead" schedule shall contain the same information for the next seven days.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Definition of time-block (3.1(x)), definition of scheduled generation (3.1(s)), Regulation 5.10–5.11 (revision procedures)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Grid operation planning (Regulation 4.2), schedule revision procedures (Regulation 5.10–5.11), forecasting methodologies (Regulation 5.4–5.6)

## Chunk 35: Wind Generator Schedule Revision Procedure

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5 – Forecasting and Scheduling Code / Regulation 5.10

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5.10

**TITLE:** Wind Generator Schedule Revision Procedure – 16 Revisions Maximum

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5.10 specifies the procedure for wind generator schedule revisions. Wind generators (excluding collective transactions) may revise their schedule by giving advance notice to SLDC. Revised schedules become effective from the 4th time-block following the time-block in which notice was given (i.e., a 45-minute delay: 15 minutes for notice reciept + 45 minutes for SLDC processing and communication to operators). This ensures SLDC has time to coordinate grid-wide changes. A maximum of 16 revisions per day is permitted, enabling wind generators to respond to changing wind conditions while preventing excessive revision overhead on SLDC systems.

**KEY TOPICS:** Wind Generator, Schedule Revision, 16 Revisions, Notice Advance, 4th Time-Block, Effective Time, Grid Coordination

**TEXT:**
5.10 The schedule of wind generators connected to the State grid (excluding collective transactions) may be revised by giving advance notice to the SLDC. Such revisions shall be effective from 4th time block, the first being the time-block in which notice was given. There may be maximum of 16 revisions during the day.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Definition of time-block (3.1(x)), Regulation 5.1 (2% revision threshold)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Solar generator revision procedures (Regulation 5.11), revision threshold (Regulation 5.1)

## Chunk 36: Solar Generator Schedule Revision Procedure

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5 – Forecasting and Scheduling Code / Regulation 5.11

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5.11

**TITLE:** Solar Generator Schedule Revision Procedure – 9 Revisions Maximum, Daytime Only

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5.11 specifies the revision procedure for solar generators. Solar generators (excluding collective transactions) may revise their schedule by advance notice to SLDC, with the same effective-timing rule as wind (effective from 4th time-block). However, solar revisions are limited to 9 per day (half of wind's 16) and are restricted to the window from 05:30 to 19:00 hours—the operational window when solar irradiation is significant (per Regulation 5.12). This reflects the inherent differences in renewable variability: wind can change rapidly at any time; solar changes are predictable and confined to daylight hours.

**KEY TOPICS:** Solar Generator, Schedule Revision, 9 Revisions, 05:30 to 19:00 Hours, Daytime Window, 4th Time-Block, Solar Irradiation

**TEXT:**
5.11 The schedule of solar generator/generators connected to the State grid (excluding collective transactions) may be revised by giving advance notice to the SLDC. Such revisions shall be effective from 4th time block, the first being the time-block in which notice was given. There may be maximum of 9 revisions during the day starting from 05:30 hours to 19:00 hours of that day.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Definition of time-block (3.1(x)), Regulation 5.1 (2% revision threshold), Regulation 5.12 (AvC applicability window)

## Chunk 37: Available Capacity Applicability Window

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5 – Forecasting and Scheduling Code / Regulation 5.12

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5.12

**TITLE:** Available Capacity Applicability – 24 Hours for Wind, Daylight for Solar

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5.12 clarifies the applicability periods for Available Capacity (AvC) used in deviation calculations. Wind generators must declare their full AvC for all 24 hours of the day, as wind can occur at any time. Solar generators must declare their AvC only between 05:30 and 19:00 hours (when meaningful solar irradiation occurs); outside this window, AvC is zero or not applicable. This distinction ensures that deviation charge calculations do not penalize solar generators for "zero generation" during nighttime, which would be physically impossible.

**KEY TOPICS:** Available Capacity, AvC Applicability, 24 Hours, Solar Irradiation, 05:30 Hours, 19:00 Hours, Wind Availability, Daylight Window

**TEXT:**
5.12 The declared Available Capacity (AvC) for a wind generating plant shall be applicable for the entire 24 hours in a day whereas considering the availability of solar irradiation only during the day, the AvC for a solar generating plants shall be applicable only between 05:30 to 19:00 hours.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Definition of Available Capacity (3.1(e)), Regulation 5.11 (solar revision window), Absolute Error formula (3.1(a))

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Solar generator revision timing (Regulation 5.11), deviation calculation (Regulation 12.1, Table II)

## Chunk 39: Commercial Impact and Generator Responsibility

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5 – Forecasting and Scheduling Code / Regulation 5.14–5.16

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5.14–5.16

**TITLE:** Generator Responsibility for Deviations, QCA Payment Responsibility, and Payment Security

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5.14 establishes that any commercial impact (deviation charges) resulting from deviation from schedule is borne by the generator, either directly or through their appointed QCA. Regulation 5.15 clarifies that when a QCA is appointed, the QCA bears responsibility for all deviation charges on behalf of the generator. The regulation mandates a written agreement between generator and QCA specifying QCA's liability. Critically, the QCA must provide payment security equal to 110% of average expected monthly deviation charges, in the form of Bank Guarantee or Revolving Letter of Credit. For the first year, this security is calculated based on mock-trial average deviations for reference 50 MW wind and 25 MW solar pooling stations. From year two onward, security is re-assessed based on the prior year's actual average deviations. This financial security ensures deviation charges can be recovered even if a generator or QCA defaults.

**KEY TOPICS:** Commercial Impact, Generator Responsibility, QCA Responsibility, Deviation Charges, Written Agreement, Payment Security, 110% Bank Guarantee, Revolving LC, Financial Accountability, Mock Trial

**TEXT:**
5.14 Any commercial impact on account of deviation from schedule based on the forecast shall be borne by the wind and/or solar generator, either directly or transacted through the representing QCA.

5.15 In case of QCA appointed by renewable energy generator for forecasting and scheduling work, the QCA shall be responsible for the deviation charges payable on behalf of the generator. The individual generator and QCA shall execute an agreement specifying that the QCA shall be responsible for all obligations/liabilities arising out of the forecasting and scheduling work carried out by him on behalf of the renewable energy generator. The QCA shall provide payment security to the extent of 110% against deviation charges in the form of Bank Guarantee / Revolving LC. The QCA may ensure similar payment security mechanism back to back with respective generators. The payment security amount for the first year shall be worked out considering average deviations observed during the mock trial for different set of sites:

a) Wind generating plant of approximate 50 MW capacity at Pooling Sub-Station.
b) Solar generating plant of approximate 25 MW capacity at Pooling Sub-Station.

For second year and onwards the payment security shall be reviewed based on the average deviations observed during the first year (previous year) and applying the aforesaid mechanism, the security amount shall be worked out and the same shall be provided by the QCA.

5.16 When the QCA is appointed by the generator for the purpose of these Regulations, the responsibility for all the payments related to deviation charges payable on behalf of the RE generators shall be that of QCA. The QCA shall be held responsible for the dues payable/receivable on behalf of the generator, if the generator fails to pay the deviation charges payable under these Regulations through the QCA. The RE generators and QCA shall sign an agreement in this regard.

## Chunk 43: Commercial Mechanism – Generator Payment Framework

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 8 – Commercial Mechanism and Deviation Settlement / Regulation 8.1

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8.1

**TITLE:** Commercial Mechanism – Four-Part Framework for Power Sale and Deviation Settlement

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 8.1 establishes the commercial framework for how wind and solar generators are paid and how deviations are settled. Four distinct scenarios are defined: (a) Intra-state sales: Generators selling power within Gujarat are paid at the agreed tariff based on actual generation (not schedule), so they do not bear deviation charges. (b) Inter-state sales: Generators selling outside Gujarat are paid per PPA tariff and must pay deviation charges per CERC Deviation Settlement Mechanism and Forecasting Regulations 2014. (c) Self-consumption: Generators self-consuming power within Gujarat have their self-generated energy set off against their consumption per Commission orders, without deviation charges. (d) Deviation liability: Any generator deviating from schedule bears deviation charges per Tables-I and II of Regulation 8. This four-part framework clarifies the regulatory treatment of each transaction type.

**KEY TOPICS:** Commercial Mechanism, Intra-State Sale, Inter-State Sale, Self-Consumption, Actual Generation, Tariff, Deviation Charges, CERC Framework, Payment Settlement

**TEXT:**
8. COMMERCIAL MECHANISM AND DEVIATION SETTLEMENT

The commercial mechanism for deviation settlement is stated below:

8.1 The commercial mechanism for deviation settlement is stated below:

a) The wind or solar generators connected to the State grid and selling power within the State shall be paid by the buyer as per actual generation at the tariff agreed in the power purchase agreement or wheeling agreement.

b) The wind or solar generators connected to the State grid and selling power outside the State shall be paid by the buyer as per the terms of agreement at the tariff agreed in the power purchase agreement or wheeling agreement and pay deviation charges as per the CERC (Deviation Settlement Mechanism and Forecasting) Regulations, 2014 as amended from time to time.

c) The wind or solar generators connected to the State grid and self-consuming power within the State, accounting of such energy generated shall be set off against consumption as per prevailing orders passed by the Commission.

d) The wind or solar generators who deviate from its given schedule shall be liable to pay deviation charges as per the provisions of these Regulations given at Table-I and Table-II.

## Chunk 44: Inter-State Generators – Separate Schedule and State-Level Settlement

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 8 – Commercial Mechanism and Deviation Settlement / Regulation 8.2

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8.2

**TITLE:** Inter-State Generators – Dual Scheduling and State-Level Settlement

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 8.2 addresses the unique situation of generators connected to Gujarat transmission/distribution network but selling power outside the state (inter-state). Such generators must submit a separate schedule to SLDC specifically for their inter-state energy generation. SLDC prepares a state-level deviation settlement account for such generators to measure their impact on the Gujarat periphery (grid boundary). Even though these generators pay inter-state deviation charges to CERC, they must also pay intra-state deviation charges to SLDC (to the State Pool Account) if their deviations negatively impact the state grid. This dual-accounting approach ensures that inter-state transactions do not destabilize the state's grid operations without appropriate compensation.

**KEY TOPICS:** Inter-State Generator, Separate Schedule, State Deviation Settlement, State Periphery, Grid Impact, CERC Deviation Charges, SLDC Deviation Charges, Dual Liability

**TEXT:**
8.2 The QCA /individual generator selling power/consuming power outside the State of Gujarat but connected with the State transmission network/distribution network shall give separate schedule for the energy generation as per these Regulations to the SLDC. The deviation settlement account for such generators shall be prepared by the SLDC for measurement of deviation of energy by such generator and its impact on the State periphery. Such generators shall pay the deviation charges within the State in the State Pool account in case of deviations by them.

## Chunk 46: Deviation Charges for Wind Generators – Table I

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 8 – Commercial Mechanism and Deviation Settlement / Regulation 8.5

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8.5 with Table-I

**TITLE:** Deviation Charges for Wind Generators – Four-Tier Penalty Structure Based on Absolute Error

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 8.5 specifies deviation charges payable by wind generators or their QCA when actual injection deviates from scheduled generation. The charges are tiered based on Absolute Error percentage (the magnitude of deviation relative to available capacity). (1) Absolute Error ≤12%: No charge (tolerance band). (2) Absolute Error >12% but ≤20%: ₹0.25 per unit (MWh) for energy beyond 12% up to 20%. (3) Absolute Error >20% but ≤28%: ₹0.25 per unit (12–20%) + ₹0.50 per unit (20–28%). (4) Absolute Error >28%: ₹0.25 (12–20%) + ₹0.50 (20–28%) + ₹0.75 per unit (beyond 28%). The regulation includes a proviso: inter-state wind generators pay CERC deviation charges, not these GERC charges; SLDC accounts for this separately. This tiered structure incentivizes forecast accuracy while accommodating normal wind variability (≤12% tolerance).

**KEY TOPICS:** Deviation Charges, Wind Generators, Absolute Error, Tiered Penalty, Per Unit Cost, ₹0.25, ₹0.50, ₹0.75, Tolerance Band, Intra-State, Inter-State Proviso

**TEXT:**
8.5 In the event of actual generation of a wind generating station or a pooling station, as the case may be, being less or more than the scheduled generation, the deviation charges for shortfall or excess generation shall be payable by the wind generator or the QCA appointed on its behalf, as the case may be, to the State DSM Pool, as given in Table - I below.

Table - I

Sr. No. | Absolute Error in the 15-minute time block | Deviation Charges payable to State DSM Pool
---|---|---
1 | ≤ 12% | None
2 | >12% but ≤20% | At Rs. 0.25 per unit for the shortfall or excess energy for absolute error beyond 12% and up to 20%
3 | >20% but ≤28% | At Rs. 0.25 per unit for the shortfall or excess energy beyond 12% and up to 20% + Rs. 0.50 per unit for balance energy beyond 20% and up to 28%
4 | > 28% | At Rs. 0.25 per unit for the shortfall or excess energy beyond 12% and up to 20% + Rs. 0.50 per unit for balance energy beyond 20% and up to 28% + Rs. 0.75 per unit for balance energy beyond 28%

Provided that deviation charges for under or over injection by wind generator connected to the State grid and selling power outside the State shall be payable by wind generators as per the framework provided by the CERC Regulations. The accounting for this purpose shall be done by the SLDC.

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Solar deviation charges Table-II (Regulation 8.6), deviation accounting (Regulation 12), payment mechanism (Regulation 13), inter-state treatment (Regulation 8.2)

**TITLE:** Deviation Charges for Solar Generators – Four-Tier Penalty Structure Based on Absolute Error

The proviso mirrors wind: inter-state solar generators pay CERC charges; SLDC accounts for state-level impact separately. This regulatory differentiation reflects the fundamental physics of renewable variability.
