## Chunk 48: De-Pooling of Settlement by QCA

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 8 – Commercial Mechanism and Deviation Settlement / Regulation 8.7

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8.7

**TITLE:** De-Pooling of Settlement Accounts – QCA Responsibility for Individual Generator Allocation

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 8.7 reiterates that when a QCA manages all settlement activities for a pooling station, the QCA must also de-pool (disaggregate) both the energy deviations and the resulting deviation charges back to each individual generator in the pool. The de-pooling must follow one of the methodologies specified in Regulation 16 (proportional to available capacity, energy generated, individual absolute error, or mutually agreed method). This ensures financial transparency: each generator knows exactly how much charge it owes or credit it receives, proportionally allocated based on its contribution to the pooled deviation.

**KEY TOPICS:** De-Pooling, Settlement, QCA, Individual Generator, Pooling Station, Deviation Charges, Methodology, Proportional Allocation

**TEXT:**
8.7 When all settlement pertaining to a pooling station is carried out by the QCA, in that case the QCA shall also de-pool the energy deviations as well as deviation charges to each generator for each time-block as defined in 'De-pooling mechanism'.

## Chunk 55: Deviation Accounting – Absolute Error Calculation

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 12 – Deviation Accounting / Regulation 12.1

**PROVISION:** Regulation 12.1

**TITLE:** Deviation Accounting – Computation by SLDC

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 12.1 establishes SLDC's role in deviation accounting. SLDC calculates the Absolute Error (%) for each 15-minute time block at two levels: (a) For each pooling station (aggregate of all generators at that station), and (b) For each individual generator that feeds energy directly to a substation (not pooled). After calculating Absolute Errors, SLDC prepares Energy Accounts specifying the Deviation Charges owed by each pooling station or individual generator, based on Tables-I (wind) and II (solar). SLDC then communicates these accounts to the respective entity and collects the applicable deviation charges per Regulation 8 framework. SLDC is required to adjust (net off) the deviation charges collected from renewable generators against charges payable to them, maintaining the State Pool Account on a revenue-neutral basis.

**KEY TOPICS:** Deviation Accounting, Absolute Error, Pooling Station, Individual Generator, Energy Account, Deviation Charges, SLDC Calculation, Collection, Revenue Neutral

**TEXT:**
12 Deviation Accounting

Deviation settlement for the State shall be governed by the following provisions:

12.1 Computation of Deviation Charges: Deviation charges shall be computed in the following manner:

a) SLDC to calculate Absolute Error occurred in the scheduled energy and actual energy for each pooling station and for each generator which feed the energy directly to the substation.

b) Energy Account specifying the Deviation Charges for the renewable energy generators based on these Regulations prepared by the SLDC for the pooling stations/wind energy generators/solar generators, with consideration of the actual deviation in the energy from scheduled energy made by the pooling station or individual generator directly feeding energy in to the substation.

c) SLDC to prepare the energy account specifying the deviation made in the scheduling by the pooling substation or individual generator directly feeding to sub-station and collect the deviation settlement charges from the entity concerned for the amount payable by them as per the provisions of these Regulations.

d) SLDC is required to adjust the deviation settlement charges received from the renewable energy generators/QCAs under these Regulations in the State Pool for deviation charges payable / receivable. The said State DSM pool account is required to be maintained as revenue neutral in accordance with current practice.

The above mechanism shall be applicable up to 31st July, 2019 without any financial implications (as stated in Regulation 8 above).

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Definition of Absolute Error (3.1(a)), Regulation 8.5 (wind deviation charges), Regulation 8.6 (solar deviation charges), Regulation 8 (commercial mechanism)

## Chunk 56: Settlement of Deviation Charges

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 12 – Deviation Accounting / Regulation 12.2

**PROVISION:** Regulation 12.2

**TITLE:** Settlement of Deviation Charges – Proportional Allocation and Collection

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 12.2 details the settlement process after Absolute Errors are calculated. (a) SLDC computes deviations for distribution licensees, conventional generators, and renewable generators in proportion to their respective deviations, ensuring fair allocation of balancing costs across all grid participants (not just renewables). (b) SLDC collects deviation charges from renewable generators (pooling stations and individual generators) at the rates specified in Tables-I and II. (c) The collected charges are adjusted in the State Pool Account, netting charges receivable against charges payable (e.g., if a generator overgenerates in one time-block and undergenerates in another, charges offset). (d) The State DSM Pool Account is maintained as revenue-neutral, meaning total charges collected equal total payments made, with no profit or loss to the grid operator.

**KEY TOPICS:** Settlement, Deviation Charges, Proportional Allocation, Collection, SLDC, Renewable Generators, State Pool, Revenue Neutral, Net-Off, Payable/Receivable

**TEXT:**
12.2 Settlement of deviation charge

a) SLDC shall compute the deviation from schedule and compute the deviation charges payable/receivable for the distribution licensees / conventional generators/renewable generators in proportion to their respective deviation.

b) SLDC shall collect deviation charge from the RE generators (Pooling station/individual generator connected with the sub-station) based on the charges for deviation as specified in these Regulations.

If payment is delayed beyond 2 days, interest accrues at 0.04% per day, incentivizing prompt payment and compensating SLDC for working capital costs.

## Chunk 60: De-Pooling of Deviation Charges – Four Methodologies

---
**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 16 – De-Pooling of Deviation Charges

**PROVISION:** Regulation 16.1–16.2

**TITLE:** De-Pooling Methodology – Options and Consensus Requirement

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 16.1 requires QCA to de-pool (disaggregate) energy deviations and deviation charges from the pooling station to each individual member generator using one of four options: (a) Proportional to available capacity of each generator (simple, fixed proportion), (b) Proportional to energy generated in each time-block by each generator (variable, performance-based), (c) Proportional to absolute error of individual generator schedule (error-based, incentivizing accuracy), (d) Any other mutually agreed criteria (flexibility). The QCA must declare which methodology applies and may adopt only one method consistently for all pooling station members. Regulation 16.2 specifies that methodology finalization requires majority consensus (>50%) measured in terms of MW capacity. Once finalized, the methodology cannot be changed without majority consent. This ensures fairness and prevents unilateral imposition of unfavorable allocation methods on smaller generators.

**KEY TOPICS:** De-Pooling, Deviation Charges, Energy Deviations, Four Options, Proportional Allocation, Available Capacity, Energy Generated, Absolute Error, Mutual Agreement, Majority Consensus, MW Capacity

**TEXT:**
16 De-pooling of Deviation charges:

16.1 QCA shall de-pool the energy deviations as well as deviation charges to each generator connected at the respective pooling station, using one of the following options:

a) In proportion to available capacity of each generator,

b) In proportion to energy generated in each time block by each generator,

c) In proportion to absolute error of Individual generator schedule.

d) Any other methodology/criteria mutually agreed between QCA and generators.

They may adopt any one of the above methods, declaring that the same shall apply to all the members of the pooling station.

16.2 Finalization of a methodology shall be on majority (≥ 51%) consensus basis measured in terms of MW capacity and a methodology once finalized shall not be changed without majority consensus.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Definition of Deviation (3.1(j)), Definition of Available Capacity (3.1(e)), Definition of Absolute Error (3.1(a)), Regulation 8.3 (QCA de-pooling responsibility)

- Dispute resolution requires directive intervention
- Technical or operational difficulties emerge

Directions are issued either suo motu (on Commission's own motion) or on application by any affected party. They must be "considered necessary in furtherance of the objective and purpose" of these regulations—i.e., facilitating renewable energy grid integration while maintaining stability. This provides a mechanism for regulatory agility without requiring full formal amendment.

**TITLE:** Commission Power to Amend – Formal Regulatory Modification Authority

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 19 reserves the Commission's general authority to formally vary, alter, modify, or amend any provision of these regulations at any time. This is a standard provision in all regulatory instruments, establishing that regulations are not static. Amendment authority enables GERC to:
- Adjust deviation charge rates based on grid experience
- Tighten or relax tolerance bands (12% for wind, 7% for solar) based on technology improvement
- Update QCA qualifying criteria to reflect changing industry standards
- Modify forecasting and scheduling procedures based on operational experience
- Incorporate technological advances (e.g., AI forecasting, faster data systems)

Full regulatory amendments typically follow formal procedures (consultation, stakeholder notice, etc.) under the Electricity Act 2003 and Natural Justice principles, though Regulation 19 states only the power itself.
