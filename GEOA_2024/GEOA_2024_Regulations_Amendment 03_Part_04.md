TITLE: Amendment to Regulation 17.6(viii) – Banking Facility Charge Rate and Validity Framework
SUMMARY: Section 3 substitutes Regulation 17.6(viii) of the Principal Regulations to specify the banking charge rate and its effective period. The amended regulation sets the banking charge rate at Rs. 1.50 per unit (MWh/kWh), effective from the date of notification of the Third Amendment Regulations (September 25, 2025) through March 31, 2026. If the Commission notifies revised charges through a separate regulation before March 31, 2026, those revised charges supersede the Rs. 1.50/unit rate. This provision directly implements the temporal framework established in Section 2 (Regulation 1(4)).

**B. SUBSTANTIVE AMENDMENT DETAILS:**

**1. Banking Charge Rate:**

**Specified Rate:** Rs. 1.50 per unit

**Unit Definition:** [The document does not explicitly define "unit," but in electricity regulatory context, standard interpretation is:]
- **Primary Interpretation:** Per MWh (Megawatt-hour) – Standard in tariff and open access regulations
- **Secondary Interpretation:** Per kWh (Kilowatt-hour) – Less likely given tariff document conventions
- **Technical Meaning:** Per unit of electrical energy transmitted through banking mechanism

**Calculation Basis:** If an open access consumer or generator uses banking facility for 100 MWh in a month, the banking charge would be:
- **100 MWh × Rs. 1.50/MWh = Rs. 150 (Total Banking Charge for that month)**

**Rate Justification:** The amendment does not provide justification, but banking charges typically cover:
- Transmission costs for facilitating inter-temporal energy storage
- Settlement and metering costs
- Administrative charges for banking facility operations

---

**2. Effective Date – Commencement:**

**Start Date (Primary):** "from the date of notification of these Regulations"

**Actual Commencement Date:** **September 25, 2025**
(Date of publication in Gujarat Government Gazette Extraordinary, Extra No. 273)

**Gazette Reference:** Thursday, September 25, 2025 / Asvina 3, 1947 (VS)

**Technical Interpretation:** 
The phrase "date of notification of these Regulations" refers to the date of official publication of the Third Amendment Regulations (Notification No. 08 of 2025) in the Official Gazette, which is September 25, 2025. Open access consumers and generators must apply Rs. 1.50/unit rates from this date.

---

**3. Effective Date – Expiry (Primary):**

**Expiry Date (Terminal):** "up to 31st March 2026"

**Interpretation:** 
- Banking charges at Rs. 1.50/unit remain valid through March 31, 2026 (inclusive)
- On or after April 1, 2026, unless a separate notification is issued before March 31, 2026, the applicability of these rates ceases
- Duration: Approximately 6 months (September 25, 2025 – March 31, 2026)

**Practical Implications:**
- Billings for banking services in March 2026 → Apply Rs. 1.50/unit (if billing month ends before March 31, 2026)
- Billings for banking services in April 2026 onwards → Apply revised rates (if separate notification issued) or revert to prior rates

---

**4. Effective Date – Expiry (Alternative/Earlier Revision):**

**Condition for Early Termination:** 

"or earlier date on which the Commission notifies such charges through a separate notification of Regulation"

**Mechanics:**
- The Commission retains power to revise banking charges before March 31, 2026
- **Method:** Issue a "separate notification of Regulation" (formal regulatory amendment)
- **Not through:** Circulars, Office Memorandums, or informal announcements
- **Effect:** The new charges specified in that separate notification supersede Rs. 1.50/unit

**Example Scenario:**
If on December 15, 2025, the Commission publishes "GERC Terms & Conditions for Green Energy Open Access (Fourth Amendment) Regulations, 2025" specifying new banking charges of Rs. 1.75/unit:
- Effective date of new rates: Date of publication of Fourth Amendment (e.g., December 15, 2025)
- Expiry date of Rs. 1.50/unit rates: December 15, 2025 (earlier than March 31, 2026)
- New applicable rate: Rs. 1.75/unit from December 15, 2025 onwards

---

**5. Continuity Provision – Post-Expiry Framework:**

**Transition Language:** 

"Thereafter the banking charge for the period starting from the separate notification by Regulations notified by the Commission shall be applicable"

**Breakdown:**
- **"Thereafter"** = After the expiry date (March 31, 2026 or earlier revision date)
- **"the banking charge for the period starting from"** = New rates apply from the date of the new regulation
- **"the separate notification by Regulations notified by the Commission"** = The separate regulatory amendment issued by GERC

**Meaning:** 
This clause establishes that after Rs. 1.50/unit rates expire, the **next set of banking charges specified in a subsequent GERC regulation** becomes automatically applicable. This creates a framework for seamless regulatory succession:

1. **Scenario A (Proactive Revision):** Commission issues new rates before March 31, 2026 → Those rates apply from publication
2. **Scenario B (No Action):** Commission does not issue new rates by March 31, 2026 → [Gap in regulation – not addressed by this document]
3. **Scenario C (Retroactive Notification):** Commission issues new rates after March 31, 2026 with retrospective applicability → Rates apply from publication or date specified in new regulation

**F. BANKING CHARGE MECHANICS IN GREEN ENERGY OPEN ACCESS:**

**Context (from Principal Regulations 2024, not detailed in amendment):**

Banking facility allows open access consumers to:
- Store surplus renewable energy generated in one period (e.g., high solar generation month)
- Withdraw equivalent energy in another period (e.g., lower renewable generation month)
- Avoid seasonal/temporal mismatch in renewable energy availability

**Charge Application:**
- Charged on the volume of energy banked (drawals or deposits, depending on regulation)
- Rate: Rs. 1.50/unit (from Sept 25, 2025)
- Example: If a consumer banks 50 MWh in May and draws 50 MWh in August, banking charge is 50 MWh × Rs. 1.50 = Rs. 75

**Recovery Mechanism:** [Not specified in this amendment – likely in full Regulation 17.6]
- Could be charged on deposits, drawals, or net banking
- Could be recovered through monthly/bi-monthly billing
- Recovery may be from open access consumers, generators, or both

**G. REGULATORY CONTEXT AND IMPLICATIONS:**

**1. Green Energy Policy Signal:**
The Rs. 1.50/unit banking charge suggests:
- Encouragement of seasonal/inter-temporal renewable energy smoothing
- Moderate cost to facilitate banking (not prohibitive, but not free)
- Support for distributed solar/wind generators with storage aspects

**2. Revenue Generation:**
GERC/Distribution Licensee revenue from banking charges:
- Example for Gujarat: If total green energy open access banking in a quarter = 5,000 MWh
- Banking charge revenue = 5,000 MWh × Rs. 1.50 = Rs. 7.5 lakhs per quarter
- This revenue likely contributes to SLDC/RLDC operation costs

**3. Competitive Benchmark:**
Rs. 1.50/unit is a moderate rate in Indian electricity sector:
- **CERC Wheeling Charges:** Typically 0.5-2.5% of energy (varies by state)
- **State Banking Charges:** Vary widely; comparison data not provided in document
- **Equivalency:** At Rs. 6-8/kWh average tariff, Rs. 1.50/unit = ~18-25% of tariff cost (moderate impact)

**4. Six-Month Review Window:**
The March 31, 2026 expiry suggests:
- Commission reviewing banking charge adequacy
- Potential adjustment for cost recovery or policy revision
- Stakeholder feedback period for charging mechanism

**H. TRANSITIONAL COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS:**

**For Open Access Consumers:**
- From Sept 25, 2025: Apply Rs. 1.50/unit to all banking transactions
- Before March 31, 2026: Monitor GERC notifications for revised rates
- After March 31, 2026: Apply rates specified in subsequent GERC regulation

**For Distribution Licensees/SLDCs:**
- From Sept 25, 2025: Update billing systems to Rs. 1.50/unit
- Maintain records of banking transactions for revenue recovery
- Notify customers of revised rates if earlier notification received

**For Generators (Captive/Renewable):**
- Ensure banking charge calculations in Power Purchase Agreements or open access arrangements reflect Rs. 1.50/unit
- Review pass-through mechanisms if rates change after March 31, 2026
