## SYNTHESIS: FOURTH AMENDMENT TO GERC GREEN ENERGY OPEN ACCESS REGULATIONS 2024

### OVERVIEW OF AMENDMENTS

The Fourth Amendment to the GERC Terms & Conditions for Green Energy Open Access Regulations 2024 (Notification No. 02 of 2026) modifies two coordinated provisions:

1. **Regulation 1(4)** - Short Title, Extent and Commencement
   - Establishes commencement and duration framework for banking charges
   - Sunset date: 30 June 2026
   - Alternative early termination by Commission notification

2. **Regulation 17.6(viii)** - Banking Facility and Charges
   - Prescribes specific rate: Rs. 1.50 per unit
   - Mirrors the same sunset mechanism as Regulation 1(4)
   - Establishes procedure for post-June-2026 charges through separate notification

### REGULATORY STRUCTURE: COORDINATION OF TWO AMENDMENTS

Both amendments establish a unified, time-bound framework through parallel modifications:

**REGULATION 1(4) PROVISION:**
"The Banking Charge specified in the Regulation 17.6 shall be effective from the date of notification of these Regulations up to 30th June 2026 or earlier date on which the Commission notifies such charges through a separate notification of Regulation."

**REGULATION 17.6(viii) PROVISION:**
"The Banking Charge at rate of Rs. 1.50 per unit shall be effective from the date of notification of these Regulations up to 30th June 2026 or earlier date on which the Commission notifies such charges through a separate notification of Regulation. Thereafter, the banking charge for the period starting from the separate notification by Regulations notified by the Commission shall be applicable."

**COORDINATION LOGIC:**
- Regulation 1(4) establishes the **procedure and timeline** for banking charge applicability
- Regulation 17.6(viii) specifies the **quantum (Rs. 1.50/unit)** and repeats the same procedure/timeline
- Both provisions reference each other implicitly and must be applied together for complete effect
- The "earlier date" alternative in both provides regulatory flexibility and responsiveness to market conditions

### BANKING CHARGE MECHANICS

**CHARGE RATE (Regulation 17.6(viii)):**
- Amount: Rs. 1.50 per unit
- Unit: Kilowatt-hour (kWh) - standard electricity measurement
- Applicability: All open access consumers utilizing banking facilities under Green Energy Open Access scheme

**APPLICABLE CONSUMERS:**
- Renewable energy generators (solar, wind, hybrid) operating under open access
- Consumers availing banking to store excess generation for future withdrawal
- Applicable in all four distribution zones of Gujarat: DGVCL, MGVCL, PGVCL, UGVCL (and private licensees)

**ECONOMIC EFFECT:**
- A consumer banking 1000 kWh would pay Rs. 1,500 as banking charge during the March-June 2026 period
- Charge is in addition to other applicable charges: wheeling charge, transmission charge, cross-subsidy surcharge, additional surcharge, losses
- Consumer must factor this cost into renewable investment economics and banking strategy

### SUNSET MECHANISM: INTENTIONAL TEMPORARY DESIGN

The 30 June 2026 automatic expiry is **intentionally temporary** for three regulatory reasons:

1. **DATA COLLECTION PHASE**: A 3-month window allows the Commission to gather utilization data, track consumer response, and assess banking facility effectiveness in promoting renewable energy in open access.

2. **POLICY EVALUATION**: Commission can evaluate whether the Rs. 1.50/unit rate is optimal for encouraging green energy participation, discouraging speculative banking, or balancing distribution licensee interests.

3. **AVOIDANCE OF INDEFINITE COMMITMENT**: The sunset prevents indefinite application of rates without periodic review. Any extension requires affirmative Commission action, ensuring regular policy assessment.

### POST-EXPIRY FRAMEWORK: PROCEDURE FOR FUTURE CHARGES

The phrase "Thereafter, the banking charge for the period starting from the separate notification by Regulations notified by the Commission shall be applicable" creates the following framework:

**SCENARIO 1: Commission Renotifies Same Charge**
- Future order specifies: Rs. 1.50/unit (or modified rate)
- Duration: Specified in future notification (e.g., next quarter, six months, annual)
- Legal effect: New charge begins immediately upon separate notification

**SCENARIO 2: Commission Modifies Charge Upward**
- Future order may increase rate based on cost data collected during March-June 2026
- Example: "Rs. 2.00/unit effective from 1 July 2026"
- Requires separate regulatory notification (cannot rely on Section 3 amendment)

**SCENARIO 3: Commission Reduces or Eliminates Charge**
- Future order may reduce (e.g., Rs. 1.00/unit) or eliminate banking charges
- Reflects policy decision that banking should be incentivized or be cost-free to promote green energy
- Also requires separate notification

**SCENARIO 4: No Action by Commission**
- If Commission takes no action after 30 June 2026
- Banking charges cease to apply
- Open access banking would continue without banking-specific charges (though other normal charges apply)

**FINANCIAL IMPACT:**

- Applies during March-June 2026 period only (subject to Commission extension/modification)
- Uncertain beyond 30 June 2026 (creates planning uncertainty for post-June investments)

**REGULATORY IMPACT:**
- Signals Commission intent to monitor banking facility utilization
- Suggests potential future policy adjustment based on data collected
- Creates incentive/disincentive for banking depending on whether consumers find Rs. 1.50/unit acceptable

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR PARTICIPANTS:**
- Renewable generators should evaluate banking economics using Rs. 1.50/unit as the cost

- Banking strategy should account for temporary nature of current rate structure
- Early banking decisions will provide feedback to Commission for July 2026+ policy

### INTERACTION WITH OTHER CHARGES

Banking charges apply **in addition to:**
- **Wheeling Charge**: For transmission of power through STTN/DTCN
- **Transmission Charge**: Applicable for interstate transmission (if applicable)
- **Cross-Subsidy Surcharge (CSS)**: Applied to open access consumers
- **Additional Surcharge (AS)**: For open access transactions
- **Loss Charges**: Banking inherently involves transmission losses (typically 3-5%)
- **Demand Charges**: If applicable under open access consumer category
- **Energy Charges**: Separate energy charges may apply

Banking charge is a **separate cost**, not a replacement for above charges.
