TITLE: Information System - Mandatory publication of GEOA-related data by State Load Despatch Centre

SUMMARY:
Regulation 24 mandates the State Load Despatch Centre (SLDC) to maintain and publish a comprehensive information system on its web portal containing real-time and periodic GEOA data. The SLDC must publish: (1) monthly reports on GEOA consumer status (active/inactive), (2) annual reports on GEOA metrics and trends, (3) Floor Rates for short-term power (reference pricing for GEOA transactions), (4) current information on Energy Handed Over Via (EHV) flows from GEOA generators to specific drawl points, and (5) Energy losses/Wheeling losses for transmission and distribution systems by technology and route. The portal must maintain current data with a maximum 1-month hosting lag, ensuring timely, accessible information for market participants, regulators, and consumers. This transparency supports price discovery in short-term markets, enables benchmarking of wheeling losses, prevents information asymmetries, and facilitates regulatory oversight. The requirement reflects GERC's commitment to transparent, data-driven renewable energy markets.

24. Information System

The State Load Despatch Centre (SLDC) shall maintain an information system and shall publish on its website the following information pertaining to the Green Energy Open Access on year-to-year basis:

(i) A monthly status report on the number of Green Energy Open Access consumers;

(ii) An annual report on the Green Energy Open Access indicating the number of consumers, capacity, quantum of energy allocated and wheeled during the year;

(iii) Floor Rates for short-term Green Energy Open Access power transactions floated on day-ahead market;

(iv) Current information on Energy Handed Over Via (EHV) from different Green Energy Generating Stations to various drawl points across the State;

(v) Information on Energy losses and Wheeling losses for transmission and distribution systems for different technology of renewable generating stations with respect to different routes/drawal points.

The information as mentioned hereinabove shall be in the website for not more than one (1) month lag from the date of availability of information with the SLDC.

TITLE: Reports - Monthly reporting format for GEOA consumer activity

SUMMARY:
Regulation 25 specifies the monthly reporting format that Nodal Agencies (SLDC for inter-state, STU for medium/long-term intra-state, Distribution Licensee for distribution network) must submit to GERC regarding GEOA consumer activity. Each monthly report entry covers: (1) Green Energy Open Access Consumer Name, (2) Injection Point (generator location/feeder point), (3) Drawal Point (consumer consumption location), (4) Capacity in MW (allocated contract capacity), and (5) Month (reporting period). This standardized format enables GERC to aggregate GEOA data across all nodal agencies, track consumer activity, identify inactive/unutilized allocations, and monitor compliance with Regulation 15 (Utilization/Non-Utilization). The monthly granularity provides actionable insights for regulatory supervision while the structured data format minimizes reporting burden and inconsistencies. Reports are typically due within 15-30 days of month-end per GERC orders. This transparency mechanism is essential for regulatory oversight and market monitoring.

PROVISION: Regulation 26
TITLE: Communication Facility - Real-time data transfer requirement for GEOA operations

SUMMARY:
Regulation 26 mandates real-time bidirectional communication infrastructure between GEOA consumers (generators and load centers), grid operators (SLDC, ALDC, LMU), and distribution licensees for accurate energy accounting and operational control. For consumers with ≥1 MW contract demand, direct communication with the State Load Despatch Centre (SLDC) is mandatory via Remote Terminal Unit (RTU) or equivalent SCADA link, enabling real-time monitoring of generation/consumption and emergency curtailment/control commands. For consumers 100 kW to <1 MW, direct communication with the Area Load Despatch Centre (ALDC) is mandatory, providing area-level monitoring and coordination. The communication link must support: (1) real-time kWh/kVARh export, (2) frequency and voltage parameters, (3) meter data transmission, (4) warning/tripping signals for protection, (5) manual override for emergency disconnect, and (6) bi-directional commands from SLDC/ALDC. Infrastructure must be secure, redundant, and meet CEA/GERC standards. The requirement ensures grid safety, prevents unauthorized deviations, enables fast emergency response, and provides accurate billing data. This is critical for integrating distributed renewable generation safely into centralized grid operations.

26. Communication Facility

All Green Energy Open Access consumers shall provide real-time data communication facility from their generating station and/or load centre to:

(i) In case of consumers with contract demand of 1 MW and above – Direct link with the State Load Despatch Centre (SLDC) through Remote Terminal Unit (RTU) or equivalent SCADA communication system having capability to transfer real-time data related to generation/consumption, frequency, voltage, meter data, warning and tripping signals, and manual override for emergency disconnect and shall be capable of receiving commands from the SLDC.

(ii) In case of consumers with contract demand of 100 kW to less than 1 MW – Direct communication with the Area Load Despatch Centre (ALDC) / Local Monitoring Unit (LMU) with capabilities as mentioned above.

(iii) The communication facilities as mentioned above shall be in accordance with the provisions of the Grid Code and Distribution Code applicable to the State.
