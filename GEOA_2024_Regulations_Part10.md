PROVISION: Regulation 18
TITLE: Curtailment Priority in case of constrained conditions in transmission/distribution system

SUMMARY:
Regulation 18 specifies the curtailment order when transmission or distribution system constraints require load shedding to maintain grid stability. Green Energy Open Access receives priority in curtailment decisions. The hierarchy prioritizes: (1) non-GEOA short-term power transactions first (fossil fuel-based), (2) GEOA short-term energy second, (3) GEOA medium-term energy third, (4) GEOA long-term energy fourth, and (5) Discom-procured power last (lowest priority). This reverse priority ensures that reliable long-term commitments and green energy arrangements face minimal disruption. The framework balances grid operational needs with renewable energy promotion objectives, as GEOA consumers pay transmission/wheeling charges and deserve prioritized treatment. Discom load shedding is the last resort, protecting essential supply to its retail consumers. This provision supports the policy objective of promoting non-discriminatory open access for renewable energy while maintaining distribution licensee's ability to serve retail customers during contingencies.

18. Curtailment Priority in case of constrained conditions in transmission/distribution system

In case of constrained conditions in the transmission system and/or distribution system of the State, the Green Energy Open Access shall not be curtailed unless and until all other sources of energy are curtailed in the following order of priority:

(i) Non-Green Energy Open Access short-term power transactions (Fossil Fuel based) shall be curtailed first;

(ii) Green Energy Open Access short-term energy shall be curtailed second;

(iii) Green Energy Open Access medium-term energy shall be curtailed third;

(iv) Green Energy Open Access long-term energy shall be curtailed fourth;

(v) Distribution Licensee's own procurement/power shall be curtailed last.

PROVISION: Regulation 19
TITLE: Dispute Resolution - Procedure for addressing disputes and grievances

SUMMARY:
Regulation 19 establishes a two-tier dispute resolution framework. First-tier disputes between GEOA consumers and State Nodal Agencies are resolved by the SNA itself through its dispute resolution procedures. Parties dissatisfied with SNA decisions may appeal to the Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (GERC) within 30 days of the SNA order. The GERC Commission shall dispose of all disputes within three months of receiving the appeal. This streamlined process ensures timely, expedited resolution while respecting the SNA's primary coordination role. The 30-day appeal window prevents stale claims while the 3-month Commission decision timeline prevents case pendency. This mechanism complements existing CGRF (Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum) procedures for retail consumer complaints but applies specifically to GEOA contractual and operational disputes. The regulation balances consumer rights with efficient grid administration.

19. Dispute Resolution

Any dispute arising between a Green Energy Open Access consumer and the State Nodal Agency with regard to matters relating to Green Energy Open Access, shall be resolved in the manner as prescribed in the regulations/orders issued by the State Nodal Agency from time to time.

In case of any dispute arising out of the application of these Regulations between the Green Energy Open Access consumer and any agency (State Nodal Agency/Licensee) or any other matter relating to Green Energy Open Access, the aggrieved person may make an appeal to the Commission within a period of 30 days from the date of order/decision of the State Nodal Agency or licensee.

The Commission shall dispose of such dispute/appeal within three months from the date of receipt of appeal by the Commission.

PROVISION: Regulation 20
TITLE: Metering of Green Energy Open Access consumers

SUMMARY:
Regulation 20 establishes metering standards ensuring accurate energy accounting and billing for GEOA transactions. For consumers with contract demand ≥1 MW, four-quadrant Automatic Meter Reading (ABT) compliant meters meeting Central Electricity Authority (CEA) Regulations, 2006 are mandatory. These meters capture active energy (kWh), reactive energy (kVARh), and directional power flow in all four quadrants, enabling precise billing and deviation settlement. For consumers with capacity <1 MW, Special Energy Meters (SEMs) compliant with CEA Regulations, 2006 are required, providing simpler single-direction metering suitable for smaller installations. All meters must be ABT-compatible for real-time communication with SLDC/ALDC systems supporting deviation settlement, banking adjustments, and settlement accounting. The regulation incorporates CEA technical standards by reference, ensuring meter reliability and data integrity. Metering calibration, testing, and seal integrity are governed by relevant CEA/GERC orders. This framework enables transparent, dispute-free billing and robust energy accounting across the diverse GEOA consumer base.

20. Metering of Green Energy Open Access consumers

In case of Green Energy Open Access consumers:

(1) Where the contract demand for the Green Energy Open Access consumer is 1 MW or more, the meter used shall be of the type known as 'four-quadrant ABT meter' and shall be of the type as approved by the CEA in accordance with the CEA (Metering) Regulations, 2006.

(2) Where the contract demand for the Green Energy Open Access consumer is less than 1 MW, the meter used shall be 'Special Energy Meter' that is four-quadrant capable for ABT metering as specified in the CEA (Metering) Regulations, 2006.

(3) All these meters shall be in conformity with the CEA Regulations, 2006 on metering requirements.

PROVISION: Regulation 21
TITLE: Reactive Energy Charges

SUMMARY:
Regulation 21 addresses reactive energy charges (kVARh) incurred by GEOA consumers when procuring reactive power support alongside green energy. Reactive energy charges follow the Grid Code provisions applicable to the distribution licensee's area (intra-state) or the relevant Commission (inter-state via ISTS). If a GEOA consumer simultaneously procures power from the distribution licensee (in addition to open access generation), that consumer may incur reactive energy charges based on the applicable tariff rate established by the Commission for that consumer category in the licensee's Tariff Order. These charges compensate licensees for reactive power support services, which are necessary for voltage stability and system losses reduction. The regulation ensures transparent, non-discriminatory reactive energy pricing while respecting existing Grid Code provisions. GEOA consumers benefit from four-quadrant metering (Regulation 20) that accurately quantifies reactive power flow for fair billing.

PROVISION: Regulation 22
TITLE: Compliance with Grid Code, Distribution Code and Deviation Settlement Mechanism Regulations

SUMMARY:
Regulation 22 establishes mandatory compliance with three foundational regulatory instruments. GEOA consumers must comply with the Grid Code (intra-state operations and inter-state connections via ISTS) governing transmission system operations, voltage limits, frequency standards, and protection requirements. The Distribution Code requirements apply to all operations on distribution systems, addressing safety, islanding prevention, load-shedding procedures, and customer service standards. DSM (Deviation Settlement Mechanism) Regulations govern financial settlement of generation-consumption mismatches, ensuring all deviations from scheduled delivery are financially settled. These three codes/regulations form the operational and commercial framework within which GEOA operates. The provision ensures GEOA consumers do not operate in a vacuum but integrate fully with established grid operational standards, preventing system instability, unsafe conditions, and settlement disputes. Non-compliance may result in GEOA cancellation, penalties, or civil/criminal action per the Electricity Act. This hierarchical compliance structure balances consumer flexibility with grid security and fair dealing.

PROVISION: Regulation 23
TITLE: Collection and Disbursement of Charges

SUMMARY:
Regulation 23 establishes the financial flow framework for GEOA-related charges ensuring transparent, efficient revenue collection and appropriate distribution to service providers. All GEOA charges (transmission, wheeling, banking, deviation settlement, standby, and miscellaneous fees per Regulation 17) are collected by the State Nodal Agency (SNA) and relevant distribution licensee (for distribution network charges). The collected charges are disbursed by the SNA to the respective transmission licensee(s) (for transmission charges), distribution licensee(s) (for wheeling and distribution charges), and other service providers as applicable. This centralized collection via SNA and proportional disbursement ensures (1) single collection point convenience for GEOA consumers, (2) no double-collection risk, (3) transparent accounting of charge distribution, and (4) timely payment to service providers. The mechanism prevents licensees from directly billing consumers for GEOA services, avoiding disputes over charge calculation and payment verification. This framework is essential for the SNA's coordinative role and enables the trilateral relationship (Consumer-SNA-Licensee) to operate efficiently.
