DOCUMENT TYPE: Regulation

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Distribution Licence

PROVISION: Regulation 20

TITLE: Development and Maintenance of Distribution System

SUMMARY:
Regulation 20 establishes the foundational obligation of Distribution Licensees to develop and maintain an efficient, coordinated, and economical distribution system in their Area of Licence for the purpose of supplying electricity. This provision sets the quality and performance standard for all distribution licensees.

KEY TOPICS: Distribution System, Development, Maintenance, Efficiency, Economy

TEXT:
20. Development and Maintenance of Distribution System

The Distribution Licensee shall have the duty to develop and maintain an efficient, co-ordinated and economical distribution system in its Area of Licence for the purpose of supplying electricity.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Regulation 2(i)(f) (Definition of Distribution Licence)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 21 (Obligation to supply on demand), Regulation 32 (Standards of Performance), Regulation 33 (Cooperation with other licensees)

DOCUMENT: GERC (Distribution Licence) Regulations, 2005 – Notification No 4 of 2005

DOCUMENT TYPE: Regulation

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Distribution Licence

PROVISION: Regulation 21

TITLE: Obligation to Supply on Demand

SUMMARY:
Regulation 21 establishes the Distribution Licensee's obligation to provide electricity supply to any premises within the Area of Licence upon application by the owner or occupier, within one month of receipt (or such longer period as specified by the Commission for extensions requiring new substations or main extensions). Applications must be in the prescribed form with procedures approved by the Commission. The licensee must provide necessary electric plant or lines. However, persons are not entitled to separate supplies unless they pay the price determined by the Commission. The licensee must always endeavor to procure adequate power of appropriate quality for supply to consumers.

KEY TOPICS: Supply Obligation, Demand Response, Connection Timeline, Quality of Supply, Pricing

TEXT:
21. Obligation to supply on demand

(i) Subject to the provisions of the Act, the Distribution Licensee shall, on the application of the owner or occupier of any premises within the Area of Licence, give supply of electricity to such premises within one month of the receipt of such application or within such period as may be specified by the Commission if such supply requires extension of distribution mains or new substations.

(ii) Where the owner or occupier of any premises requires supply of electricity under the terms of clause (i) above, the application shall be made in a form prescribed by the Commission and the procedure for responding to that application shall be in accordance with the procedure approved by the Commission.

(iii) The Distribution Licensee shall have the duty to provide, if required, any electric plant or electric line for providing the electric supply to the premises in terms of clause (i) above.

Provided that no person shall be entitled to demand, or to continue to receive, from a Licensee (including a Distribution Licensee) a supply of electricity for any premises having a separate supply unless he has agreed with the Licensee (including the Distribution Licensee) to pay to it such price as determined by the Commission.

(iv) The Distribution Licensee shall always endeavour to procure adequate power of appropriate quality for supply to consumers.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 22 of Electricity Act 2003

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 20 (Development and Maintenance), Regulation 22 (Power to recover expenditure), Regulation 23 (Power to require security)

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Distribution Licence

PROVISION: Regulation 22

TITLE: Power to Recover Expenditure

SUMMARY:
Regulation 22 authorizes Distribution Licensees to recover from consumers requiring supply under Regulation 21, any expenses reasonably incurred and approved by the Commission, in providing any electric line or electrical plant used for the purpose of giving that supply. This enables cost-recovery for infrastructure extensions made specifically for individual consumer connections.

KEY TOPICS: Cost Recovery, Infrastructure Expenditure, Commission Approval, Consumer Charges

TEXT:
22. Power to recover expenditure

The Distribution Licensee may charge from a person requiring a supply of electricity in pursuance of Regulation 21 above, any expenses reasonably incurred and approved by the Commission, in providing any electric line or electrical plant used for the purpose of giving that supply.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Regulation 21 (Obligation to supply on demand)

DOCUMENT: GERC (Distribution Licence) Regulations, 2005 – Notification No 4 of 2005

DOCUMENT TYPE: Regulation

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Distribution Licence

PROVISION: Regulation 23

TITLE: Power to Require Security

SUMMARY:
Regulation 23 establishes the Distribution Licensee's authority to require security from consumers requiring supply under Regulation 21, as specified by the Commission. The security covers payment of electricity charges and provision of lines/plant/meters. If security is not provided, the licensee may refuse supply or provision of infrastructure. If security becomes invalid or insufficient, the licensee may issue 30 days' notice requiring new security; failure to comply allows discontinuance of supply. The licensee must pay interest on security at bank rate or more as specified by the Commission. Security is refundable on consumer request. Consumers with pre-payment meters are exempt from security requirements.

KEY TOPICS: Security Deposit, Consumer Security, Refund, Interest, Pre-payment Meter Exemption

TEXT:
23. Power to require security

(i) Subject to the provisions of Section 47 of the Act, a Distribution Licensee may require any person, who requires a supply of electricity in pursuance of Regulation 21 above, to give it reasonable security, as specified by the Commission, for the payment to it of all monies, which may become due to it -

(a) in respect of the electricity supplied to such persons; or

(b) where any electric line or electrical plant or electric meter is to be provided for supplying electricity to person, in respect of the provision of such line or plant or meter;

and if that person fails to give such security, the Distribution Licensee may, if it thinks fit, refuse to give the supply or to provide the line or plant or meter for the period during which the failure continues.

(ii) Where any person has not given such security as is mentioned in clause (i) above or the security given by any person has become invalid or insufficient, the Distribution Licensee may, by notice, require that person, within thirty days after the service of the notice, to give it reasonable security for the payment of all monies which may become due to it in respect of the supply of electricity or provision of such line or plant or meter.

(iii) If the person referred to in clause (ii) above fails to give such security, the Distribution Licensee may, if it thinks fit, discontinue the supply of electricity for the period during which the failure continues.

(iv) The Distribution Licensee shall pay interest equivalent to the bank rate or more, as may be specified by the Commission, on the security referred to in clause (i) above and refund such security on the request of the person who gave such security.

(v) The Distribution Licensee shall not be entitled to require security in pursuance of clause (i)(a) above if the person requiring the supply is prepared to take the supply through a pre- payment meter.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 47 of Electricity Act 2003, Regulation 21 (Obligation to supply on demand)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 34 (Obligation to install meter), GERC Security Deposit Regulations

PROVISION: Regulation 24

TITLE: Open Access

SUMMARY:
Regulation 24 establishes that, subject to the various phases of introduction of open access under Section 42(2) of the Electricity Act 2003, the Distribution Licensee shall arrange for provision of non-discriminatory open access to its distribution system to any eligible persons in accordance with the Open Access Regulations issued by the Commission. This ensures that the distribution system is available on a non-discriminatory basis for competitive electricity procurement.

KEY TOPICS: Open Access, Non-Discrimination, Distribution System Access, Competition

TEXT:
24. Open Access

Subject to the various phases of introduction of open access under Section 42(2) of the Act, the Distribution Licensee shall arrange for the provision of non-discriminatory open access to its distribution system to any persons in accordance with the Open Access Regulations issued by the Commission.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 42(2) of Electricity Act 2003, GERC Open Access Regulations

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 26 (Prohibition of Undue Preference), Regulation 44 (Expected Revenue Calculation and Tariffs)

DOCUMENT: GERC (Distribution Licence) Regulations, 2005 – Notification No 4 of 2005

DOCUMENT TYPE: Regulation

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Distribution Licence

PROVISION: Regulation 25

TITLE: Prohibition of Subsidies

SUMMARY:
Regulation 25 prohibits Distribution Licensees from granting any subsidy or subvention to any person or to any Other Business of the licensee, except for subsidies granted by the State Government pursuant to Section 65 of the Electricity Act 2003. This protection ensures that regulated distribution tariffs do not subsidize cross-subsidies between consumer classes or support unregulated business ventures.

KEY TOPICS: Subsidy Prohibition, Licensed Business, Other Business, State Government Authority

TEXT:
25. Prohibition of subsidies

The Distribution Licensee shall not give any subsidy or subvention to any person or any Other Business of the Distribution Licensee, except a subsidy granted by the State Government pursuant to Section 65 of the Act.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 65 of Electricity Act 2003, Regulation 2(i)(h) (Licensed Business), Regulation 2(i)(i) (Other Business)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 28 (Other Business of Distribution Licensee), Regulation 40 (Accounts)

DOCUMENT: GERC (Distribution Licence) Regulations, 2005 – Notification No 4 of 2005

DOCUMENT TYPE: Regulation

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Distribution Licence

PROVISION: Regulation 26

TITLE: Prohibition of Undue Preference

SUMMARY:
Regulation 26 prohibits Distribution Licensees from showing undue preference or unreasonably discriminating against any person, class of persons, or licensee, or their consumers, in providing services or conducting the Licensed Business. A proviso protects licensees from breach findings when undue preference results from State Government directions or Commission orders. Any question as to the meaning of undue preference or unreasonable discrimination shall be decided by the Commission, whose decision is final. This regulation implements the non-discrimination principle of the Electricity Act 2003.

KEY TOPICS: Non-Discrimination, Undue Preference, Fair Treatment, Consumer Equality

TEXT:
26. Prohibition of Undue Preference

(i) The Distribution Licensee shall not, in providing services required of it under the Distribution Licence and in general conduct of the Licensed Business, show undue preference to any person or class of persons and shall not unreasonably discriminate against any person or class of persons or Licensee or generating company or its consumers or other Licensee's consumers.

Provided that the Distribution Licensee shall not be deemed to be in breach of its obligations under the Distribution Licence if any undue preference results from compliance with any directions of the State Government under the Act or the State Act or in the implementation of any order passed by the State Commission.

(ii) Any question as to the meaning of undue preference or unreasonable discrimination shall be decided by the Commission, whose decision in this regard shall be final.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 22 of Electricity Act 2003, Regulation 2(i)(h) (Licensed Business)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Regulation 24 (Open Access), Regulation 30 (Anti-competitive practices)
