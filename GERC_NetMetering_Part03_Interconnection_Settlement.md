## CHUNK 20

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Section 8 (Interconnection with the Grid: Standards and Safety)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8(1)-(3) (General Interconnection Standards and Connectivity Levels)

**TITLE:** Grid Interconnection Standards and Voltage-Level Connectivity Requirements

**SUMMARY:**
Rooftop solar PV system interconnection with distribution licensee network must comply with Central Electricity Authority (Technical Standards for Connectivity to the Grid) Regulations 2007, CEA (Technical Standards for Connectivity of Distributed Generation Resources) Regulations 2013, and GERC (Terms and Conditions of Intra-State Open Access) Regulations 2011. Additionally, interconnection must conform to CEA (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations 2010. A 5% variation in rated system capacity is permissible provided technical grid interconnection requirements are met. Connectivity voltage levels are determined by consumer connected load: (1) up to 6 kW: 240V single-phase, (2) 6-18.65 kW: 415V three-phase, (3) 18.65-100 kW/kVA: 415V three-phase, (4) above 100 kW/kVA: HT level. HT consumers may install systems at LT or HT voltage and connect to their LT/HT system. These provisions align distributed solar generation with existing distribution system voltage architecture.

**KEY TOPICS:** Interconnection Standards, CEA Technical Standards, Safety Compliance, Voltage Level Connectivity, 5% Capacity Variation, Single-Phase, Three-Phase, HT (High Tension), LT (Low Tension)

**TEXT:**
(1) The interconnection of the Rooftop Solar PV System with the network of the distribution licensee shall be made as per the Central Electricity Authority (Technical Standards for Connectivity to the Grid) Regulations, 2007, CEA (Technical Standards for Connectivity of the Distributed Generation Resources) Regulations, 2013 and GERC (Terms and Conditions of Intra-State Open Access), Regulations, 2011 as amended from time to time.

The interconnection of the Rooftop Solar PV System with the distribution system of the licensee shall conform to the relevant provisions of Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations, 2010 as amended from time to time.

Provided that a variation in the rated capacity of the system within a range of five percent (5%) shall be allowed;

Provided further that the system qualifies the technical requirements for grid interconnection with the network of the distribution licensee.

(2) The connectivity levels at which the Rooftop PV Solar System shall be connected with the grid are as specified below:

| Sr. No. | Connected load of Eligible Consumer | Connectivity level |
|---------|--------------------------------------|-------------------|
| 1. | Upto 6 kW | 240 V- single phase |
| 2. | Above 6 kW and upto 18.65 kW | 415 V-Three phase |
| 3. | Above 18.65 kW and upto 100 kW/kVA | 415 V-Three phase |
| 4. | Above 100 kW/kVA | HT level |

(3) The above connectivity norms are applicable to all the Rooftop Solar PV System for seeking connectivity with the network of the distribution licensees. HT consumers may install Rooftop Solar PV System at LT/HT voltage and connect them to their LT/HT system for interconnection of Rooftop Solar PV System with the local distribution licensee's grid, for which the relevant provisions of the Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations, 2010, as amended from time to time, Central Electricity Authority (Standards of connectivity of distributed generation resources) Regulations, 2013 and GERC (Terms and Conditions of Intra-State Open Access) Regulations, 2011 as amended from time to time shall be applicable.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Central Electricity Authority regulations (Technical Standards 2007, Distributed Generation 2013, Safety 2010), GERC Open Access Regulations 2011, Regulation 7 (TFR assessment of voltage connectivity), Regulation 13 (metering at specified voltage levels)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Voltage connectivity level determines meter type (Regulation 13); single-phase systems up to 6 kW connect at consumer's LT connection voltage (240V); three-phase systems require 415V infrastructure; HT consumers (above 100 kW/kVA) follow open access framework; CEA standards codify frequency, harmonics, and protective relaying requirements

## CHUNK 21

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Section 8 (Interconnection with the Grid: Standards and Safety)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8(4)-(6) (Responsibility for Operation, Maintenance, and Liability)

**TITLE:** Responsibility Allocation for System Maintenance, Safety, and Liability

**SUMMARY:**
The rooftop solar PV energy generator is responsible for safe operation, maintenance, and defect rectification of their system up to the interconnection point. Beyond the interconnection point, the distribution licensee is responsible for safe operation, maintenance, and defect rectification, including the net meter. The consumer/solar PV owner is solely responsible for any accident (fatal/non-fatal) to humans or animals caused by back feeding from the rooftop solar PV system when grid supply is off, as determined by the Chief Electrical Inspector. The distribution licensee reserves the right to disconnect the consumer's installation at any time to prevent accidents or damage. Testing as per EN 50160 and distribution licensee standards ensures power quality from the rooftop solar PV system. Any alternate source of supply (battery, diesel generator, backup power) must be restricted to the consumer's network, with the consumer responsible for preventing such power from extending to the distribution licensee's LT grid on grid supply failure.

**KEY TOPICS:** Responsibility Allocation, Consumer Responsibility, Licensee Responsibility, Interconnection Point, Back Feeding, Liability, Anti-Islanding, Power Quality Testing (EN 50160), Alternate Power Sources

**TEXT:**
(4) The Rooftop Solar PV Energy Generator shall be responsible for safe operation, maintenance and rectification of defects of its system up to the interconnection point beyond which the responsibility of safe operation, maintenance and rectification of any defects in the system including the net meter shall rest with the distribution licensee.

(5) The consumer/ Rooftop Solar PV Energy Generator shall be solely responsible for any accident to human being/ animals whatsoever (fatal/non-fatal/departmental/non-departmental) that may occur due to back feeding from the Rooftop Solar PV System when the grid supply is off, based on the issue decided by the Chief Electrical Inspector. The distribution licensee reserves the right to disconnect the consumer's installation at any time in the event of such exigencies to prevent accident or damage to men and materials.

(6) The tests as per EN 50160 and as per distribution licensee's standards shall be carried out by the Chief Electrical Inspector to ensure the quality of power generated from the Rooftop Solar PV System.

(7) Any alternate source of supply shall be restricted to the consumer's network and the consumer shall be responsible to take adequate safety measures to prevent battery power/diesel generator power/back-up power extending to distribution licensee's LT grid on failure of distribution licensee's grid supply.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 8(15) (manual isolating switch requirements for emergency disconnection), Regulation 8(10) (anti-islanding protection), Regulation 8(16) (synchronization protection features and control diagrams), Annexure-V (inverter anti-islanding and protection standards)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Consumer responsibility for back-feeding prevention connects to anti-islanding requirement (Regulation 8(10), IEC-62116); interconnection point responsibility demarcation critical for warranty and insurance claims; EN 50160 is European standard for power quality at low-voltage distribution systems; alternate power sources (battery storage) increasingly relevant with Regulation 13.5 provision for check meters

## CHUNK 22

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Section 8 (Interconnection with the Grid: Standards and Safety)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8(8)-(9) (Licensee Disconnection Rights and Defect Rectification Procedures)

**TITLE:** Distribution Licensee Disconnection Rights and Defect Remediation Timeline

**SUMMARY:**
The distribution licensee has the right to disconnect the rooftop solar PV system from its network under three conditions: (1) emergencies or maintenance requirements on the licensee's system, (2) hazardous conditions on the licensee's system caused by operation of the rooftop solar PV system or protective equipment (as determined by licensee/transmission licensee/SLDC), (3) adverse electrical effects (power quality problems) from the rooftop solar PV system affecting other consumers' electrical equipment (as determined by licensee). Upon identifying a defect, the licensee notifies the rooftop solar PV generator with a 2-day rectification deadline. This framework balances consumer rights with the licensee's obligation to maintain safe, reliable distribution system operations and protect other consumers from power quality degradation.

**KEY TOPICS:** Licensee Disconnection Rights, Emergency Disconnection, Maintenance Disconnection, Hazardous Conditions, Power Quality Problems, Defect Rectification (2-day deadline), Fault Determination by Licensee/SLDC

**TEXT:**
(8) The distribution licensee shall have the right to disconnect the Rooftop Solar PV System from its system at any time in the following conditions:

(i) Emergencies or maintenance requirement on the distribution licensee's system,

(ii) Hazardous conditions existing on the distribution licensee's system due to operation of Rooftop Solar PV System or protective equipment as determined by the Distribution Licensee/Transmission Licensee/SLDC, and

(iii) Adverse electrical effects, such as power quality problems, on the electrical equipment of other consumers of the distribution licensee caused by the Rooftop Solar PVS system as determined by the distribution licensee.

(9) Subject to sub Regulation 8(4) above, the distribution licensee may call upon the Rooftop Solar PV generator to rectify the defect within two days of such intimation.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 8(4) (responsibility demarcation - consumer responsible up to interconnection point), Regulation 8(12) (protective functions and automatic disconnection requirements)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Hazardous condition determination authority rests with SLDC (State Load Dispatch Centre) for grid-level issues; 2-day rectification timeline ensures rapid remediation of power quality or safety hazards; power quality monitoring by licensee protects other consumers from harmonics, voltage flicker, etc. caused by faulty solar inverters; interconnection point responsibility allocation (Reg 8(4)) determines whether defect is on consumer or licensee side

## CHUNK 23

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Section 8 (Interconnection with the Grid: Standards and Safety)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8(10)-(13) (Anti-Islanding Protection, Synchronization, and Protective Functions)

**TITLE:** Anti-Islanding, Synchronization, and Protective Devices Requirements

**SUMMARY:**
Rooftop solar PV systems must detect unintended islanding conditions and have anti-islanding protection to prevent unfavorable conditions including grid failure. IEC-62116 standard must be followed to test islanding prevention measures. Every system must be equipped with automatic synchronization device; systems with inverters that have synchronization built-in do not require separate synchronization device. Systems operating in parallel with the electricity system must have protective functions: (1) Over and under voltage trip (>110% or <80% voltage, clearing within 2 seconds), (2) Over and under frequency trip (50.5 Hz or <47.5 Hz, clearing within 0.2 seconds), (3) Circuit fault prevention (system ceases energizing if fault in circuit), (4) Voltage and frequency sensing with time delay to prevent energizing de-energized circuit and requiring 60-second voltage/frequency stability before reconnection, (5) Anti-islanding function (cease energizing within 2 seconds of island formation). Equipment must include: (A) Suitable circuit breakers capable of interrupting maximum available fault current, (B) Single-device failure prevention (no single device failure compromises safety/reliability), (C) Paralleling device capable of withstanding 220% of nominal interconnection voltage, and (D) Voltage fluctuation limit (not greater than ±5% at interconnection point).

**KEY TOPICS:** Anti-Islanding Protection, IEC-62116 Standard, Synchronization Device, Over/Under Voltage Trip, Over/Under Frequency Trip, Fault Protection, De-Energization Prevention, Circuit Breaker, 220% Voltage Withstand, Voltage Flicker (±5% limit)

**TEXT:**
(10) The Rooftop Solar PV System should be capable of detecting an unintended islanding condition. This system must have anti-islanding protection to prevent any unfavourable conditions including failure of supply. IEC-62116 shall be followed to test islanding prevention measure for grid connected photovoltaic inverters.

(11) Every Rooftop Solar PV System shall be equipped with automatic synchronization device:

Provided that Rooftop Solar PV System using inverter shall not be required to have separate synchronization device, if the same is inherently built into the inverter.

(12) The Rooftop Solar PV System operating in parallel with electricity system shall be equipped with the following protective functions to sense abnormal conditions on electricity system and cause the Rooftop Solar PV System to be automatically disconnected from the electricity system or to prevent the Rooftop Solar PV System from being connected inappropriately to the electricity system;

(i) Over and under voltage trip functions if voltage reaches above 110% or below 80% respectively with a clearing time upto two (2) seconds;

(ii) Over and under frequency trip functions, if frequency reaches 50.5 Hz or below 47.5 Hz with a clearing time upto 0.2 seconds;

(iii) The Rooftop Solar PV System shall cease to energize the circuit to which it is connected in case of any fault in the circuit;

(iv) A voltage and frequency sensing and time delay function to prevent the Rooftop Solar PV System from energizing a de-energized circuit and to prevent the Rooftop Solar PV System from reconnecting with electricity system unless voltage and frequency is within the prescribed limits and are stable for at least sixty (60) seconds; and

(v) A function to prevent the Rooftop Solar PV System from contributing to the formation of an unintended island, and cease to energize the electricity system within two (2) seconds of the formation of an unintended island.

(13) The equipment of the Rooftop Solar PV System shall meet the following requirements:

(i) Circuit Breakers or other interrupting equipment shall be suitable for their intended application with the capability of interrupting the maximum available fault current expected at their location,

(ii) The Rooftop Solar PV System and associated equipment shall be so designed that the failure of any single device or component shall not potentially compromise the safety and reliability of the electricity system and

(iii) Paralleling device of the Rooftop Solar PV System shall be capable of withstanding 220% of the nominal voltage at the interconnection point.

(14) Every time the Rooftop Solar PV System of the Eligible Consumer is synchronized with the distribution system, it shall not cause voltage fluctuation greater than ±5% at the point of inter connection.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Annexure-V (Inverter Standards codifying IEC compliance, anti-islanding, harmonics), CEA Technical Standards 2007 and 2013, IEC-62116, IEC 61000 (voltage flicker standards)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Anti-islanding protection is critical safety requirement to prevent live conductor energy when grid is off (addresses consumer liability in Regulation 8(5)); voltage/frequency limits aligned with Indian grid standards (50 Hz nominal ±5% or ±0.2 Hz); 2-second clearing time for voltage-related faults critical for equipment protection; CEI testing confirms compliance (Regulation 7, Stage 11)

## CHUNK 24

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Section 8 (Interconnection with the Grid: Standards and Safety)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8(15)-(19) (Isolation Switch, Commissioning Tests, and Certification)

**TITLE:** Manual Isolation Switch, Commissioning Tests, and Certificate Requirements

**SUMMARY:**
After maintenance and safety procedures consideration, the distribution licensee may require a manually operated isolating switch between the rooftop solar PV system and electricity system. This switch must: (1) Allow visible verification that separation is accomplished, (2) Include clear open/closed position indications, (3) Be reachable quickly and conveniently 24/7 without requiring consumer clearance, (4) Be lockable in open position, (5) Not be rated for load break nor have over-current protection, (6) Be located at least 2.44 m above ground level. Prior to first synchronization, consumer and licensee must agree on protection features and control diagrams. The power conditioning unit must filter harmonics and other distortions before grid injection. Technical standards and inverter standards must comply with Annexure-V or CEA-specified standards. Commissioning tests must be conducted with representatives of consumer, GEDA, and distribution licensee present; commissioning certificate signed by all parties establishes the official commissioning date for tariff purposes (APPC rate lock-in). Commissioning certificate must include: solar panel details (manufacturer, type, capacity), inverter details (type, size), total capacity, and meter details (type, accuracy, serial number).

**KEY TOPICS:** Manual Isolation Switch, Lockable Switch, 2.44m Height Requirement, Commissioning Test, Commissioning Certificate, Power Conditioning Unit, Harmonics Filtering, Protection Features, Control Diagrams

**TEXT:**
(15) After considering the maintenance and safety procedures, the distribution licensee may require a Rooftop Solar PV System to provide a manually operated isolating switch between the Rooftop Solar PV System and the electricity system, which shall meet following requirements:

(i) Allow visible verification that separation has been accomplished;

(ii) Include indications to clearly show open and closed positions;

(iii) Be capable of being reached quickly and conveniently twenty four (24) hours a day by licensee's personnel without requiring clearance from the applicant;

(iv) Be capable of being locked in the open position;

(v) May not be rated for load break nor may have feature of over-current protection; and

(vi) Be located at a height of at least 2.44 m above the ground level.

(16) Prior to synchronization of the Rooftop Solar PV System for the first time with the distribution system of the licensee the applicant and the licensee shall agree on the protection features and control diagrams.

(17) The power conditioning unit shall have the features of filtering out harmonics and other distortions before injecting the energy into the system of the distribution utility. The technical standards, power quality standards and inverter standards shall be as per Annexure – V of these Regulations or any other standards as may be specified by CEA from time to time.

(18) The commissioning test of the Rooftop Solar PV System shall be carried out in the presence of representatives of consumer/owner of Rooftop Solar PV System, GEDA and concerned officer of the distribution licensee. The commissioning certificate shall be signed by all the above named parties. Date of commissioning shall be recognised from the date on which successful commissioning test is conducted.

(19) The Commissioning certificate must contain of the following details:

I. Details of Solar PV panels including name of the manufacturer, type, size/capacity of the panels, etc.;

II. Details regarding inverter, types of inverters and size;

III. Total capacity of the Solar PV Plant;

IV. Details of meter installed along with the types of meter accuracy, serial number, etc.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 7 (Procedure for Application Stage 15 - GEDA commissioning), Regulation 9 (Energy Accounting and Settlement - commissioning date determines APPC rate), Annexure-V (Inverter Standards and Harmonics limits), CEA regulations for power quality

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Isolation switch is key safety device for licensee personnel during grid maintenance; 2.44m height ensures accessibility and prevents accidental ground contact; harmonics filtering requirement (Annexure-V specifies <3% individual harmonics, <5% THD) prevents power quality degradation to neighboring consumers; commissioning date is fixed point in time for APPC rate determination and project life calculations (25 years)

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Section 9 (Energy Accounting and Settlement)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 9 (Preamble) and Regulation 9.2 (Residential and Government Consumers)

**TITLE:** Energy Accounting Framework and Settlement for Residential/Government Consumers

**SUMMARY:**
Energy injected prior to commissioning is deemed inadvertent power; consumers receive no compensation for such pre-commissioning energy. For each billing period, the licensee shows separately: quantum injected by consumer, electricity supplied by licensee, net billed electricity for payment, and net exported energy after consumption adjustment. For residential and government consumers: If electricity supplied exceeds generation during billing period, licensee raises invoice for net consumption at consumer's prevailing tariff. If generation exceeds consumption, licensee purchases surplus at APPC rate determined in the year of system commissioning, applicable for entire 25-year project life (e.g., system commissioned in 2016-17 receives FY 2015-16 APPC rate). Banking of surplus energy is allowed within one billing cycle. Licensee may also raise invoices for other charges as allowed by GERC. This net metering design encourages solar investment while ensuring cost-reflective tariffs for surplus energy injection.

**KEY TOPICS:** Net Metering, Energy Accounting, Inadvertent Power, Surplus Energy Purchase, APPC Rate (25-Year Lock), Banking (One Billing Cycle), Residential Consumer, Government Consumer, Net Billing

**TEXT:**
Any energy injected prior to commissioning shall be deemed as inadvertent power. The consumer/Solar PV owner is not eligible to receive any monetary compensation for such inadvertent power.

9.1 For each billing period, the licensee shall show the quantum of electricity injected by Eligible Consumer/Solar PV system in the grid, electricity supplied by the distribution licensee, net billed electricity for payment by the consumer and net exported energy after adjustment against the consumption separately.

(1) For Residential and Government consumers

9.2 In the event the electricity supplied by the distribution licensee during any billing period exceeds the electricity generated by the Eligible Consumer's Rooftop Solar PV System, the distribution licensee shall raise invoice for the net electricity consumption at the consumer's prevailing tariff;

In the event the electricity injected exceeds the electricity consumed during the billing period, such excess injected electricity shall be purchased by the concerned Distribution Licensee at the APPC rate determined by the Commission for the year in which the Rooftop Solar PV System is commissioned for whole life of the Rooftop Solar PV System; e.g. for the Rooftop Solar PV System commissioned during 2016-17, the APPC rate determined by the Commission for FY 2015-16 shall be applicable.

Banking of energy shall be allowed within one billing cycle of the consumer.

The distribution licensee in addition to consumer tariff shall be eligible to raise invoice for any other charges as allowed by the Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission from time to time.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 9.3, 9.4, 9.5 (consumer-type variants), Regulation 7, Stage 15 (commissioning date establishment), Regulation 9.1 (accounting framework), Regulation 12 (Applicability of Other Charges - exemptions from transmission, wheeling, surcharges)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** APPC rate lock mechanism encourages long-term solar project planning; 25-year reference aligns with typical solar panel lifetime; banking within billing cycle (typically 30 days) prevents indefinite carry-forward of surplus; residential consumer settlement is simplest model; demand charges still apply despite net metering (Regulation 7 provision via Annexure-IV agreement); REC mechanism discussed in Regulation 11 (not applicable for residential consumers under Reg 11.1)

## CHUNK 28

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Section 9 (Energy Accounting and Settlement)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 9.5 and 9.6 (Industrial/Commercial Consumers - Type 2(b), REC Mechanism; and Wheeling Power Settlement)

**TITLE:** Energy Settlement for REC-Registered Consumers and Wheeling Power

**SUMMARY:**
Industrial, commercial, and other consumers utilizing both energy attribute and renewable attribute while registered under REC mechanism follow 15-minute time-block accounting for REC generation tracking. Accounting structure: If grid supply exceeds generation in billing period, licensee invoices net consumption at prevailing tariff. If generation exceeds consumption after adjustment within 15-minute time block, licensee purchases surplus energy at 85% of APPC rate (vs. 100% for non-REC consumers), applicable for 25-year project life. The 15% reduction reflects value capture by REC mechanism, where consumer separately monetizes renewable attribute through REC sales. This pricing differential incentivizes REC registration while compensating licensee for REC benefit loss. Energy accounting for wheeling power from non-rooftop solar projects for captive use follows the same provisions as Regulations 9.3-9.5 (15-minute blocks and applicable surcharges). These provisions create regulatory arbitrage for consumer choice: full APPC for RPO-only consumers vs. 85% APPC for REC-trading consumers.

**KEY TOPICS:** REC Mechanism, 15-Minute Time Block, 85% APPC Rate, Renewable Attribute Monetization, REC Trading, Wheeling Power, Captive Use

**TEXT:**
(4) For Industrial, Commercial and Other Consumers utilizing the 'energy attribute' of the generated solar energy from the Roof top Solar PV System and utilizing the 'renewable attribute' for REC.

9.5 In the event the electricity supplied by the distribution licensee during any billing period exceeds the electricity generated by the Eligible Consumer's Rooftop Solar PV System, the distribution licensee shall raise invoice for the net electricity consumption at the consumer's prevailing tariff;

In the event the electricity injected exceeds the electricity consumed during the billing period, such excess injected electricity after adjustment of consumption in 15 minute time block shall be purchased by the concerned Distribution Licensee at the 85% of APPC rate determined by the Commission for the year in which the Rooftop Solar PV System is commissioned for whole life of the project;

9.6 The energy accounting and settlement for wheeling power from solar projects other than Rooftop set up for captive purpose shall be in line with the provision at Para 9.3 to 9.5.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 9.4 (RPO-only settlement), Regulation 11 (REC mechanism and framework), Regulation 13.3 (ABT-compliant meters), Central Electricity Authority regulations for REC issuance procedures

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** 85% APPC price reduction reflects REC value captured separately (REC value typically ₹5,000-15,000 per MWh depending on solar market surplus); 15-minute block granularity enables precise REC generation tracking; REC registered consumers may choose not to bank surplus energy (immediate REC issuance instead) or bank for 15-minute carry-forward; wheeling power provision extends settlement framework to solar projects set up on leased land or third-party sites for captive consumption

**TITLE:** Dispute Resolution, RPO Credit Framework, and Exemptions from Transmission/Wheeling Charges

**SUMMARY:**
Disputes arising from regulations or their interpretation are decided by GERC following prescribed procedures (Regulation 10). Regarding RPO (Renewable Purchase Obligation): Quantum of electricity consumed by eligible consumers not defined as obligated entities from rooftop solar under net metering qualifies toward distribution licensee's RPO compliance; no REC is issued as generated solar energy is used to meet licensee's RPO (Regulation 11.1). Industrial, commercial, and other consumers utilizing both energy and renewable attributes shall credit solar energy generated and consumed within 15-minute time blocks toward meeting consumer's RPO; no REC issued for this generation. Surplus energy exported during 15-minute blocks purchased by licensee is credited toward licensee's RPO, with no REC issued (Regulation 11.2). Exception: If consumer opts to use renewable attribute under REC mechanism, entire solar energy generated in 15-minute block is considered for REC issuance (Regulation 11.2 proviso). Regarding charges (Regulation 12): Rooftop solar PV systems under net metering are exempted from transmission charge, transmission loss, wheeling charge, wheeling loss, cross-subsidy surcharge, and additional surcharge. These exemptions significantly reduce cost of surplus energy for consumers, effectively subsidizing solar penetration.

10 Dispute Resolution

10.1 In case of any dispute pertaining to these Regulations and its interpretation, it shall be decided by the Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission by observing the prescribed procedure.

11 Solar Renewable Purchase Obligation

11.1 The quantum of electricity consumed by the Eligible Consumer, who is not defined as an obligated entity, from the Rooftop Solar PV System under net-metering arrangement shall qualify towards compliance of Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO) for the distribution licensee and no REC shall be issued as the generated solar energy shall be used to meet the Distribution licensee's RPO. Industrial, Commercial and other than Residential and Government Consumers, utilizing both, 'energy attribute' as well as 'renewable attribute' of the generated solar energy, the solar energy generated and consumed by such consumers during the 15 minute time block shall be credited towards meeting the consumer's RPO and no REC shall be issued for such generation.

11.2 However, surplus energy, if any, exported during the 15 minute time block purchased by distribution licensee, shall be credited towards meeting the Distribution Licensee's RPO and no REC shall be issued for such generation. Provided that if such consumers utilize the 'renewable attribute' to get Renewable Energy Certificate under the REC mechanism then entire solar energy generated in 15 minute time block shall be considered for issuing REC.

12 Applicability of other charges

12.1 The Rooftop Solar PV System under net metering arrangement, installed on the Eligible Consumer's premises, shall be exempted from Transmission Charge, Transmission Loss, Wheeling Charge, Wheeling Loss, Cross Subsidy Surcharge and Additional Surcharge.
