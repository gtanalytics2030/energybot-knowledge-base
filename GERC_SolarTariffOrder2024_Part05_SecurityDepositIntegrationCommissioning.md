DOCUMENT TYPE: Tariff Order

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / 2.9 Clause 3.9: Security Deposit & Delay in Commissioning of Solar Power Project

PROVISION: Clause 2.9

TITLE: Security Deposit, Bank Guarantee, and Delay-in-Commissioning Consequences — Stakeholder Comments and Commission's Decision

SUMMARY:
The discussion paper proposed that, given GETCO's load-flow-study-based permission process and the need to avoid idling transmission resources, Solar Power Project developers furnish a Bank Guarantee of Rs. 10 lakhs per MW to GETCO based on allotted transmission capacity. If the developer fails to commission the capacity within the prescribed period (12 months for 1-100 MW, 15 months for >100-200 MW, 18 months for >200-400 MW, from date of allotment of transmission capacity), GETCO shall encash the Bank Guarantee. The evacuation system must be ready before SCOD or the prescribed timeframe (whichever is earlier), failing which no waiver of Liquidated Damages is available. At least 10% of allotted capacity must be commissioned within one month of evacuation-line charging (or the prescribed timeframe, whichever earlier), failing which long-term transmission charges become payable for that 10% until commissioned; the balance 90% must be commissioned within one year of charging (or the prescribed timeframe), failing which STU cancels the connectivity/Open Access for the uncommissioned capacity, the developer forfeits any claim and pays relinquishment charges, and STU re-lists the cancelled capacity as spare capacity. Developers facing unforeseen delays may seek a Commission extension. Competitive-bidding-route projects are governed by their approved bid documents/PPA instead. GUVNL submitted that since PPAs already provide for SCOD extension on force majeure/transmission-delay grounds, commissioning delay should be governed by the respective PPA/Agreement, with the developer first approaching the concerned DISCOM for extension. The Commission ruled that for projects selling to DISCOM under PPA, delay shall be governed by the respective PPA/Agreement, while for projects set up for third-party sale/captive use, delay consequences shall be governed by this Order.

KEY TOPICS: Security Deposit, Bank Guarantee, Rs 10 Lakhs per MW, GETCO, Evacuation Line, Commissioning Timeline, Liquidated Damages, Relinquishment Charges, STU, Force Majeure, SCOD, PPA Governed Delay

2.9 Clause 3.9 Security Deposit & Delay in Commissioning of Solar Power Project

2.9.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The objective of specifying tariff framework for procurement of solar power is to promote development of renewable energy in the state. A procedure of giving permission for the proposed Solar projects, based on the load flow studies has been followed by the GETCO. Thus, the proposed evacuation system from the pooling station of Solar projects forms part of the overall GETCO System. While timely completion of power evacuation system for such Solar projects is essential, timely execution of project is also equally important. Non-completion of Solar projects leads to idling of transmission resources. The security deposit is furnished by the project developer in order to assure GETCO about the seriousness of the project. It is proposed that for the new control period.

The Solar Power Project Developer shall be required to provide Bank Guarantee @ Rs. 10 lakhs per MW to GETCO based on allotment of transmission capacity and in case the Developer fails to commission the capacity within the time-period mentioned hereunder, GETCO shall encash the Bank Guarantee.

| Sr. No. | RE Capacity in MW | Period for commissioning the entire evacuation line along with bays and metering system |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 01 | 1 MW to 100 MW | 12 months from the date of allotment of transmission capacity |
| 02 | >100 MW to 200 MW | 15 months from the date of allotment of transmission capacity |
| 03 | >200 MW to 400 MW | 18 months from the date of allotment of transmission capacity |

The Solar Project Developer shall ensure and prove that the Evacuation System consist of Transmission and /or Distribution System shall be ready prior to SCOD or aforesaid timeframe, whichever is earlier. Failure to it, the project developer is not eligible to get any waiver in Liquidated Damages payable by it, in terms of Agreement/ PPA.

The Solar Power Project Developer shall commission the project for at least 10% of the allotted capacity within one month of charging the evacuation line or as per timeframe stipulated table above, whichever is earlier, failing which, the Developer shall be liable to pay long-term transmission charges for 10% of the allotted capacity until such 10% of the allotted capacity is commissioned.

The balance 90% capacity shall require to be commissioned within one year of charging of evacuation line or as per timeframe stipulated above, whichever is earlier, failing which STU shall cancel the connectivity and Open Access granted, to the extent of capacity not commissioned and the RE developer shall have no claim on such capacity and pay relinquishment charges as determined by the Commission. Further, STU shall include such cancelled capacity in the list of spare available capacity for RE integration to be published on their website for prospective consumers.

If the Solar Project Developer (as Generator/Consumer/Licensee) fails to commission the entire allocated evacuation system along with bays and metering system within stipulated time-period due to unforeseen reasons, they may approach to the Commission seeking for extension of time period.

In case of Solar Power Project set up under competitive bidding route, in that case the aforesaid provision shall be governed by the provisions of approved bid documents/PPA.

2.9.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

GUVNL submitted that the DISCOMs and Generator enter into PPAs for procurement of power from the Solar Power Project for 25 years. The PPA includes the provisions for extension of time limit for Scheduled Commissioning Date on account of various force majeure event and delay in operationalization of transmission evacuation system. In view of this, GUVNL suggested that the delay in commissioning of the project shall be considered as per the provisions of the respective PPA/Agreement with concerned DISCOM. Therefore, in case of delay in commissioning of project, the developer shall first approach the concerned DISCOM for time extension as per the provisions of the PPA/ Agreement.

2.9.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission notes that in case of Solar Power Project selling power to DISCOM under PPA, the delay in commissioning of the project shall be considered as per the provisions of the respective PPA / Agreement with concerned DISCOM. However, in respect of Solar Power Plant set up for third party sale / captive use, the consequences for delay in commissioning of projects shall be governed as provided in this Order.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) provisions on force majeure and SCOD extension

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / 2.10 Clause 3.10: Exemptions from Demand Cut

PROVISION: Clause 2.10

TITLE: Exemption from Demand Cut — Proposal Withdrawn on Stakeholder Objection

SUMMARY:
The discussion paper proposed exempting Solar Power Projects from demand cut to the extent of 50% of installed capacity in cases of captive consumption and third-party sale within the State, applicable prospectively. GUVNL objected, noting that neither the Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023 nor the GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations 2024 provide for such an exemption, and suggested removing the provision. The Commission agreed with GUVNL and decided to remove the provision related to exemption from demand cut to the extent of 50% of installed capacity of solar power project in case of captive consumption and third-party sale; accordingly, this exemption does not appear in the final Order.

KEY TOPICS: Demand Cut, Exemption Removed, 50% Installed Capacity, Captive Consumption, Third Party Sale, GUVNL Objection

2.10 Clause 3.10 Exemptions from Demand Cut

2.10.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The discussion paper propose exemption from demand cut to the extent of 50% of installed capacity of solar power project in case of captive consumption and third-party sale within the State.

The above exemptions provided by the State Government shall be applicable for the prospective period.

2.10.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

GUVNL submitted that such exemption is not provided in the Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy, 2023. Further, GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024 also do not provide for such exemption from demand cut.

Accordingly, it is suggested to remove the provision of exemption from demand cut.

2.10.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission agree to the suggestion made by GUVNL and decided to remove the provision related to exemption from demand cut to the extent of 50% of installed capacity of solar power project in case of captive consumption and third-party sale.

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / 2.11 Clause 3.11: Procedure for Integration of Solar Power Project with the Grid

PROVISION: Clause 2.11

TITLE: Grid Integration Procedure — Compliance Certificate Requirements — Stakeholder Comments and Commission's Decision

SUMMARY:
The discussion paper proposed that, prior to commissioning and grid integration, Solar generators/developers submit a certificate (signed by an authorised signatory not below the rank of CMD/CEO/MD/Full Time Director) to SLDC, GEDA, and the concerned Distribution Licensees before SCOD declaration, confirming compliance of the generating station and main equipment (Solar PV Modules, Inverters, Auxiliary Systems) with CEA Technical Standards for Connectivity and Communications, CEA (Measures relating to Safety and Electricity Supply) Regulations 2010, the Gujarat Grid Code, and ALMM Certificate requirements (if applicable), alongside document submission to SLDC. SLDC requested two additional sub-paragraphs: (i) that GEDA, STU, and transmission/distribution licensees may verify compliance with prudent checks and request additional requirements from the generating station; and (ii) that GEDA ensure developer compliance before testing/commissioning and issuing the Commissioning Certificate, with non-compliant certificates deemed legally invalid, with commissioning allowed by GEDA/DISCOMs/GETCO representatives upon verified compliance with CEA Connectivity Standards and ALMM certification, recorded in the Commissioning Certificate. SLDC also suggested aligning connectivity/Open Access grant provisions with those in the Wind-Solar Hybrid Tariff Order. The Commission agreed to SLDC's modifications and incorporated them into the Order; regarding the connectivity-grant alignment suggestion, the Commission noted this is also the subject matter of a pending GETCO Petition under the Commission's consideration.

KEY TOPICS: Grid Integration Procedure, Authorised Signatory Certificate, CEA Technical Standards, ALMM Certificate, SCOD Declaration, GEDA Compliance Verification, Commissioning Certificate Validity, Wind-Solar Hybrid Tariff Order, GETCO Petition

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / 2.12 Clause 3.12: Commissioning of the Solar Power Project

PROVISION: Clause 2.12

TITLE: Commissioning Certification Process — GEDA's Role — Stakeholder Comments and Commission's Decision

SUMMARY:
The discussion paper proposed that, following the grid integration procedure, commissioning of Solar Power Projects be certified by GEDA in the presence of GETCO and/or Distribution Licensee representatives, with GEDA ensuring PV Modules are installed per applicable ALMM Certificates and BIS Indian Standards on Renewable Energy, energy flow into the grid recorded at site meters, and generation witnessed by an authorised DISCOM/GETCO representative. GEDA coordinates the commissioning date with DISCOMs/GETCO, and representatives of GEDA, GETCO, and/or Distribution Licensees must sign the commissioning documents; non-signature by any representative invalidates the "commissioning." The commissioning certificate must include Solar PV Module details (with RFID and ALMM Certificate details, if applicable) and Inverter details, and must ensure real-time generation data transfer to SLDC via RTU. GEDA requested relief from joint certification responsibility (alongside DISCOM/GETCO) for small projects injecting below 66 KV, citing the high volume of such projects and its manpower scarcity, suggesting joint inspection with GEDA be limited to projects injecting at 66 KV or above. The Commission held that GEDA, as the Nodal Agency for RE project development in the State, has a third-party government verification role that cannot be undermined, and decided not to make any changes in this regard — GEDA's joint certification role is retained for all Solar Power Projects regardless of voltage level.

KEY TOPICS: Commissioning Certification, GEDA Nodal Agency, ALMM Certificate, BIS Indian Standards, RFID Details, Real Time Data, RTU, Joint Certification, 66 KV Threshold, GEDA Manpower Constraint

2.12 Clause 3.12: Commissioning of the Solar Power project

The discussion paper proposes that after following the procedure of integration of Solar Power Project with grid, the commissioning of Solar Power Projects be followed. Commissioning with respect to the Solar project shall be certified by GEDA in presence of GETCO and / or Distribution Licensee representative. GEDA should ensure that PV Modules are installed as per ALMM Certificate issued for PV Modules by concerned Authority, if applicable, and as per Indian Standards on Renewable Energy notified by BIS and energy has flown into the grid and recorded in the energy meters installed at project site and witnessing of such generation of electricity by representative authorised by DISCOM and /or GETCO. The GEDA shall co-ordinate with DISCOMs and/or GETCO for fixing date of commissioning of plant. The representatives of GEDA, GETCO and / or Distribution Licensees shall sign on the commissioning of project by recording necessary details. Non-signing of documents of commissioning of Solar Projects by any of the representative of above entity, shall not qualify as valid 'commissioning' of the project. The commissioning certificate consists of the details of the Solar PV Modules along with RFID details, ALMM Certificate etc., if applicable and details of Inverter etc. Further, it shall also ensure that generation data from the Solar project shall also transferred in the real time basis through RTU to SLDC.

2.12.1 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

GEDA requested to relieve it from the responsibility of Joint certification along with DISCOM/GETCO in case of small projects injecting power below 66 KV level as the number of such projects are significant and GEDA has scarcity of manpower. To reduce the time period of commissioning of Solar Power Projects, it is requested to consider for Joint inspection with GEDA only in case where injection of energy is at 66 KV level or above.

2.12.2 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission notes that GEDA is the Nodal Agency for development of RE projects in the State. The Commission is of the view that the role of GEDA as third party Government Agency in verification of commissioning of RE projects cannot be undermined. Therefore, the Commission decides not to make any changes in this regard.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: ALMM Certificate requirements; BIS Indian Standards on Renewable Energy
