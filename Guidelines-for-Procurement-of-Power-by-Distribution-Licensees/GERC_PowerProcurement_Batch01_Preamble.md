DOCUMENT: GERC Guidelines for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees

DOCUMENT TYPE: Notification / Guideline / Regulatory Order

HIERARCHY: Preamble

PROVISION: Preamble

TITLE: Statutory Authority and Objective of Power Procurement Guidelines

SUMMARY:
The Preamble to GERC Notification No. 2 of 2013 establishes the statutory foundation for the Guidelines for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees. It references Section 86(1)(b) of the Electricity Act, 2003, which empowers the State Commission to regulate the electricity purchase and procurement process of distribution licensees, including the price at which electricity is procured from generating companies, licensees, or other sources. The guidelines are framed to ensure standardized, transparent, and economically efficient procurement of power in accordance with the Commission's regulatory mandate.

KEY TOPICS: Electricity Act 2003, Section 86, Power Procurement, Distribution Licensee, Procurement Process, Tariff Regulation, State Commission Authority, GERC, Transparent Procurement, Economic Procurement

TEXT:

**Preamble**

Section 86 (1) (b) of the Electricity Act, 2003, envisages that the State Commission shall regulate electricity purchase and procurement process of distribution licensees including the price at which electricity shall be procured from the generating companies or licensees or from other sources through agreements for purchase of power for distribution and supply. In fulfilment of duty caste upon the Commission as above, the Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission frames these guidelines for procurement of power by the Distribution Licensees in the State of Gujarat.

**Statutory Duty and Context**

1. Sections 42 and 43 of the Electricity Act, 2003, provide that it is the duty of Distribution Licensee to ensure electricity supply to the consumers within the licensed area of supply on request. The power purchase cost is the major cost element in the aggregate revenue requirement of Distribution Licensees.

2. In order to ensure standardization and reduce subjectivity in power procurement and to protect consumers' interest through a process of transparent and economic procurement of power, the Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (GERC) hereby directs all Distribution Licensees in the State to follow the procedure laid down in these Guidelines for procurement of power.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 86(1)(b) of Electricity Act 2003, Section 42 of Electricity Act 2003, Section 43 of Electricity Act 2003, GERC Notification No. 2 of 2013

CROSS-REFERENCES: Power Purchase Agreements (PPA) referenced throughout the Guidelines, Tariff Determination Processes under GERC authority, Distribution Codes and Supply Codes, Competitive Bidding Guidelines (Ministry of Power referenced in subsequent provisions)

PROVISION: Item 3

TITLE: Annual Power Procurement Plan Submission and 5-Year Forecast Requirements

SUMMARY:
Item 3 mandates that Distribution Licensees submit an annual power procurement plan by 31st January each year covering a 5-year horizon. The plan must include: (a) peak load and energy forecasts for their licensed area of supply for each of the successive 10 years, prepared for the overall Area of Supply; (b) anticipated power supply position for each of the successive five years with projections for additional power procurement requirements; and (c) hourly load duration projections for each of the successive 5 years. These forecasts form the basis for determining power procurement strategy and identifying whether additional power procurement is required.

KEY TOPICS: Power Procurement Plan, Annual Submission, Distribution Licensee, Forecasting, Peak Load Forecast, Energy Forecast, Load Duration Projection, 5-Year Plan, 10-Year Projection, Procurement Strategy

TEXT:

**Item 3 – Annual Power Procurement Plan Submission**

Every year by 31st January, the Distribution licensees shall submit power procurement plan for 5 years which will include:

a. Peak load and energy forecasts of their respective license areas for each of the successive 10 years. The peak load and energy forecasts shall be made for the overall Area of Supply.

b. Anticipated power supply position for each of the successive five years along with the projections for additional requirement of procurement of power, if any.

c. Hourly load duration projection for each of the successive 5 years.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Electricity Act 2003 (general regulatory framework), GERC's broader tariff determination framework

CROSS-REFERENCES: Related to Item 4 and Item 5 (long-term and medium-term tie-up requirements), which are based on these forecasts; Item 6 (competitive bidding process), which is initiated based on identified procurement requirements

PROVISION: Item 4

TITLE: Long-Term and Medium-Term Power Tie-Up Requirements for Fifth Year

SUMMARY:
Item 4 establishes a mandatory requirement that Distribution Licensees maintain long-term or medium-term power tie-up arrangements to meet at least 75% of the load requirement for the duration of the fifth year. If the Distribution Licensee has a shortfall in meeting 75% of the fifth-year load requirement through existing long-term or medium-term arrangements, the licensee must initiate the process of long-term procurement of power to bridge the gap. This provision ensures predictable power availability and reduces reliance on short-term purchases.

KEY TOPICS: Long-term Procurement, Medium-term Procurement, Power Tie-Up, Load Requirement, Fifth Year Projection, 75% Requirement, Procurement Initiation, Power Security

TEXT:

**Item 4 – Long-Term and Medium-Term Tie-Up for Fifth Year (75% Requirement)**

Distribution Licensees shall have long-term / medium-term tie up to meet load requirement of at least 75% duration of the fifth year. In case of any shortfall to meet load requirement of 75% of duration of the fifth year through long-term / medium-term arrangement, the Distribution Licensee shall initiate the process of long-term procurement of power.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Item 3 (Annual Power Procurement Plan Submission), Item 5 (Third Year Requirements), Item 6 (Competitive Bidding Process), Item 7 (Long-term/Medium-term Procurement through Competitive Bidding)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Related to long-term power security planning; Item 5 establishes a more stringent 85% requirement for the third year; Item 6 and Item 7 outline the competitive bidding process that must be initiated when shortfalls are identified

HIERARCHY: Guideline Provision

PROVISION: Item 5

TITLE: Long-Term and Medium-Term Power Tie-Up Requirements for Third Year

SUMMARY:
Item 5 establishes a more stringent mandatory requirement than Item 4, requiring Distribution Licensees to maintain long-term or medium-term power tie-up arrangements to meet at least 85% of the load requirement for the duration of the third year. If the licensee has a shortfall in meeting this 85% threshold through existing long-term or medium-term arrangements, the licensee must initiate the process of medium-term procurement of power. This escalating requirement (75% for fifth year, 85% for third year) ensures greater power security in the nearer term.

KEY TOPICS: Long-term Procurement, Medium-term Procurement, Power Tie-Up, Load Requirement, Third Year Projection, 85% Requirement, Procurement Initiation, Escalating Coverage Requirements

TEXT:

**Item 5 – Long-Term and Medium-Term Tie-Up for Third Year (85% Requirement)**

Distribution Licensees shall have long-term / medium-term tie up to meet load requirement of at least 85% of duration of the third year. In case of any shortfall to meet load requirement of 85% of duration of the third year through long-term / medium-term arrangement, the Distribution Licensee shall initiate the process of medium-term procurement of power.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Item 3 (Annual Power Procurement Plan Submission), Item 4 (Fifth Year Requirements), Item 6 (Competitive Bidding Process), Item 14 (Short-term Competitive Bidding)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Related to progressive power security planning; Item 4 establishes a less stringent 75% requirement for the fifth year; Item 6 and Item 14 outline the competitive bidding processes for medium-term and short-term procurement gaps
