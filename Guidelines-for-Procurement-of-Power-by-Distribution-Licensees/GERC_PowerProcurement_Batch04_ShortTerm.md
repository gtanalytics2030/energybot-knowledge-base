DOCUMENT: GERC Guidelines for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees

DOCUMENT TYPE: Notification / Guideline / Regulatory Order

HIERARCHY: Guideline Provision

PROVISION: Item 13

TITLE: Short-Term Power Procurement Arrangements Through Transparent Competitive Processes

SUMMARY:
Item 13 authorizes Distribution Licensees to enter into short-term power purchase arrangements when: (a) they need to procure power on a short-term basis without long-term commitments; (b) there is a shortfall due to any reason whatsoever in meeting projected requirements; or (c) there is a failure in the supply of electricity from any approved source of supply during the year for any reason. In such cases, licensees may procure through power exchanges or through a transparent process of open tendering and competitive bidding. This provision provides flexibility for demand variability and supply disruptions while maintaining commitment to competitive processes.

KEY TOPICS: Short-term Procurement, Power Shortfall, Supply Failure, Power Exchanges, Competitive Bidding, Open Tendering, Transparent Process, Distribution Licensee Flexibility, Short-term Agreement

TEXT:

**Item 13 – Short-Term Power Procurement – General Authority**

Where the Distribution Licensee is to procure power on a short-term basis or there is a shortfall due to any reason whatsoever, or failure in the supply of electricity from any approved source of supply during the year, for any reason whatsoever, the licensee may enter into a short-term arrangement or agreement for procurement of power through power exchanges or through a transparent process of open tendering and competitive bidding.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Item 14 (Competitive Bidding for Short-term Procurement), Item 15 (Short-term Bid Process), Item 6 (Competitive Bidding Standard)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Item 4 and Item 5 (long-term/medium-term tie-up requirements that may create shortfalls); Item 12 (PPA Submission); Related to power exchange operations and spot market procurement; Ministry of Power Guidelines for short-term procurement (referenced in Item 14)

PROVISION: Item 14

TITLE: Short-Term Competitive Bidding Process – Ministry of Power Guidelines and GERC Deviation Approval

SUMMARY:
Item 14 specifies that Distribution Licensees implementing short-term power procurement through competitive bidding must follow the Ministry of Power's 'Guidelines for Short-Term Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees through Tariff based bidding process' (notified 15/05/2012). The provision establishes three-tier approval requirements: (a) GERC approval of bid documentation unless using Central Government standard documents; (b) generally no deviations allowed without prior GERC approval, though essential deviations can be approved by GERC; (c) Procurers must follow GERC-approved annual short-term procurement plans with notification to GERC; and (d) any short-term procurement exceeding the approved annual plan requires prior GERC approval. This provision adapts the MoP framework to GERC oversight.

KEY TOPICS: Short-term Procurement, Competitive Bidding, Tariff-based Bidding, Ministry of Power Guidelines, Bid Documentation, GERC Approval, Deviation Approval, Procurement Plan, Annual Procurement Limit, Excess Procurement

TEXT:

**Item 14 – Short-Term Competitive Bidding Process**

In case of procurement of power through competitive bidding, the Distribution Licensees shall initiate the process for short-term power procurement in accordance with the Ministry of Power's 'Guidelines for Short-Term Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees through Tariff based bidding process' notified by the Ministry of Power on 15/05/2012 and in force from time to time. As provided in the MoP Guidelines:

a. The bid documentation shall be prepared in accordance with the MoP guidelines and the approval of the GERC shall be obtained unless the bid documents are as per the standard bid documents issued by the Central Government.

b. Generally no deviation shall be allowed from these Guidelines. However, if it is essential to have the deviation from these Guidelines, the same could be done with the prior approval of the GERC.

c. The Procurer(s) shall procure short-term power as per the plan approved by the GERC or appropriate body as may have been constituted for the purpose by the GERC. In such case the Distribution Licensees will intimate about the initiation of the procurement process to the GERC.

d. In case procurement of short-term power exceeds the approved annual short-term procurement plan, the Procurer(s) shall obtain prior approval from the GERC or any appropriate body as may have been constituted for the purpose by the GERC.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Ministry of Power Guidelines dated 15/05/2012 for Short-Term Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees through Tariff based bidding process, Item 13 (Short-term Procurement Authority), Item 15 (Short-term Bid Process), Item 7 (Long-term/Medium-term Competitive Bidding)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Central Government standard bid documents; GERC's Annual Procurement Plan approval process; Item 7 mirrors structure for long-term/medium-term bidding; Short-term power exchanges operations; Ministry of Power Guidelines supersede or work in conjunction with GERC authority

PROVISION: Item 15

TITLE: Short-Term Bid Process – Single-Stage RFP and Publication Requirements

SUMMARY:
Item 15 specifies that for short-term power procurement, Distribution Licensees (Procurers) shall employ a single-stage bidding process by inviting Request for Proposal (RFP). The Procurer or authorized representative must prepare the bid document in accordance with Item 14 guidelines. The RFP notice must be published in at least two national newspapers and uploaded on the company website to ensure wide publicity. Bidding must necessarily follow a competitive bidding process. This provision emphasizes transparency and accessibility for potential suppliers while maintaining a streamlined single-stage evaluation process appropriate for short-term transactions.

KEY TOPICS: Short-term Procurement, Request for Proposal, RFP, Single-stage Bidding, Bid Process, Competitive Bidding, Public Notice, National Newspapers, Company Website, Procurement Publicity

TEXT:

**Item 15 – Short-Term Bid Process – Single-Stage RFP and Publicity**

For procurement on short-term basis, a single-stage bid process by inviting Request for Proposal (RFP) shall be adopted. Procurer or authorized representative shall prepare bid document in line with these Guidelines.

The Procurer shall publish an RFP notice in at least two national newspapers and upload the same on company website to accord it wide publicity. The bidding shall necessarily be by way of competitive bidding.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Item 13 (Short-term Procurement Authority), Item 14 (Competitive Bidding Guidelines), Item 16 (PPA Signing Following Bid Process)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Ministry of Power Guidelines for Short-Term Procurement (Item 14); Related to public notification and transparency principles in power procurement; Item 10 establishes similar public notice requirements for draft PPA approval (but using newspapers and Secretary GERC rather than company website)

DOCUMENT: GERC Guidelines for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees

DOCUMENT TYPE: Notification / Guideline / Regulatory Order

HIERARCHY: Guideline Provision

PROVISION: Item 16

TITLE: PPA Signing, Bid Conformity Certification, and Tariff Adoption Process

SUMMARY:
Item 16 outlines the post-bid process for short-term power procurement. After concluding the bid evaluation process, the Standing Committee evaluating RFP bids must provide a certificate confirming conformity of the bid process evaluation to RFP document provisions. The Procurer must similarly certify conformity of the bid process to the Guidelines to GERC. The selected bidder is then awarded the PPA. If the procured quantum and tariff fall within GERC's approval in the Distribution Licensee's Annual Revenue Requirement (ARR), the tariff is considered adopted by GERC. In all other cases, the Procurer must petition GERC for tariff adoption within 2 days of PPA signing, and GERC must communicate its decision within 7 days of submission. This provision establishes ex-post tariff adoption procedures for short-term procurement within ARR limits.

KEY TOPICS: Power Purchase Agreement, PPA Signing, Tariff Adoption, ARR, Annual Revenue Requirement, Bid Evaluation, Standing Committee, Conformity Certificate, GERC Petition, Tariff Determination

TEXT:

**Item 16 – PPA Signing and Tariff Adoption Procedure**

The PPA shall be signed with the selected bidder(s) consequent to the selection process.

a. After the conclusion of bid process, the Standing Committee constituted for evaluation of RFP bids shall provide appropriate certificate on conformity of the bid process evaluation to the provisions of the RFP document. The Procurer shall provide a certificate on the conformity of the bid process to these Guidelines to the GERC.

b. If the quantum of power procured and tariff determined are within the overall approval granted by the GERC in Annual Revenue Requirement (ARR) of the respective year, then the same will be considered to have been adopted by the GERC.

In all other cases, the Procurer(s) shall submit a petition to the GERC for adoption of tariff within two days from the date of signing of PPA. The GERC should communicate the decision within seven days from the date of submission of petition.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Item 14 (Competitive Bidding Guidelines), Item 15 (RFP Process), Item 13 (Short-term Procurement), GERC's Tariff Determination framework, ARR (Annual Revenue Requirement) approval process

CROSS-REFERENCES: Relates to GERC's ARR proceedings for Distribution Licensees; Standing Committee procedures; Item 10 addresses similar approval procedures for draft PPAs but with 90-day timeframe; Short-term tariff adoption is expedited (2-7 days) compared to long-term process (90 days in Item 10)
