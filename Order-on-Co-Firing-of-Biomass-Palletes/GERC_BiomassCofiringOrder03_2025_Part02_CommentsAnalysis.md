## CHUNK 1: Section 2.1 - Non-Compliance of Biomass Policy Provisions

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 03 of 2025 - Methodology for Co-firing of Biomass Pellets and Recovery of Its Costs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Order - Decision Analysis

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Section 2.1

**PROVISION:** Section 2.1 - Non-Compliance of Provisions Specified in Policy for Biomass Utilization in Coal Based Thermal Power Plant

**TITLE:** Commission's Ruling on Non-Compliance Consequences for Co-firing Obligations

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission addressed the stakeholder (GUVNL) submission regarding consequences of non-compliance with the MoP Policy's mandatory co-firing obligations. The Biomass Policy requires all coal-based thermal power plants to blend 5% biomass pellets (increasing to 7% after two years), with specific requirements based on boiler mill type: bowl mill plants must use non-torrefied pellets, while bowl & race mill and bowl & tube mill plants must use torrefied biomass pellets. GUVNL contended that any consequences for non-compliance should be solely the responsibility of generators and not passed through to beneficiaries. The Commission agreed, clarifying that the obligation to utilize specified percentages of biomass pellets is the responsibility of thermal generators, and therefore procuring entities (beneficiaries) cannot be held responsible for generator non-compliance.

**KEY TOPICS:** Biomass Blending Obligation, Non-Compliance Consequences, MoP Policy, Mandatory Co-firing, Coal-based Thermal Power Plants, Beneficiary Responsibility, Generator Obligation, Five Percent Blend, Seven Percent Obligation, Bowl Mill, Bowl & Race Mill, Bowl & Tube Mill, Torrefied Biomass, Non-torrefied Biomass

**TEXT:**
2.1 Clause 2.1 (i to iii) Non-Compliance of provisions specified in Policy for Biomass utilization in coal based thermal power plant

2.1.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Commission in the Approach paper under Clause 2.1 (i to iii) outlined the salient features of the revised MoP Policy for utilization of biomass in coal based thermal power plant dated 08.10.2021 with regard to type of material and blending percentage of biomass pellets as given below:

(i) All coal based thermal power plants of power generation utilities with bowl mill, shall on annual basis mandatorily use 5 per cent blend of biomass pellets made, primarily, of agro residue along with coal with effect from one year of the date of issue of this guideline. The obligation shall increase to 7 percent with effect from two years after the date of issue of this order and thereafter.

(ii) All coal based thermal power plants generation utilities with bowl & race mill, shall on annual basis mandatorily use 5% blend of biomass pellets (torrefied only) made, primarily, of agro residue along with coal. This is to be complied with within one year starting from this order. Two years from the date of issue of this order and thereafter the obligation will increase to 7 percent.

(iii) All coal based thermal power plants of power generation utilities with bowl & tube mills, shall on annual basis mandatorily use 5 % blend of torrefied biomass pellets with volatile content below 22% primarily made of agro residue along with coal. This is to be complied within one year.

2.1.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

GUVNL submitted that any consequences towards non-compliance of the co-firing of biomass pellets by generating stations should be solely to the account of the generators and should not be a pass through to the beneficiaries

2.1.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission noted the submission of GUVNL. It is clarified that the obligation of utilizing specified percentage of biomass pellet along with coal as specified in Biomass Policy is the responsibility of thermal generators and hence the procuring entities (beneficiaries) cannot be held responsible for non-compliance on part of thermal generators.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Ministry of Power Biomass Policy dated 08.10.2021; Ministry of Power Revised Policy dated 08.10.2021; Ministry of Power Addendum dated 03.05.2023 and 16.06.2023

## CHUNK 2: Section 2.2 - Technical Feasibility Study and Capex Treatment

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 03 of 2025 - Methodology for Co-firing of Biomass Pellets and Recovery of Its Costs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Order - Decision Analysis

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Section 2.2

**PROVISION:** Section 2.2 - Technical Feasibility Study by Generators and Capex Requirement

**TITLE:** Commission's Ruling on Technical Feasibility Study Costs and Capital Expenditure Treatment

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission addressed cost responsibility for technical feasibility studies and capital expenditure required for biomass co-firing compliance. The Approach Paper proposed that generators consult with Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEM) regarding technical feasibility and operational impact before conducting feasibility studies per the Biomass Policy, with OEM reports and feasibility studies shared with beneficiaries, and study costs borne by beneficiaries. For capital expenditure under Section 62 of Electricity Act (regulated generators), capex would be treated as additional capitalization subject to prudent check during true-up. For Section 63 generators (merchant power), capex may be allowed as one-time reimbursement or annuity stream. GUVNL requested that study costs and capex be discovered through competitive bidding. The Commission agreed that generators must conduct feasibility studies through reputed consultants at competitively bid rates and share information with beneficiaries, but declined to fix benchmark prices due to varied scope, technologies, and plant life-ages.

**KEY TOPICS:** Technical Feasibility Study, Capex, Cost Recovery, OEM Consultation, Competitive Bidding, Additional Capitalization, True-up, Section 62, Section 63, Electricity Act 2003, Prudent Check, One-time Reimbursement, Annuity Stream, Generator Responsibility

**TEXT:**
2.2 Clause 3 (h) and (i to k) - Technical feasibility study by generators / Requirement of Capex

The Commission in clause 3(h) of the approach paper provided that the generating Power Plants shall consult the OEM regarding technical feasibility and impact on operation parameters before undertaking the technical feasibility study in terms of biomass Policy as stated above. The report of OEM and technical feasibility study shall be shared with the beneficiary for their comments and arriving at consensus. The cost of the study shall be borne by the beneficiary (ies).

The Commission under clause 3 (i to k) specified the treatment of Capex infused by the thermal generating stations under Section 62 and Section 63 of the Act, under commercial operation as on date of MoP notification dated 08.10.2021 for Biomass Utilization for Power Generation through Co-firing in Coal Based Power Plants.

The Commission specified that for thermal generating plant under section 62 of the Act, the capex would be treated as additional capitalization, subject to prudent check by the Commission at the time of True-up of the relevant control period. For thermal Generating Stations, under section 63 of the Act, under commercial operation as on date of MOP notification dated 08.10.2021, the capex, subject to applicable provisions of the PPA, may be allowed as one-time reimbursement or annuity stream over a period as may be determined by the Commission.

2.2.1 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

GUVNL submitted that the cost of technical feasibility study and CAPEX requirement towards compliance of co-firing of biomass pellets in thermal power plants should be reasonable and discovered through competitive bidding by the generators in a fair and a transparent manner. Further, the Commission is requested to fix the benchmark price for the same.

2.2.2 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission noted the suggestions of GUVNL. The generator supplying power to distribution licensees under Section 62 or 63 of the Electricity Act, 2003, shall conduct the technical feasibility study and ascertain the CAPEX requirement through reputed consultant at rate discovered through transparent competitive bidding process and share the information with the beneficiaries. The Commission feel that fixing the benchmark price at this stage does not seem advisable considering the varied nature of scope, different technologies and different life-age of the power plants.

## CHUNK 3: Section 2.3 - Renewable Purchase Obligation and RPO Compliance

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 03 of 2025 - Methodology for Co-firing of Biomass Pellets and Recovery of Its Costs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Order - Decision Analysis

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Section 2.3

**PROVISION:** Section 2.3 - Renewable Purchase Obligation and Its Compliance

**TITLE:** Commission's Ruling on RPO Compliance for Biomass Co-firing Generation

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission addressed stakeholder submissions regarding whether energy generated from biomass co-firing can be counted toward Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO) compliance. The MoP Policy allows obligated entities (DISCOMs) to meet RPO by purchasing electricity from biomass co-firing. Abellon Clean Energy suggested allowing thermal power plants to purchase power from standalone Waste-to-Energy (WTE) plants instead of co-firing, arguing both achieve the same objective of biomass fuel disposal. Abellon also requested that thermal power plants be allowed to purchase biomass power rather than co-fire. GUVNL requested clarification that electricity supplied by generators using biomass to distribution licensees should count toward the licensee's RPO compliance. The Commission noted that the present proceedings concern biomass utilization in coal-based thermal plants, not standalone WTE plants. The Commission confirmed the MNRE clarification of 26 September 2019 that power generated from co-firing biomass in coal-based thermal plants is renewable energy and is eligible for non-solar RPO. RPO framework and procurement of electricity from standalone biomass/MSW plants are governed by respective tariff orders issued by the Commission.

**KEY TOPICS:** Renewable Purchase Obligation, RPO Compliance, Biomass Co-firing, Renewable Energy, Non-solar RPO, MNRE Clarification, DISCOMs, Obligated Entities, Waste-to-Energy, Biomass Power Plants, GERC Regulations 2010, Tariff Orders

**TEXT:**
2.3 Clause 2.1 (vii) (d): Renewable Purchase Obligation and its Compliance

2.3.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Commission in the Approach Paper referred the provision of Clause 2.1 (viii)(d) of the revised policy for utilization of biomass in coal based thermal power plant dated 08.10.2021 which states that the Obligated Entities such as Discoms can meet their Renewable Purchase Obligations (RPO) by buying such generation of co-firing.

2.3.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

M/s Abellon Clean Energy (Abellon) submitted that thermal power plant developer can buy power from the existing Waste to Energy (WTE) power plants. There may be existing WTE plants having no valid PPAs and new PPA will take time to be signed. If Thermal Developers buy Power from existing WTE Power plants, the consumption would be considered as part of Renewable Purchase Obligation of Thermal power plant Developers, and they do not need to co-fire that much Biomass in their Coal based power plant.

M/s Abellon further submitted that for the purpose of RPO compliance instead of co-blending of Biomass Pellets in thermal power plant. Thermal Power Plant to be allowed to purchase Biomass Power and fulfil their RPO obligation. The objector further submitted that since, the objective of the Biomass policy is to dispose Biomass fuel, either co-firing the Biomass fuel or purchasing energy from Biomass plants the same objective is being achieved.

GUVNL pointed out that in the Approach paper, it has been stated that the Generators using biomass pellets for co-firing shall approach the Commission on completion of each financial year with a petition for claiming RPO compliance. GUVNL requested to specify that in cases of supply of electricity by such Generators to Distribution Licensee, the same shall be construed towards the RPO Compliance of Distribution Licensee.

2.3.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission notes that the present proceedings are related to utilization of biomass in coal based thermal power plants' and not with respect to purchase of power from standalone Waste to Energy plants. The MNRE vide letter dated 26.9.2019 clarified that the power generated from co-firing of biomass in coal based thermal power plants is renewable energy and is eligible for meeting non-solar RPO. The Obligated Entities under the RPO framework has been defined by the Commission in GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Energy Sources) Regulations 2010 and the subsequent amendments thereof. The procurement of electricity from standalone Biomass / Waste to Energy based power projects by distribution licensee and other shall governed by the provisions under the respective Biomass / MSW Tariff order issued by the Commission from time to time.

## CHUNK 4: Section 2.4 - Pricing of Biomass Pellets

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 03 of 2025 - Methodology for Co-firing of Biomass Pellets and Recovery of Its Costs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Order - Decision Analysis

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Section 2.4

**PROVISION:** Section 2.4 - Pricing of Biomass Pellet

**TITLE:** Commission's Ruling on Biomass Pellet Price Benchmarking Mechanism

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission addressed biomass pellet pricing mechanism as specified in the revised MoP Policy dated 03.05.2023. The policy requires region-wise price benchmarking conducted by a Committee constituted under CEA leadership for a five-year period with provision for periodic escalation. The benchmark price does not include transportation cost from pellet manufacturing to thermal power plants; TPPs determine transportation separately in a fair and transparent manner. Thermal power plants must procure benchmark-priced pellets on a first-come-first-served basis. If TPPs cannot procure at benchmark price, they may conduct short-term tenders, ensuring fair and transparent discovery and comparability to benchmarked prices. Recommendations of the price benchmarking committee became effective from 1 January 2024. Meanwhile, power utilities were directed to conduct short-term tenders for immediate biomass requirements. Abellon objected, arguing that pellet manufacturing costs are higher than biomass fuel costs and suggesting that only biomass costs be considered, excluding manufacturing costs. The Commission retained provisions for using benchmark costs recommended by the CEA-led Committee for computing landed cost of biomass pellets at thermal power plants, noting that both Non-Torrefied and Torrefied biomass pellets are allowed under MoP Policy.

**KEY TOPICS:** Biomass Pellet Price, Benchmarking, CEA Committee, Landed Cost, Transportation Cost, First-come-first-served, Short-term Tender, Region-wise Pricing, Escalation, Manufacturing Cost, Non-Torrefied Pellets, Torrefied Pellets, Fair and Transparent Pricing

**TEXT:**
2.4 Clause 2.3 (iii): Pricing of Biomass Pellet

2.4.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Commission in the Approach Paper referred the Clause 2.3 (iii) of the revised MoP Policy dated 03.05.2023 for Biomass utilization in coal based thermal power Plant, wherein it is stated as under:

The price benchmarking would be carried out region-wise by a Committee constituted under the leadership of CEA for a period of 5 years with provision for periodic escalation. The benchmark price will not include the transportation cost from pellet manufacturing unit to the TPPs. TPPs shall determine the transportation cost from pellets manufacturing units to TPPs in a fair and transparent manner. The procurement of benchmark priced pellets shall be carried out on a first come, first serve basis. If TPPs are unable to procure biomass pellets at the benchmark price, they have the option to go for a short-term tender. However, it is important for TPPs to ensure that the price discovered in the short-term tender is determined in a fair and transparent manner. The price discovered should also be comparable to the benchmarked prices. The recommendation of price benchmarking committee will be effective from 1.1.2024. Meanwhile, the power utilities shall go for short-term tenders for meeting the immediate requirement of biomass pellets for their TPPs.

2.4.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

M/s Abellon submitted that the Biomass pellets manufacturing cost is higher as compared to using Biomass fuel itself. Therefore, while considering the Biomass fuel cost, the pellets manufacturing cost may be removed and only Biomass cost is to be considered.

2.4.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission notes that as per the MoP policy for Biomass utilization in coal based thermal power plant both the Non-Torrefied/ Torrefied biomass pellets are allowed to use for co-firing in Coal Based Thermal Power Plants. The Commission decides to retain provisions related to the benchmark cost for biomass pellet recommended by the Committee constituted under the leadership of CEA for computing the landed cost of Biomass pellet at location of Thermal power plant.

## CHUNK 5: Section 2.5 - Type of Biomass Pellet and Volatile Content

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 03 of 2025 - Methodology for Co-firing of Biomass Pellets and Recovery of Its Costs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Order - Decision Analysis

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Section 2.5

**PROVISION:** Section 2.5 - Type of Biomass Pellet and Volatile Content

**TITLE:** Commission's Ruling on Biomass Pellet Type Specifications and Volatile Content Limits

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission addressed specifications for types of biomass pellets based on boiler mill design in thermal power plants. The MoP Revised Policy dated 03.05.2023 specifies that coal-based thermal power plants with bowl & tube mills shall mandatorily use 5% blend of torrefied biomass pellets with volatile content below 22%, primarily made of agro residue, to be complied within one year. Reliance Industries Limited (RIL) objected, asserting that non-torrefied briquettes/pellets are abundantly available near Gujarat while torrefied biomass availability is limited, and therefore the requirement should not apply to Gujarat plants. RIL also submitted that based on operational experience, volatile content of biomass always remains higher, making a 22% ceiling difficult to meet, and requested removal of the volatile content limit. The Commission noted that the Revised MoP Policy recommends using Non-torrefied and torrefied briquettes/pellets depending on pulverizer design: thermal plants with bowl mills shall use non-torrefied biomass, while those with bowl and race mills or bowl and tube mills shall use torrefied biomass. The Commission found that limiting volatile material in biomass fuel is important to avoid emissions from thermal plants and decided to retain the provisions related to biomass pellet type and volatile content as specified in the revised MoP Biomass Policy.

**KEY TOPICS:** Torrefied Biomass, Non-torrefied Biomass, Volatile Content, Boiler Mill Type, Bowl Mill, Bowl & Race Mill, Bowl & Tube Mill, Pulverizer Design, Agro Residue, Emissions Control, Technical Feasibility, 22% Volatile Limit, Biomass Pellet Specifications

**TEXT:**
2.5 Clause 2.1(iii): Type of Biomass Pellet and volatile content

2.5.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Commission in the Approach Paper referred to the provisions of Clause 2.1 (iii) under MoP revised Policy dated 03.05.2023 for Biomass utilization in coal based thermal power plant under, wherein it is stated that all coal based thermal power plants of power generation utilities with bowl & tube mills, shall on annual basis mandatorily use 5 % blend of torrefied biomass pellets with volatile content below 22% primarily made of Agro residue along with coal. This is to be complied within one year.

2.5.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

M/s Reliance Industries Limited (RIL) submitted that Non-torrified briquettes/pellets are available in abundance in areas near by Gujarat whereas availability of torrified biomass is limited and hence, this requirement should not be applicable for power generation plants in Gujarat. RIL submitted that based on their own operational experience volatile content of Biomass always remains higher. Hence it will be difficult to meet any such volatile content ceiling therefore limit of 22% should be removed.

2.5.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission notes that in the Revised Policy for Biomass utilization in coal based thermal power plant issued by MoP, it is recommended to use of Non-torrified and torrified briquettes/pellets depending on the design of pulverizer in a thermal power plant viz : thermal power plants' having bowl mill shall use non-torrefied biomass pellets, whereas thermal power plant having bowl and race mill and bowl and tube mill shall use torrified biomass pellets, limiting the volatile material in a biomass fuel within specified limit is important to avoid the emission from the thermal plant.

In view of above, the Commission decides to retain the provision related to type of biomass pellet and volatile content as specified in the revised Biomass Policy issued by MoP.

**TITLE:** Commission's Ruling on Seven-Year Minimum Contract Period for Biomass Supply

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission addressed the minimum contract period for procuring biomass pellets by generating utilities as specified in the MoP Revised Policy dated 03.05.2023. The policy requires a minimum seven-year contract period to avoid delays in awarding contracts annually and to build up long-term sustainable supply chains. The policy provides for firm pricing for the first contract year, with yearly rate variation from the second year onwards based on contract terms. The Commission agreed that the seven-year minimum period is a conscious policy decision of MoP intended to maintain fuel supply chains and enable long-term sustainable operation of biomass co-firing thermal plants, and decided to retain this provision as per the revised MoP Biomass Policy.

## CHUNK 7: Section 2.7 - Methodology for Electricity Estimation from Biomass

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 03 of 2025 - Methodology for Co-firing of Biomass Pellets and Recovery of Its Costs

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulatory Order - Decision Analysis

**HIERARCHY:** Chapter 2 / Section 2.7

**PROVISION:** Section 2.7 - Methodology for Estimation of Electricity Generated from Biomass Co-fired Coal Based Thermal Power Plants

**TITLE:** Commission's Ruling on Electricity Estimation Methodology for Conventional and Cogeneration Plants

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission addressed the methodology for estimating electricity generated from biomass in biomass co-fired coal-based thermal power plants, including captive and co-generation plants. The MoP Revised Policy specifies a two-step formula: Step-1 estimates electricity generated at generator terminal based on the ratio of biomass heat input (Qb×Gb) to total heat input (coal and biomass), then multiplies by gross electrical energy generated at generator terminal. Step-2 adjusts for transmission and auxiliary losses to calculate ex-bus electrical energy. Reliance Industries objected, arguing the methodology does not differentiate between conventional plants and cogeneration plants. RIL noted that in cogeneration plants, part of steam generated from biomass is used for process requirements, and the present methodology does not capture heat used for process, thus giving less credit to power generation. RIL proposed an alternative methodology to account for steam used for process needs. The Commission analyzed the proposal and decided that the underlying principle is the proportion of heat input from biomass to total heat input for power generation (as specified in revised MoP Policy). This principle applies equally to conventional and cogeneration plants: in both cases, energy output is estimated proportionally to biomass heat input. The Commission decided not to modify the methodology for conventional thermal plants or cogeneration plants, as the principle of proportion applies uniformly.

**KEY TOPICS:** Electricity Estimation Methodology, Biomass Heat Input, GCV (Gross Calorific Value), Generator Terminal, Ex-bus Energy, Cogeneration Plants, Conventional Thermal Plants, Captive Power Plants, Heat Input Proportion, Transmission Losses, Auxiliary Losses, Step-1 Formula, Step-2 Formula

**TEXT:**
2.7 Clause 3.5: Methodology for estimation of electricity generated from biomass co-fired coal based Thermal Power Plants, including captive and co-generation power plants co-firing biomass.

2.7.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Commission referred to the Clause No 3.5 of the MoP revised Policy wherein the methodology is specified for estimation of electricity generated from biomass in biomass co-fired coal based Thermal Power Plants, including captive and co-generation power plants co-firing biomass as given below:

Step-1:
1. The electricity generated from Biomass shall be estimated at Generator Terminal on monthly basis in accordance with the following formulae:
Eb(G)=[(QbxGb)/((QcxGc)+(QbxGb))] xE(GT)

Where,
Eb(G) = Electrical energy generated by biomass at Generator terminal during the month (kWh);
Qb = Quantity of biomass consumed during the month (kg)
Gb = Weighted average Gross Calorific Value (GCV) of biomass consumed during month (kCal/kg)
E(GT) = Gross electrical energy generated at Generator Terminal during the month (kWh)
Qc = Quantity of coal burnt during the month (kg)
Gc = Weighted average GCV of coal burnt during the month (kCal/kg)

2. The product (Qb x Gb) represents heat (in Kcal) input through biomass during the month and shall be estimated on monthly basis by applying following formulae:
Qb x Gb (kCal) = {opening balance of biomass (kg) X weighted average GCV of opening balance of biomass (kCal/kg)} + {quantity of bio- mass received during the month (kg) X weighted average GCV of biomass received during the month (kcal/kg)} - {closing stock of biomass (kg) X weighted average GCV of the closing balance of biomass (kCal/kg)}

3. The product (Qc x Gc) represents heat (in Kcal) input through coal during the month (kcal) and shall be estimated on monthly basis by applying the following formulae:
Qc x Gc (kCal) = {opening balance of coal (kg)X weighed average GCV of opening balance of coal (kCal/kg)} + {quantity of coal received during the month (kg) X weighted average GCV of coal received during the month (kCal/kg)} – {closing stock of coal(kg) X weighted average GCV of the closing balance of coal (kCal/kg)}

Step-2:
1. The ex-bus electrical energy generated by using biomass shall be estimated on monthly basis by applying following formulae:
Eb (ex-bus) = Ebx (G){1-[(Ex(GT)-ESO)/Ex(GT)]}

Where,
Eb (ex-bus) = Electrical energy generated by biomass ex-bus during the month (kWh).
Eb(G) = Electrical energy generated by biomass at Generator terminal during the month arrived at Step-1(kWh)
E(GT) = Total electrical energy generated at generator terminal during the month (kWh)
ESO = Total Energy Sent Out(ex-bus) during the month (kWh)

2.7.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

M/s RIL submitted that the methodology proposed in the Approach Paper does not differentiate between conventional Plants and Cogeneration Plants. In case of Cogeneration plant, part of the steam generated from co-firing of Biomass is used for process requirement. The present methodology does not capture such heat of Biomass used for process and thus effectively gives less credit to Power.

The objector suggested to consider an alternate methodology for Estimation of Electricity Generated from Biomass, and electricity equivalent to the quantum of steam generated and used for process requirement.

2.7.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling

The Commission has gone through the methodology proposed by the objector for estimation of electricity generated from Biomass. The Commission notes that biomass pellets can be used in captive/cogeneration power plants similar to independent thermal power plant. The Commission is therefore of the view that the methodology for estimation of electrical energy generation from Biomass shall also be applicable to the captive/cogeneration power plant using co-firing of biomass pellet along with Coal.

With regard to contention of the objector that methodology proposed in the discussion paper does not differentiate between conventional Plants and Cogeneration Plants, the Commission is of the view that in case of thermal power plant/captive power plant, the entire heat generated from coal and biomass is used to generate power. Whereas, in case of co-generation plant, only part of the heat, is used to generate power. But the underlying principle remains the same i.e. the proportion of heat input from biomass to total heat input for power generation as specified in revised MoP Biomass Policy.

Principle of proportion shall be the basis to work out the energy generated from biomass. The energy output is estimated in proportion to the heat input from biomass out of total heat input from biomass and coal. Accordingly, the methodology specified in this order shall also be applicable for Captive as well as cogeneration power plant without any modification.

In view of above the Commission decides not to make any changes / modification in the Methodology for estimation of electricity generated from biomass co-fired coal based Thermal Power Plants.
