The preamble establishes the policy's foundation in India's climate action commitments outlined at the 26th Conference of the Parties (COP26). India's Prime Minister outlined five key targets under the "Panchamrit" pledge: achieving 500 GW of non-fossil energy capacity by 2030; meeting 50 percent of energy requirements from renewable energy by 2030; reducing projected carbon emissions by one billion tonnes from current levels until 2030; reducing carbon intensity of the economy by 45 percent by 2030 (over 2005 levels); and achieving Net Zero emissions by 2070. a) India will reach its non-fossil energy capacity to 500 GW by 2030.

b) India will meet 50 percent of its energy requirements from renewable energy by 2030.

c) India will reduce the total projected carbon emissions by one billion tonnes from now onwards till 2030.

d) India will reduce the carbon intensity of its economy by 45 percent by 2030, over 2005 levels.

e) India will achieve the target of Net Zero by 2070.

This strategic framework underlines India's leadership role in global climate action, a resilient future and its dedication to sustainable development.

## CHUNK 3: Preamble - Renewable Energy Integration Challenges and PSP Role

---
DOCUMENT: Gujarat Pumped Storage Project Policy 2025

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 1 / Preamble / Subsection 1.1

PROVISION: Preamble (Section 1.1)

TITLE: Pumped Storage Projects as Solutions to Renewable Energy Variability

SUMMARY:
This section establishes the rationale for pumped storage projects (PSPs) within India's renewable energy transition. It notes that India's shift to clean energy, driven by climate concerns, has created grid stability challenges due to the intermittent nature of renewable sources (wind and solar). Pumped storage projects are presented as proven, effective long-duration energy storage solutions. PSPs mitigate renewable energy variability while maintaining grid reliability, stability, and power system inertia. By storing energy when grid supply exceeds demand and discharging during peaks, PSPs advance global clean energy objectives while maintaining essential grid support functions.

KEY TOPICS: Renewable Energy Integration, Grid Stability, Pumped Storage Projects, Energy Variability, Energy Storage Solutions, Intermittency Management, Power System Inertia, Grid Reliability, Clean Energy Agenda, Renewable Penetration

TEXT:
Inspired by the "Panchamrit" pledge, India is undergoing a significant transition to clean energy. The growing awareness of rising average surface temperatures is inspiring policymakers and leaders to champion innovative, sustainable, and reliable energy solutions—driving progress toward enhanced energy security and greater environmental stewardship.

Amid this shift, integrating renewable energy (RE) sources such as wind and solar into the electricity grid has introduced challenges related to grid stability and resilience. To address these issues, Pumped Storage Projects (PSPs) have emerged as a proven and effective solution for large-scale energy storage. By mitigating the variability inherent in RE generation, PSPs play a crucial role in advancing the global clean energy agenda while maintaining the reliability, stability of the power systems through the discharge of stored energy and inertia as per system requirements i.e. generating clean energy when needed and utilizing the surplus RE when the grid is overwhelmed.

PROVISION: Preamble (Section 1.4)

TITLE: Energy Storage Systems and Load-Demand Balance Requirements

SUMMARY:
This section identifies energy storage systems as vital for addressing the balance between net load (load demand minus renewable supply) and conventional generation. PSPs address variability and intermittency challenges while supporting overall grid stability and conventional generation optimization within the state's energy portfolio.

PSP, being one of the most proven among presently available long-duration energy storage technologies, would therefore be the preferred choice to meet the stated requirement.

## CHUNK 7: Preamble - Ministry of Power Initiatives and Policy Framework

---
DOCUMENT: Gujarat Pumped Storage Project Policy 2025

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 1 / Preamble / Subsection 1.5

PROVISION: Preamble (Section 1.5)

TITLE: Central Government Measures for PSP Promotion and Development

SUMMARY:
This section documents the Ministry of Power's (MoP) measures to promote pumped storage project development at the national level. MoP has implemented several initiatives including: prescribing Energy Storage Obligation targets for utilities; waiving Inter State Transmission System (ISTS) charges; issuing guidelines promoting PSP development; and releasing Tariff Based Competitive Bidding (TBCB) guidelines for procurement of storage capacity and stored energy from PSPs. These central government actions provide the regulatory and financial framework within which state-level policies like Gujarat's PSP Policy must operate.

KEY TOPICS: Ministry of Power, Energy Storage Obligation, ISTS Charges Waiver, PSP Development Guidelines, Tariff Based Competitive Bidding, TBCB Guidelines, Storage Capacity Procurement, Energy Procurement, Central Policy Framework

TEXT:
To facilitate the growth of PSP in the country, Ministry of Power (MoP) has taken several measures such as, prescribing Energy Storage Obligation targets for utilities, waiver of Inter State Transmission System (ISTS) charges, notification of guidelines for promotion of development of PSPs and notification of Tariff Based Competitive Bidding (TBCB) guidelines for procurement of storage capacity/stored energy from PSPs.
