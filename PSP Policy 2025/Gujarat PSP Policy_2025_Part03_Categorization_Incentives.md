DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 7 / Categorization and Allotment

PROVISION: Section 7 - Introduction

TITLE: Four-Category Allotment Framework for PSP Sites

SUMMARY:
The policy establishes a four-category classification system for prospective PSP sites, each with distinct allocation mechanisms and utilization rights. All sites are evaluated against the categorization framework to determine which allocation pathway applies. The four categories are: Category I (nomination basis to CPSUs/SPSUs/JVs), Category II (competitive bidding for private developers/CPSUs/SPSUs), Category III (competitive bidding for private developers/CPSUs/SPSUs with open access), and Category IV (self-identified off-stream projects). This categorization framework provides flexibility in site allocation while maintaining strategic government oversight through Category I and II reservations.

## CHUNK 23: Category I - Nomination Basis Allotment

---
DOCUMENT: Gujarat Pumped Storage Project Policy 2025

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 7 / Categorization and Allotment

PROVISION: Section 7 - Category I

TITLE: Category I PSP Sites: Nomination-Based Allotment to Government Undertakings

SUMMARY:
Category I includes prospective PSP sites allocated on a nomination basis directly to Central Public Sector Undertakings (CPSUs), State Public Sector Undertakings (SPSUs), and Joint Ventures (JVs) formed between CPSUs and/or SPSUs. These sites are strategically reserved to serve state energy requirements and are exclusively for captive consumption by government entities. Nomination-basis allocation allows the government to direct development of key sites to established public sector infrastructure developers without competitive bidding processes. Category I sites represent the government's strategic reserve for state energy security.

KEY TOPICS: Category I, Nomination Basis, CPSU, SPSU, Joint Venture, Government Undertakings, Strategic Reserve, State Requirements, Exclusive Reservation, Public Sector Development

TEXT:
Category I on nomination basis: The sites shall be allotted to CPSUs / SPSUs / Joint Ventures (JVs) between CPSUs and/or SPSUs.

## CHUNK 24: Category II - Competitive Bidding for Government-Reserved Sites

---
DOCUMENT: Gujarat Pumped Storage Project Policy 2025

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 7 / Categorization and Allotment

PROVISION: Section 7 - Category II

TITLE: Category II PSP Sites: Competitive Bidding with State Reservation

SUMMARY:
Category II includes sites that may be awarded to private developers, CPSUs, or SPSUs through competitive bidding (TBCB mechanism). Sites in Category I and II are exclusively reserved to cater to state requirements and are not available for other purposes. The list of Category I and II sites is notified by the State Government from time to time, allowing flexibility in strategic resource allocation. These categories together form the government's reserved portfolio for state energy security and grid stability requirements, while Category II allows competition to optimize developer selection and tariff discovery.

## CHUNK 25: Category III - Competitive Bidding for Open Access Projects

---
DOCUMENT: Gujarat Pumped Storage Project Policy 2025

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 7 / Categorization and Allotment

PROVISION: Section 7 - Category III

TITLE: Category III PSP Sites: Open Access Projects for Competitive Bidding

SUMMARY:
Category III includes sites awarded to private developers, CPSUs, or SPSUs through competitive bidding, but with a critical difference from Categories I and II: these sites are NOT reserved exclusively for state requirements. Stored energy and capacity from Category III projects can be utilized for multiple purposes: captive consumption, third-party sales, open access consumer sales, power exchange sales, or DISCOM/utility sales. Category III represents the government's framework for private-sector-led PSP development with commercial freedom, while still maintaining state oversight through the bidding and approval process. The list of Category III sites is notified by the State Government.

KEY TOPICS: Category III, Competitive Bidding, Open Access, Private Development, Commercial Use, Captive Consumption, Third-Party Sales, Power Exchange, Utility Sales, Flexible Utilization, Commercial Freedom

TEXT:
Category III: Sites may be awarded to private developers, CPSUs/SPSUs through competitive bidding / TBCB. These sites are not covered under Category I & II. The stored energy/capacity of such projects can be utilised for captive consumption, third-party or open access consumers, power exchange sales, or for DISCOMs / Utilities, etc and not reserved exclusively for the requirements of the State. The list of these sites will also be notified by the State Government from time to time.

## CHUNK 26: Category IV - Self-Identified Off-Stream Closed Loop Projects

---
DOCUMENT: Gujarat Pumped Storage Project Policy 2025

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 7 / Categorization and Allotment

PROVISION: Section 7 - Category IV (Part 1)

TITLE: Category IV PSP Sites: Developer Self-Identified Off-Stream Closed Loop Projects

SUMMARY:
Category IV comprises sites that are self-identified by any developer, agency, or organization and are away from riverine systems (not dependent on river/stream proximity). Category IV sites are NOT included in the government's pre-identified lists for Categories I, II, or III. Only Off-Stream Closed Loop PSP projects qualify for Category IV status. Stored energy/capacity can be utilized for captive consumption, third-party/open access consumers, power exchange sales, or DISCOM/utility sales. Category IV represents a fully open-door mechanism for private developers with green-field opportunities while maintaining environmental safeguards (off-stream requirement) and regulatory oversight.

TEXT:
Category IV, Self-identified: Site(s) are those that are away from the riverine system and are not included in the list of Category I, II and III. These sites, self-identified by any developer/agency/organization, can only be developed as Off-Stream Closed loop PSPs. The stored energy/capacity of such projects can be utilised for captive consumption, third-party or open access consumers, power exchange sales, or for DISCOMs / Utilities, etc.

## CHUNK 27: Category IV - Self-Identified Projects Allotment Process

---
DOCUMENT: Gujarat Pumped Storage Project Policy 2025

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 7 / Categorization and Allotment

PROVISION: Section 7 - Category IV (Part 2)

TITLE: Category IV Project Application and Allotment Procedure

SUMMARY:
For Category IV self-identified sites, applicant organizations must submit an application along with a pre-feasibility report addressing specific technical components on the Nodal Agency's online portal. If multiple applications are received for the same site, a competitive bidding process determines the successful developer. The State Government retains the right of first refusal over total project capacity or part thereof. The detailed procedures and terms and conditions for Category IV allotment are outlined in separate Implementation Guidelines to be issued by EPD/GUVNL/GPCL. This process ensures both openness to developer initiative and government oversight.

KEY TOPICS: Category IV Application Process, Pre-Feasibility Report, Online Portal, Multiple Applications, Competitive Bidding, Right of First Refusal, Implementation Guidelines, State Control, Developer Initiative

TEXT:
For allotment of self-identified sites, an application along with a pre-feasibility report needs to be submitted by project proponents on the online portal of the Nodal Agency. In case of multiple applications for the same site, allotment will be through a competitive bidding process. The State Government shall have the right of first refusal in respect of the total project capacity or part thereof.

The detailed procedure and terms & conditions will be outlined in the Implementation Guidelines.

PROVISION: Section 7 - Category IV Special Note on Pre-Existing Applications

TITLE: Special Merit-Based Consideration for Pre-Policy Applications

SUMMARY:
A special provision allows the State Government to consider pre-existing self-identified site applications on record with the Department prior to this policy's notification on a merit-basis, case-by-case review. Evaluation factors include: application chronology, progress in obtaining necessary clearances and No Objection Certificates (NoCs), applicant capability for renewable energy utilization as PSP input, cost optimization, and energy security considerations. This provision creates a transition mechanism ensuring that serious developers with applications in progress pre-policy are not disadvantaged while maintaining quality standards. The provision emphasizes that due regard shall be given to inter-alia chronology, clearance progress, RE utilization capability, cost optimization, and consumer energy security—indicating flexibility within a structured framework.

## CHUNK 29: Incentives for Category I and II Projects

---
DOCUMENT: Gujarat Pumped Storage Project Policy 2025

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 8 / Incentives

PROVISION: Section 8.1 - Incentives for Category I and II

TITLE: Incentive Package for Government-Reserved PSP Projects (Category I & II)

SUMMARY:
Category I and II projects serving state energy requirements receive an incentive package designed to support project viability. Incentives include: exemption from Electricity Duty on input renewable energy (subject to future amendments in the Electricity Duty Act 1958); Revenue Department policy support for government land allocation (aligned with wind/solar/hybrid/hydrogen land policies); and water charge relaxation provisions. For off-stream closed loop projects, water charges apply for one-time allotment and recurring requirements at applicable rates. For on-stream and off-stream open loop projects, no water charges are levied. These incentives reflect government support for state-strategic projects while maintaining cost-sharing principles through water charges for on-stream operations.

KEY TOPICS: Incentives, Electricity Duty Exemption, Land Allocation Policy, Water Charges, Off-Stream Closed Loop, On-Stream Projects, Open Loop PSP, Renewable Input, Government Support, Cost Sharing

TEXT:
8.1 The following incentives shall be available for PSP awarded to the developer under Category I and II of this Policy:

a) Exemption from Electricity Duty on input renewable energy. Further exemption from Electricity Duty shall be subject to amendment in the Electricity Duty Act, 1958 as amended from time to time

b) The Revenue Department will make a separate policy on the allocation of Government land to PSP developers. This will be in align with the land allocation policy for Wind/Solar/Hybrid and Green Hydrogen Plants.

c) Relaxation in Water Charges:

• for off-stream closed loop' PSPs, charges will be levied for one-time water allotment and recurring water requirement, as per applicable rates, and

• for on-stream and off-stream open loop PSPs, no water charges will be levied.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Electricity Duty Act 1958; Land allocation policies for renewable energy; water resource regulations

CROSS-REFERENCES: Related to Category I in Section 7; connected to Category II in Section 7; ties to implementation procedures in Implementation Guidelines

---
DOCUMENT: Gujarat Pumped Storage Project Policy 2025

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 8 / Incentives / Subsection 8.4

PROVISION: Section 8.4.3 - Private Land (Part 1)

TITLE: Private Land Acquisition Responsibility and Local Area Development Requirements

SUMMARY:
Responsibility for acquiring private land rests solely with the PSP developer. Developers must comply with Local Area Development (LAD) requirements determined by the Government of Gujarat on a case-by-case basis until formal and comprehensive LAD guidelines are notified. After guidelines notification, developers must scrupulously follow the formal guidelines including amendments as issued by the Government from time to time. LAD obligations cover benefits to local communities from project development and infrastructure improvements in the project vicinity. This provision emphasizes that while developers bear acquisition responsibility, they also carry social responsibility through structured local development commitments.

KEY TOPICS: Private Land, Land Acquisition, Developer Responsibility, Local Area Development, LAD Requirements, Community Benefits, Case-by-Case Determination, Formal Guidelines, Social Responsibility

TEXT:
8.4.3 Private Land

The responsibility for acquiring private land shall rest solely with the PSP developer.

a) The Developer shall be obligated to comply at their own cost with Local Area Development (LAD) requirements for PSPs as determined by the GoG on a case-by-case basis, until formal and comprehensive LAD guidelines are notified. Upon such notification, the Developer shall scrupulously comply with LAD guidelines, including any subsequent amendments thereof, as issued by the GoG from time to time.
