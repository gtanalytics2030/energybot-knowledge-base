## CHUNK 1: SECTION-2 - INTRODUCTION TO POWER GENERATION MANAGEMENT STANDARDS

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.1 Introduction

**PROVISION:** Section 2.1

**TITLE:** Introduction to Power Generation Management Standards – Scope and Operational Guidelines

**SUMMARY:** Section-2 provides operational guidelines for power generating stations covering four primary aspects: outage planning to coordinate maintenance and ensure grid availability, generation scheduling and despatch coordination with SLDC, frequency and voltage management for stable grid operations, and Black Start procedures for system restoration. These standards apply to all Generating Companies, Captive Power Plants, and licensee-owned generating stations exceeding 5 MW capacity feeding into the grid.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Power Station Operations; guidelines and standards
- Outage Planning; maintenance coordination
- Generation Scheduling; despatch procedures
- Frequency Management; stability maintenance
- Reactive Power Management; voltage stability
- Voltage Management; system stability
- Black Start Operations; system restoration procedures

**TEXT:**

**2.1 INTRODUCTION:**

2.1.1 These Standards provide guidelines for operation of power stations covering aspects of:

(a) Outage planning of generating stations,

(b) Generation scheduling and despatch,

(c) Frequency / Voltage / Reactive power management,

(d) Black Start operations etc.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section-2 subsections 2.2 through 2.6 (detailed operational guidance)

## CHUNK 2: SECTION-2.2 - OUTAGE PLANNING FOR GENERATING UNITS

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.2 Outage Planning

**PROVISION:** Section 2.2

**TITLE:** Outage Planning – Coordination Requirements and Release Authorization for Generating Units

**SUMMARY:** Generating Companies and Captive Power Plants must coordinate outage plans with SLDC to ensure adequate power availability. Generating units exceeding 5 MW capacity feeding the grid cannot be removed from service without specific SLDC release authorization. All outage occurrences must be reported to SLDC with expected restoration times, and any changes to restoration times must be immediately communicated to SLDC.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Outage Coordination; SLDC communication
- Generation Availability; grid system needs
- Generating Unit Capacity; 5 MW threshold
- SLDC Release Authorization; mandatory before removal
- Restoration Time; communication requirement
- Change Notification; mandatory updates

**TEXT:**

**2.2 OUTAGE PLANNING**

2.2.1 The Generating Companies/Captive Power Plants (CPP) shall coordinate with SLDC for their outage plans to ensure adequate availability of power to the Grid System.

2.2.2 Notwithstanding provision in any approved outage plan, no generating unit of a Generating Company or Captive Power Plant feeding the grid in excess of 5 MW shall be removed from service without specific release by the State Load Despatch Centre (SLDC).

2.2.3 Whenever an outage occurs, the user shall inform SLDC about the expected restoration time. Any change in expected restoration time shall be communicated to SLDC.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.1 (Generation Management scope)
- Section 2.3 (Generation scheduling and despatch)
- Section 1.4 (SLDC authority and responsibility)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** The 5 MW threshold is critical. Units ≥5 MW require SLDC release; units <5 MW have different compliance requirements. Outage planning is not discretionary – all outages must be coordinated with SLDC and restoration times must be communicated.

## CHUNK 3: SECTION-2.3 - GENERATION SCHEDULING AND DESPATCH - AVAILABILITY DECLARATIONS

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.3 Generation Scheduling and Despatch (2.3.1-2.3.5)

**PROVISION:** Section 2.3 (Subsections 2.3.1 through 2.3.5)

**TITLE:** Generation Scheduling and Despatch – Day-Ahead Availability Declarations and Capacity Planning

**SUMMARY:** All Generating Companies and licensee-owned generating stations must provide day-ahead availability declarations to SLDC by 09:00 hours, including ¼-hourly MW/MVAR output and maximum MWh availability for the 24-hour period (00:00-24:00 hours). Captive Power Plants (5 MW and above) must submit import/export figures similarly. Hydro stations must account for reservoir levels and water usage restrictions. Thermal stations must declare ex-bus capacity for each ¼-hour time block. These declarations form the basis for generation scheduling and capacity planning.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Day-Ahead Scheduling; advance availability planning
- Availability Declaration; ¼-hourly granularity
- MW/MVAR Output; power delivery capacity
- Time Block; 24-hour period, 15-minute intervals
- Hydro Power; reservoir levels and constraints
- Thermal Power; declared capacity ex-bus
- Capacity Planning; scheduling basis
- Captive Power Plants; 5 MW threshold

**TEXT:**

**2.3 GENERATION SCHEDULING AND DESPATCH**

2.3.1 All Generating Companies as well as Generating Stations owned by Licensee shall provide ¼ hourly MW / MVAR / maximum MWH availability (00.00 – 24.00 hours) of all Generating Units, to SLDC daily on day ahead basis by 09.00 hours.

2.3.2 CPPs (5MW and above capacity) feeding in to the grid shall provide the ¼ hourly import / export figures to SLDC daily on day ahead basis by 09.00 hours.

2.3.3 In working out the MW / MVAR / maximum ex-bus MWh, hydro power stations shall take into account their respective prevailing reservoir levels and any other restrictions on water usage and shall report the same to SLDC.

2.3.4 The total ex-bus MWh, which can be actually delivered by Generating Companies during the day, shall also be declared. The maximum capacity for each time block (¼ hour) and the total declared available capacity (MW) for the day, including limitation on generation during any specific period of the day as declared by the Generating Companies along with inputs from CPPs as well as Generating Stations owned by Licensee, shall form the basis of generation scheduling.

2.3.5 In case of thermal stations, the declaration shall be for the capacity of the Generating Station (excluding units closed due to orders from SLDC) to deliver ex-bus MWh for each time block of the day (¼ hour). The capacity of the Generating Plant hereinafter referred to as declared capacity shall form the basis of generation scheduling.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.3.6 through 2.3.13 (scheduling consolidation and despatch)
- Section 2.1 (Generation Management scope)
- Section 1.4 (SLDC authority)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Day-ahead declarations by 09:00 are non-negotiable. The ¼-hourly granularity is essential for grid balancing. Failure to declare accurately impacts the entire scheduling process.

## CHUNK 4: SECTION-2.3 - GENERATION SCHEDULE PREPARATION AND CONSOLIDATION

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.3 Generation Scheduling (2.3.6-2.3.8)

**PROVISION:** Section 2.3 (Subsections 2.3.6 through 2.3.8)

**TITLE:** Generation Schedule Preparation – SLDC Consolidation and Merit-Order Dispatch Criteria

**SUMMARY:** SLDC obtains ¼-hourly MW entitlements from Inter-State Generating Stations through the Western Region Load Despatch Centre (WRLDC) by 11:00 hours on a day-ahead basis. SLDC consolidates all generation data (Generating Companies, CPPs, licensee-owned stations) and produces a day-ahead ¼-hourly generation schedule, accounting for demand estimates and planned drawal schedules agreed with WRLDC. When preparing despatch schedules, SLDC prioritizes generation stations according to distribution licensees' preferences while respecting technical constraints; alternatively, if preferences are unavailable, SLDC uses relative commercial costs based on latest updated cost structures furnished by generating entities.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Inter-State Entitlements; Western Region coordination
- WRLDC Communication; ¼-hourly MW entitlements
- Schedule Consolidation; SLDC coordination hub
- Day-Ahead Planning; 24-hour advance scheduling
- Demand Estimation; ¼-hourly granularity
- Drawal Schedule; planned inter-state transfers
- Merit-Order Dispatch; cost-based prioritization
- Technical Constraints; operational feasibility

**TEXT:**

2.3.6 SLDC shall obtain from Western Region Load Despatch Centre (WRLDC) the ¼ hourly MW entitlements from Inter State Generating Stations by 11.00 hours on a day ahead basis.

2.3.7 SLDC shall produce a day ahead ¼ hourly generation schedule after consolidation of the data provided by the Generating Companies/inputs from CPP and Generating Stations owned by Licensee and WRLDC. It will take into account the ¼ hourly demand estimates and latest planned drawal schedule agreed with WRLDC.

2.3.8 In preparation of the despatch schedule, SLDC shall take into account, the priority of the Generation Stations provided by the Distribution Licensees keeping in mind the technical constraints. In case it is not available, the relative commercial costs to the Licensees for the operation of the Generating Units based on latest updated cost structure furnished by Generating Companies/ CPPs shall be used.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.3.1-2.3.5 (availability declarations)
- Section 2.3.9-2.3.13 (schedule finalization and despatch)
- Section 1.4 (SLDC authority)
- Grid Code (inter-state coordination framework)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** SLDC is the central coordinator. Schedule preparation follows a three-way consolidation: intra-state generating companies, CPPs, and inter-state entitlements from WRLDC. Merit-order dispatch is cost-driven when licensee preferences are absent.

## CHUNK 5: SECTION-2.3 - GENERATION SCHEDULE OPTIMIZATION CRITERIA

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.3 Generation Scheduling (2.3.9-2.3.10)

**PROVISION:** Section 2.3 (Subsections 2.3.9 and 2.3.10)

**TITLE:** Generation Schedule Optimization – Transmission Constraints, Load Requirements, and Reactive Power Management

**SUMMARY:** SLDC prepares the day-ahead generation schedule considering four optimization factors: (1) transmission system constraints from time to time, (2) ¼-hourly load requirements as estimated by SLDC based on inputs from all Distribution Licensees and CPPs, (3) availability of generation from all generating entities and their respective constraints, and (4) optimization of both MW and MVAR outputs from generating stations. SLDC may additionally require Generating Companies, CPPs, and licensee-owned units to generate MVAR within their respective capability limits to maintain station bus voltages within specified maximum and minimum bands.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Schedule Optimization; multi-factor criteria
- Transmission Constraints; real-time system conditions
- Demand Estimation; ¼-hourly forecasting
- Generation Availability; constraint mapping
- MW/MVAR Optimization; dual-output balancing
- Reactive Power; voltage stability support
- Capability Limits; machine operating boundaries
- Bus Voltage Bands; specified operating range

**TEXT:**

2.3.9 SLDC shall prepare the day ahead generation schedule keeping in view the following:

(a) Transmission system constraints from time to time.

(b) ¼ hourly load requirements as estimated by SLDC based on the inputs provided by all Distribution Licensees and CPPs.

(c) The availability of generation from Generating Companies, Inter State Generating Companies and CPPs as well as Generating Stations owned by Distribution Licensee together with any constraints in each case.

(d) Optimisation of both MW/MVAR outputs from Generating Stations.

2.3.10 SLDC may also require Generating Companies / CPPs as well as Generating Units owned by Distribution Licensee to generate MVAR within their respective capability limit to maintain station bus voltages within the specified band of maximum and minimum.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.3.1-2.3.8 (prior scheduling steps)
- Section 2.4.3 and 2.5 (frequency and voltage management procedures)
- Section 2.8.3 (capability curves and reactive power commitments)
- Section 1.4 (SLDC authority)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** The schedule is not purely economic – transmission constraints and reactive power requirements override merit-order in many scenarios. Capability curves are binding limits on MVAR provision.

## CHUNK 6: SECTION-2.3 - TENTATIVE SCHEDULE ISSUANCE AND OPERATIONAL UPDATES

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.3 Generation Scheduling (2.3.11-2.3.13)

**PROVISION:** Section 2.3 (Subsections 2.3.11 through 2.3.13)

**TITLE:** Tentative Schedule Issuance and Operational Updates – SLDC Communication of Schedules and Real-Time Rescheduling

**SUMMARY:** SLDC issues a tentative generation/import/export schedule for the following day to all Generating Companies and CPPs by 18:00 hours, including units not required to operate. Generating Companies must promptly report to SLDC regarding changes in unit availability, capability, or unexpected situations affecting operations. CPPs and licensee-owned generating stations must similarly report export changes. SLDC advises users as soon as possible of necessary rescheduling in response to operational updates.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Tentative Schedule; official issuance by SLDC
- Communication Timing; 18:00 hours deadline
- Unit Status Changes; availability and capability updates
- Unexpected Situations; operational anomalies reporting
- Real-Time Rescheduling; dynamic schedule adjustments
- CPP Reporting; export changes
- Prompt Communication; time-critical notifications

**TEXT:**

2.3.11 SLDC shall intimate tentative generation schedule / import / export schedule for the following day to all Generating Companies / CPPs (including any generating unit not required to be run) by 18.00 hours.

2.3.12 Generating Companies shall promptly report to SLDC regarding changes of Generating Unit availability or capability, or any unexpected situation, which would affect its operation.

2.3.13 All CPPs and Generating Stations owned by Licensee shall similarly report regarding their export to SLDC. SLDC shall advise users as soon as possible of any necessary rescheduling.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.3.1-2.3.10 (schedule preparation)
- Section 2.7 (despatch schedule execution)
- Section 1.4 (SLDC authority and real-time control)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** The 18:00-hour schedule issuance is firm. Real-time changes must be reported immediately to enable dynamic rescheduling. Failure to report status changes jeopardizes schedule compliance.

## CHUNK 7: SECTION-2.4 - FREQUENCY MANAGEMENT - OPERATING BAND AND HIGH-FREQUENCY CONTROL

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.4 Frequency Management (2.4.1-2.4.3)

**PROVISION:** Section 2.4 (Subsections 2.4.1 through 2.4.3)

**TITLE:** Frequency Management – Operating Band, High-Frequency Response, and Backing-Down Procedures

**SUMMARY:** SLDC endeavors to operate the power system within a frequency band of 49.0 Hz to 50.5 Hz. When frequency rises above 50 Hz, SLDC initiates three sequential control measures: (1) advising hydro stations to reduce generation without water spillage and issuing instructions to other generating stations, (2) monitoring generation scheduling compliance and requesting excess-generation units to conform to schedule, and (3) requesting thermal stations to reduce generation without requiring oil support. If frequency continues rising to 50.5 Hz despite these measures, SLDC implements a backing-down schedule ordered by variable cost (highest-cost units backing down first) to reduce generation in a rational manner aligned with the day-ahead schedule.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Frequency Band; 49.0 Hz to 50.5 Hz operating range
- High-Frequency Response; generation reduction measures
- Hydro Station Control; minimize water spillage
- Schedule Conformance; monitoring and compliance enforcement
- Thermal Station Backing Down; cost-based ordering
- Variable Cost Hierarchy; merit-order for reduction
- Backup Procedure; logical backing-down sequence

**TEXT:**

**2.4 FREQUENCY MANAGEMENT**

2.4.1 The SLDC shall endeavour to run the system within the frequency band of 49.0 Hz to 50.5 Hz.

2.4.2 The SLDC shall be vigilant when the frequency begins to rise above 50 Hz and initiate the following measures to bring down the frequency to around 50 Hz.

(a) Advise hydro stations to reduce generation without water spillage and give appropriate instructions to other Generating Stations.

(b) Check generation scheduling Vs generation and request concerned Generating Company as well as the CPPs and Generating Stations owned by the Distribution Licensee with excess generation to conform to generation schedule.

(c) Advise thermal stations to reduce the generation to the required extent but not to the extent requiring oil support.

2.4.3 In case frequency rises to 50.5 Hz in spite of above measures, the SLDC shall:

(a) Advise all the Generating Units as well as CPPs and Generating Units owned by Licensee to implement backing down schedule which shall always be updated in the merit order, to reduce the generation considering variable cost in reducing order (i.e. Units with the highest variable will back down first); or in logical manner with due consideration to day ahead generation schedule.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.4.4-2.4.8 (low-frequency response and system separation)
- Section 2.8 (Generating Company standards for frequency compliance)
- Grid Code (frequency standards and inter-regional coordination)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** The frequency band 49.0-50.5 Hz is the operational target. Hydro generation reduction protects environmental objectives. Backing-down is variable-cost ordered – not arbitrary.

## CHUNK 8: SECTION-2.4 - FREQUENCY MANAGEMENT - CRITICAL FREQUENCY THRESHOLD AND SYSTEM ISLANDING

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.4 Frequency Management (2.4.4)

**PROVISION:** Section 2.4.4

**TITLE:** Frequency Management at Critical Threshold – System Islanding from Western Grid

**SUMMARY:** If frequency continues to rise beyond 50.5 Hz despite backing-down measures, and if an import condition exists at Connection Points/Interface Points with the Western Grid, SLDC may, in consultation with WRLDC, isolate the Gujarat power system from the rest of the Western Grid to the extent feasible for system security. This extreme measure protects local system stability when inter-regional power transfers exacerbate high-frequency conditions.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Critical Frequency Threshold; 50.5 Hz ceiling
- System Islanding; grid separation procedure
- Western Grid Interface; regional coordination
- Import Condition; power flow direction trigger
- WRLDC Consultation; regional decision-making
- System Feasibility; operational constraints
- System Security; primary objective

**TEXT:**

2.4.4 In case the frequency still has a rising trend even beyond 50.5 Hz, and if there is import at Connection Points/ Interface Point with Western Grid, SLDC in consultation with WRLDC may island the Gujarat State system from the rest of the Western Grid as may be feasible for system security.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.4.1-2.4.3 (preceding frequency control measures)
- Section 2.4.5-2.4.8 (low-frequency management and system restoration)
- Section 3.9 (Islanding Scheme – transmission system)
- Grid Code (inter-regional operational procedures)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Islanding is a last-resort emergency measure. It requires WRLDC consultation and import conditions at interface points. The objective is system security when normal measures fail.

## CHUNK 9: SECTION-2.4 - FREQUENCY MANAGEMENT - LOW-FREQUENCY RESPONSE MEASURES

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.4 Frequency Management (2.4.5-2.4.6)

**PROVISION:** Section 2.4 (Subsections 2.4.5 and 2.4.6)

**TITLE:** Frequency Management – Low-Frequency Response and Load Shedding Procedures

**SUMMARY:** When frequency begins to fall below 49.5 Hz, SLDC initiates three sequential measures: (1) monitoring generation versus schedule and requesting under-generating units to conform to schedule, (2) monitoring for excess drawal at Connection/Interface Points and advising non-compliant licensees to restrict drawl within schedule, and (3) advising generating stations to synchronize standby machines if available. If frequency continues falling and reaches the critical threshold of 49.0 Hz, SLDC advises Distribution Licensees to shed load manually, and licensees shall not restore normal loading without SLDC authorization.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Low-Frequency Threshold; 49.5 Hz trigger
- Generation Conformance; under-generation correction
- Drawal Monitoring; excess offtake detection
- Distribution Licensee Coordination; drawl restrictions
- Standby Unit Synchronization; rapid generation addition
- Critical Frequency; 49.0 Hz load shedding trigger
- Manual Load Shedding; emergency procedure
- Load Restoration Control; SLDC authorization requirement

**TEXT:**

2.4.5 SLDC shall be vigilant when frequency begins to fall below 49.5 Hz and initiate the following measures to raise the frequency to approach 50 Hz:

(a) Check generation Vs generation schedule and request concerned Generating Companies as well as CPPs and Generating Stations owned by the Licensee with less generation to conform to generation schedule.

(b) Check whether there is any excess drawl at any Connection Point/ Interface Point by any Distribution Licensee and advise such Licensee/ Company to restrict its drawl within schedule.

(c) Advise generating stations to synchronise standby machines if available.

2.4.6 In case the frequency still falls and reaches 49.0 Hz the SLDC shall advise the Distribution Licensees to shed load manually. The Distribution Licensees shall not restore normal load shedding unless and until permitted by SLDC.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.4.1-2.4.4 (high-frequency management)
- Section 2.4.7-2.4.8 (critical low-frequency response)
- Section 3.8 (Load Shedding – transmission system procedures)
- Section 4.2.1-4.2.5 (Distribution Licensee demand scheduling)
- Grid Code (frequency standards and load shedding protocols)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** The 49.0 Hz threshold triggers mandatory load shedding. Load restoration is not automatic – it requires explicit SLDC authorization. Frequency deviation management is a shared responsibility between generation and distribution sides.

## CHUNK 10: SECTION-2.4 - FREQUENCY MANAGEMENT - CRITICAL LOW-FREQUENCY THRESHOLD AND SYSTEM ISLANDING

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.4 Frequency Management (2.4.7-2.4.8)

**PROVISION:** Section 2.4 (Subsections 2.4.7 and 2.4.8)

**TITLE:** Frequency Management at Critical Low Threshold – System Islanding and Automatic Frequency Regulation

**SUMMARY:** If frequency continues to fall below 49.0 Hz and reaches 48.5 Hz, SLDC may, in consultation with WRLDC, examine the possibility of isolating the Gujarat power system from the rest of the Western Grid if an export condition exists at Connection/Interface Points. This extreme measure protects system integrity when local generation cannot meet local demand. Automatic load frequency relays operate as assistive devices to maintain the 50 Hz frequency target during normal operations.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Critical Low-Frequency Threshold; 48.5 Hz
- System Islanding; frequency-based separation trigger
- Export Condition; power flow direction
- WRLDC Consultation; regional coordination
- Automatic Load Frequency Relays; frequency stabilization devices
- System Integrity; protection objective

**TEXT:**

2.4.7 In case the frequency still continues to fall and reaches 48.5 Hz, SLDC shall in consultation with WRLDC examine the possibility to island the Gujarat State system from rest of Western Grid if there is export at Connection Point / Interface Point with other states in the region.

2.4.8 Automatic load frequency relays would be one of the devices to assist to maintain frequency at 50 Hz.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.4.1-2.4.6 (preceding frequency management measures)
- Section 3.9 (Islanding Scheme – transmission planning)
- Section 3.8 (Load Shedding – demand reduction)
- Grid Code (frequency protection and system restoration)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** The 48.5 Hz threshold is critical. Islanding at low frequency is a last resort when export capacity exhausts. Automatic relays complement SLDC's manual frequency management.
