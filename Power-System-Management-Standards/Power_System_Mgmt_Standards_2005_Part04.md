## CHUNK 1: SECTION-3 - INTRODUCTION TO TRANSMISSION SYSTEM MANAGEMENT STANDARDS

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-3 Transmission System Management Standards → Subsection 3.1 Introduction

**PROVISION:** Section 3.1

**TITLE:** Introduction to Transmission System Management Standards – Scope and Operational Framework

**SUMMARY:** Section-3 formulates guidelines for transmission grid operation covering five primary operational aspects: data management and system studies, load despatch and communication, voltage and reactive power management, demand management, and system operation. These Standards serve as guidelines for the State Transmission Utility (STU) to efficiently, coordinatedly, and economically operate the transmission system for reliable electricity transmission across Gujarat.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Data Management; system study inputs
- System Studies; load flow, stability, reactive power, fault analysis
- Load Despatch; scheduling and operational coordination
- Communication; inter-station voice and data links
- Voltage Management; bus voltage regulation
- Reactive Power Management; MVAR optimization
- Demand Management; load-line coordination
- System Operation; operational procedures and protocols

**TEXT:**

**3.1 INTRODUCTION:**

3.1.1 This section formulates guidelines for grid operation covering following aspects:

(a) Data management and system studies.

(b) Load despatch and communication

(c) Voltage and reactive power management.

(d) Demand management.

(e) System operation.

3.1.2 These Standards shall serve as guidelines for the STU to operate the transmission system for providing an efficient, coordinated and economical system of electricity transmission.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**

## CHUNK 2: SECTION-3.2 - DATA MANAGEMENT AND SYSTEM STUDIES - DATA COLLECTION, STORAGE, AND SCADA SYSTEMS

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-3 Transmission System Management Standards → Subsection 3.2 Data Management and System Studies

**PROVISION:** Section 3.2

**TITLE:** Data Management and System Studies – System Data Collection, Storage, SCADA Time-Stamping, and Periodic Studies

**SUMMARY:** For efficient transmission system planning, STU must collect, store, and manage detailed up-to-date data of the complete system to enable system studies as required. System data collection shall generally conform to specifications in Annexure-1. SCADA systems at SLDC shall include appropriate real-time data time-stamping facilities at preferably 5-to-10-second intervals to facilitate event analysis and data verification. STU shall periodically conduct load flows under different operating conditions, steady-state and transient stability limit analysis, reactive power compensation location and extent studies, fault level calculations at various system points, transient and dynamic studies, and any other studies required for system reliability.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- System Data; comprehensive collection and storage
- Annexure-1 Specifications; data inventory standards
- SCADA Time-Stamping; 5-10 second intervals
- Event Analysis; data verification capability
- Load Flow Studies; multiple operating scenarios
- Stability Studies; steady-state and transient analysis
- Reactive Power Studies; compensation requirements
- Fault Level Analysis; short-circuit capacity calculations
- Dynamic Studies; system response under disturbances

**TEXT:**

**3.2 DATA MANAGEMENT AND SYSTEM STUDIES**

3.2.1 For planning the Transmission System for efficient operation the STU should collect, store & manage detailed up to date data of the complete system. This will enable in carrying out system studies whenever required. The details of system data to be collected and stored shall generally be as indicated in Annexure- 1.

3.2.2 SCADA system at SLDC shall be equipped with appropriate real time data stamping facility at preferably 5 to 10 seconds interval for facilitating event analysis and data verification.

3.2.3 The STU shall periodically carryout the following system studies.

(a) Load flows under different conditions of operation.

(b) Steady state and transient stability limits of the system.

(c) Location and extent of reactive power compensation required in the system for maintaining satisfactory voltage profile in the system.

(d) Fault levels at different points in the system.

(e) Transient & dynamic studies.

(f) Any other studies as may be required.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section-3.24.2 (Annexure-1: detailed data specifications)
- Section 2.9 (Generation Monitoring – real-time data)
- Section 3.4 (Communication infrastructure)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Data quality and completeness are foundational. Annexure-1 specifies the complete data inventory requirement. SCADA time-stamping at 5-10 seconds is critical for post-event analysis.

## CHUNK 3: SECTION-3.3 - LOAD DESPATCH - SLDC FUNCTIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-3 Transmission System Management Standards → Subsection 3.3 Load Despatch

**PROVISION:** Section 3.3

**TITLE:** Load Despatch – SLDC Functions Under Section 32(2) of Electricity Act 2003 and Additional Operational Functions

**SUMMARY:** Under Section 32(2) of the Electricity Act, 2003, SLDC functions include: (1) optimum scheduling and despatch of electricity within the State according to contracts with licensees and generating companies, (2) monitoring grid operations, (3) maintaining accounts of electricity quantities transmitted through the state grid, (4) exercising supervision and control over intra-state transmission systems, and (5) conducting real-time operations for grid control and despatch through secure and economic state grid operation per Grid Standards and Grid Code. Additionally, SLDC performs: daily generation and load scheduling with despatch instructions, monitoring line MW/MVAR/bus voltages/frequency, monitoring generation output and import/export at connection/interface points, coordinating transmission system restoration post-blackout, finalizing transmission outage plans in coordination with generating stations and distribution systems to maintain security standards, coordinating automatic under-frequency load shedding (AUFLS) installation with transmission licensees, coordinated operation of major transmission lines and generation for frequency/voltage profile maintenance, coordination of adequacy studies under severe disturbances, standing instruction issuance for system separation, and inter-state grid management coordination with regional power committees and WRLDC.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Statutory Functions; Section 32(2) Electricity Act 2003
- Optimum Scheduling; contract-based despatch
- Grid Monitoring; real-time operational visibility
- Electricity Accounting; quantity measurement and records
- Intra-State Supervision; transmission system control
- Real-Time Operations; secure and economic dispatch
- Daily Scheduling; generation and load planning
- System Monitoring; line flows, voltages, frequency
- Generation Coordination; output and import/export tracking
- Restoration Coordination; post-blackout procedures
- Outage Planning; maintenance coordination
- AUFLS Coordination; automatic load shedding
- Frequency/Voltage Maintenance; system stability
- Adequacy Studies; severe disturbance analysis
- System Separation Procedures; islanding protocols
- Inter-State Coordination; WRLDC and RPC liaison

**TEXT:**

**3.3 LOAD DESPATCH**

3.3.1 According to the Section 32 (2) of Electricity Act, 2003, the following are the functions of State Load Despatch Centre, which shall :

(a) be responsible for optimum scheduling and despach of electricity within a State, in accordance with the contracts entered into with the Licensees or the Generating Companies operating in that State;

(b) monitor the grid operations;

(c) keep accounts of the quantity of electricity transmitted through the State grid;

(d) exercise supervision and control over the intra-state transmission system;

(e) be responsible for carrying out real time operations for grid control and despatch of electricity within the State through secure and economic operation of the State grid in accordance with the Grid Standards and the state Grid Code.

3.3.2 The SLDC shall also perform the following functions.

(a) Daily generation, load scheduling and issuing despatch instructions.

(b) Monitoring line MW and MVAR drawls, EHT bus Voltages and Frequency.

(c) Monitoring generation output, export/import at various Connection Points / Interface Points.

(d) Co-ordinating restoration process after partial or total blackouts in the Transmission System.

(e) Finalisation of outage plan for Transmission System for carrying out maintenance, construction, modification, diversion etc., in co-ordination with the Generating Stations and Distribution System so that the security standards of the Transmission System are not affected.

(f) Co-ordination with Transmission Licensee for installation of automatic under frequency load shedding (AUFLS) for automatic tripping of non-critical Loads as identified by the Distribution Licensee and informed to SLDC,

(g) Co-ordinated operation of major Transmission Lines and generation to maintain frequency and voltage profile within set limits.

(h) Co-ordination of the studies to review adequacy of the system and inter system operating practices under unusually severe system disturbances in association with STU / the Transmission Licensee.

(i) Issue of standing instructions to operating staff of each system to deal with separation of the system from inter-connected network.

(j) Co-ordinate with Regional Power Committee and WRLDC for Inter State Grid Management and event analysis as well as for implementation of corrective measures.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 32(2) of Electricity Act, 2003
- Grid Code (Grid Standards, operational procedures)
- Section 2.1-2.9 (Generation scheduling and despatch)
- Section 4.2 (Distribution Licensee demand scheduling)
- Section 1.4 (SLDC responsibility)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Section 32(2) defines SLDC's statutory mandate. The additional functions in 3.3.2 expand this mandate with operational specificity. SLDC is the central coordinator for all grid operations.

## CHUNK 5: SECTION-3.5 - REACTIVE POWER MANAGEMENT - OPTIMIZATION OF REACTIVE RESOURCES AND RESERVES

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-3 Transmission System Management Standards → Subsection 3.5 Reactive Power Management

**PROVISION:** Section 3.5

**TITLE:** Reactive Power Management – Optimization of Reactive Resources and Reserve Management

**SUMMARY:** STU/Transmission Licensee shall endeavor for optimum use of existing reactive resources and reactive reserves in the transmission system to meet steady-state voltage limits at all buses in the transmission system as set out in the "Transmission System Planning and Security Standard" of the Grid Code. This includes strategic deployment of shunt reactors, capacitors, synchronous condensers, and generation-provided reactive power to maintain voltage stability across the transmission network.

## CHUNK 6: SECTION-3.6 - VOLTAGE MANAGEMENT - MONITORING, REGULATION, AND CONTROL MEASURES

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-3 Transmission System Management Standards → Subsection 3.6 Voltage Management

**PROVISION:** Section 3.6

**TITLE:** Voltage Management – Real-Time Voltage Monitoring, Regulation Methods, and Corrective Control Actions

**SUMMARY:** STU/Transmission Licensee shall monitor voltage levels at all EHT substations on real-time basis through installed data acquisition systems. System voltage shall be regulated by taking feasible measures to regulate reactive power flows and frequency. Local voltage problems shall be remedied to the extent possible by operating local transformer taps per Grid Code provisions or in consultation with SLDC. Under high-voltage conditions, SLDC shall (a) request generating stations to decrease MVAR generation per capability curves, (b) switch in bus reactors where provided in consultation with WRLDC, (c) switch out capacitors (if in-service), (d) reduce taps of 400/220 kV and 220/132 kV interconnecting transformers, (e) switch out one circuit of double-circuit transmission lines (if lightly loaded), and (f) implement other necessary measures. Under low-voltage conditions, SLDC shall (a) request generating stations to increase MVAR generation per capability curves, (b) switch out bus reactors (if in-service), (c) switch in capacitors, (d) increase system voltage by changing taps of 400/220 kV and 220/132 kV transformers, (e) restore circuits under outage, and (f) instruct manual load shedding.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Real-Time Monitoring; data acquisition systems
- Voltage Regulation; reactive power and frequency control
- Transformer Tap Control; local voltage adjustment
- High-Voltage Response; generation reduction, reactive equipment control
- Low-Voltage Response; generation increase, reactive equipment control
- Bus Reactor Control; dynamic switching
- Capacitor Control; dynamic switching
- Double-Circuit Line Control; circuit isolation for lightly-loaded conditions
- Interconnecting Transformer Tap Control; multi-level voltage adjustment
- Load Shedding; demand reduction for voltage support
- Consultation Protocols; SLDC and WRLDC coordination

**TEXT:**

**3.6 VOLTAGE MANAGEMENT**

3.6.1 The STU / Transmission Licensee shall monitor voltage levels at all EHT substations of its transmission system on real time by installing data acquisition system.

3.6.2 System voltage shall be regulated by taking all feasible measures to regulate system reactive power flows and frequency.

3.6.3 All local voltage problems shall be remedied to the extent possible by operating local transformer taps by Transmission and Distribution Licensees in accordance with the provisions of the Grid code or in consultation with the SLDC.

3.6.4 In case of system high voltage, the SLDC shall take the following measures:

(a) Request Generating Stations to decrease MVAR generation in accordance with machine capability curves.

(b) Switch in bus reactors where provided in consultation with WRLDC.

(c) Switch out capacitors where provided (if they are switched in).

(d) Arrange to reduce taps of 400/220 kV and 220/132 kV Interconnecting Transformers.

(e) Switch out one circuit of double circuit transmission line (if lightly loaded).

(f) Any other measures considered necessary.

3.6.5 In case of system low voltage, the SLDC shall take the following measures:

(a) Request Generating Stations to increase MVAR generation in accordance with machine capability curves.

(b) Switch out bus reactors (if they are switched in).

(c) Switch in capacitors.

(d) Arrange to increase system Voltage by changing the taps of 400/220 kV and 220/132 kV Interconnecting Transformers.

(e) Restoration of circuits under outage.

(f) Instruct manual load shedding

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 3.5 (Reactive Power Management)
- Section 3.8 (Load Shedding)
- Section 2.5 (Generation Voltage and Reactive Power Management)
- Grid Code (voltage standards and operational procedures)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Voltage control is hierarchical: local transformer taps first (if possible), then SLDC-coordinated reactive equipment switching, then generation dispatch, finally load shedding. Each measure has specific operational conditions.

## CHUNK 8: SECTION-3.8 - LOAD SHEDDING - EXCESS DRAWAL DETECTION AND EMERGENCY LOAD TRIPPING

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-3 Transmission System Management Standards → Subsection 3.8 Load Shedding

**PROVISION:** Section 3.8

**TITLE:** Load Shedding – Excess Drawal Monitoring, Scheduling Compliance, and Under-Frequency Relay Installation

**SUMMARY:** SLDC shall check for excess drawal at any Connection Points/Interface Points by any distribution licensee and advise such licensee to restrict drawal within the agreed schedule. If no excess drawal exists and generation increase is not feasible, load shedding shall be ordered by SLDC by tripping selected lines as determined necessary and appropriate at the time. Under-frequency relays shall be appropriately set and provided by transmission licensee for load shedding purposes. Once installed, under-frequency relays shall not be bypassed except with specific SLDC authorization.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Excess Drawal Monitoring; off-schedule consumption detection
- Distribution Licensee Accountability; drawal restriction enforcement
- Generation Capacity Limits; availability constraints
- Load Shedding Authority; SLDC ordered tripping
- Line Selection; operational judgment for shedding
- Under-Frequency Relays; automatic load shedding devices
- Relay Bypass Prohibition; operational discipline
- SLDC Authorization; bypass approval requirement

**TEXT:**

**3.8 LOAD SHEDDING**

3.8.1 SLDC shall check whether there is any excess drawl at any Connection Points/ Interface Points by any Distribution Licensee and advise him to restrict his Drawl within schedule. If there is no such excess Drawl, and there is also no possibility of increasing the generation by the Generating Companies, load shedding should be ordered by SLDC by tripping such of the lines as found necessary and appropriate at that time. Under frequency relays appropriately set shall be provided for the purpose by the Transmission Licensee wherever possible and once installed, shall not be bypassed under any circumstances unless specifically permitted by SLDC.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.4.6 (Frequency Management – low-frequency load shedding)
- Section 3.3 (SLDC load despatch functions)
- Section 3.6 (Voltage Management – low-voltage load shedding)
- Section 4.2 (Distribution Licensee demand scheduling)
- Grid Code (load shedding procedures and relay settings)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Load shedding is a multi-step process: first excess drawal check, second generation feasibility check, then emergency shedding if necessary. Under-frequency relays are mandatory automatic safety devices; bypass is prohibited without authorization.
