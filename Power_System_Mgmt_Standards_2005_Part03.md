## CHUNK 1: SECTION-2.5 - VOLTAGE AND REACTIVE POWER MANAGEMENT - CAPABILITY CURVES AND SYSTEM STUDIES

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.5 Voltage and Reactive Power Management

**PROVISION:** Section 2.5

**TITLE:** Voltage and Reactive Power Management – Capability Curves, Load Flow Studies, and Voltage Control

**SUMMARY:** Generating Companies and CPPs must provide updated capability curves to SLDC, STU, and Transmission Licensee indicating maximum and minimum reactive power (MVAR) limits for all units and restrictions, if any, to enable accurate system studies and effective transmission operation. CPPs must similarly furnish net reactive capability available for export/import. Load flow studies shall be conducted for different operating conditions to maintain node voltages within specified limits. Based on these studies, SLDC shall instruct generating entities to maintain specified voltage levels at connection points through transformer tap changing and MVAR reserves per capability curves. High transient voltages during load throw-off and switching operations must be controlled within specifications.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Capability Curves; MVAR limits and restrictions
- Reactive Power Capability; MVAR provision limits
- System Studies; load flow analysis
- Node Voltage; operating range specifications
- Transformer Tap Changing; voltage regulation method
- MVAR Reserves; operating margin management
- Transient Voltage; switching operation control
- CPP Coordination; reactive power export/import

**TEXT:**

**2.5 VOLTAGE AND REACTIVE POWER MANAGEMENT**

2.5.1 Generating companies shall make available to SLDC, STU and Transmission Licensee the up-to-date capability curves for all Generating Units indicating restrictions if any, to allow accurate system studies and effective operation of the Transmission System. CPPs shall similarly furnish the net reactive capability that will be available for export / import from the Transmission System.

2.5.2 Load flow studies shall be conducted for different conditions of operation commonly encountered. The voltages of all the node points shall have to be maintained within the specified limits. On the basis of these studies SLDC shall instruct Generating Companies and CPPs to maintain specified voltage levels at connecting Points / Interface Points by transformer tap changing to the extent possible and use MVAR reserves in accordance with technical limits of Generating Units as per capability curves. High transient voltages, which may generally occur during load throw off and switching operations, should be controlled to within specifications.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.4 (Frequency Management – reactive power effects)
- Section 2.8.3 (Generating Company standards – capability curves)
- Section 3.5-3.6 (Transmission Voltage and Reactive Power Management)
- Grid Code (transmission voltage standards)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Capability curves are binding operational boundaries. Reactive power provision is not optional – it is a technical obligation tied to generating unit capability.

## CHUNK 2: SECTION-2.6 - BLACK START OPERATIONS - CAPABILITY MAINTENANCE AND MOCK DRILLS

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.6 Black Start Operations

**PROVISION:** Section 2.6

**TITLE:** Black Start Operations – Capability Records, Operation Plans, and Annual Mock Drills

**SUMMARY:** SLDC maintains and updates records of generating station Black Start capabilities and associated operation plans. Generating Companies are responsible for implementing planned Black Start procedures upon SLDC instruction and progressively increasing generation according to demand intimated by SLDC. SLDC may arrange mock orientations of Black Start preparedness on a prearranged basis at least annually to validate procedures and provide corrections.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Black Start Capability; system restoration readiness
- Black Start Procedures; planned response sequences
- SLDC Record Management; capability documentation
- Generation Ramp-Up; progressive demand response
- Mock Drill; annual preparedness verification
- Procedure Corrections; improvement mechanism

**TEXT:**

**2.6 BLACK START OPERATIONS**

2.6.1 The SLDC shall maintain and keep updated a record of Generating Station Black Start capabilities and associated Generating Station Black Start operation plans. The Generating Companies shall be responsible for commencing their planned Black Start procedure on the instruction of the SLDC and steadily increasing their generation according to the demand intimated by the SLDC.

2.6.2 The SLDC may arrange for mock orientation of Black Start preparedness in the system at least once in a year on a prearranged programme to provide for corrections.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.1 (Generation Management scope – Black Start operations)
- Section 3.9 (Islanding Scheme – system restoration planning)
- Section 4.7.1 (Distribution Licensee restoration procedures)
- Grid Code (system restoration and Black Start procedures)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Black Start capability is essential for system recovery post-blackout. Annual mock drills validate procedure effectiveness and identify gaps.

## CHUNK 3: SECTION-2.7.1 - SCHEDULE OF DESPATCH - GENERATION DESPATCH AND INSTRUCTION FORMAT

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.7 Schedule of Despatch → 2.7.1 Generation Despatch

**PROVISION:** Section 2.7.1 (Subsections 2.7.1.1 and 2.7.1.2)

**TITLE:** Generation Despatch – Daily Schedule Compliance and Despatch Instruction Format

**SUMMARY:** All Generating Companies and CPPs must regulate generation/export according to the daily generation schedule issued by SLDC. All generating units above 5 MW (including CPPs and licensee-owned stations) are subject to SLDC despatch instructions to the extent of their export to STU/Transmission Licensee. SLDC issues despatch instructions on the same level as the day-ahead schedule unless rescheduling is requested. Absent revised instructions, entities shall operate per the available ¼-hourly day-ahead schedule. Despatch instructions must be in standard format and include date, time, station name, unit identity, operator information, and communication details. Instructions may address unit start/stop, reserve levels, MVAR generation/absorption for voltage maintenance, Black Start procedures, and standby unit readiness.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Daily Schedule Compliance; mandatory adherence
- Despatch Authority; SLDC exclusive issuance
- 5 MW Threshold; obligation application level
- Standard Format; communication specification
- Instruction Content; unit start/stop, MVAR, Black Start, standby
- Communication Details; operator identification and logging
- Schedule Baseline; day-ahead ¼-hourly default

**TEXT:**

**2.7 SCHEDULE OF DESPATCH**

**2.7.1 Generation Despatch**

2.7.1.1 All Generating Companies shall regulate generation and CPPs shall regulate their export according to the daily generation schedule. All Generating Units, above 5 MW including CPPs as well as Generating Stations owned by Licensee, will be subject to despatch instructions of SLDC. CPPs as well as Generating Stations owned by Licensee will be subject to these instructions to the extent of their respective exports to the STU/ Transmission Licensee. SLDC shall issue similar despatch instructions to all Generating Companies / CPPs on same level as previously available for day ahead schedule unless rescheduling is requested by Generating Companies/ CPPs. In the absence of any revised despatch instructions by SLDC, Generating Companies and CPPs shall generate / export according to the available ¼ hourly day ahead generation schedule. Despatch instruction shall be in the standard format. These instructions will recognise declared availability and other parameters, which have been made available by the Generating Company to SLDC. These instructions shall include date, time, name of the Generating Station, Generating Units (total export in case of CPP), name of operators sending and receiving the same.

2.7.1.2 Despatch instructions may include:

(a) Switching a generator in or out of service.

(b) Details of reserve to be carried on a unit.

(c) Increasing or decreasing MVAR generation/absorption to maintain voltage profile based on margin level.

(d) Beginning pre-planned black start procedures.

(e) Holding generating units on standby if available.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.3 (Generation Scheduling – daily schedule basis)
- Section 2.7.2-2.7.3 (Communication and compliance requirements)
- Section 1.4 (SLDC authority and responsibility)
- Grid Code (despatch protocols)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Despatch instructions are binding operational orders. Standard format ensures clarity. The 5 MW threshold applies here: smaller units have lesser despatch obligations.

## CHUNK 4: SECTION-2.7.2 - COMMUNICATION WITH GENERATING COMPANIES - DESPATCH INSTRUCTION TRANSMISSION AND CONFIRMATION

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.7 Schedule of Despatch → 2.7.2 Communication

**PROVISION:** Section 2.7.2

**TITLE:** Communication with Generating Companies – Despatch Instruction Transmission, Confirmation, and Logging

**SUMMARY:** SLDC transmits despatch instructions via email, fax, teleprinter, telephone, PLCC (Power Line Carrier Communication), VSAT, or any other convenient communication mode. All instructions are confirmed by concerned officers with name and designation at both sending and receiving ends, with proper logging at each end. Oral instructions must be complied with immediately, followed by written confirmation via fax, teleprinter, or equivalent method.

All such oral instructions shall be complied with immediately, and written confirmation shall be issued by Fax, Teleprinter or otherwise.

## CHUNK 5: SECTION-2.7.3 - ACTION REQUIRED BY GENERATING COMPANIES - COMPLIANCE OBLIGATIONS AND TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.7 Schedule of Despatch → 2.7.3 Action Required by Generating Companies

**PROVISION:** Section 2.7.3 (Subsections 2.7.3.1 through 2.7.3.4)

**TITLE:** Action Required by Generating Companies – Despatch Compliance, Equipment Requirements, and Failure Reporting

**SUMMARY:** All Generating Companies, CPPs, and licensee-owned stations must promptly comply with SLDC despatch instructions unless safety of plant or personnel is jeopardized. Non-compliance must be immediately reported to SLDC with reasons. All generating units must have Automatic Voltage Regulators (AVRs) in-service and governors available/in-service, capable of automatic frequency-responsive output adjustment within capability limits. Units must report losses or changes in operational capability, including AVR/governor removal. De-synchronization without SLDC instruction is prohibited except for plant/personnel safety grounds, which must be promptly reported. CPPs must report capability changes affecting despatch compliance. Abnormal voltage/frequency operations must be reported promptly. Unit synchronization without SLDC instruction is prohibited except in emergencies per Grid Code contingency planning, with prior intimation.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Despatch Compliance; mandatory adherence
- Safety Exceptions; plant/personnel protection grounds
- AVR Requirement; Automatic Voltage Regulator mandatory
- Governor Requirement; speed governor in-service
- Frequency Response Capability; automatic adjustment within limits
- Operational Capability Changes; loss/alteration reporting
- De-Synchronization Restrictions; SLDC authorization requirement
- Unit Synchronization Restrictions; SLDC authorization with emergency exception
- Abnormal Operation Reporting; voltage and frequency anomalies
- CPP Capability Reporting; status affecting despatch

**TEXT:**

2.7.3 Action required by Generating Companies

2.7.3.1 All Generating Companies and CPPs as well as Generating Stations owned by Licensee shall comply promptly with despatch instructions issued by SLDC unless such instruction affect the safety of plant or personnel. They shall promptly inform SLDC in the event of any unforeseen difficulties in complying with an instruction. All Generating Units shall have Automatic Voltage Regulators (AVR) in service. All Generating Units shall have the governors available and in service and must be capable of automatic increase or decrease in output within the normal declared frequency range and within their respective capability limits. Generating Companies shall immediately inform SLDC by telephone of any loss or change (temporary or otherwise) of / in the operational capability of any generating unit which is synchronised to the system or which is being used to maintain system reserve. Generating Companies shall inform SLDC of any removal of AVR and / or governor from service along with reasons thereof. CPPs shall similarly inform any change in status affecting their ability in complying with despatch instructions. Generating Companies including CPPs shall not de-synchronise Generating Units without instruction from SLDC except on the grounds of safety to the plant and personnel, which shall be promptly reported to SLDC.

2.7.3.2 Generating Companies and CPPs shall report any abnormal voltage and frequency related operation of generating units / feeders promptly to SLDC. Generating companies including CPPs shall not synchronise Generating Units without instruction from SLDC.

2.7.3.3 In the event of any emergency in the Generating Station, the Generating Company may synchronise units with the grid with prior intimation in the interest of safe operation of the grid following standing instructions developed for such purpose under "contingency planning" of the Grid Code.

2.7.3.4 Should a Generating Company fail to comply with any of the above provisions, it shall inform SLDC promptly of this failure with reasons.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.7.1-2.7.2 (Despatch instructions and communication)
- Section 2.8.2 (Generating Company standards – power factor)
- Section 2.8.3 (Generating Company standards – reactive power commitment)
- Grid Code (contingency planning; system restoration)
- Section 1.5 (Non-Compliance and remedial action)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** AVR and governor in-service is mandatory. All capability changes must be reported by telephone immediately. De-synchronization without authorization is prohibited except for safety. Emergency synchronization is permitted with prior intimation.

## CHUNK 6: SECTION-2.8 - STANDARDS TO BE MET BY GENERATING COMPANIES - VOLTAGE, FREQUENCY, REACTIVE POWER, AND WHEELING REQUIREMENTS

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.8 Standards to be Met by Generating Companies

**PROVISION:** Section 2.8

**TITLE:** Standards to Be Met by Generating Companies – Voltage/Frequency Compliance, Power Factor, Reactive Power, and Wheeling Commitments

**SUMMARY:** Generating Companies, CPPs, and licensee-owned stations must comply with voltage and frequency standards and limits specified in the IEGC/Grid Code for power transmission. Thermal and nuclear generating units shall not operate at leading power factor, except for unit charging procedures where leading power factor operation is permitted per capability curves. Transmission Licensees shall incorporate wheeling agreement conditions requiring generating entities to commit to: (1) reactive power capability (MVAR), (2) economic back-down level, (3) loading increment rate from back-down to maximum continuous rating (MW/Min), (4) loading decrement rate from maximum continuous rating to back-down (MW/Min), and (5) pickup rates on synchronizing under cold start, warm start, and hot start conditions, within plant safety limits.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- IEGC/Grid Code Compliance; voltage and frequency standards
- Thermal/Nuclear Unit Power Factor; lagging operation requirement
- Leading Power Factor Exception; unit charging procedures
- Capability Curves; operating envelope boundaries
- Reactive Power Commitment; MVAR availability
- Economic Back-Down Level; minimum generation floor
- Loading Rates; increment and decrement rates (MW/Min)
- Synchronizing Pickup Rates; cold/warm/hot start conditions
- Wheeling Agreements; contractual requirements
- Safety Limits; plant personnel protection

**TEXT:**

**2.8 STANDARDS TO BE MET BY THE GENERATING COMPANIES: -**

2.8.1 The IEGC/Grid Code specifies voltage and frequency standards with limits of variation for transmitting the power, which the Generating Company including CPPs as well as Generating Stations owned by Licensee have to comply with.

2.8.2 Thermal/nuclear Generating Units shall not run at leading power factor. However, for the purpose of charging, Generating Unit may be allowed to operate at leading power factor as per the respective capability curves.

2.8.3 The Transmission Licensee shall incorporate conditions in wheeling agreement requiring the generating companies and CPPs as well as Generating Stations owned by Licensee, to commit:

(a) Reactive power capability (MVAR)

(b) Economic back down level.

(c) Loading increment rate from back down level to maximum continuous rating (MW/Min).

(d) Loading decrement rate from maximum continuous rating to back down level (MW/Min).

(e) Pickup rates on synchronising in MW to the extent that it does not jeopardise safety of its plant and personnel under the following conditions:

 Cold Start.
 Warm Start.
 Hot Start.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.5 (Voltage and Reactive Power Management)
- Section 2.4 (Frequency Management)
- Section 2.7.3 (Despatch compliance and equipment)
- IEGC/Grid Code (voltage/frequency standards)
- Section 3.13 (Protection Coordination)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** The power factor restriction on thermal units is specific: lagging operation only, except during charging. Wheeling agreements codify these technical commitments.

## CHUNK 7: SECTION-2.9 - MONITORING OF GENERATION - REAL-TIME DATA AND PARAMETER MONITORING

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-2 Power Generation Management Standards → Subsection 2.9 Monitoring of Generation

**PROVISION:** Section 2.9

**TITLE:** Monitoring of Generation – Real-Time Data Systems and Generation Parameter Monitoring

**SUMMARY:** Transmission Licensees must provide Transmission Licensees must provide SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition) systems and point-to-point voice and data communication links to SLDC to receive real-time generation data: frequency, MW output, MVAR output, MW/MVAR flow in outgoing lines, and voltage at interconnection bus. SLDC establishes monitoring procedures for tracking generating plant parameters: declared gross generation capacity, unit loading rate, unit unloading rate, and active/reactive power delivery following despatch instructions.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- SCADA System; real-time data acquisition
- Data Communication Links; voice and data channels
- Real-Time Parameters; frequency, MW, MVAR, voltage
- Interconnection Bus Voltage; system interface monitoring
- Outgoing Line Flows; power delivery tracking
- Declared Capacity; baseline for comparison
- Loading Rates; ramp-up/down speed monitoring
- Despatch Compliance; power delivery verification

**TEXT:**

**2.9 MONITORING OF GENERATION**

2.9.1 The Transmission Licensee shall provide facilities such as SCADA system and point-to-point voice and data communication links to SLDC for receiving the following real time data from generating companies:

(a) Frequency.

(b) MW output.

(c) MVAR output.

(d) MW and MVAR flow in outgoing lines.

(e) Voltage at interconnection bus.

2.9.2 The SLDC shall establish suitable procedure for monitoring the following parameters of a generating plant:

(a) Declared gross generation capacity.

(b) Loading rate of a unit.

(c) Un-loading rate of a unit.

(d) Active and Reactive power delivery following despatch instructions.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.3 (Generation Scheduling – availability data)
- Section 2.7 (Despatch instructions)
- Section 2.4 (Frequency Management – real-time monitoring)
- Section 2.5 (Voltage and Reactive Power Management)
- Section 3.2 (Transmission Data Management and System Studies)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Real-time monitoring is bidirectional: Transmission Licensees provide infrastructure; SLDC establishes monitoring procedures. SCADA is the backbone of operational visibility.
