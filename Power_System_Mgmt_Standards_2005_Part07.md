## CHUNK 2: SECTION-4.2 - DEMAND AND SUPPLY SCHEDULING - DISTRIBUTION LICENSEE ADVANCE PLANNING AND SCHEDULING

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-4 Distribution System Management Standards → Subsection 4.2 Demand and Supply Scheduling

**PROVISION:** Section 4.2

**TITLE:** Demand and Supply Scheduling – Distribution Licensee Day-Ahead Demand Submission and SLDC Scheduling Coordination

**SUMMARY:** All Distribution Licensees, including licensees operating their own generating units (e.g., AECO), shall provide ¼-hourly MW/MVAR/maximum MWh requirement for their supply area to SLDC daily on day-ahead basis by 09:00 hours. SLDC produces a day-ahead ¼-hourly demand schedule consolidating distribution licensee data and accounting for ¼-hourly generation availability and planned drawal schedules agreed with WRLDC. The day-ahead demand schedule is prepared considering transmission system constraints and demand requirements. SLDC may require distribution licensees to maintain power factor at 0.9 and above. SLDC shall intimate the supply schedule for the following day to all distribution licensees by 18:00 hours.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Distribution Licensee Obligation; advance demand declaration
- Demand Forecast; ¼-hourly granularity
- MW/MVAR/MWh Requirement; 24-hour period specification
- Day-Ahead Basis; advance planning requirement
- SLDC Consolidation; demand aggregation
- Generation Coordination; generation availability constraint
- Transmission Constraints; system security consideration
- Power Factor; 0.9 minimum requirement
- Schedule Issuance; 18:00 hours deadline
- Own Generation; self-supply capability

**TEXT:**

**4.2 DEMAND AND SUPPLY SCHEDULING**

4.2.1 All Distribution Licensees (including Licensees having own Generating Units viz AECO) shall provide ¼ hourly MW / MVAR / maximum MWH requirement (00.00 – 24.00 hours) of their respective area of supply, to SLDC daily on day ahead basis by 09.00 hours.

4.2.2 SLDC shall produce a day ahead ¼ hourly demand schedule after consolidation of the data provided by the Distribution Licensee. It will take into account the ¼ hourly generation availability including latest planned drawal schedule agreed with WRLDC.

4.2.3 SLDC shall prepare the day ahead Demand schedule keeping in view the following:

(a) Transmission System constraints from time to time.

(b) ¼ hourly Demand requirements as estimated by SLDC.

4.2.4 SLDC may also require the Distribution Licensee to maintain the power factor at 0.9 and above.

4.2.5 SLDC shall intimate the supply schedule for the following day to all Distribution Licensees by 18.00 hours.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 2.3 (Generation Scheduling – supply-side coordination)
- Section 3.3 (SLDC load despatch – demand-side coordination)
- Section 4.7 (Load Management – demand reduction procedures)
- Grid Code (power factor and scheduling standards)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Distribution licensees provide demand forecasts; SLDC aggregates and schedules supply. The 09:00 deadline is firm. Power factor 0.9 minimum is a distribution licensee obligation.

## CHUNK 3: SECTION-4.3 - LOAD MONITORING - STATION LOGS, REGISTERS, AND HOURLY METERING

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-4 Distribution System Management Standards → Subsection 4.3 Load Monitoring

**PROVISION:** Section 4.3

**TITLE:** Load Monitoring – Station Log Sheets, Operational Registers, and Meter Reading Documentation

**SUMMARY:** Distribution licensees shall maintain station log sheets at 66 kV and higher-voltage substations documenting hourly readings of meters (current, load, voltage, etc.) and station operations. A separate register shall be maintained for daily energy meter readings for both energy received and energy supplied. This documentation provides the operational record for load monitoring, demand verification, and post-event analysis.

**KEY TOPICS:**

- Hourly Meter Readings; current, load, voltage
- Station Operations; operational event recording
- Energy Meter Register; energy received/sent tracking
- Daily Energy Reconciliation; supply accounting
- Record Maintenance; continuous documentation

**TEXT:**

**4.3 LOAD MONITORING**

4.3.1 Station Log Sheets for 66kV Substations and substations at higher than 66kV voltage and Registers for station operations duly recording the hourly readings of the meters such as current, load, voltage etc., and the station operations shall be maintained at each station. A separate register for the daily energy meter readings for both the energy received and energy sent out shall be maintained along with the above.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**

## CHUNK 4: SECTION-4.4 - LOAD BALANCING - UNBALANCED LOAD LIMITS AND MONITORING

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-4 Distribution System Management Standards → Subsection 4.4 Load Balancing

**PROVISION:** Section 4.4

**TITLE:** Load Balancing – Unbalanced Load Limits and Three-Phase Monitoring

**SUMMARY:** Distribution licensees shall limit unbalanced load on the LT side of distribution transformers to a maximum of 10% of peak load. Secondary currents and voltages of distribution transformers shall be recorded at least monthly during expected peak load hours across all three phases. This monitoring ensures balanced three-phase loading, minimizes losses, reduces neutral current, and maintains transformer operating efficiency.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Unbalanced Load Limit; 10% of peak load maximum
- LT Side Monitoring; secondary winding phase balance
- Distribution Transformers; step-down equipment
- Three-Phase Recording; current and voltage per phase
- Monthly Recording; frequency requirement
- Peak Load Hours; critical measurement period
- Neutral Current Reduction; balance objective
- Transformer Efficiency; loss minimization

**TEXT:**

**4.4 LOAD BALANCING**

4.4.1 As far as possible the Distribution Licensee shall limit the unbalanced load on the LT side of the distribution transformers within 10% of the peak load.

4.4.2 The secondary currents and voltages of the distribution transformers shall be recorded at least once a month during expected peak load hours on all the phases.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 4.3 (Load Monitoring)
- Section 4.5 (Voltage Monitoring and Control)
- Section 4.6 (Data Logging)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** The 10% unbalance limit is operational. Three-phase phase recording at peak load reveals imbalance patterns requiring rebalancing action.

## CHUNK 5: SECTION-4.5 - VOLTAGE MONITORING AND CONTROL - DATA LOGGING AND VOLTAGE BAND MAINTENANCE

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-4 Distribution System Management Standards → Subsection 4.5 Voltage Monitoring and Control

**PROVISION:** Section 4.5

**TITLE:** Voltage Monitoring and Control – Real-Time 11 kV Monitoring and LT Voltage Maintenance

**SUMMARY:** Voltage monitoring at each substation feeding the 11 kV distribution system shall be carried out via data logging equipment. Voltage monitoring on the LT side of distribution transformers shall be conducted at a minimum of two transformers per 11 kV feeder: one at the beginning and one at the tail-end. Voltage improvements shall be achieved by operating 22/11 kV substation tap changers where available, and by contacting transmission licensee operators at connection/interface points to adjust sending-end voltage. This two-level voltage control (local and remote) maintains customer voltage within specification.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- 11 kV Monitoring; primary distribution voltage
- Data Logging; continuous voltage recording
- LT Side Monitoring; secondary distribution voltage
- Transformer Sampling; two per feeder minimum
- Feeder Coverage; beginning and tail-end locations
- Voltage Tap Control; substation transformer taps
- Remote Coordination; transmission interface voltage adjustment
- Communication Protocol; operator contact procedures
- Voltage Band Specification; customer supply limits

**TEXT:**

**4.5 VOLTAGE MONITORING AND CONTROL**

4.5.1 The Voltage monitoring at each Substation feeding 11 kV Distribution System shall be carried out by data logging.

4.5.2 The Voltage monitoring on the LT side of the distribution transformers shall be carried out by the Distribution Licensee as deemed necessary to monitor the voltage levels for at least two numbers of transformers in each 11 kV feeder as follows:

(a) One Transformer at the beginning of the feeder.

(b) One Transformer at the tail end of the feeder.

4.5.3 Improvement to voltage conditions shall be achieved by operating tap changer wherever available in 22 / 11 kV substations and by contacting over telephone the operators of Transmission Licensee at the Connection Point / Interface Point, to correct Voltage at the sending end whenever required.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 4.4 (Load Balancing – voltage effects on unbalance)
- Section 4.6 (Data Logging – voltage data requirements)
- Section 3.6 (Transmission Voltage Management – 22/11 kV control)
- Grid Code (voltage supply standards)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Two-level voltage control: local tap changers at 22/11 kV, remote coordination at transmission interface. Monitoring at feeder beginning and end captures voltage drop across the feeder.

## CHUNK 6: SECTION-4.6 - DATA LOGGING - COMPREHENSIVE OPERATIONAL DATA RECORDING AND RECORD MAINTENANCE

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-4 Distribution System Management Standards → Subsection 4.6 Data Logging

**PROVISION:** Section 4.6

**TITLE:** Data Logging – Hourly Logging of Key Parameters and Centralized Record Management

**SUMMARY:** All important operational data—voltage, current, power factor, MW, transformer tap position, transformer oil/winding temperature—shall be logged on hourly basis at all substations 66 kV and above feeding distribution lines. Records shall be maintained (at centralized location or each substation per licensee practice) including: operation/maintenance manuals, maintenance registers for equipment and batteries, interruption registers, line clearance registers, and equipment registers. A detailed analysis of logged data shall be conducted regularly to assess equipment performance, identify overloading conditions, and determine major maintenance necessity. This systematic data management enables predictive maintenance and performance optimization.

- Equipment Registers; asset inventory

**TEXT:**

**4.6 DATA LOGGING**

4.6.1 All-important data such as Voltage, Current, Power factor, MW, Transformer data such as tap position, oil/winding temperature, etc shall be logged on hourly basis in all Substations 66kV and above feeding distribution lines.

4.6.2 The following records among others shall be maintained for each Substation either at centralised location or at each Substation as per the practices of Distribution Licensee. However, the list is suggestive but not exhaustive:

(a) Operation and maintenance manuals for the equipment and the entire station consisting of the details of operation of the station and maintenance of equipment,

(b) Maintenance registers for the equipment and station batteries,

(c) Interruption Registers,

(d) Line Clearance Register,

(e) Equipment registers.

4.6.3 A detailed analysis of the above data shall be made to assess the performance of the equipment, overloading conditions and the necessity for major maintenance.

## CHUNK 10: SECTION-4.10 - MAINTENANCE - COMPREHENSIVE MAINTENANCE FRAMEWORK FOR DISTRIBUTION SYSTEMS

**DOCUMENT:** Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Gujarat Power System Management Standards) 2005

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation / Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Document → Section-4 Distribution System Management Standards → Subsection 4.10 Maintenance

**PROVISION:** Section 4.10 (Subsections 4.10.1 through 4.10.9)

**TITLE:** Maintenance – Maintenance Planning, Execution, and Continuous Improvement Framework

**SUMMARY:** Distribution licensees shall prepare maintenance manuals and programs for various distribution system components. Proper records shall be maintained documenting scheduled maintenance work, equipment faults/malfunctions, remedial actions, and component-level maintenance history. Maintenance prerequisites include: (1) system ability to handle overload during emergencies, (2) material quality, (3) trained and equipped staff, and (4) maintenance schedules. Maintenance work consists of routine inspection/cleaning/testing/adjustments (preventive maintenance) distinguished from breakdown repairs. Maintenance schedules shall cover inspection, preventive maintenance, and overhauls for all components: distribution transformers 500 KVA+, pole-mounted transformers/capacitors, 22/11 kV circuit breakers, LT breakers, pole-mounted reclosers, distribution lines, LT lines/switches/fuses, and service connections. Effective maintenance includes: pre- and post-season inspections, immediate defect rectification, abnormal condition disconnection, manufacturer instruction compliance, continuous test result documentation, maintenance history tracking, safety precaution use, and schedule periodic review. Off-schedule inspections include: special inspections post-severe weather, emergency inspections during breakdowns, follow-up inspections for temporary faults, and check inspections by engineers. This comprehensive framework ensures distribution system reliability and customer service quality.

**4.10 MAINTENANCE**

4.10.1 The Distribution Licensee, for the guidance of the operation and maintenance staff shall prepare suitable maintenance manuals and programs for the various components of the Distribution System. Proper records shall be kept duly updating the maintenance work done as per schedule, the details of faults, malfunctions etc., encountered in the lines and equipment during the period, the remedial action taken, etc., for each component of the distribution system.

4.10.2 The following pre-requisites shall be first ensured for the satisfactory maintenance:

(a) The ability of the system to meet the probable over-loading due to transfer of loads from the adjacent systems during emergencies.

(b) The quality of the materials used.

(c) Trained and adequately equipped maintenance staff.

(d) Schedule of maintenance for each component of the system.

4.10.3 The maintenance work shall consist of routine inspection, cleaning, testing and adjustments, if any required and is different from the work carried out after a break down of any equipment in service, for restoring the same to the working condition, which cannot be planned in advance.

4.10.4 The maintenance schedules drawn shall cover the following:

(a) Inspection

(b) Preventive maintenance

(c) Overhauls

4.10.5 Inspection

(a) This shall include the periodical inspection in service for a check on the condition of the equipments/lines in service to verify the faults and defects that may develop during its operation so that advance action can be taken to rectify the defects in a planned manner and prevent breakdowns.

4.10.6 Preventive maintenance

(a) This shall cover the periodical work including tests required to determine the electrical and mechanical strengths to ascertain the suitability in service and ensure proper working condition. The schedule drawn shall be on the basis of data obtained from inspection and maintenance checks, giving priority to the defects encountered during operation of the line or equipment.

4.10.7 Overhauls

(a) This shall cover the preventive maintenance work to be done on the equipment based on the past experience and manufacturers' recommendations and involves major disassembly of the equipments. The schedule drawn shall be based on the normal life expectancy of the equipment or data obtained from inspection and maintenance checks.

(b) The maintenance schedules shall be drawn for all the following components of the Distribution System separately:

(i) Power transformers and distribution transformers of 500 KVA and above.

(ii) Pole mounted distribution transformers and capacitors.

(iii) 22 and 11 kV circuit breakers along with all the associated switchgear.

(iv) LT circuit breakers.

(v) Pole mounted auto-reclosers.

(vi) 22 and 11 kV distribution lines including G.O.S.

(vii) LT lines including switches and fuses.

(viii) The Licensee‟s authorised representative including meter reader should report the visible abnormalities of service connection to the appropriate authority for taking corrective action.

4.10.8 Effective maintenance work shall be ensured keeping the following guidelines for the efficient working of the distribution system and for preventing accidents that may arise due to failure of any of the components. However, the following guidelines are suggestive but not exhaustive.

(a) The distribution lines shall be inspected thoroughly before and after the rainy season.

(b) The defects noticed during inspection should be rectified at the time of inspection itself if they are of minor nature, whenever and wherever possible. In case of such of the defects, which cannot be rectified easily, the same have to be attended to at the earliest possible occasion duly chalking out a program in advance.

(c) If abnormal conditions such as excessive heating or arcing or prohibitively low clearances etc., are observed, the equipment or the line shall be immediately disconnected and rectification of defects carried out.

(d) Manufacturers' instructions shall always be given due consideration and implemented.

(e) A continuous record of all the test results shall be maintained.

(f) Appropriate inspection/maintenance checks/history sheets shall be maintained containing details of all inspection and maintenance work done.

(g) All the required safety precautions/safety devices shall be used while carrying out the maintenance works.

(h) The maintenance schedule shall be periodically reviewed by the Distribution Licensee in the light of previous experience and updated to include all possible improvements required for ensuring adequate maintenance, prevention of accidents and reduction in interruptions.

4.10.9 Off-schedule inspections

Inspections of the following nature shall be carried out to maintain the system at the required level of reliability in operation.

(a) Special inspections: These shall be made immediately after severe weather conditions, such as heavy windstorms, thunderstorms and rains to detect any damage or breakage of poles, insulators, conductors and/or equipment, and necessary action taken.

(b) Emergency inspections: These shall be carried out on a line during its breakdown, to locate and identify the cause of trouble as early as possible in order to restore the power supply.

(c) Follow up inspections: Whenever one or more short time interruptions are noticed which may have taken place due to temporary faults, the inspection shall be carried out to locate and identify the cause of interruptions and suitable maintenance action shall be taken whenever and wherever necessary.

(d) Check inspections: The designated engineer in charge of the distribution system shall make these inspections periodically as a check on the conditions of the line and equipment and the efficacy of maintenance. He shall point out such defects, which might not have been noticed by the maintenance staff in the first instance.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:**
- Section 4.3-4.6 (Monitoring and data logging basis for maintenance)
- Section 4.9 (Safety Coordination – maintenance safety)
- "Standard of Performance of Licensee" (GERC, separate document)
- Section 3.17-3.23 (Transmission Maintenance – comparable framework)

**KEY INSIGHT FOR ENERGY BOT:** Distribution maintenance is hierarchical: inspection → preventive maintenance → overhauls. Manufacturer instructions and safety integration are non-negotiable. Continuous improvement review is mandatory.
