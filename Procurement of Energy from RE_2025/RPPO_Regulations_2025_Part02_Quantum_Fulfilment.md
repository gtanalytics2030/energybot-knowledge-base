**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 4(1)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 4(1) - Quantum of RPPO - Table-1 Minimum Purchase Percentages

**TITLE:** Regulation 4(1): Minimum Quantum of Purchase from Renewable Sources (Table-1)

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 4(1) establishes the minimum quantum of electricity that obligated entities must purchase or generate from renewable sources during each financial year. The regulation prescribes specific percentages for six renewable energy categories: Wind Renewable Energy, Hydro Renewable Energy, Distributed Renewable Energy, Other Renewable Energy, and Total Renewable Energy. The quantum is worked out based on Table-1, which specifies minimum purchase percentages from FY 2024-25 through FY 2029-30. Total renewable energy targets escalate from 29.91% in 2024-25 to 43.33% in 2029-30.

**KEY TOPICS:** RPPO Quantum, Minimum Purchase, Renewable Energy Percentage, Wind Energy, Hydro Power, Distributed Renewable, Other Renewable, Financial Year Targets, Energy Obligation Escalation, Obligated Entity

**TEXT:**

4. Quantum of Renewable Power Purchase Obligation (RPPO)

(1) The Obligated Entity shall, during each year, purchase or generate and consume such quantum of electricity (in kWh) from renewable sources and storage not less than the quantum of electricity (in kWh), worked out as per Table-1 and Table-2 given below:

Note 1: The wind renewable energy component shall be met by energy produced from Wind Power Projects (WPPs) commissioned after the 31st March, 2024.

Note 2: The hydro renewable energy component shall be met only by energy produced from Hydro Power Projects [including Pump Storage Projects (PSPs) and Small Hydro Projects (SHPs)], commissioned after the 31st March, 2024:

Provided that the hydro renewable energy component may also be met out of the free power being provided to the State/DISCOM from the Hydro Power Projects commissioned after the 31st March, 2024:

Provided further that the hydro renewable energy component may also be met from Hydro Power Projects located outside India as approved by the Central Government on a case- to- case basis.

Note 3: The distributed renewable energy component shall be met only from the energy generated from renewable energy projects that are less than 10 MW in size and shall include solar installations under all configurations notified by the Central Government:

## Chunk 13

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 4(2)-(4)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 4(2)-(4) - RPPO Inter-Category Adjustments

**TITLE:** Regulation 4(2)-(4): Adjustments and Flexibility in RPPO Category Compliance

**SUMMARY:**
Regulations 4(2) through 4(4) provide flexibility in meeting individual category targets. Shortfalls in wind renewable energy can be offset by excess hydro renewable energy in the same year, and vice versa. Excess wind or hydro renewable energy in one category can be counted toward the "Other Renewable Energy" category. Conversely, excess in the "Other Renewable Energy" component can be used to meet shortfalls in wind or hydro renewable energy targets. These provisions allow obligated entities to optimize their renewable energy portfolio while maintaining aggregate compliance.

**KEY TOPICS:** Inter-Category Adjustment, Wind Hydro Flexibility, Excess Energy Utilization, Shortfall Offset, Category Balancing, Other Renewable Energy Adjustment, Optimization, Energy Interchange, Portfolio Flexibility

**TEXT:**

(2) Any shortfall in achievement of stipulated wind renewable energy consumption in a particular year maybe met with hydro renewable energy which is in excess of that energy component for that year and vice- versa.

(3) The balance excess energy consumption under wind renewable energy or hydro renewable energy component in that year, may be considered as part of other renewable energy component.

(4) Any excess energy consumption under Other renewable energy component in a particular year, may be utilized to meet the shortfall in achievement of stipulated Wind renewable energy or Hydro renewable energy consumption.

**PROVISION:** Regulation 4(1)(i)-(v) - Energy Storage Obligation (Table-2) and Applicability

**TITLE:** Regulation 4(1)(i)-(v): Energy Storage Obligation and Conditions

**SUMMARY:**
Energy storage obligation (Table-2) requires obligated entities to ensure that a minimum percentage of total energy consumed (1.0% in 2024-25, escalating to 3.5% in 2029-30) includes renewable energy stored in energy storage systems. At least 85% of total energy stored on an annual basis must be procured from renewable energy sources. Energy storage from renewable sources counts toward fulfillment of total RPPO. Minimum percentages for each RPPO category must be met separately. These provisions apply through March 31, 2030, with continuation of 2029-30 targets if new targets are not issued.

**KEY TOPICS:** Energy Storage Obligation, Storage System, Battery Storage, Renewable Energy Storage, Storage Percentage, Energy Storage Requirement, 85% Renewable Threshold, Annual Storage Basis, Renewable Integration, Storage Technology

**TEXT:**

(i) The following percentage of total energy consumed shall be renewable energy along with/through storage as provided in Table-2 below.

2029-30 | 3.5%

## Chunk 15

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 4(7)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 4(7) - Consumption Calculation Methodology

**TITLE:** Regulation 4(7): Computation of Obligated Entity Consumption

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 4(7) specifies how to calculate the consumption of obligated entities for RPPO purposes. Consumption includes: (i) total energy purchased/consumed from various sources (PPAs, exchanges, other projects); (ii) energy consumed from power plants owned exclusively or jointly by the entity, including standby sets; (iii) applicable transmission and distribution losses for power delivery; (iv) exclusion of energy sales (with associated T&D losses); (v) special treatment of banking arrangements. Proper T&D loss accounting is critical to accurate RPPO compliance calculations.

**KEY TOPICS:** Consumption Calculation, Transmission and Distribution Losses, Energy Purchase, Self-Generation, Banking Arrangement, Power Purchase Agreement, Energy Exchange, Loss Accounting, T&D Losses, Auxiliary Consumption, Standby Generation

**TEXT:**

(7) The consumption of the Obligated Entity shall be computed by taking into account the following, namely:-

(i) the total energy purchased/ consumed from various sources, inter alia, including the purchases under Power Purchase Agreement(s), through energy exchanges, and from other projects etc.

(ii) the energy consumed (excluding auxiliary consumption) from the power plants, owned exclusively or jointly, by it. This shall include the standby generating set (s) also.

(iii) the applicable Transmission and Distribution losses (T&D losses for short) for conveyance of power from the point of purchase/ generation to the point of consumption in relation to Clause (i) and (ii) shall be considered as part of consumption by the obligated entity while deciding the RPPO and its fulfilment;

(iv) the sale of energy out of the energy so purchased/generated, inter alia, including transmission and distribution losses borne by it for conveyance of power from the point of purchase/generation to the point of such sale, shall be excluded:

Provided that in case it is not feasible to identify such losses separately, the average T&D losses as per Clause (iii) shall be considered.

(v) in case of the banking arrangement(s), the energy banked/ returned by the Distribution Licensee shall be considered as sale under Clause (iv) and the energy received shall be treated as purchased under Clause (i) and (iii).

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 4(1) (RPPO quantum); Regulation 5 (Fulfilment); Regulation 8 (Registration and reporting); Regulation 9 (Compliance monitoring); Tariff Regulations (T&D loss determination)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** T&D loss accounting in tariff orders; SLDC scheduling and banking practices; Power exchange trading; Open access wheeling charges and losses

## Chunk 16

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 4(8)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 4(8) - Applicability to Open Access and Non-DISCOM Consumers

**TITLE:** Regulation 4(8): RPPO for Open Access Consumers and Non-Licensee Generators

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 4(8) extends RPPO applicability to any person consuming power from sources other than a distribution licensee or captive plant, including those using open access. Such consumers must comply with the RPPO targets specified in Table-1 and storage obligations in Table-2 for their consumption from such sources. A key provision states that unless an obligated entity proves that energy was procured from renewable sources, it is presumed to come from conventional (non-renewable) sources. This shifts the burden of proof to the obligated entity.

**KEY TOPICS:** Open Access Consumer, Non-Distribution Consumption, Burden of Proof, Renewable Energy Assumption, Presumption of Conventional Source, Third-Party Generation, Evidence of RE Procurement, Obligated Entity Obligation, Power Verification

**TEXT:**

(8) Any person/consumer, who consumes power from any source (generation/purchase), interalia, including purchase through Open Access, but other than in his capacity as a consumer of Distribution Licensee or by consumption from a Captive Generating Plant, the RPPOs provided at the Table-1 and storage Obligation at Table-2 under this Regulation shall be applicable in respect of his consumption from such sources, subject to provisions under these Regulations.

Provided that the energy purchased/generated and consumed by Obligated Entity, shall be considered to have been arranged from the sources other than the RE Sources, unless such Obligated Entity establishes to the satisfaction of the State Agency that such energy was availed from RE Sources.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 2 (Definitions - Open Access, Obligated Entity); Regulation 4(1) (RPPO Targets in Table-1 and Table-2); Regulation 5 (Fulfilment mechanisms); Regulation 7 (State Agency role); Regulation 8 (Registration and reporting)

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 4(9)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 4(9) - Commission's Discretion to Revise Targets

**TITLE:** Regulation 4(9): Commission's Powers for Suo-Motu or Request-Based Revision of RPPO

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 4(9) grants the Commission discretionary authority to revise RPPO percentage targets or permit inter-category adjustments beyond those specified in Regulation 4(1), either suo-motu or upon request from obligated entities. Such revisions may be made if the Commission determines that obligated entities face power supply constraints, factors beyond their control, or for cogent reasons. The revision must account for the non-availability of renewable energy or renewable energy certificates in specific RPPO categories for one or more years.

**KEY TOPICS:** Commission Discretion, Target Revision, Suo-Motu Order, Category Adjustment, Power Supply Constraint, Non-Availability of RE, Obligated Entity Relief, Regulatory Flexibility, Force Majeure, Extraordinary Circumstances

**TEXT:**

(9) The Commission may, keeping in view the power supply constraints or other factors beyond the control of the Obligated Entity(ies) or for any cogent reasons, Suo-Moto or at the request of an Obligated Entity, revise the percentage targets or allow inter category adjustment over and above those permissible under Regulation 4 of these Regulations taking into account the non-availability of such renewable energy or RE certificates in respect of any one or more categories of the RPPO, for a year(s) for which Renewable Power Purchase Obligations have been fixed as per Regulation 4 of these Regulations.

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5(1) - Fulfilment of RPPO - Yearly and Quarterly Basis

**TITLE:** Regulation 5(1): Obligated Entity's Requirement to Meet RPPO Annually and Quarterly

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5(1) mandates that each obligated entity must meet renewable power purchase obligations separately for each category (Wind Energy, Hydro Power, Distributed Renewable Energy, Other Renewable Energy, and Energy Storage) on a yearly basis. The regulation encourages quarterly compliance achievement, though yearly compliance is the binding requirement. This structure ensures consistent renewable energy procurement throughout the fiscal year.

**KEY TOPICS:** RPPO Compliance, Yearly Obligation, Quarterly Endeavor, Category-Wise Compliance, Wind Energy Compliance, Hydro Power Compliance, Distributed Renewable Compliance, Energy Storage Compliance, Quarterly Reporting, Financial Year Compliance

(1) Each of the Obligated Entity shall meet, on yearly basis, the RPPOs separately under each of the categories (i.e. for Wind Energy, Hydro Power Purchase (HPO), Distributed Renewable Energy, Other Renewable Energy as well as Renewable Energy along with storage as specified in the Regulation 4 of these Regulations:

Provided that the Obligated Entity shall endeavour to meet RPPOs under each category on quarterly basis.

## Chunk 19

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5(2)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5(2) - Methods of Meeting RPPO

**TITLE:** Regulation 5(2): Mechanisms for Meeting RPPO Targets

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5(2) specifies five primary mechanisms through which obligated entities can meet their RPPO: (i) purchase of renewable energy from generating stations; (ii) purchase from persons or platforms facilitating power exchange from RE sources; (iii) purchase of free power/royalty from state government (subject to limitations); (iv) energy generated from their own RE sources; (v) receipt of energy under banking agreements from RE sources. T&D losses incurred in procurement count toward RPPO fulfillment. Obligated entities may also redeem previously held certificates. Energy sold out of RE availability does not offset RPPO. Rooftop solar PV under net/gross metering arrangements count toward RPPO. Green hydrogen/ammonia production from renewable energy counts toward RPPO compliance.

**KEY TOPICS:** RPPO Fulfilment Methods, Direct Procurement, Renewable Energy Generation, Banking, Free Power, Royalty Power, Energy Exchange, Certificate Redemption, T&D Losses, Rooftop Solar, Net Metering, Green Hydrogen, REC Mechanism Exclusion

**TEXT:**

(2) The Obligated Entity may meet its Renewable Power Purchase Obligation (RPPO), as specified in Regulation 4 of these Regulations, from the renewable sources relevant to respective categories of RPPOs, including the following, namely. -

(i) purchase of energy from generating stations, based on RE Sources;

(ii) purchase of energy from any other person, or any platform facilitating exchange of power, which would arise from RE Sources;

(iii) purchase of free power (royalty) of the State Government by the Distribution Licensee depending upon the renewable source from where such free power has arisen subject to the condition and limitation as may be prescribed by the Central Government from time to time;

(iv) the energy generated from its own RE Sources, if any; and

(v) receipt of energy under banking arrangement(s) which has, as per the agreement(s) arisen from RE Sources:

Provided that the applicable T&D losses borne by the Obligated Entity in relation to procurement of energy shall also be included for computing the quantum of energy procured for each of such sources:

Provided further that the Obligated Entity may also meet RPPOs by redeeming, wherever permissible, the certificates already held by it:

Provided further that the renewable energy sold, if any, by the Obligated Entity to any other entity out of availability as per Clause (i) to (v) above as well as the T&D losses borne by the Obligated Entity from a point of purchase/generation to the point of such sale, shall not qualify for offsetting its RPPOs:

Provided further that the electricity consumed by the Obligated Entity from the projects set up under REC mechanism as per Regulation 10 of these Regulations shall not qualify for offsetting RPPOs:

Provided further that the quantum of electricity consumed by the Consumer/Prosumer through rooftop solar PV system under net metering/net billing/group metering arrangements in any year, shall qualify towards compliance for RPPO of the Distribution Licensee or the Consumer/Prosumer, as the case may be, for that year in accordance with the provisions contained in Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016 as amended from time to time.

Provided further that in accordance with Ministry of Power, Govt of India letter no. 23/02/2022-R&R, dated 17.02.2022, the renewable energy consumed for the production of Green Hydrogen/Green Ammonia shall be counted towards RPO compliance of consuming entity. The renewable energy consumed beyond obligation of the consumer shall count towards RPPO compliance of the DISCOM in whose area the project is located.

## Chunk 20

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 5(3)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 5(3) - Shortfall Remedy and Certificate Multiplier

**TITLE:** Regulation 5(3): Addressing RPPO Shortfalls and Certificate Conversion

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 5(3) addresses situations where obligated entities expect shortfalls in meeting category-specific RPPO targets. Shortfalls must be remedied by procuring additional renewable energy or certificates from appropriate renewable technology sources before the fiscal year closes. Shortfalls in one category may be offset by surplus in another category (as per Regulation 4(2)-(4)). If CERC REC Regulations provide for inter-category certificate convertibility using multipliers, those multipliers apply to calculate equivalent energy conversion. Certificates from other RPPO categories can be purchased using the multiplier to offset category-specific shortfalls.

**KEY TOPICS:** Shortfall Remedy, Category Offset, Energy Procurement, REC Purchase, Certificate Multiplier, Inter-Category Conversion, Compliance Deadline, CERC Regulations, Energy Conversion Factor, Obligated Entity Obligation

**TEXT:**

(3) In case the Obligated Entity expects any shortfall in meeting RPPOs of one or more categories in a year, it shall meet such shortfall by procuring energy, or certificates, from RE Sources based on respective technology (ies) before close of that year:

Provided that the shortfall for a particular category of RPPOs in a year may be offset, to the extent permissible under Table-1 & 2 as provided in Sub- regulation (4) of Regulation 4 of these Regulations, by adjustment of surplus availability under other RPPO category:

Provided further that in case the CERC (REC) Regulations, 2022 or the Procedure made thereunder, provide for inter category convertibility of certificates on the basis of Certificate multipliers such multipliers shall be applicable for offsetting the shortfalls or computing surplus, and shortfall in any RRPO category may be met by purchasing certificates related to Other RPPO categories by suitably applying such Certificate multiplier approved by the Central Commission.
