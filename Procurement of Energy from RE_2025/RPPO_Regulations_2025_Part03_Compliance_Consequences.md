## Chunk 22

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 6(1)-(4)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 6(1)-(4) - Certificates under Central Commission Regulations

**TITLE:** Regulation 6(1)-(4): Role of RECs in RPPO Compliance

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 6 establishes that renewable energy certificates (RECs) issued under CERC REC Regulations 2022 and 2010 are valid instruments for discharging RPPO obligations. Obligated entities and state agencies must follow CERC REC regulations in matters relating to REC certificates. Certificate details purchased from power exchanges or electricity traders must be reported to the state agency. Obligated entities are eligible to apply for certificates for renewable energy generation that remains unadjusted or unsold within the year, following CERC REC procedures and the Commission's directions. The purchaser/user of electricity is exclusively entitled to offset RPPO through non-REC renewable energy purchases and obtain RECs for surpluses.

**KEY TOPICS:** Renewable Energy Certificate, REC, CERC Regulations, Certificate Trading, REC Issuance, Green Attributes, Obligated Entity Rights, Accreditation, Certificate Market, Energy Attribute Unbundling

**TEXT:**

6. Certificates under the Regulations of the Central Commission. –

(1) Subject to the terms and conditions contained in these Regulations, the Certificates issued under the CERC (REC) Regulations, 2022 and CERC (REC) Regulations, 2010 shall be the valid instruments for the discharge of RPPO.

(2) Further, subject to the provisions of these Regulations and directions as the Commission may give from time to time, the Obligated Entity as well as the State Agency shall act in accordance with the CERC (REC) Regulations, 2022 and CERC (REC) Regulations, 2010 in regard to matter relating to the REC Certificates for fulfilment of RPPO.

(3) The details of Certificates, alongwith its source, purchased by the Obligated Entities from the Power Exchanges or through Electricity Traders, in terms of the Regulations of the Central Commission mentioned in Sub-regulation (1), shall be submitted to the State Agency.

(4) The Obligated Entity shall be eligible to apply for the certificates, in accordance with the provisions of CERC REC Regulations, 2022 read with Procedure framed thereunder and these Regulations as well as the directions, if any, issued by the Commission, for such part of energy purchased/generated from the RE Sources of respective RPPO category which remains unadjusted/unsold in the year.

(5) The purchaser/user of electricity shall be exclusively entitled for offsetting the RPPO and getting RECs for surpluses, if any, purchase of renewable energy, other than the purchases under REC mechanism.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** CERC REC Regulations 2022; CERC REC Regulations 2010; Regulation 2 (Certificate definition); Regulation 5 (RPPO fulfilment through certificates); Regulation 11 (REC Mechanism Purchase); Power Market Regulations

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** REC issuance procedures; Green attributes; Certificate trading; Market mechanism; Obligated entity accreditation; Power exchange participation

## Chunk 23

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 6(6)-(9)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 6(6)-(9) - State Agency Role in REC Accreditation and Generator Eligibility

**TITLE:** Regulation 6(6)-(9): State Agency's REC Accreditation Functions and Generator Restrictions

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 6(6)-(9) outlines the state agency's critical role in REC administration. Upon application by an obligated entity, the state agency must verify entitlement and initiate accreditation process for certificate issuance. Renewable energy generators selling power to distribution licensees under PPAs (not REC mechanism) are ineligible for REC accreditation for that energy against which the DISCOM claims RPPO offset. State agencies must ensure no unauthorized REC permissions are granted to generators ineligible per Regulation 6(7), and must obtain certificates from supplying entities confirming that energy supplied has not been double-counted for RPPO purposes.

**KEY TOPICS:** State Agency Verification, REC Accreditation, Generator Eligibility, Power Purchase Agreement, Double Counting Prevention, Certificate Issuance Authority, Generator Rights, Energy Attribution, Compliance Certification

**TEXT:**

(6) On application by the Obligated Entity for issuance of certificate, the State Agency shall, after satisfying itself, about the entitlement of that Obligated Entity initiate the process with regard to the accreditation of the Obligated Entity for issuance of certificates.

(7) Renewable energy generator selling power to the Distribution Licensee under Power Purchase Agreement(s), other than those under REC mechanism, shall not be eligible for accreditation under the REC mechanism for issuance of Renewable Energy Certificates (hereinafter to be referred as "RECs") in respect of the renewable energy against which the distribution licensee is eligible to offset its RPPOs.

(8) The State Agency and the other concerned agency(ies) shall ensure that no permission is accorded under REC mechanism in favour of any renewable energy generator for accreditation under REC mechanism and also for issuance of RECs in respect of the energy for which they are not eligible for such accreditation as per the Sub-regulation (7) of this Regulation.

(9) The State Agency and the other concerned agency(ies) before according any permission, in favour of Renewable Energy Generator, for accreditation as well as issuance of RECs, shall also require to see that such generator has provided a Certificate from the concerned entity, to whom such generator is selling or otherwise, supplying energy from its Renewable Energy project, to the extent that such energy has not been/ shall not be, adjusted for offsetting the RPPOs for getting Certificate by such RE generator, as per the provisions of these Regulations.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 7 (State Agency designation and functions); CERC REC Regulations 2022; Regulation 6(7) - Exclusion of generators under PPAs; Power Purchase Agreements

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Generator accreditation procedures; Double-counting prevention mechanisms; Energy tracking and verification; CERC REC framework; State Agency authority

Regulation 7(1) designates the Gujarat Energy Development Agency (GEDA) of the Government of Gujarat as the State Agency responsible for RPPO administration under these regulations and the CERC REC Regulations 2022. **KEY TOPICS:** State Agency, Gujarat Energy Development Agency, GEDA, State Government, Agency Designation, Capability Requirements, Administrative Authority, Regulatory Delegation, Alternative Agency

7. State Agency. –

(1) The Gujarat Energy Development Agency, Government of Gujarat shall be the State Agency for the purpose of CERC (REC) Regulations, 2022 and these Regulations to cater the State specific issues without prejudice to the power and functions conferred under CERC (REC) Regulations, 2022:

Provided that the Commission may designate any other agency as State Agency after satisfying itself that such agency has a required capability of performing its functions provided under these Regulations and CERC (REC) Regulations, 2022 or procedure framed by the Central Agency under CERC REC Regulations, 2022.

## Chunk 25

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 7(2)-(5)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 7(2)-(5) - State Agency Functions

**TITLE:** Regulation 7(2)-(5): State Agency Operations and Responsibilities

**SUMMARY:**
State agencies must function according to CERC REC Regulations 2022, including associated procedures, in conjunction with these regulations and Commission directives. The state agency develops and maintains an RPPO web portal for obligated entity registration and RPPO compliance monitoring and reporting, formulating suitable procedures for portal operation. After portal development, the state agency declares it operational through public notice, coordinating with the national web portal. The state agency submits quarterly and yearly compliance status reports to the Commission in specified format, potentially recommending appropriate action if obligated entities fail to meet targets. The Commission determines and fixes state agency remuneration and charges for function discharge.

**KEY TOPICS:** State Agency Function, Web Portal Development, RPPO Registration, Compliance Monitoring, Reporting Procedure, Quarterly Report, Yearly Report, Operational Declaration, Public Notice, Performance Recommendation, Remuneration, Administrative Coordination

**TEXT:**

(2) The State Agency shall function as per the provisions of CERC (REC) Regulations, 2022 including the procedures framed under it, read with these Regulations and directions, if any, issued by the Commission from time to time.

(3) Development of web portal for monitoring of RPPO:

(i) The State Agency shall, in consultation with the Commission, develop and maintain RPPO Web-portal for registration by the Obligated Entities and for the compliance, monitoring and reporting etc. of RPPOs and simultaneously formulate suitable Procedures for smooth functioning of Web-portal in relation to such activities.

(ii) The State Agency shall, after the RPPO web-portal is developed and Procedures are formulated under Clause 7 (3) (i), through public notices, declare the Web-portal to be operative:

Provided that such development shall be suitably coordinated with the facility available/planned under the National Web-portal developed for the purpose.

(4) RPPO compliance reporting:

(i) The State Agency shall, submit on quarterly as well as yearly basis status report to the Commission in respect of compliance of Renewable Power Purchase Obligation by the Obligated Entity (ies) in the format at Appendix–I of these Regulations. The reporting format may be modified by the Commission from time to time.

(ii) The State Agency may also recommend appropriate action to the Commission, if required, for compliance of the Renewable Power Purchase Obligation.

(5) The Commission may from time to time fix the remuneration and charges payable to the State Agency for discharge of its functions under these Regulations and CERC (REC) Regulations, 2022.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** CERC REC Regulations 2022; Regulation 8 (Obligated entity registration and reporting); Regulation 9 (Compliance and monitoring); Appendix-I (Reporting format); National Web Portal Framework

## Chunk 26

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 8(1)-(3)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 8(1)-(3) - Obligated Entity Registration and Data Submission

**TITLE:** Regulation 8(1)-(3): Obligated Entity Registration and Quarterly Reporting Requirements

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 8(1) mandates that obligated entities, including those already registered offline, must register online on the RPPO web portal within three months of portal declaration or when becoming obligated entities, whichever is later. Registration must be accompanied by requisite information on quarterly and annual basis per procedures established by the state agency. Regulation 8(2) grants obligated entities exclusive rights to adjust energy consumed from renewable sources in any sequence to meet category-specific RPPOs and obtain certificates for surplus renewable procurement. Regulation 8(3) requires obligated entities to submit details of total consumption, renewable energy purchases/consumption, certificates procured, shortfalls, and compliance plans quarterly (before end of sixth week of succeeding quarter) and annually (by May 15 of succeeding year) until the RPPO web portal becomes fully operational.

**KEY TOPICS:** Online Registration, RPPO Web Portal, Obligated Entity Registration, Quarterly Reporting, Annual Reporting, Energy Adjustment, Category-Wise Compliance, Certificate Tracking, Shortfall Justification, Renewable Procurement Plan

**TEXT:**

8. Registration and reporting by the Obligated Entities. –

(1) The Obligated Entities, including those already registered off line with the State Agency, shall mandatorily register themselves online on RPPO Web- portal within three months from the date on which the RPPO Web-portal is declared, under Clause (ii) of Sub-regulation (3) of Regulation 7 to be operative, or from the date on which the entity qualifies for being an Obligated Entity under these Regulations, whichever is later, and shall also furnish requisite information, on quarterly and annual basis as per the Procedure formulated by the State Agency under Clause (i) of Sub- regulation (3) of Regulation 7.

(2) The Obligated Entity shall have the exclusive right to adjust, in any sequence, the energy consumed by it from RE Sources, as per Sub- regulation (2) of Regulation 5 of these Regulations for meeting its RPPOs for relevant category and also for getting certificates for the surplus procurement of renewable energy, if any, after offsetting the RPPOs.

(3) The Obligated Entities shall submit, online, necessary details, duly taking into account the provisions specified under Regulations 4 and 5 of these Regulations, regarding total consumption of electricity, power purchased from RE Sources or Renewable Energy Certificate(s) procured and the shortfall, or surpluses as the case may be, in meeting the RPPOs along with the reasons for shortfalls, if any, and the plans for fulfilment of RPPO as well as any other information as the State Agency may require, on quarterly basis before the end of the sixth week of the succeeding quarter and annual consolidated report on or before the 15th May of the succeeding year:

Provided that till such time the RPPO Web-portal becomes fully operative under Clause (ii) of Sub-regulations (3) of Regulation 7, such information shall be submitted in physical form to the State Agency.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 7(3) (Web portal development and declaration); Regulation 4 (RPPO targets); Regulation 5 (Fulfilment mechanisms); Regulation 8(4)-(6) (Verification and publication); Appendix-I (Reporting formats)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Web portal registration procedures; Online data submission; Quarterly compliance deadlines; Annual filing deadlines; Category-wise energy adjustment

## Chunk 28

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 9

**PROVISION:** Regulation 9 - Compliance and Monitoring

**TITLE:** Regulation 9: Obligated Entity Compliance Filing and Reporting

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 9 establishes the compliance and monitoring framework. Obligated entities procuring/consuming power above 1 MW from conventional sources must file a compliance petition with the Commission after the financial year ends, providing RPPO fulfillment details in specified formats before June 30 of the succeeding year. Obligated entities below 1 MW must provide details to the state agency, which then files the collective compliance petition on their behalf before June 30. All obligated entities must upload renewable energy purchase and RPO compliance status quarterly on their websites; state agencies must also upload compliance details from obligated entities on their sites. Compliance formats are part of the regulations (Appendix-I) and may be revised by the Commission as required.

**KEY TOPICS:** Compliance Petition, Obligated Entity Filing, Financial Year Compliance, RPPO Fulfillment, Quarterly Publication, Compliance Format, State Agency Petition, Website Disclosure, Reporting Deadline, June 30 Filing

**TEXT:**

9. Compliance and Monitoring:

The Obligated Entities who are procuring/consuming electricity from sources other than renewable energy sources having capacity of above 1 MW shall require to file a Petition before the Commission after completion of respective financial year providing the details of the Renewable Purchase Obligation fulfilled by them in compliance to the provisions of this Regulations prior to 30th June of the succeeding financial year. The obligated entities shall also require to provide the details for compliance of RPPO in specified format which are part of these Regulations provided in Appendix-1 on quarterly basis. Such format may be revised by the Commission as may require from time to time.

Provided that in case of the Obligated Entity procuring/consuming power below 1 MW from sources other than renewable energy sources, shall provide the details of RPPO compliance by them to the State Agency. In such case, the State Agency shall require to file RPPO compliance Petition before the Commission on completion of respective financial year providing the details of the Renewable Purchase Obligation fulfilled by such obligated entities in compliance to the provisions of these Regulations prior to 30th June of the succeeding financial year.

Provided further that the Obligated Entity shall upload the details of Renewable Energy Purchase and status of RPO Compliance on quarterly basis on its website. The State Nodal Agency shall also upload the RPO Compliance details submitted by the Obligated Entity on its Website.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 4 (RPPO targets); Regulation 8 (Registration and reporting); Regulation 10 (Consequences of default); Appendix-I (Compliance reporting formats); Electricity Act 2003 Section 86

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Annual compliance petitions; Quarterly reporting cycles; Website publication timelines; June 30 submission deadline; Aggregate compliance reporting by state agencies

## Chunk 29

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 10(1)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 10(1) - Penalty for RPPO Non-Compliance

**TITLE:** Regulation 10(1): Penalty Computation for Renewable Energy Shortfall

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 10(1) addresses the consequence of obligated entity failure to fulfill RPPO and not purchasing certificates to cover shortfalls. The Commission may direct the obligated entity to pay an additional penalty for shortfall in renewable energy consumption targets, calculated as twice the TOE (Tonne of Oil Equivalent) value per unit of shortfall, as specified in Energy Conservation Act 2001 Section 26(3). The illustration shows: 1 TOE = 11,630 kWh; TOE Value (as of 26.12.2023) = INR 21,650; therefore, Penalty per kWh = 2 × (21,650 / 11,630) = INR 3.72 (rounded). Penalty funds are deposited into a dedicated account created and maintained by the obligated entity or state agency, to be used (as Commission directs) partly for certificate purchase and partly for transmission/sub-transmission infrastructure for renewable evacuation. Obligated entities cannot use penalty funds without prior Commission approval; the Commission may empower state agency officers to purchase certificates from power exchanges on behalf of the obligated entity using available penalty funds.

**KEY TOPICS:** Penalty, RPPO Shortfall, Tonne of Oil Equivalent, TOE Value, Non-Compliance Fine, Penalty Calculation, Financial Consequence, Energy Conservation Act, Penalty Fund, Certificate Purchase, Infrastructure Development, Evacuation Infrastructure

**TEXT:**

10. Consequences of default. –

(1) Where, during any year, the Obligated Entity does not fulfil the Renewable Purchase Obligation as provided in these Regulations and also does not purchase the certificates, the Commission may direct the Obligated Entity to pay the additional penalty for shortfall in specified renewable energy consumption targets as per sub-section (3) of Section 26 of The Energy Conservation Act in terms of twice TOE value.

Provided that value of TOE value shall be considered as per the MoP notification in force on the date of determination of such penalty amount.

Illustration
1. Computation of Penalty as per TOE value specified by Ministry of Power Notification in force. 1 TOE = 11,630 KWh,
2. Value of 1 TOE: INR 21,650.00 (As per MoP Gazette Notification dated 26.12.2023),
3. Penalty per 1 unit (KWh) of unmet RPO1:
2 x INR 21,650/11,630 KWh = INR 3.72 (Rounded off)

The penalty amount as the Commission may determine on the basis of the shortfall in units of RPPO, be deposited into a separate fund, to be created and maintained by such Obligated Entity or State Agency as the case may be.

Provided that the fund so created shall be utilised, as may be directed by the Commission partly for purchase of the certificates and partly for development of transmission/sub-transmission infrastructure for evacuation of power from generating stations based on RE Sources:

Provided further that the Obligated Entities shall not be authorized to use the fund created in pursuance of these Regulations without prior approval of the Commission:

Provided also that the Commission may empower an officer of the State Agency to procure from the Power Exchange(s) or through Electricity Traders the required number of certificates to the extent of the shortfall in the fulfilment of the obligations, out of the amount available in the fund.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Section 26(3) of Energy Conservation Act 2001; Ministry of Power Gazette Notification dated 26.12.2023; Regulation 4 (RPPO targets); Regulation 5 (Fulfilment); Regulation 9 (Compliance monitoring); Regulation 13 (Power to remove difficulties)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Energy conservation framework; Penalty fund management; Certificate procurement using penalty funds; Transmission infrastructure for renewable evacuation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 10(2)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 10(2) - Penalty Under Section 142 of Electricity Act

**TITLE:** Regulation 10(2): Reporting Failure and Obligated Entity Non-Compliance Penalties

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 10(2) addresses penalties for obligated entities that fail to furnish requisite information per Regulation 8 and/or fail to comply with obligations to purchase renewable energy percentage or certificates. Such entities are liable for penalties as may be imposed by the Commission under Section 142 of the Electricity Act 2003. However, if an obligated entity faces genuine difficulty in meeting RPPO due to non-availability of certificates, it may approach the Commission with complete details requesting carry-forward of shortfall per Regulation 5(3). If the Commission consents to carry-forward, Regulation 10(1) or Electricity Act Section 142 penalty provisions do not apply.

**KEY TOPICS:** Information Reporting, Obligated Entity Failure, Non-Compliance Penalty, Electricity Act Penalty, Genuine Difficulty, Certificate Non-Availability, Carry-Forward Request, Commission Discretion, Penalty Waiver, Hardship Relief

**TEXT:**

(2) Where any Obligated Entity fails to furnish requisite information, as provided under Regulation 8, and/or fails to comply with the obligation to purchase the required percentage of energy from RE Sources as provided under these Regulations or fails to purchase the Renewable Energy Certificates, it shall be liable for penalty as may be imposed by the Commission under Section 142 of the Act:

Provided that in case of genuine difficulty in complying with the Renewable Power Purchase Obligation because of non-availability of certificates, the Obligated Entity may approach, the Commission along with complete detail for carry forward of shortfall in the RPPO as per Sub-regulation (3) of Regulation 5 of these Regulations:

Provided further that where the Commission has consented to carry forward of compliance requirement, the provisions of Sub-regulation (1) of these Regulations or the provisions of Section 142 of the Act shall not be invoked.

## Chunk 31

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Procurement of Energy from Renewable Sources) Regulations, 2025

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Regulation 11(1)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 11(1) - Purchase of Electricity Under REC Mechanism

**TITLE:** Regulation 11(1): Energy Purchased Under REC Mechanism Definition and Rate Setting

**SUMMARY:**
Regulation 11(1) defines "Energy Purchased under REC Mechanism" as energy consumed by an obligated entity from renewable sources under Power Purchase Agreements or arrangements on or after January 18, 2010, with specific provisions that such energy purchase does not entitle the obligated entity to claim green attributes. The Commission may fix the rate and associated conditions for electricity purchase by the distribution licensee under REC mechanism, considering the Average Cost of Power Purchase (APPC) from various sources, at intervals it deems appropriate. The rate previously fixed by the Commission for APPC vide Order No. 4 of 2024 dated 28.08.2024 shall apply for the period up to March 31, 2024.

**KEY TOPICS:** REC Mechanism, Energy Purchase, Green Attributes, Power Purchase Agreement, Rate Determination, Average Cost of Power Purchase, APPC, Commission Rate Setting, Non-Attribute Energy, Mechanism Purchase

**TEXT:**

11. Purchase of Electricity under REC Mechanism.

(1) The term, "Energy Purchased under REC Mechanism", shall mean the energy consumed by an Obligated Entity from RE Sources, under the Power Purchase Agreement(s) or any other arrangement on or after, 18th January 2010 with specific provisions that such purchase shall not entitle the Obligated Entity to deal with the green attributes for the quantum of energy so purchased.

The Commission may, fix the rate and other associated conditions, by duly taking into account the Average Cost of Power Purchase by the Distribution Licensee from different sources, at such interval as it may consider appropriate, for purchase of electricity by the Distribution Licensee, under REC mechanism:

Provided that the rate fixed by the Commission for Average Pooled Purchase Cost (APPC) vide Order No. 4 of 2024 dated 28.08.2024 shall be considered as the rate fixed under this Regulations for the period upto 31.03.2024.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 2 (Energy Purchased under REC Mechanism definition); Regulation 6 (REC Regulations); CERC REC Regulations 2022; GERC Tariff Orders (APPC determination); Order No. 4 of 2024
