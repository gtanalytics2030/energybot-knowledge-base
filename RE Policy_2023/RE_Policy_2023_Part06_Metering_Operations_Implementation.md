## CHUNK 36: METERING – STANDARDS, POINTS, AND ABT COMPLIANCE

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 21 – Metering

PROVISION: Section 21.1, 21.2, 21.3, 21.4, 21.5

TITLE: Metering Requirements, Interface Standards, and ABT Compliance Framework

SUMMARY:
Section 21 establishes comprehensive metering standards. Section 21.1 mandates that consumers and DISCOMs comply with applicable regulations, standards, and codes from GERC and CEA on metering, connectivity, and safety. Section 21.2 specifies that metering and interconnection points are located at CTU/STU/DISCOM connections where connectivity is granted for power injection from RE projects. Section 21.3 requires interface metering to conform to CEA (Installation and Operation of Meters) Regulations 2014 and amendments as stipulated by CTU/STU/DISCOM. Section 21.4 mandates that RE developers install Remote Terminal Units (RTU) and communication systems for transferring real-time data to concerned Load Dispatch Centres for monitoring. Section 21.5 requires ABT-compliant meters, check meters, and standby meters at metering points per applicable orders/regulations/codes, with each renewable energy project installing ABT-compliant meters for energy accounting. These comprehensive provisions ensure accurate, transparent energy metering and grid monitoring.

KEY TOPICS: Metering Standards, Interface Metering, CEA Regulations, RTU Installation, ABT Compliance, Check Meters, Standby Meters, Real-Time Data, Load Dispatch Centre, Communication Systems, Energy Accounting

TEXT:

21. Metering 

21.1 Consumers and DISCOMs shall comply with the provisions of applicable regulations, standards, and codes notified by various authorities, such as GERC and CEA, on aspects like metering, connectivity, and safety. 

21.2 The metering point and interconnection point shall be the point of connection at the CTU/ STU substation / DISCOM network, as the case may be, where connectivity is granted by CTU I STU/ DISCOM for injection of power from RE project. 

21.3 Interface metering shall conform to the Central Electricity Authority (Installation and Operation of Meters) Regulations 2014 and amendments thereto, as stipulated by CTU / STU / DISCOM, as the case may be. 

21.4 The RE developers shall also have to install Remote Terminal Unit (RTU) and communication system at the RE project for transferring the real time data to concerned Load Dispatch Centre for monitoring purpose. 

21.5 For the purpose of energy accounting, the ABT compliant meter, check meter and standby meter shall be installed at the metering point, as per the provisions of applicable order / regulations / codes from time to time. Further, for the purpose of energy accounting, each renewable energy project shall have to install ABT compliant meters.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GERC Applicable Regulations, CEA (Installation and Operation of Meters) Regulations 2014 and amendments, Electricity Act 2003, Grid codes

CROSS-REFERENCES: Metering provisions in Section 11.3.1-11.3.3 (hybrid metering), Grid connectivity in Section 20, Energy settlement in Section 14, Forecasting and scheduling in Section 23

## CHUNK 37: ISTS-CONNECTED RENEWABLE ENERGY PROJECTS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 22 – ISTS Connected Renewable Energy Projects

PROVISION: Section 22

TITLE: Interstate Transmission System (ISTS) Connected RE Projects Framework

SUMMARY:
Section 22 establishes that ISTS-connected renewable energy projects are governed in accordance with applicable regulations, orders, and guidelines as notified by the Central Government and/or CERC from time to time. This provision recognizes that projects exporting power across state boundaries operate under central transmission utility (CTU) jurisdiction and CERC regulatory oversight, distinct from state-level projects. By explicitly deferring to central frameworks, the policy avoids overlap and ensures coordination between state and national renewable energy development objectives while maintaining regulatory consistency across state boundaries.

KEY TOPICS: ISTS-Connected Projects, Interstate Transmission, CERC Regulations, Central Government Guidelines, Cross-State Power Export, CTU Jurisdiction, Regulatory Coordination

TEXT:

22. ISTS connected renewable energy projects 

ISTS-connected renewable energy projects shall be governed in accordance with the applicable regulations, orders and guidelines as notified by the Central Government and/or CERC from time to time.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: CERC Regulations (as amended), Central Government Guidelines, Electricity Act 2003, CERC Orders

CROSS-REFERENCES: Central Electricity Authority standards in Section 20.2, Grid integration in Section 20, Metering standards in Section 21

## CHUNK 38: FORECASTING AND SCHEDULING OF RENEWABLE ENERGY PROJECTS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 23 – Forecasting and Scheduling

PROVISION: Section 23.1 and 23.2

TITLE: Forecasting, Scheduling, and Deviation Management Framework

SUMMARY:
Section 23 establishes that forecasting and scheduling of renewable energy projects are governed by GERC regulations and orders issued from time to time for intra-state projects, ensuring predictable and reliable grid operations. Interstate projects fall under CERC regulations as applicable. This framework recognizes that renewable energy's variable output requires sophisticated forecasting and scheduling mechanisms to maintain grid stability and enable accurate settlement of deviations. By deferring to regulatory bodies' expertise, the policy ensures technical optimization and grid reliability while maintaining jurisdictional clarity between state and central regulators.

KEY TOPICS: Forecasting, Scheduling, Renewable Energy Variability, GERC Regulations, CERC Regulations, Deviation Management, Grid Operations, Predictability, Interstate Projects

TEXT:

23. Forecasting and scheduling 

23.1 Forecasting and scheduling of renewable energy projects shall be governed by GERC regulations and orders as issued from time to time. 

23.2 Interstate projects shall be governed by CERC regulations, as applicable.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GERC Orders and Regulations (issued from time to time), CERC Regulations and Orders, Electricity Act 2003

CROSS-REFERENCES: Grid stability provisions in Section 20, ISTS-connected projects in Section 22, Energy settlement in Section 14, Grid integration in Section 20.2

HIERARCHY: Section 24 – Operation and Maintenance

PROVISION: Section 24

TITLE: Operation and Maintenance Responsibility and Technical Standards

SUMMARY:
Section 24 establishes that operation and maintenance of dedicated evacuation lines is carried out at the cost of RE projects per applicable technical standards and best practices. This provision ensures that developers bear the responsibility for maintaining critical infrastructure essential to power delivery, creating incentives for efficient project operations while placing responsibility with parties best positioned to manage costs and performance. Reference to technical standards and best practices ensures maintenance quality while allowing flexibility in implementation approaches.

24. Operation and maintenance

## CHUNK 40: FACILITATION FOR POLICY IMPLEMENTATION – GUVNL AND GEDA ROLES

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 25 – Facilitation for Implementation of Policy

PROVISION: Section 25.1, 25.2, 25.3, 25.4

TITLE: Policy Implementation Framework – GUVNL Coordination and GEDA as State Nodal Agency

SUMMARY:
Section 25.1 establishes GUVNL (Gujarat Urja Vikas Nigam Limited) as the implementing, facilitating, coordinating, and monitoring agency for the policy. Section 25.2 designates GEDA (Gujarat Energy Development Agency) as the State Nodal Agency (SNA) for: (a) Registration of Projects; (b) Accreditation and recommendation of RE Projects for REC mechanism registration; (c) Certification of project commissioning; (d) Monthly progress reporting to GUVNL/EPD; (e) Any other assigned functions. Section 25.3 requires SNA to formulate project registration modalities, procedures, terms, and conditions, facilitating RE developers through a 'Single Window Web-System' for automated registration/approval via online mode. The SNA must develop Standard Operating Procedures and helpdesk support. The single window system integrates registration, approval, and project allocation functionalities. Section 25.4 requires SNA to take quarterly progress reports from developers, review and monitor progress, address queries, provide guidance, conduct inspections as needed, and fast-track project implementation. These provisions centralize implementation oversight while reducing transaction costs for developers through single-window systems.

KEY TOPICS: GUVNL, State Nodal Agency, GEDA, Project Registration, Accreditation, Single Window Web-System, Automated Approval, Quarterly Monitoring, Fast-Tracking, REC Mechanism, Commissioning Certification

TEXT:

25. Facilitation for implementation of policy 

25.1 GUVNL shall be the implementing, facilitating, coordinating and monitoring agency for this Policy. 

25.2 GEDA shall act as the State Nodal Agency (SNA) for the following functions: 
a. Registration of Projects 
b. Accreditation and recommending Renewable Energy Projects for registering with the Central Agencyunder REC mechanism 
c. Certifying the commissioning of Renewable Energy Projects 
d. Submit a monthly progress report of the activities mentioned above to the G UVN L/E PD. 
e. Any other functions assigned. 

25.3 The modalities, procedures, terms, and conditions, etc. for the registration of projects shall be formulated by the SNA. The SNA shall facilitate the RE Developers by developing a 'Single Window Web- System' for RE project. The RE Developers shall be required to upload the requisite documents on this web-portal. The registration completion and approval thereof shall be issued automatically through online- mode and made available on the web-portal itself. To enable a faster registration process, smooth functioning and adequate assistance to the RE Developers, the SNA shall prescribe Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) or guidelines and web-portal service helpdesk, etc. The single window web-system shall be integrated with all the functionalities such as registration, approval, allocation of the renewable energy projects etc. 

25.4 The SNA shall take quarterly progress reports of RE projects from project developer. The SNA shall review and monitorthe project progress, address queries and problems of RE developers, provide necessary guidance and clarifications, conduct inspections (if required), etc., and thereby endeavor to assist the RE Developers in fast-tracking the implementation of projects.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GEDA Establishment Guidelines, GUVNL Mandate, REC Mechanism (CERC Regulations), Government of Gujarat Administrative Framework

CROSS-REFERENCES: Project developer definition in Section 5 (Definition 11), Registration process timelines in Sections 17.4-17.5, Security deposit procedures in Section 17, Commissioning requirements in Section 17.3

HIERARCHY: Section 25 – Facilitation for Implementation of Policy

PROVISION: Section 25.5 and 25.6

TITLE: Transmission Capacity Disclosure, Grievance Resolution, and Policy-Level Coordination

SUMMARY:
Section 25.5 requires GETCO (Gujarat Energy Transmission Corporation) to facilitate RE developers by making public disclosure of RE integration capacity available district-wise and substation-wise on its website, updated regularly. Section 25.6 establishes a committee under Principal Secretary (Energy & Petrochemicals Department) to facilitate resolution of policy-level issues, grievances, and concerns (if any), remove difficulties, and ensure smooth policy implementation. This high-level coordination mechanism ensures responsive governance while maintaining regulatory clarity and providing escalation pathways for unresolved developer concerns. Together, these provisions create transparency in infrastructure availability and institutional accountability for policy implementation.

KEY TOPICS: GETCO Disclosure, Transmission Capacity, District-Wise Availability, Substation-Wise Capacity, Grievance Resolution, Policy-Level Committee, Institutional Coordination, Principal Secretary, Implementation Facilitation

TEXT:

25.5 The State Transmission Utility (GETCO) shall facilitate the RE Developers by making public disclosure of RE integration capacity available district-wise / substation-wise on its website and updating it regularlyto enable the RE Developers identify potential sites. The connectivity with the STU network shall be governed as per the connectivity procedures approved by GERC, as amended from time to time. 

25.6 A committee under the chairmanship of the Principal Secretary (Energy & Petrochemicals Dept. — Government of Gujarat) shall facilitate the resolution of policy-level issues, grievances, and concerns (if any) of projects, removing difficulties, etc. under this Policy, to ensure smooth implementation of the Policy.

## CHUNK 42: PLANT AND MACHINERY – ELIGIBILITY AND MANUFACTURING STANDARDS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 26 – Plant and Machinery

PROVISION: Section 26.1, 26.2, 26.3

TITLE: Equipment Eligibility and Manufacturing Standards for Renewable Energy Projects

SUMMARY:
Section 26.1 specifies that only new plant and machinery are eligible for installation under the policy, excluding reconditioned or second-hand equipment that may have uncertain reliability. Section 26.2 establishes that solar PV modules and wind turbines must be approved by MNRE under RLMM (Revised List of Models and Manufacturers for wind) or ALMM (List of Approved Models and Manufacturers for solar), with applicable MNRE guidelines and amendments. Section 26.3 requires solar PV cells and modules to comply with Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) standards. These provisions ensure equipment quality, international best practices, and manufacturer accountability while supporting domestic manufacturing standards through BIS compliance, balancing technology advancement with quality assurance.

KEY TOPICS: New Plant and Machinery, Second-Hand Equipment, ALMM, RLMM, MNRE Approval, Wind Turbines, Solar Modules, Manufacturing Standards, BIS Standards, Quality Assurance

TEXT:

26. Plant and machinery 

26.1 Only new plant and machinery shall be eligible for installation under this Policy. 

26.2 Solar PV modules and wind turbines that are approved by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, Government of India, under RLMM or ALMM, as applicable, shall be eligible. The guideline of Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, Government of India and its amendment from time to time for the same shall be applicable. 

26.3 The solar PV cells and modules shall be compliant with the BIS standards.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: MNRE Guidelines and Approvals, RLMM (issued by MNRE), ALMM (issued by MNRE), Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)

## CHUNK 43: PROTOTYPE WIND TURBINE PROJECTS – TESTING AND CERTIFICATION FRAMEWORK

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 27 – Prototype Projects

PROVISION: Section 27.1, 27.2, 27.3, 27.4, 27.5, 27.6

TITLE: Prototype WTG Installations – Type-Testing, Certification, and Grid Synchronization

SUMMARY:
Section 27 establishes a framework for prototype wind turbine generators (WTGs) to facilitate technological advancement. Section 27.1 recognizes benefits of higher hub heights, higher capacity WTGs, facilitating WTG manufacturers and developers to install prototypes. Section 27.2 allows prototype WTG installation for type-testing per international standards per MNRE guidelines. Registration is conducted by SNA, which issues commissioning certificates or grid synchronization reports per applicable guidelines. Section 27.3 mandates compliance with MN RE/NIWE/CEA/GERC guidelines and regulations. Section 27.4 requires all prototype WTG components to be new and unused; second-hand components are prohibited. Components are eligible for customs/excise duty exemption per Customs and Excise Department notifications. Section 27.5 specifies that prototype WTGs are owned by manufacturers and cannot be sold until the model is listed in RLMM. Section 27.6 provides that energy generated from prototype WTGs is treated as inadvertent energy until the developer executes commercial arrangements post-RLMM listing. This framework supports domestic wind technology development while maintaining grid safety and eventually facilitating commercialization.

KEY TOPICS: Prototype WTGs, Type-Testing, Type-Certification, Internationally Accredited Certifying Agencies, RLMM Inclusion, Grid Synchronization, New Components, Customs Duty Exemption, Manufacturer Ownership, Inadvertent Energy, Commercial Arrangements

TEXT:

27. Prototype Projects 

27.1 Considering the benefits of technological advancements in wind turbines in respect of higher hub heights, higher capacity etc., the policy aims to facilitate WTG manufacturers and RE developers to install prototype WTGs. 

27.2 Installation of prototype WIGs shall be permissible to carry out type-testing for a type-certificate from internationally accredited certifying agencies in accordance with the guidelines and procedures for installation of prototype wind turbine models notified by MNRE from time to time. Registration will be done by SNA. SNA shall issue commissioning certificate or grid synchronization report as per the applicable guidelines and procedures as notified by MNRE, NIWE, or any other such authority from time to time. 

27.3 The prototype WTGs shall have to ensure due compliance and adherence to applicable guidelines, procedures, orders and regulations as notified by MN RE, NIWE, CEA, GERC, or any other authority from time to time. 

27.4 The components or items procured or imported for the manufacturing of prototype WTGs shall have to be new and unused. Second-hand components and machines shall not be allowed. The components or items procured or imported forthe manufacturing of prototype WTGs shall be eligible for customs and excise duty exemption as per the notifications of the Customs and Excise Departments from time to time. 

27.5 The prototype WTGs shall be owned by the concerned wind turbine manufacturer and shall not be sold to any party until the model is included in the Revised List of Models and Manufacturers of Wind Turbines (RLMM). 

27.6 Energy generated from such prototype WTGs shall be considered as inadvertent energy until the RE developer executes a commercial arrangement for the said prototype WIG after the model gets listed in RLMM.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: MNRE Guidelines and Procedures, NIWE Standards, CEA Safety Regulations, GERC Regulations, RLMM (Ministry of New and Renewable Energy), Customs and Excise Department Notifications

CROSS-REFERENCES: Plant and machinery standards in Section 26, Grid integration standards in Section 20, Metering standards in Section 21, Commissioning certification in Section 25.2

## CHUNK 44: LAND ALLOCATION FOR RENEWABLE ENERGY PROJECTS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 28 – Land

PROVISION: Section 28.1, 28.2, 28.3

TITLE: Land Availability and Allocation Framework for RE Projects

SUMMARY:
Section 28 establishes that RE projects may be set up on private land or on government waste land allotted by the Revenue Department or state nodal agency (GEDA). Revenue department allocates government waste land for RE project development in consultation with Energy and Petrochemicals Department. The government may allocate government waste land at concessional rates to RE projects supplying power to distribution licensees for Gujarat consumers, providing cost advantages for projects serving state consumers. Land allocation for wind and wind-solar hybrid projects is based on optimum utilization of entire land parcels, encouraging efficient land use. This framework balances private sector flexibility with strategic government support for distributed benefits while encouraging optimal infrastructure design.

KEY TOPICS: Land Allocation, Private Land, Government Waste Land, Concessional Rates, Revenue Department, Energy Department, Optimum Land Utilization, Wind Projects, Hybrid Projects

TEXT:

28. Land 

28.1 The RE project may be setup on private land, or government waste land allotted by the Revenue Department / state nodal agency. Revenue department will allocate government waste Land for development of RE projects in consultation with the Energy and Petrochemicals department GoG. 

28.2 State Government may allocate government waste land at concessional rates to RE projects supplying power to the distribution licensee for the consumers of Gujarat. 

28.3 The allocation of government waste land for wind I wind and solar hybrid projects shall be made based on optimum utilization of the entire land parcel.

## CHUNK 45: REPOWERING OF WIND PROJECTS – FRAMEWORK AND OBLIGATIONS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 29 – Repowering of Wind Projects

PROVISION: Section 29.1, 29.2, 29.3, 29.4, 29.5, 29.6, 29.7

TITLE: Wind Project Repowering – Replacement with Advanced Turbines and Timeline Framework

SUMMARY:
Section 29.1 establishes the objective to re-energize old, small-sized, inefficient wind turbines by replacing them with bigger, more efficient turbines with improved technology (rotor diameters, blades, heights, hub heights), optimizing land and infrastructure utilization. Repowering must occur on or before 25-year project completion or extended agreement term. Section 29.2 specifies that developers with wind turbines completing 25 years or about to complete 25 years from commissioning must repower within 6 months of policy issuance if their agreements with GETCO and DISCOM expire or extend within one year of policy issuance; they are eligible for benefits under this policy. Section 29.3 provides that developers failing to repower at project expiry must decommission and surrender connectivity; if on leased land, they must surrender leasehold rights. Section 29.4 establishes repowered project life as 25 years or actual turbine life, whichever is earlier. Section 29.5 allows full or partial repowering without ceiling limits during operation. Section 29.6 requires repowering notification to beneficiary with prior SNA consent. Section 29.7 requires SNA commissioning certification; SNA's certified date becomes the repowering date. These provisions incentivize modernization while protecting infrastructure and regulatory oversight.

KEY TOPICS: Repowering, Wind Turbine Replacement, Decommissioning, Leasehold Rights, 25-Year Life, Partial Repowering, SNA Certification, Advanced Technology, Capacity Addition

TEXT:

29. Repowering of wind projects 

29.1 With an objective to re-energize the old, small-sized and inefficient wind turbines and replace with bigger and more efficient wind turbines with better technology (improved rotor diameters, larger blades, taller towers and pole lengths, increased hub heights, etc.) so as to optimally utilize the existing land and infrastructure, the repowering of wind turbine generators shall have to be done by the RE developers on or before the completion of 25 years from the date of commissioning of the project or extended term of the agreement. 

29.2 The repowering of wind turbine generators shall have to be done by the RE developers within six months from the date of issuance of this policy whose wind turbine generators have either completed the 25 years or about to complete the 25 years from the date of commissioning of the project. The developer whose agreements with GETCO and DISCOM are either extended or expired within one year from the date of issuance of this policy shall be eligible for the repowering. 

29.3 If the Wind Project developer fails to repower its wind turbine generator at the expiry of the project's life! term of agreement / extended terms of agreement, as the case may be, such RE developer shall have to decommission the wind power project and surrender the connectivity, and if the WIG is set up on leased land, they shall also have to surrender their leasehold rights to Government. 

29.4 The life of the repowered project shall be 25 years or the actual life of the turbines, whichever is earlier. 

29.5 RE developer shall be allowed for full! partial repowering without any ceiling limit during the operation period. 

29.6 The repowering of projects shall have to be done under intimation to the beneficiary and with the prior consent of the SNA. 

29.7 After completion of repowering RE Developer is required to inform SNA for certification of repowering. Date certified by SNA shall be considered as date of Repowering.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Technology advancement standards, Decommissioning procedures, Leasehold agreement termination, GEDA/SNA certification procedures

CROSS-REFERENCES: Wind project framework in Section 10, Hybrid Type-A projects in Section 11.3.1, Evacuation facility in Section 17.4-17.6, Security deposits for evacuation in Section 17
