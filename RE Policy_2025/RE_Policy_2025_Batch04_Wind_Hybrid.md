**PROVISION:** Section 9.4 - Wind Project Wheeling Agreement Extension

**TITLE:** Wind Energy: Wheeling Agreement Life Extension for Existing Projects

**SUMMARY:** Section 9.4 provides a critical mechanism for extending the operational life of existing wind projects beyond their original agreement term. If a wind project's wheeling agreement has a term of less than 25 years, Distribution Companies may execute a new wheeling agreement for the remaining period. The total combined wheeling period must equal 25 years or the full project lifespan, whichever is earlier. New agreement terms follow the prevailing GERC tariff order applicable on the date of the new wheeling agreement. The extension is subject to: (1) GEDA granting continuing operation permission for the wind turbine generator, and (2) GETCO extending the Bulk Power Transmission Agreement (BPTA). This provision ensures long-term revenue certainty for wind projects and enables asset utilization beyond the traditional 25-year life horizon.

**TEXT:**

After expiry of a wheeling agreement having terms less than 25 years for a wind project, DISCOMs may execute a wheeling agreement for the remaining period, considering the total life of 25 years/project life, whichever is lesser, in accordance with the prevailing GERC tariff order on the date of the wheeling agreement, subject to GEDA granting continuing operation of the wind turbine generator and GETCO extending the Bulk Power Transmission Agreement (BPTA).

**PROVISION:** Section 10.1 - Wind-Solar Hybrid: Complementarity and BESS Integration

**TITLE:** Wind-Solar Hybrid Projects: Technology Complementarity and Storage Rationale

**SUMMARY:** Section 10.1 establishes the strategic foundation for wind-solar hybrid projects. The policy recognizes that solar and wind energy generation are complementary—solar peaks during the day while wind generation varies independently, often supplementing daytime solar with early morning and evening wind. Co-location of these two technologies with or without Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) helps: (1) minimize generation variability by leveraging different generation profiles; (2) optimize utilization of available infrastructure (land, transmission systems); (3) reduce power variability through combined resource smoothing; (4) enable more stable and predictable aggregate generation from the combined resource. This hybrid approach is a core strategy for integrating high RE penetration while maintaining grid stability.

**TEXT:**

Solar and wind energy generation being complementary to each other, the 'co-location' of the two technologies, with or without Battery Energy Storage System (BESS), would help in minimizing variability, apart from optimally utilizing available infrastructure, including land and transmission systems.

**TITLE:** Type-A Hybrid Projects: Technical Conditions for Converting Vanilla Projects

**SUMMARY:** Section 10.3.2 provides three critical conditions for Type-A hybrid projects (existing-to-hybrid conversion): (a) **Transmission Capacity Constraint:** Total combined wind and solar power injection after hybridization shall not exceed the transmission capacity or grid connectivity allowed/sanctioned by GETCO/STU for this purpose. If grid system augmentation is required per system studies by GETCO/STU due to added wind/solar capacity, RE developers must undertake such additions at their own cost (though policy focuses on minimum augmentation through optimized utilization of existing transmission). (b) **Measurement and Apportionment:** Solar and wind power must be measured separately at the pooling/sending-end substation, with injection at the receiving-end substation apportioned on a pro-rata basis per common meter reading at the receiving-end substation. (c) **Transmission Capacity Availability Assessment:** RE developers must approach GETCO/STU to determine transmission capacity available for additional wind/solar generation, with GETCO/STU providing relevant data on transmission utilization. These conditions balance developer cost optimization with grid stability and system adequacy.
