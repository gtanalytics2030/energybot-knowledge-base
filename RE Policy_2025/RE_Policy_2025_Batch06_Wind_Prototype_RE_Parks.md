**HIERARCHY:** Section 13 (Wind Prototype Projects)

**PROVISION:** Section 13.1 - Wind Prototype Projects Framework and Authorization

**TITLE:** Wind Prototype Projects: Type-Testing and Technology Validation Framework

**SUMMARY:** Section 13.1 establishes a dedicated framework for Wind Prototype Projects designed to enable type-testing and validation of wind turbine generators (WTGs) of different designs and capacities in Gujarat's wind zones. The framework permits: (1) Development of prototype projects using new or different wind turbine designs not yet commercially deployed in India, enabling domestic testing of global turbine models; (2) Type-testing in actual wind resource and terrain conditions of Gujarat to validate performance before commercial manufacturing or scaling; (3) Demonstration of technological feasibility and commercial viability in Indian grid and operational contexts. These prototype projects operate outside normal commercial generation frameworks—any electrical energy generated during prototype testing (inadvertent energy) is non-billable experimental output. Only after successful type-testing and commercial validation may prototype projects transition to commercial energy generation. This framework positions Gujarat as a wind technology validation hub.

**PROVISION:** Section 13.2 - ALMM Compliance for Wind Prototype Turbines

**TITLE:** Wind Prototype Projects: Accepted List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM) Requirement

**SUMMARY:** Section 13.2 mandates that all Wind Turbine Generators (WTGs) deployed in prototype projects must comply with the Accepted List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM) for wind energy as defined in Section 2.2 of the policy. The ALMM requirement ensures type-approved designs meeting international and Indian standards are tested—preventing unsafe or non-standard equipment from being deployed. ALMM compliance ensures: (1) Turbines meet technical specifications and safety standards; (2) Test results are comparable across projects; (3) Successful prototypes can scale using pre-validated designs. The ALMM framework is issued and updated by the designated authority (typically GEDA/Ministry of New and Renewable Energy), ensuring continuous evolution with emerging turbine designs.

**PROVISION:** Section 13.4 - Inadvertent Energy Treatment and Non-Billing Framework

**TITLE:** Wind Prototype Projects: Inadvertent Energy Non-Billable Status and Experimental Output Classification

**SUMMARY:** Section 13.4 establishes that any electrical energy generated during the prototype testing phase is classified as "inadvertent energy"—unintended output from the testing process rather than commercial product. Inadvertent energy is explicitly non-billable, meaning no commercial revenue is earned from testing-phase generation, and no tariff payments are due to the State or grid operators. This treatment recognizes prototype testing is primarily focused on technology validation rather than revenue generation. The non-billable status: (1) Reduces commercial pressure during testing allowing focus on performance validation; (2) Enables test data collection without revenue implications; (3) Provides cost relief for developers during extended validation periods. Upon successful commercial validation (Section 13.5), the project transitions to commercial status where energy becomes billable.

**PROVISION:** Section 13.5 - Commercial Validation and Transition to Billable Energy Generation

**TITLE:** Wind Prototype Projects: Commercial Viability Certification and Energy Generation Transition

**SUMMARY:** Section 13.5 establishes the transition pathway from prototype testing (non-billable inadvertent energy) to commercial operation (billable energy). Once the prototype WTG has been successfully tested and commercially validated—meaning performance meets design specifications, turbine demonstrates operational stability, and commercial viability is confirmed—the project may transition to commercial energy generation status. Upon successful commercial validation: (1) Electrical energy generated from the WTG becomes billable and commercially valuable; (2) Standard tariff arrangements apply (captive, third-party sale, or DISCOM purchase per Section 19); (3) Metering and settlement follow commercial frameworks per Section 17; (4) Project operates as a standard wind generation facility. The transition requires formal certification from the SNA/GEDA confirming successful type-testing and commercial readiness. This staged approach—test-then-commercialize—reduces risk while ensuring only proven technologies achieve commercial deployment.

**PROVISION:** Section 14.1 - Renewable Energy Parks Definition and Scope

**TITLE:** Renewable Energy Parks: Dedicated Zones for Multi-MW Scale RE Generation

**SUMMARY:** Section 14.1 defines Renewable Energy (RE) Parks as dedicated zones comprising minimum aggregate capacity of 50 MW, hosting multiple RE generation projects (solar, wind, hybrid, biomass, emerging tech) within a consolidated site or geographically cohesive area. RE Parks consolidate: (1) Land-use efficiency through shared infrastructure (roads, fencing, access); (2) Grid integration at single transmission connection point; (3) Reduced transmission/distribution losses through concentrated generation; (4) Simplified environmental and social clearances for larger unified development. The 50 MW minimum scale ensures parks achieve infrastructure-sharing benefits and command specialized project management. RE Parks may house projects from single or multiple developers and can include complementary facilities (BESS, transmission substations, control centers). Parks function as RE-dedicated industrial zones optimizing land, transmission, and operational resources.
