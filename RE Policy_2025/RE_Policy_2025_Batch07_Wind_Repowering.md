**TITLE:** Wind Repowering and Life Extension: Strategic Framework for Aging Turbine Replacement

**SUMMARY:** Section 15.1 establishes a comprehensive framework for wind repowering—the strategic replacement of aging wind turbines with newer, higher-capacity models—and life extension—extending operational life of existing turbines beyond original design life. Repowering converts aging wind assets into high-performance modern facilities.

**PROVISION:** Section 15.2 - Eligible Turbines for Repowering: Age and Operational Criteria

**TITLE:** Wind Repowering: Eligibility Criteria and Turbine Assessment Framework

**SUMMARY:** Section 15.2 establishes eligibility criteria for wind turbines considered for repowering and life extension. A wind turbine is eligible for repowering if it meets one or more of the following criteria: (1) Turbines that have completed their original design life (typically 20-25 years); (2) Turbines installed prior to a specified reference date (e.g., January 1, 2000) ensuring focus on truly aging assets; (3) Turbines with below-average capacity factors indicating performance degradation due to age or technological obsolescence; (4) Turbines requiring major component replacement (gearbox, blades, generator) making comprehensive replacement economically preferable to continued operation. Eligibility assessment involves: (1) Technical inspection verifying turbine condition and remaining life; (2) Performance analysis comparing historical capacity factors to modern turbine performance; (3) Economic analysis determining whether repowering yields better returns than continued operation or decommissioning; (4) Environmental assessment identifying site suitability for modern turbines. This multi-criteria approach targets repowering investments toward genuinely aging turbines with meaningful performance improvement potential.

**PROVISION:** Section 15.3 - Minimum Energy Enhancement Requirement (1.5x Factor)

**TITLE:** Wind Repowering: Mandatory 1.5x Energy Enhancement Minimum Target

**SUMMARY:** Section 15.3 establishes a critical performance requirement: repowered wind sites must achieve minimum 1.5x energy enhancement compared to baseline—meaning the new turbines must generate at least 50% more energy annually than the original turbine configuration. The 1.5x factor is calculated as: (New Repowered Site Annual Generation / Original Turbine Annual Generation) ≥ 1.5. This minimum enhancement target ensures: (1) Meaningful capacity improvement justifying site development costs and grid interconnection investments; (2) Substantial grid capacity increase from limited sites (transmission reuse without augmentation); (3) Economic viability for developers offsetting decommissioning and installation costs; (4) Environmental returns on land and infrastructure reuse (1.5x generation = 1.5x carbon offset from same site footprint). The 1.5x requirement drives site-specific technology selection: higher-capacity turbines, advanced blade designs, improved hub heights, and optimized turbine placement to maximize generation at each location. Achieving 1.5x enhancement validates site repower decision.

**TITLE:** Wind Repowering: 25-Year Operational Life Extension and Policy Continuation

**SUMMARY:** Section 15.4 establishes operational life extension provisions. Repowered wind turbines shall be authorized to operate for a minimum 25-year period from the date of repowering commissioning, ensuring a full generation cycle from modern turbine deployment. The 25-year extended life provision: (1) Recognizes modern turbine design life (typically 20-25 years with proper maintenance); (2) Provides long-term investment certainty enabling financing and returns realization; (3) Aligns with policy stability—benefits and tariff mechanisms remain consistent over the 25-year period; (4) Enables amortization of repowering capex over extended timeframe improving project economics. Upon completion of the initial 25-year extended life period: (1) Turbines may continue operation under existing arrangements (power purchase, wheeling) if technically sound and economically viable; (2) Policy frameworks applicable at that future date govern continued operations; (3) Life extension beyond 25 years is permissible under then-applicable policy frameworks. The 25-year foundation provides stable long-term operational certainty while maintaining policy flexibility for post-2050 operations.

**PROVISION:** Section 15.5 - Continuation of Existing PPAs with DISCOMs

**TITLE:** Wind Repowering: DISCOM Power Purchase Agreement Continuation and Succession

**SUMMARY:** Section 15.5 establishes that existing Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) between original turbine developers and Distribution Companies (DISCOMs) may be continued or renegotiated for repowered projects without requiring fresh competitive bidding. Specifically: (1) Original PPA Continuation: If the repowered project involves the same developer and site, the original DISCOM PPA may be continued without rebidding, avoiding transaction delays and providing certainty to both developer and DISCOM; (2) PPA Renegotiation: Alternatively, parties may mutually renegotiate the PPA reflecting new tariff levels, extended term, and changed operational parameters (enhanced capacity, new interconnection point); (3) Tariff Basis: The tariff applicable to repowered projects shall be governed by prevailing GERC tariff orders at the time of repowering commissioning—typically cost-based tariff mechanisms for existing-site repowering. This streamlined PPA approach: (1) Reduces regulatory friction relative to new competitive bidding; (2) Recognizes value of existing DISCOM relationships; (3) Enables rapid project progression without tender delays; (4) Supports DISCOM procurement continuity. PPA continuation is a key enabler of repowering economics.

**PROVISION:** Section 15.11 - Micro-Siting Relaxation and Installation Flexibility

**TITLE:** Wind Repowering: Relaxed Turbine Placement Constraints and Site Optimization

**SUMMARY:** Section 15.11 establishes critical micro-siting flexibility for repowered projects—enabling optimized turbine placement within repowering sites to maximize energy generation. The repowering framework relaxes micro-siting constraints from standard requirements (5 rotor diameters [5D] × 7 rotor diameters [7D] minimum spacing between turbines) to a tighter spacing of 3D × 5D for repowering projects. Specifically: (1) Relaxation Rationale: Modern turbines produce narrower wakes (air downwind disturbance) and have advanced control systems minimizing inter-turbine losses, enabling closer spacing; (2) Tighter Spacing Benefit: 3D × 5D spacing enables deployment of additional turbines within existing repowering site footprint—increasing capacity from same land area; (3) Energy Maximization: Optimized placement using wind flow modeling and advanced algorithms identifies highest-yield turbine positions achieving 1.5x energy enhancement target; (4) Existing Land Reuse: Relaxed spacing reduces need for additional land acquisition making repowering more economically feasible; (5) Approval Process: Relaxed micro-siting requires site-specific wind resource and wake modeling study justifying placement, reviewed by GEDA/SNA. This relaxation is a key enabler of 1.5x energy enhancement targets at repowering sites.

**PROVISION:** Section 15.12 - Decommissioning Protocol and Old Turbine Removal

**TITLE:** Wind Repowering: Responsible Turbine Decommissioning and Site Remediation

**SUMMARY:** Section 15.12 establishes mandatory decommissioning protocols ensuring environmentally responsible removal of aging turbines. Decommissioning requirements: (1) Planning and Notification: Developers must notify GEDA/SLDC 6 months prior to decommissioning planned, enabling dispatch planning and grid adjustment; (2) Turbine Dismantling: Old turbines must be fully dismantled—tower, nacelle, blades—using qualified contractors following safety standards; (3) Foundation Removal: Turbine foundations (concrete plinths buried 1-2 meters) must be excavated and removed unless: (a) Reuse for new turbine foundation if at same location, or (b) Retained in-ground with written landowner consent for non-interference; (4) Material Segregation: Dismantled materials (steel, fiberglass, copper, electronics) are segregated for recycling per waste management plan (Section 15.13); (5) Site Remediation: Post-removal site is restored to pre-construction condition—land leveling, vegetation restoration, elimination of hazards; (6) Environmental Monitoring: Decommissioning activities are monitored for air/water quality, dust control, and ecosystem impact per environmental clearance conditions; (7) Waste Documentation: Decommissioning waste is tracked per circular economy requirements (Section 30) with disposal certificates confirming recycling or responsible disposal. Comprehensive decommissioning ensures repowering does not compromise environmental quality of aging turbine sites.
