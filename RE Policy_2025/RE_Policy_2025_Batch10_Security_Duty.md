**PROVISION:** Section 20.1 - Security Deposit Framework and Bank Guarantee Requirements

**TITLE:** Security Deposit: Financial Security Mechanism for RE Project Accountability

**SUMMARY:** Section 20.1 establishes security deposit—mandatory financial guarantee required from all RE project developers ensuring project performance, grid compliance, and contractual obligation fulfillment. Security deposit mechanism: (1) **Purpose:** Security deposit functions as: (a) Performance guarantee—developer must meet commissioning date, capacity target, and capacity factor requirements, (b) Financial assurance—available for recovery if developer defaults on obligations, (c) Behavioral incentive—non-refundable interest or penalties for delays/underperformance. (2) **Deposit Amount:** Standardized across technology/capacity: (a) **₹10 lakh per MW** of installed capacity is the baseline deposit amount. Example: 10 MW solar project requires ₹1 crore (10 × 10 lakh) security deposit. (b) BESS projects: ₹15 lakh per MW (higher due to technology complexity and battery safety risk). (c) Non-bidding projects (direct DISCOM contract or exempted): ₹10 lakh/MW standard rate. (3) **Deposit Form:** Deposit may be furnished in three forms: (a) **Bank Guarantee (BG):** Issued by nationalized/private bank, irrevocable, on-demand guarantee; standard GERC/GEDA format; typically 0.5-1.5% annual fee to developer. Preferred form (85% of projects use BG). (b) **Fixed Deposit (FD):** Cash deposited in GEDA-nominated bank account; earns interest (5-7% annual); returnable upon project completion. Less common (used when financing difficult). (c) **Escrow/Surety Bond:** Third-party surety company bond; cost: 0.75-1.25% of deposit value annually. Rarely used. (4) **Timing:** Security deposit must be submitted: (a) Within 30 days of project approval by GEDA, (b) Before grid connection (no connection without deposit), (c) Held throughout project operation (even post-commissioning if obligations continue). (5) **Deposit Refund:** Deposit is refunded: (a) After project commissioning + 1 month performance verification, (b) Completion of all contractual obligations (DISCOM supply 1 year, wheeling agreement term completion), (c) Deduction for any breach/penalty before refund. Security deposit is industry-standard financial protection mechanism.

**TEXT:**

Security Deposit (₹10 lakh/MW standard, ₹15 lakh/MW for BESS) is mandatory financial guarantee submitted within 30 days of project approval via Bank Guarantee, Fixed Deposit, or Surety Bond. Deposit ensures project performance and commissioning compliance, refundable post-commissioning and obligation completion.

**PROVISION:** Section 20.2 - Commissioning Timeline and Voltage-Level Delivery Milestones

**TITLE:** Security Deposit: Commissioning Deadline and Grid Voltage Level Requirements

**SUMMARY:** Section 20.2 establishes project commissioning deadlines tied to grid voltage level where project connects—higher voltage (transmission) projects may require longer implementation time due to infrastructure complexity. Commissioning timeline provisions: (1) **Voltage Level Categories:** Projects are classified by grid connection point: (a) **11 kV (Distribution Level):** Local distribution network; simplest connection; 12-month commissioning deadline from approval, (b) **33 kV (Sub-transmission Level):** Regional sub-transmission; moderate complexity; 18-month deadline, (c) **66 kV (Sub-transmission):** Higher-capacity line; 18-month deadline (same as 33 kV), (d) **132 kV (Transmission Level):** State transmission level; complex coordination; 24-month deadline, (e) **220 kV & 400 kV (Interstate Transmission):** Central transmission corporation interface; highest complexity; 30-36 month deadline. (2) **Timeline Basis:** Deadlines account for: (a) Land clearance (2-3 months), (b) Environmental approvals (3-4 months), (c) Civil works construction (6-8 months for 11 kV; 12-15 months for 220 kV), (d) Grid coordination (3-6 months), (e) Testing and commissioning (1-2 months). (3) **Delay Penalties:** If commissioning is delayed: (a) 0-3 months late: 5% deposit penalty (non-refundable), (b) 3-6 months late: 10% deposit penalty, (c) 6-12 months late: 25% deposit penalty, (d) >12 months late: 50% deposit forfeiture + project cancellation. Penalties incentivize timely implementation. (4) **Extension Mechanism:** Developer may request extension: (a) Force majeure events (pandemic, natural disaster, war): extension up to 6 months without penalty, (b) Grid delay (GETCO delay in transmission): extension matching grid delay period, (c) Environmental delay (monsoon preventing construction): up to 3 months. Extensions require documentation and SNA approval. (5) **Commissioning Verification:** GEDA conducts commissioning inspection verifying: (a) Installed capacity matches declared capacity (metering confirms), (b) Technical specifications met (ALMM compliance check), (c) Grid connection stable (synchronization test), (d) Performance minimum met (initial generation data). Upon successful verification, timeline compliance is confirmed.

**PROVISION:** Section 20.3 - Minimum Capacity Achievement Requirement and Commissioning Compliance

**TITLE:** Security Deposit: Installed Capacity Certification and 10% Minimum Commissioning Threshold

**SUMMARY:** Section 20.3 establishes capacity achievement requirements ensuring developers commission announced capacity. Capacity commissioning provisions: (1) **Full Capacity Commissioning Requirement:** Projects must commission 100% of declared capacity within commissioning deadline. Example: 50 MW wind project must fully commission all 50 MW by month 24 (132 kV deadline), not partial 30 MW. (2) **Partial Commissioning Exception:** In exceptional cases, phased commissioning is permitted: (a) **10% Minimum Threshold:** At least 10% of declared capacity (e.g., 5 MW of 50 MW wind) must be commissioned within deadline. This 10% threshold allows partial operation while completing remaining capacity. (b) **Remaining Capacity Timeline:** Remainder (90%) must be commissioned within 6 additional months (extended deadline), (c) **Phasing Rationale:** Allows developers to start revenue generation while completing construction, reducing cash flow strain. (3) **Capacity Verification:** Installed capacity is verified via: (a) **Metering Data:** RTU meters confirm continuous generation matching declared nameplate capacity within ±2% tolerance, (b) **Performance Test:** Operating project must achieve 95%+ availability (ratio of actual hours available to total hours) for 30-day period post-commissioning, (c) **Nameplate vs. Installed:** Nameplate capacity (WTG rated power) matches installed capacity registered with GEDA. (4) **Penalty for Shortfall:** If project commissions <10% of declared capacity within deadline: (a) Security deposit is forfeited (50% minimum), (b) Project approval is cancelled, (c) Developer is blacklisted from future project approvals (1-2 year ban). (5) **Partial Commissioning Penalty:** If phased approach is used (10% commissioned, remainder extending beyond): (a) 5% deposit penalty per month of delay (after 6-month grace for remaining 90%), (b) Energy penalty: project is charged higher grid charges (10% surcharge) during extended commissioning period. Capacity achievement requirement ensures promised generation is delivered.

**PROVISION:** Section 20.4-20.8 - Capacity Factor Guarantee, Repowering Deposit, BESS Deposit, and Special Cases

**TITLE:** Security Deposit: Performance Guarantees and Technology-Specific Provisions

**SUMMARY:** Sections 20.4-20.8 establish specialized security deposit provisions for different project types and performance guarantees. **(20.4) Capacity Factor Guarantee:** Projects declare expected capacity factor—ratio of actual generation to maximum possible generation. Example: 10 MW solar in Gujarat typically 20% CF = 10 × 20% × 8760 = 17,520 MWh/year. If actual CF falls below contracted guarantee (e.g., <19% actual vs. 20% contracted), DISCOM receives tariff adjustment. From developer perspective: security deposit backs underperformance penalty if CF guarantee is missed. Large wind projects (>25 MW) often include 28-30% CF guarantee; if not met, deposit partially forfeited. **(20.5) Repowering Deposit Specifics:** Wind repowering projects (Section 15) have modified deposit requirements: (a) Deposit reduced by 30% from ₹10 lakh/MW → ₹7 lakh/MW, (b) Rationale: repowering reuses existing site with proven wind resource—lower risk than greenfield project, (c) Deposit reduction incentivizes repowering. Example: 30 MW wind repowering deposit = 30 × 7 lakh = ₹2.1 crore (vs. ₹3 crore for greenfield wind). **(20.6) BESS Deposit Specifics:** BESS projects (Section 11) have higher deposit: (a) Standard BESS (independent or RE-paired): ₹15 lakh/MW, (b) Rationale: BESS technology complexity and safety risk (battery thermal runaway, electrolyte spill) justifies higher security. (c) Example: 50 MW/200 MWh BESS deposit = 50 × 15 lakh = ₹7.5 crore. **(20.7) Hybrid Project Deposit:** Wind-solar hybrid projects (Section 10) deposit: (a) Calculated per component: Wind component deposits per wind rate + Solar component per solar rate, (b) Example: 30 MW wind + 20 MW solar hybrid = (30 × ₹10L) + (20 × ₹10L) = ₹5 crore total deposit. (c) Hybrid projects sometimes eligible for 10% discount if achieving synergistic benefits. **(20.8) Non-Bidding Project Deposit:** Small exempted projects (Section 19.3) and direct DISCOM contracts: (a) Same ₹10 lakh/MW deposit as competitive bidding, (b) No deposit reduction despite lower competitive pressure. (c) Reduced deposit would incentivize cheating on capacity claims—GEDA maintains equal deposit. Special deposit provisions recognize technology-specific risks while incentivizing beneficial project types (repowering).

**PROVISION:** Section 21.1 - Electricity Duty Framework and Applicability

**TITLE:** Electricity Duty: Tax on Electricity Generation and Levy Mechanism

**SUMMARY:** Section 21.1 establishes electricity duty—tax on electricity generation applicable to all RE projects in Gujarat. Electricity duty framework: (1) **Statutory Basis:** Electricity Duty is levied under **Gujarat Electricity Duty Act, 1958** (as amended 2003, 2008, 2020, latest amendment referenced as "as amended from time to time"). The Act empowers State government to levy duty on electricity generated within Gujarat or supplied for consumption. (2) **Duty Applicability:** Duty applies to: (a) All electricity generated by RE projects (solar, wind, hybrid, biomass, hydro, geothermal, ocean energy) in Gujarat, (b) Imported electricity from outside Gujarat consumed in Gujarat (via inter-state transmission), (c) Exception: Electricity generated for own consumption and not supplied to grid (off-grid rooftop solar self-consumption) may be exempt per government notification. (3) **Duty Rate Structure:** Electricity duty rates vary by source and consumption type: (a) **Renewable Energy (Solar, Wind, Biomass):** ₹0 (ZERO duty)—Government recognizes RE strategic importance exempting from duty to improve project economics, (b) **Thermal (Coal, Gas, Nuclear):** ₹0.50-1/kWh—Higher duty discourages thermal use, (c) **Hydroelectric:** ₹0.25-0.50/kWh (lower than thermal), (d) **Imported Power:** ₹0.75-1.50/kWh (higher to protect local generation). (4) **Collection Mechanism:** Duty is collected: (a) **At Source:** GUVNL/SLDC collects duty from generator at point of generation/first sale, (b) **Timing:** Weekly or monthly billing cycle, (c) **Payment:** Generator remits duty to State Treasury via GUVNL portal (online payment), (d) **Documentation:** Generation records (RTU data) provide duty basis—no separate metering needed. (5) **Policy Perspective:** RE exemption from electricity duty is strategic tax incentive: (a) Reduces effective RE tariff by ~5-8% (duty value on typical ₹3/kWh tariff), (b) Improves project IRR by 1-2 percentage points, (c) Encourages capital deployment to RE sector. Electricity duty exemption is major policy lever supporting RE expansion.

**PROVISION:** Section 21.2 - RE Project Duty Exemption and Duration

**TITLE:** Electricity Duty: Renewable Energy Tax Exemption Framework

**SUMMARY:** Section 21.2 details RE project electricity duty exemption—zero duty on RE generation for specified period. Exemption framework: (1) **Scope of Exemption:** Following RE technologies are exempt from electricity duty: (a) Solar energy (all types: ground-mounted, rooftop, floating, building-integrated), (b) Wind energy (on-shore, repowering), (c) Hybrid projects (wind-solar, wind-solar-BESS), (d) Biomass/Bagasse projects, (e) Small hydro (≤25 MW, per Electricity Act definition), (f) Tidal and wave energy (ocean energy), (g) Geothermal energy, (h) Emerging technologies (as per Section 12.9 future policies). (2) **Exemption Duration:** Renewable duty exemption is: (a) **Indefinite Duration:** Zero duty continues for entire 25-year generation period (Section 1 preamble, 25-year benefit window), (b) **Post-25 Year:** Beyond 25-year benefit window, duty exemption status depends on government policy at that time—policy does not bind future government. (c) **Rationale:** 25-year exemption matches project financing period (debt repayment 15-20 years, equity returns 25 years)—exemption throughout critical investment recovery period. (3) **BESS Special Treatment:** BESS projects (Section 11): (a) **Input Energy Duty:** Energy used to charge BESS is: (i) If from RE source (solar, wind): charged at RE duty rate = ₹0 (zero), (ii) If from grid (thermal, hydro mix): charged at blended grid duty rate (e.g., 30% RE + 70% thermal = 0.70 × ₹0.75/kWh = ₹0.53/kWh), (iii) If from captive coal: charged at thermal rate ₹0.75-1/kWh. (b) **Stored Energy Duty:** Energy discharged from BESS is: (i) If supplied to RE obligated entity: charged as RE (zero duty), (ii) If supplied to general grid: charged as thermal (₹0.50-0.75/kWh). (4) **Documentation and Claim:** Developers claiming RE exemption must: (a) Register project as RE project with GEDA, (b) Submit annual declaration of RE source and generation, (c) Maintain generation records via RTU (automatic). No separate exemption certificate needed. (5) **Compliance:** Revenue Department conducts periodic audits verifying: (a) Project ALMM compliance (confirming genuine RE), (b) Generation data matches RTU records (preventing fraud), (c) Duty payment compliance (zero duty correctly applied). Audits typically annual ensuring exemption legitimacy. RE duty exemption is major policy instrument—estimated ₹500-1000 crore annual tax foregone to RE sector.

**PROVISION:** Section 21.4 - Duty Compliance and Audit Procedures

**TITLE:** Electricity Duty: Compliance Verification and Revenue Department Oversight

**SUMMARY:** Section 21.4 establishes compliance and audit procedures ensuring duty obligations are met and exemptions are legitimate. Compliance framework: (1) **Self-Assessment Mechanism:** Developers are responsible for: (a) Accurate generation declaration via RTU data (automatic meter readings), (b) Correct duty calculation (RE projects: ₹0, thermal projects: applicable rate), (c) Timely duty payment (monthly to GUVNL/State Treasury), (d) Maintaining records for 7 years (audit trail). (2) **Duty Payment Process:** (a) **GUVNL Billing:** GUVNL calculates duty based on RTU-recorded generation (MWh/month), applies duty rate per technology, issues invoice to developer. (b) **Invoice Components:** Invoice itemizes: (i) Generated quantity (MWh/month), (ii) Applicable duty rate (₹0 for RE, ₹0.75 for thermal, etc.), (iii) Total duty amount, (iv) Payment due date (by 15th of following month). (c) **Online Payment:** Developer pays via GUVNL web portal, State Bank of India, or authorized banks. (d) **Receipt:** GUVNL issues payment receipt with acknowledgment number for tax compliance. (3) **Revenue Department Audit:** State Revenue Department conducts periodic audits (annual to tri-annual frequency) verifying: (a) **Technology Verification:** Inspect project to confirm ALMM compliance—verifying equipment specifications match declared type (e.g., panel model, turbine capacity). (b) **Generation Data Cross-Check:** Compare RTU records (automated meter data) with manual inspection records and developer declaration—detecting falsification. (c) **Duty Payment Compliance:** Verify all duty payments made on time, no arrears or discrepancies. (d) **Exemption Legitimacy:** For RE projects claiming exemption, verify: (i) Registration as RE project with GEDA confirmed, (ii) No thermal blending (solar farm not using backup diesel), (iii) All generation from declared RE source. (4) **Audit Outcomes:** (a) **Compliance:** Certificate issued confirming exemption validity (needed for refinancing/ownership transfer per Section 27). (b) **Minor Discrepancy:** Assessed penalty: ₹10,000-₹1 lakh per discrepancy + corrective duty payment. (c) **Major Violation (Fraud):** Criminal prosecution per IPC Section 420 (forgery, fraud); penalty: ₹10 lakh-₹1 crore + imprisonment up to 5 years. Penalties are strong deterrents. (5) **Exemption Cancellation:** Exemption may be cancelled if: (a) Project fundamentally changes (e.g., solar farm converts to thermal), (b) Fraud detected, (c) Technology downgrade (e.g., ALMM-non-compliant panels installed post-commissioning). Upon cancellation, back-duty for 3 years is assessed.
