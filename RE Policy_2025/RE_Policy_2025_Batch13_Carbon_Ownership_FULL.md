**PROVISION:** Section 26.1 - Carbon Credit Types and Eligible Technologies

**TITLE:** Carbon Credits: International & Domestic Credit Mechanisms for Emission Reduction

**SUMMARY:** Section 26.1 establishes carbon credit framework—mechanisms to monetize greenhouse gas emission reduction achieved by RE projects. Carbon credit systems: (1) **CER (Certified Emission Reduction):** (a) **Framework:** UNFCCC Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) enabling developing countries (India listed) to generate international carbon credits, (b) **Credit Definition:** 1 CER = 1 tonne CO2 equivalent (tCO2e) avoided vs. thermal generation baseline, (c) **Trading Market:** Traded on international carbon exchanges (ICE Futures, BlueNext, regional exchanges); price historically ₹500-2000/CER (varies by vintage, project type, climate). (d) **Registration:** Project registers with CDM Executive Board, submits baseline/monitoring plan (3-6 months review), upon approval, annual generation reports to UNFCCC, third-party verification, CER issued within 6 months post-verification. (e) **Eligible Projects:** Large-scale renewable (≥5 MW typically), energy efficiency, biomass per CDM methodology list (standardized). (2) **VER (Verified Emission Reduction):** (a) **Framework:** Voluntary carbon market (non-CDM), lower verification stringency, enables projects ineligible for CER (small <5 MW, emerging tech, India coal phase-out projects). (b) **Standard Examples:** Verified Carbon Standard (VCS), Gold Standard (GS, combines emissions + sustainable development), Plan Vivo (community forestry/agriculture). (c) **Price Range:** ₹200-1000/VER (lower than CER due to lower assurance), traded on voluntary exchanges (Ecosystem Marketplace, VerraVerra). (d) **Advantage:** Faster issuance (6-12 months vs. 12-18 for CER), lower cost verification. (3) **Gold Standard Credits:** (a) **Premium Tier:** Highest-quality voluntary credits meeting rigorous social/environmental criteria beyond emissions, (b) **Criteria:** Projects must demonstrate: (i) Sustainable development co-benefits (job creation, water access, health), (ii) Stakeholder consultation, (iii) Additionality (project wouldn't happen without carbon revenue). (c) **Price Premium:** ₹2000-5000/credit (3-5x CER price), attractive for ESG-focused investors, corporates meeting climate commitments. (d) **Examples:** RE projects with: women training programs, local community benefit-sharing, biodiversity protection. (4) **India Carbon Credits (ICC):** (a) **New Domestic Market:** Under Paris Agreement Article 6, India establishing domestic carbon credit trading system (as of 2023-24 pilot phase), (b) **Mechanism:** Generators reducing emissions register, generate credits, trade on India Energy Exchange (IEX) with floor/ceiling price set by government. (c) **Integration:** ICC complements international CERs; domestic market reduces external market dependence, (d) **Price Target:** Expected ₹500-1500/ICC (aligned with international pricing). (5) **Baseline Calculation:** (a) **Thermal Baseline:** Emissions avoided calculated vs. thermal generation (coal baseline most common). Example baseline: coal = 1150 g CO2/kWh (IPCC methodology). (b) **Calculation Formula:** Annual Emission Reduction = Annual Generation (MWh) × Thermal Baseline (g CO2/kWh) / 1,000,000 = Credits (tCO2e). (c) **Example:** 50 MW solar project, 20% capacity factor, 8760 hours/year, 1150 g CO2/kWh baseline = 50 × 0.20 × 8760 × 1150 / 1,000,000 = 40,656 tCO2e/year ≈ 40,656 CERs. (6) **Eligible Technologies:** (a) **Automatic:** Solar (all types), wind (on-shore, off-shore), small hydro (<25 MW), geothermal, ocean/tidal. (b) **Case-by-case:** BESS (if charged from RE), hybrid projects, waste-heat recovery, (c) **Ineligible:** Large hydro (>25 MW), nuclear (existing policy), thermal (fossil fuels). (7) **Registry & Issuance:** Carbon credits issued as electronic tokens registered in international (UNFCCC) or domestic (IEX) registries, preventing double-counting and enabling transparent trading. Registry ensures integrity—each credit represents verified, unique emission reduction.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- CER (Certified Emission Reduction); UNFCCC CDM
- VER (Verified Emission Reduction); Voluntary carbon market
- Gold Standard Credits (premium tier, sustainable development focus)
- India Carbon Credits (ICC); Domestic market pilot
- Carbon credit price (₹500-2000 CER, ₹200-1000 VER, ₹2000-5000 Gold Standard)
- Baseline calculation (1150 g CO2/kWh coal equivalent)
- Annual credit generation (MWh × baseline / 1M)
- Eligible technologies (solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, ocean)
- Ineligible technologies (large hydro, thermal, nuclear)
- Registry and issuance (UNFCCC, IEX, national registries)
- International vs. domestic markets (CER vs. ICC)

**TEXT:**

Carbon credits quantify GHG emission reduction: 1 CER = 1 tCO2e avoided. Types: CER (international CDM, ₹500-2000/credit), VER (voluntary, ₹200-1000), Gold Standard (premium, ₹2000-5000), ICC (domestic India market, ₹500-1500). Baseline: thermal coal 1150 g CO2/kWh. Generation formula: Annual credits = Annual MWh × 1150 g CO2/kWh / 1M. Eligible: solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, ocean. Registered in national/international registries preventing double-counting. CER issuance: 12-18 months (registration → verification → issuance); VER: 6-12 months (faster).

**PROVISION:** Section 26.2 - Carbon Credit Ownership and Revenue Sharing Framework

**TITLE:** Carbon Credits: Allocation of Ownership Rights and Revenue Distribution

**SUMMARY:** Section 26.2 specifies carbon credit ownership—who retains right to generate, register, and monetize credits. Ownership framework: (1) **PPA Supply Projects (DISCOM/Bilateral Contracts):** (a) **Credit Ownership:** DISCOM (power buyer) retains carbon credits bundled with energy supply, (b) **Rationale:** DISCOMs are public entities; public benefit principle—credits (representing public goods climate mitigation value) accrue to public utility, improving tariff economics for consumers. (c) **Developer Implication:** Developer's tariff is implicitly lower than equivalent thermal generation—reduction accounts for credit value. Example: Thermal baseline tariff ₹5/kWh, RE tariff ₹3/kWh; implicit credit value = ₹2/kWh (baseline cost - RE cost), (d) **DISCOM Sale:** DISCOM may sell credits to third parties, revenue accrues to DISCOM (offset against consumer tariff or reinvested in grid). (e) **Developer Benefit:** Implicit via lower tariff requirement (improving project economics vs. pure energy tariff). (2) **REC Mechanism Projects:** (a) **Credit Ownership:** Developer/project owner retains separate carbon credits (independent of energy/RECs), (b) **Rationale:** REC projects already separate attributes from energy (energy sold at market price, RECs sold separately per Section 16); carbon credits are additional attribute distinct from RECs. (c) **Developer Monetization:** Developer independently registers project with UNFCCC/IEX, submits annual generation/monitoring reports, sells credits to carbon buyers (international utilities, corporations, governments). (d) **Revenue Stream:** Separate from energy and REC revenue. Example: 50 MW solar REC project revenue = ₹3/kWh energy + ₹500/MWh REC (₹0.50/kWh equiv.) + ₹600/tCO2e CER × 40,656 tCO2e/year = ₹2.45 crore/year (₹3 + ₹0.50 + ₹2.45 = ₹5.95/kWh equivalent value). (3) **Competitive Bidding & Credit Inclusion:** (a) **Bidding Documents:** DISCOM RFB (Request for Bid) explicitly states carbon ownership policy: (i) "DISCOM retains all carbon credits" (standard), (ii) "Developer retains credits" (rare, may result in lower tariff bid). (b) **Bidders' Adjustment:** Knowing DISCOM retains credits, developers bid lower tariff (implicit credit value discount). Knowing developer retains credits, developers may bid higher tariff (explicit credit revenue value). (c) **Example:** DISCOM issues RFB for 50 MW solar with "DISCOM retains credits" → Developer bids ₹2.80/kWh (accounting for implicit credit loss). If RFB said "Developer retains credits" → Developer bids ₹2.50/kWh (compensating for lower explicit credit revenue but higher implicit tariff). (4) **Strategic Planning:** DISCOMs strategically decide carbon ownership per: (a) **Tariff Pressure:** If DISCOM under tariff regulation pressure, retains credits to lower consumer tariff, (b) **Financial Capacity:** If DISCOM has carbon trading team, sells credits for revenue; if not, lower tariff benefit is political win. (c) **RE Expansion Strategy:** Retaining credits makes RE tariff more competitive vs. thermal, accelerating coal phase-out. (5) **Time Limitations:** Carbon credit ownership is perpetual (throughout 25-year project life), extending potential to 50+ years if project repowered/refurbished. Credits generated annually per year's actual generation—no time limit on credit monetization (CER registered once, issued annually as generation verified).

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Carbon credit ownership allocation
- PPA projects: DISCOM ownership (bundled with energy)
- REC projects: Developer ownership (separate attribute)
- Implicit vs. explicit credit value
- Bidding document specification of credit ownership
- Developer tariff adjustment (response to ownership policy)
- DISCOM strategic use of credits (tariff reduction or revenue)
- Annual credit generation and monetization
- 25-year benefit window alignment (same as project life)
- Carbon trading team requirement (for DISCOM credit sale)

**TEXT:**

Carbon credit ownership: PPA projects → DISCOM retains credits (bundled with energy, implicit credit value reflected in lower tariff); REC projects → Developer retains credits (separate monetization, additional revenue stream). Bidding documents specify credit ownership affecting bidder tariff response: DISCOM retention → lower developer bid (implicit credit discount); Developer retention → higher bid (explicit credit revenue value). DISCOM may sell credits for revenue or lower tariff. Credits generated annually throughout 25-year project life; cumulative over 25 years ≈ 1,000,000 tCO2e for 50 MW solar (worth ₹15-50 crore at ₹1500-5000/credit depending on credit type).

**PROVISION:** Section 26.3 - Carbon Credit Registration and Verification Procedures

**TITLE:** Carbon Credits: UNFCCC Registration and Annual Verification Process

**SUMMARY:** Section 26.3 establishes carbon credit registration and verification procedures ensuring legitimate credit generation. Registration framework: (1) **Pre-Registration Baseline Study (0-3 Months):** (a) Developer/project owner engages UNFCCC-accredited baseline consultant to conduct baseline study—document: (i) Project details (capacity, location, technology type), (ii) Thermal baseline (coal generation avoided, historical grid mix emission factor), (iii) Additionality assessment (project wouldn't proceed without carbon revenue), (iv) Monitoring methodology (how generation will be measured annually). (b) Baseline study generates 20-30 page document submitted to UNFCCC CDM Executive Board. (c) Cost: ₹20-50 lakh baseline study (developer cost). (2) **UNFCCC CDM Registration (3-6 Months):** (a) **Application Submission:** Project owner submits: (i) Baseline study, (ii) Project Design Document (PDD, 30-50 pages detailing project, technology, emissions reduction), (iii) Host country approval letter (India Ministry of Environment & Forests approves CDM project for India). (b) **Review & Approval:** UNFCCC Executive Board reviews PDD (90-day period typical), approves baseline + monitoring plan, issues registration certificate. (c) **Timeline:** 3-6 months from submission to registration approval (can extend if UNFCCC requests clarifications). (d) **Cost:** Registration fee ₹10-20 lakh + consultant cost. (3) **Annual Monitoring & Generation Reports:** (a) **Annual Report Submission:** By March 31 following each year of operation, project owner submits to UNFCCC: (i) Annual generation data (MWh generated, from RTU records), (ii) Calculated annual carbon reduction (MWh × baseline emission factor = tCO2e), (iii) Monitoring report (any deviations from baseline assumptions, corrective actions taken). (b) **Report Contents:** Generation report must certify: (i) Meter data accuracy (±2%), (ii) No double-counting with other mechanisms (REC program, bilateral agreements—cannot claim same MWh for multiple credits), (iii) Additionality still holds (project still wouldn't proceed without carbon credit revenue). (4) **Third-Party Verification (6-12 Months Post Annual Report):** (a) **Independent Verifier:** UNFCCC-accredited verification body (e.g., SGS, DNV, TÜV) audits annual monitoring report—verifies: (i) Generation data matches metering records (RTU data), (ii) Calculation of carbon reduction accurate per baseline methodology, (iii) No double-counting identified, (iv) Project operational per approved baseline. (b) **Verification Cost:** ₹15-30 lakh/year per verification engagement. (c) **Verification Duration:** 4-8 weeks typical (auditor desk review + site visit). (d) **Output:** Verification Report confirming generation and carbon reduction accurate, ready for CER issuance. (5) **CER Issuance (Post Verification, 12-18 Months Total from Annual Report):** (a) Upon verification completion, UNFCCC issues CERs electronically into project's national registry account (India Climate Registry for India-based projects). (b) **Issuance Quantity:** Issued CERs = Verified annual carbon reduction (tCO2e). Example: 40,656 tCO2e verified → 40,656 CERs issued (1 CER per tCO2e). (c) **Timeline:** CERs issued typically 6-12 months post verification completion. (d) **Transferability:** CERs can be traded immediately upon issuance (no holding period), sold to buyers domestically or internationally. (6) **Compliance & Fraud Prevention:** (a) **Double-Counting Prevention:** All RE projects in India registered with REC program (Section 16)—REC registry cross-checks prevent same MWh from generating both REC and CER claims. (b) **Annual Audit Trail:** GEDA maintains generation records; carbon credit registrations cross-referenced ensuring no duplicated claims. (c) **Penalty:** Fraudulent carbon credit claim results in: (i) Credit cancellation, (ii) Developer blacklist (1-2 year ban from future projects), (iii) Criminal prosecution (IPC Section 420 fraud). (7) **Cumulative Impact:** Over 25-year project life, 50 MW solar project with 20% capacity factor → 40,656 tCO2e/year × 25 years = 1,016,400 tCO2e cumulative credits ≈ ₹15-50 crore value (at ₹1500-5000/credit). Carbon credit registration and verification is lengthy (12-18 months initial, 6-12 months annual) but critical for credit legitimacy and market acceptance.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Baseline study (0-3 months, ₹20-50L cost)
- UNFCCC registration (3-6 months, ₹10-20L fee)
- Baseline study components (project details, thermal baseline, additionality, monitoring)
- Project Design Document (PDD, 30-50 pages)
- Host country approval (India Ministry Environment & Forests)
- Annual monitoring report (by March 31)
- Generation data from RTU records
- Additionality verification (annual reassessment)
- Third-party verification (6-12 months, ₹15-30L cost)
- Independent auditor (UNFCCC-accredited: SGS, DNV, TÜV)
- CER issuance (post-verification, 1 CER = 1 tCO2e)
- Registry account (India Climate Registry)
- Double-counting prevention (REC registry cross-check)
- Fraud penalties (cancellation, blacklist, prosecution)
- Cumulative 25-year impact (1,016,400 tCO2e for 50 MW solar, ₹15-50 Cr value)

**TEXT:**

Carbon credit registration: Baseline study (₹20-50L) → UNFCCC registration (₹10-20L, 3-6 months) → Annual monitoring report (by March 31) → Third-party verification (₹15-30L/year, 4-8 weeks) → CER issuance (6-12 months post-verification, 1 CER per tCO2e). Total timeline: 12-18 months initial registration, then 6-12 months annual issuance. Double-counting prevention via REC registry cross-check. Fraud penalties: cancellation, blacklist, IPC Section 420 prosecution. 25-year cumulative: 50 MW solar ≈ 1M tCO2e ≈ ₹15-50 Cr value.

**PROVISION:** Section 27.1 - Change of Ownership Application and Regulatory Succession

**TITLE:** Ownership Transfer: Procedures for Project Sale and Regulatory Continuity

**SUMMARY:** Section 27.1 establishes streamlined change of ownership procedure—enabling project transfer to new owner while maintaining regulatory continuity and existing contractual protections. Change of ownership framework: (1) **Trigger Events for Ownership Transfer:** (a) **Asset Sale:** Existing developer sells RE project (physical equipment, land lease, all contracts) to new buyer (e.g., financial investor acquires operational solar farm from engineering firm). (b) **Refinancing:** Existing owner refinances project (debt restructuring, equity sale to new partner, leveraged buyout). (c) **M&A (Merger/Acquisition):** Parent company acquires project developer, ownership changes but project continues under new corporate structure. (d) **Bankruptcy/Distress:** Project transferred to insolvency trustee, then sold to auction winner (debt recovery mechanism). (e) **Founder Exit:** Founding developer retires, sells to institutional investor (private equity, pension fund). (2) **New Owner Qualification Requirements:** (a) **Financial Capacity Certification:** New owner demonstrates financial capacity to operate project 25 years—requires: (i) Bank letter of support (lender confirms credit facility availability for O&M costs), (ii) Balance sheet (minimum ₹5-10 crore net worth for ≥10 MW project, scaled proportionally), (iii) Existing project experience (reference from prior projects owned). (b) **Operational Capability Verification:** New owner must prove operational expertise—requires: (i) O&M team introduction (propose maintenance staff, engineers, technicians), (ii) Historical O&M records (demonstrate prior project maintenance quality if existing operator), (iii) GEDA site inspection (GEDA verifies proposed O&M vendor capability, facility inspection). (c) **GEDA Approval:** New owner submits ownership transfer application to GEDA with supporting documentation; GEDA approves if new owner meets financial + operational criteria (typical 15-30 day approval vs. 45 days for new project approval). (d) **Timeline Benefit:** Ownership transfer approval ≈50% faster than new project approval (saving 2-3 weeks, valuable during bidding/acquisition rush). (3) **Regulatory Continuity—No Re-Approval:** (a) **ALMM Approval Continuation:** ALMM compliance certification (validating equipment standards) transfers to new owner—no re-approval needed, no re-testing of turbines/panels. (b) **Grid Connection Continuation:** Grid connection and synchronization approval transfer to new owner—GETCO recognizes new owner as successor without requiring new grid study (BPTA Bilateral Transmission Agreement transfers, see point 4). (c) **REC Registration Continuation:** REC mechanism project registration and CERC auditor accreditation transfer to new owner—no re-accreditation needed (auditor continues annual verification). (d) **Environmental Clearance Continuation:** EIA approval and forest/wildlife clearances issued originally—continue with new owner (no re-clearance needed, government recognizes regulatory continuity). (e) **Regulatory Savings:** Avoiding re-approvals saves: 3-6 months timeline, ₹30-50 lakh approval costs (ALMM testing, EIA update, forest clearance repeat), critical for rapid M&A execution. (4) **Contract Succession & Regulatory Obligations:** (a) **PPA Succession:** Existing DISCOM Power Purchase Agreement (Section 19) transfers to new owner—agreement terms continue unchanged (same tariff, same duration, same performance guarantees). New owner becomes "project company" in PPA legal documents (technical assignment), but energy supply obligations, payment terms, penalties all continue. (b) **Wheeling Agreement Succession:** Wheeling arrangements with third parties (Section 9.3) transfer to new owner—third-party consumers continue receiving power from project under same terms. (c) **BPTA Transmission Agreement Succession:** Bilateral Point-to-Point Transmission Agreement (Section 25.5) with transmission company continues—transmission company has no objection to ownership change (transmission company cares about grid interface, not developer identity). Capacity reservation (50 MW for 50 MW project) transfers automatically. (d) **Environmental Obligations:** Environmental commitments (EIA mitigations, forest clearance conditions, wildlife protections) transfer to new owner—new owner legally responsible for compliance (if original owner violated conditions, GECA can enforce against new owner, imposing ₹1-10 lakh penalties). (e) **Legal Mechanism:** Ownership transfer deed specifies "New Owner succeeds to all contractual obligations of Original Owner"—creates binding obligation transferring all rights + duties to new owner. (5) **Security Deposit & Financial Obligations:** (a) **Security Deposit Transfer:** Original security deposit (Section 20, ₹10 lakh/MW) may be: (i) Released to original owner (new owner provides replacement deposit), or (ii) Directly transferred to new owner name (per agreement between parties). GEDA facilitates transfer ensuring ₹10 lakh/MW is always in place. (b) **Performance Guarantees:** Capacity factor guarantee (if applicable, e.g., "≥25% CF guaranteed") transfers to new owner—if guarantee not met, new owner liable for penalty (₹50-100 lakh penalty possible if CF falls significantly below contracted). (c) **Debt Assumption:** Existing project debt (bank loan, bond issuance) typically remains with original owner—new owner refinances or assumes debt per acquisition terms. Original owner released from obligation upon new owner assumption (lender consent required). (d) **Tax Liabilities:** Any unpaid taxes, electricity duties (Section 21) transfer to new owner (government can recover from new owner if original owner defaulted). (6) **Conditions for Contractual Continuity:** (a) **Technology Consistency:** Ownership transfer permits equipment maintenance/minor repairs but NOT fundamental technology change—if new owner wants to replace turbine model or panel type (technology downgrade), new approval required. Example: Changing from premium ALMM panel A to inferior panel B without re-ALMM approval prohibited. (b) **Location Consistency:** Project must remain at same location—relocation to different site requires new project approval (land clearances, environmental clearance, grid connection all change). (c) **Material Breach Remedy:** If original owner in material breach (e.g., capacity <80% contracted, operational failure, environmental violation), GEDA may impose conditions on transfer: (i) Performance bond increase (surety bond guaranteeing new owner performance), (ii) Mandatory O&M vendor (GEDA specifies approved O&M provider for technical oversight). (d) **Regulatory Compliance:** If original owner under regulatory investigation (GERC inquiry, environmental violation), transfer may be conditional on investigation closure or remediation. (7) **Timeline & Cost:** (a) **Approval Timeline:** 15-30 days from complete application (vs. 45 days for new project), (b) **GEDA Approval Fee:** ₹5-10 lakh (vs. ₹20-50 lakh for new project application fee), (c) **Legal Documentation:** Ownership transfer deed (₹10-20 lakh lawyer cost), (d) **Regulatory Compliance Documents:** Updated ALMM certificate, carbon credit transfer forms, contractual novation agreements (₹5-10 lakh total), (e) **Total Ownership Transfer Cost:** ₹20-40 lakh (vs. ₹100-200 lakh for greenfield project development), **Savings:** 3-6 months timeline, ₹80-160 lakh cost vs. new project development—major advantage for re-deployment of capital. Streamlined transfer enables efficient capital recycling—allows successful project sale to new operators, encouraging operational improvement and secondary market development.

**KEY TOPICS:**
- Change of ownership; Asset sale, refinancing, M&A, bankruptcy, founder exit
- New owner financial capacity verification (bank letter, balance sheet, net worth)
- Operational capability verification (O&M team, experience, site inspection)
- GEDA approval (15-30 days, ₹5-10L fee)
- No re-approval of existing authorizations (ALMM, grid, REC, environmental clearance)
- Regulatory continuity benefit (3-6 months saving, ₹30-50L cost saving)
- PPA succession (tariff, duration, penalties continue)
- Wheeling agreement continuation (third-party supply unchanged)
- BPTA transmission agreement transfer (capacity reservation continues)
- Environmental obligation transfer (new owner legal responsibility)
- Security deposit transfer or replacement (₹10L/MW maintained)
- Performance guarantee succession (new owner liable for underperformance penalty)
- Debt refinancing (lender consent required)
- Conditions: technology consistency (no downgrade), location consistency (no relocation)
- Material breach conditions (performance bond, O&M vendor mandate)
- Ownership transfer cost (₹20-40L total vs. ₹100-200L new project)
- Approval timeline (15-30 days vs. 45 days new project)

**TEXT:**

Ownership transfer: new owner qualifies via financial capacity (bank letter, ₹5-10Cr net worth) and operational capability (O&M team, GEDA inspection). GEDA approval 15-30 days (₹5-10L fee). Existing approvals transfer without re-approval: ALMM, grid connection, REC registration, environmental clearance (saving 3-6 months, ₹30-50L cost). PPA, wheeling, BPTA agreements all successor to new owner (contracts continue unchanged). Security deposit transfer or replace (₹10L/MW maintained). Performance guarantees, environmental obligations transfer to new owner (new owner liable). Conditions: no technology downgrade, no relocation, material breach remedies (performance bond, O&M vendor). Total cost ₹20-40L (vs. ₹100-200L new project). Streamlined transfer enables capital recycling and secondary market development.

**PROVISION:** Section 27.2 - Ownership Transfer Documentation and Title Verification

**TITLE:** Ownership Transfer: Legal Documentation and Regulatory Closure Procedures

**SUMMARY:** Section 27.2 establishes legal documentation requirements and verification procedures for ownership transfer closure. Documentation framework: (1) **Title Verification & Dispute Resolution:** (a) **Title Search:** New owner (buyer) conducts comprehensive legal title search verifying: (i) Current owner's legal right to sell (no co-ownership dispute, no legal claim against project), (ii) Land title clear (no competing land claims, no encumbrances), (iii) All contracts assigned (PPA, BPTA, wheeling agreements properly transferred). (b) **Title Insurance:** Buyer obtains title insurance policy (₹5-10 lakh premium) guaranteeing ownership legitimacy—if ownership dispute emerges post-transfer, insurance covers legal costs/damages. (c) **No Pending Litigation:** Seller warranted to disclose any pending legal cases (e.g., environmental suit, contract dispute with DISCOM, grid connection dispute). If litigation disclosed, buyer may: (i) Renegotiate price (discount for litigation risk), (ii) Request seller escrow (seller funds held pending litigation resolution), (iii) Decline purchase. (2) **Deed Execution & Registration:** (a) **Transfer Deed Preparation:** Seller + Buyer execute formal ownership transfer deed (Deed of Conveyance) transferring all equipment, land rights, contracts from seller to buyer. Deed includes: (i) Project description (capacity, location, technology, GEDA registration number), (ii) Consideration amount (purchase price in rupees), (iii) Rights transferred (equipment ownership, land lease rights, contractual rights), (iv) Obligations succeeding (environmental commitments, performance guarantees), (v) Warranties (seller confirms legal right to sell, no hidden liabilities). (b) **Deed Registration:** Deed registered at local Sub-Registrar office (revenue department) creating legal record of ownership transfer—owner-ship transfer becomes official upon Sub-Registrar registration. Registration cost: ₹1-2 lakh registration fee + stamp duty (1-2% of transaction value, e.g., ₹1-5 crore purchase = ₹1-10 lakh stamp duty). (c) **Timeline:** Deed preparation 1-2 weeks, Sub-Registrar registration 2-4 weeks = 3-6 week total legal closure. (3) **Contractual Novation Agreements:** (a) **PPA Novation:** Existing DISCOM PPA is amended via "Novation Agreement"—legal document confirming: (i) DISCOM consents to buyer assuming contractual obligations (acceptance as new project company), (ii) Tariff, payment terms, penalties continue unchanged, (iii) DISCOM has no claim against seller post-novation (seller released). Novation executed with DISCOM signature and GERC notification (regulatory record). (b) **BPTA Novation:** Similar novation with transmission company confirming buyer succeeds to transmission rights. (c) **Wheeling Agreement Novation:** If applicable, third-party off-taker consents to buyer assignment. (4) **Financial Closure & Escrow Arrangements:** (a) **Escrow Account:** Portion of purchase price (typically 10-20%) held in escrow (third-party bank custody) for 12-24 months—released upon: (i) Verification of all representations accurate (no hidden liabilities discovered), (ii) Environmental compliance confirmed (no violations outstanding), (iii) Performance guarantees met (capacity factor targets achieved). Escrow serves as buyer protection against seller misrepresentation. (b) **Payment Structure:** Typical payment: (i) 70% at closing (transfer deed registered, GEDA approval issued), (ii) 20% upon 90-day operational verification (new owner proven capable), (iii) 10% escrow (released post-12 month compliance verification).
