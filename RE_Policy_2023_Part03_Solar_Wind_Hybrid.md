## CHUNK 8: SOLAR – GROUND MOUNT SOLAR PROJECTS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 9 – Solar / Section 9.1 – Ground Mount Solar

PROVISION: Section 9.1 (Subsections 9.1.1, 9.1.2, 9.1.3)

TITLE: Ground Mount Solar Projects – Location, Procurement, and Wheeling

SUMMARY:
Section 9.1 establishes the framework for ground-mounted solar projects. Projects can be set up in solar parks or outside solar parks on government revenue land or private land. Government land or land available with the State Nodal Agency is made available to RE developers as per Clause 28 (Land allocation). Distribution Licensees may procure from solar power projects in accordance with Clause 16 (Purchase of power by DISCOMs). Wheeling of power for captive use or third-party sale is allowed upon payment of transmission and wheeling charges as per Clause 15 (Grid Charges), with energy settlement as per Clause 14 (Energy Settlement and Banking). These provisions establish the complete project development and commercial pathway for large-scale solar installations.

KEY TOPICS: Ground Mount Solar, Solar Parks, Government Land, Private Land, Distribution Licensees, Power Procurement, Wheeling Charges, Captive Use, Third-Party Sale, Energy Settlement

TEXT:

9. Solar 

9.1 Ground Mount solar: 

9.1.1 Solar projects can be setup in a solar park, or outside the solar park, on government revenue land, or on private land. Government land or land available with the State Nodal Agency shall be made available to the RE developers as per Clause No. 28 of this Policy. 

9.1.2 The Distribution Licensees may procure from solar power projects in accordance with the Clause No. 16 of this Policy. 

9.1.3 Wheeling of power for captive use or third party sale shall be allowed on payment of charges as per Clause No.15 and energy settlement will be as per Clause No. 14 of this policy.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Clause 14 (Energy Settlement and Banking), Clause 15 (Grid Charges), Clause 16 (Purchase of power by DISCOMs), Clause 28 (Land allocation)

## CHUNK 9: SOLAR – ROOFTOP SOLAR PROJECTS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 9 – Solar / Section 9.2 – Rooftop Solar

PROVISION: Section 9.2 (Subsections 9.2.1, 9.2.2)

TITLE: Rooftop Solar Projects – Net Metering, Gross Metering, and Tariff Framework

SUMMARY:
Section 9.2 establishes the framework for rooftop (distributed) solar projects. Solar projects can be installed by consumers on rooftops or within premises under net metering arrangement or gross metering arrangement, as per applicable GERC regulations. Consumers may avail incentives under existing central/state government schemes. For projects under gross metering arrangement selling to Distribution Licensees, the applicable tariff is the simple average of tariffs discovered and contracted under competitive bidding by GUVNL in the preceding 6 months (April-September or October-March), plus 20 paisa/unit. This tariff is fixed for PPAs signed in the subsequent 6-month period and remains fixed for the entire 25-year PPA term. This provision balances consumer interests with predictable long-term revenue for developers while leveraging market discovery mechanisms.

KEY TOPICS: Rooftop Solar, Distributed Generation, Net Metering, Gross Metering, GUVNL Tariff, Tariff Formula, 25-Year PPA, Market Discovery, Incentive Schemes

TEXT:

9.2 Rooftop Solar: 

9.2.1 Solar projects can be installed by consumers on rooftops or within premises under a net metering arrangement or under a gross metering arrangement, as per applicable GERC regulation from time to time. Incentives under existing central / state government schemes, as the case may be, can be availed by consumers as per the provisions of the respective scheme. 

9.2.2 For projects set up under gross metering arrangement and selling power to Distribution Licensees, the applicable tariff will be equal to the simple average of the tariff discovered and contracted under the competitive bidding process conducted by GUVNL in the preceding 6 months, i.e., either April to September or October to March, as the case may be, plus 20 paisa /unit, which shall be applicable for signing of PPAs in the subsequent 6-month period and such tariff shall remain fixed for the 25 year term of the PPA.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GERC Regulations on net metering and gross metering, Clause 14 (Energy Settlement), GUVNL competitive bidding process

CROSS-REFERENCES: Energy banking in Section 14.1-14.3, Grid charges in Section 15, DISCOMs purchase framework in Section 16, Plant and machinery standards in Section 26

## CHUNK 10: SOLAR – FLOATING AND CANAL-BASED SOLAR PROJECTS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 9 – Solar / Section 9.3 – Floating/Canal-based Solar

PROVISION: Section 9.3 (Subsections 9.3.1, 9.3.2, 9.3.3)

TITLE: Floating and Canal-Based Solar Projects – Implementation and Water Body Utilization

SUMMARY:
Section 9.3 addresses floating and canal-based solar projects, which utilize water reservoirs and canals to avoid land requirements and reduce evaporation. Such projects are implemented in consultation with Narmada, Water Resources, Water Supply, Kalpsar Department, or other relevant government authorities controlling water bodies. These authorities identify potential suitable sites and locations for project implementation. Distribution Licensees may procure from floating or canal-based solar projects in accordance with Clause 16. When DISCOMs purchase power from such projects, they pay concessional rates as royalty to the relevant irrigation or water resources authority. For floating solar projects set up for captive use or third-party sale, royalty is paid by the procurer as decided by the respective authority. This framework balances environmental benefits (reduced evaporation, no land use) with fair compensation for water body utilization.

KEY TOPICS: Floating Solar, Canal-Based Solar, Water Reservoirs, Land Avoidance, Evaporation Reduction, Water Resources, Royalty Charges, Captive Use, Third-Party Sale, Water Body Utilization

TEXT:

9.3 Floating/canal-based solar: 

9.3.1 The state has several water reservoirs and canals that can be utilized for setting up floating/canal- based solar projects. Such projects avoid the requirement for land and also reduce evaporation in water bodies. 

9.3.2 Floating or canal-based solar projects are to be implemented in consultation with Narmada, Water Resources, Water Supply, Kalpsar Department, or such other government authority having control over canals, rivers, streams, etc. Such a Government Authority shall identify potential suitable sites or locations for setting up the project. 

9.3.3 The Distribution Licensees may procure power from floating or canal-based solar projects in accordance with Clause No. 16. For floating or canal based projects, where the Distribution Licensees are purchasing power, the Distribution Licensees shall pay amount equivalents to the concessional rate for government waste land as royalty to the Irrigation Dept., Narmada Water Resources, Water Supply, Kalpsar Dept., or any such authority for the utilization of water bodies, as the case may be. In the case of floating solar project set up for captive use or third-party sale, the royalty shall be paid by the procurer as decided by the respective authority.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Clause 16 (Purchase of power by DISCOMs), Water Resources Management framework, Narmada Water Resources Department coordination

CROSS-REFERENCES: Land allocation in Section 28, Environmental benefits through renewable adoption, Grid connectivity in Section 20, DISCOMs procurement in Section 16

TITLE: Wind Projects – Ground-Based and Rooftop (Small-Scale) Installation

SUMMARY:
Section 10 establishes the framework for wind energy projects. Wind projects can be set up in wind parks or outside wind parks on government revenue land or private land, with government land made available through the State Nodal Agency as per Clause 28. Small-scale wind projects can be installed by consumers on rooftops or within premises under net metering arrangement (to offset own consumption) or gross metering arrangement (to sell entire generation to DISCOMs). RE developers may avail incentives/benefits under central or state government schemes. This provision provides flexibility in wind project scale from utility-scale installations in organized parks to distributed, small-scale rooftop installations for individual consumers, supporting diverse deployment models.

## CHUNK 13: WIND-SOLAR HYBRID – COMPLEMENTARITY AND CAPACITY RATIOS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 11 – Wind-Solar Hybrid / Section 11.1 and 11.2

PROVISION: Section 11.1 and 11.2

TITLE: Wind-Solar Hybrid Framework – Technology Complementarity and MNRE Capacity Guidelines

SUMMARY:
Section 11.1 and 11.2 establish the foundational framework for wind-solar hybrid projects, which combine complementary energy sources to minimize variability and optimize land and transmission infrastructure utilization. The capacity ratio of one resource (solar or wind) in hybrid projects is determined by the National Wind-Solar Hybrid Policy notified by MNRE via letter F. No. 238/78/2017-Wind dated 14 May 2018 and its amendments. This provision recognizes that solar generation peaks during daylight hours while wind generation patterns vary seasonally and daily, enabling better grid stability and resource utilization when combined. The hybrid approach supports the policy's objective of minimizing variability in renewable energy injection while maximizing infrastructure optimization.

KEY TOPICS: Hybrid Projects, Wind-Solar Complementarity, Technology Integration, Variability Minimization, MNRE Policy, Capacity Ratios, Infrastructure Optimization, Land Efficiency

TEXT:

11. Wind—Solar Hybrid 

11.1 Solar and wind energy generations being complementary to each other, 'hybridization' of two technologies would help in minimizing the variability apart from optimally utilizing available infrastructure, including land and transmission systems. 

11.2 Capacity of one resource (solar or wind) in the hybrid projects shall be as per the National Wind-solar Hybrid Policy notified by MNRE vide letter no. F. No. 238/78/2017-Wind dated 14th May 2018 for Wind Solar Hybrid Projects and its amendment from time to time.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: National Wind-Solar Hybrid Policy (MNRE), MNRE Letter No. F. No. 238/78/2017-Wind dated 14 May 2018

## CHUNK 14: HYBRID TYPE-A PROJECTS – CONVERSION FRAMEWORK

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 11 – Wind-Solar Hybrid / Section 11.3.1 and 11.3.2

PROVISION: Section 11.3.1 and 11.3.2

TITLE: Type-A Hybrid Projects – Conversion of Existing Plants and Hybridization Conditions

SUMMARY:
Section 11.3.1 and 11.3.2 define Type-A hybrid projects as conversion of existing or under-construction standalone wind or solar plants into hybrid projects. Wind or solar capacity under construction is measured from GEDA registration certificates or GETCO/STU evacuation permissions issued before policy issuance. Installed capacity is based on PPA or wheeling agreement capacity. Only AC integration is permitted (not DC). Wind and solar component integration occurs at plant end or pooling/sending station per DISCOM/GETCO feasibility determination. Hybridization conditions require: (a) total power injection after hybridization cannot exceed existing transmission capacity or grid connectivity sanctioned by GETCO/STU, with RE developers funding any required augmentation to receiving end substation at their cost; (b) solar and wind power generation measured separately at pooling/sending-end with apportioned injection at receiving end based on common meter readings; and (c) RE developers coordinate with GETCO/STU to determine available transmission capacity and obtain relevant data. These provisions optimize existing infrastructure while ensuring grid stability.

KEY TOPICS: Type-A Hybrid, Existing Plants, Under-Construction Plants, AC Integration, Transmission Capacity, Augmentation Costs, Energy Apportionment, Metering, Grid Stability, GEDA Registration

TEXT:

11.3 For the purpose of simplicity, wind-solar hybrid power generation plants are divided into two categories: 

11.3.1. Type-A Projects: This category includes the conversion of existing or under-construction standalone wind or solar power plants into hybrid projects. Wind or solar capacity under construction shall beconsidered based on the registration certificate issued by GEDA or evacuation permission granted by GETCO / STU to the solar or wind RE developers as the case may be, before the issuance of this policy. The installed wind or solar capacity shall be considered based capacity of power purchase agreement (PPA) or wheeling agreement. 

Only AC integration shall be permitted. The integration of wind and solar components of a wind-solar hybrid project shall be allowed at the plant end or at the pooling I sending station depending upon the feasibility issued by DISCOM / GETCO in accordance with the connectivity regulations of GERC / CERC, Safety regulations issued byCEA and all other applicable regulations / standards / code. Provided further that a separate set of main and back up ABT Compliant metering systems for the purpose of apportioning of energy shall be installed at the generating terminal / turbine output of each WTG with necessary communication facility to the GEDA / GETCO System and the energy accounting shall be undertaken accordingly. Further suitable control equipment shall be deployed for controlling the power output of the hybrid project. 

11.3.2. Hybridization of Type A projects: Existing wind power or solar power RE Developers willing to install solar PV plants or wind turbine generators, respectively, at the existing location to avail benefits underthis policy shall be allowed to do so with the following conditions: 

a) The total power injection (combined wind and solar) into the grid after hybridization shall not be more than the transmission capacity or grid connectivity allowed or sanctioned by GETCO / STU for this purpose. In the event that addition or augmentation of the existing evacuation system is required as per the system study undertaken by GETCO / STU due to the addition of wind or solar capacity, RE developers shall undertake such addition or augmentation in the system up to the receiving end substation of GETCO / STU at their own cost. However, the primary focus of this policy is to optimize the utilization of existing transmission infrastructure, technologies and design approaches towards minimum augmentation are encouraged. 

b)The solar and wind power generated from the hybrid project shall be measured separately at the pooling/sending-end sub-station and energy injection at the receiving end sub-station of GETCO / STU shall be worked out on an apportioned basis as per the common meter reading at the receiving end sub-station up to the receiving end sub-station of GETCO/ STU. 

c) The RE developers shall approach GETCO/ STU to determine the transmission capacity available to evacuate the additional wind or solar power or any augmentation that may be required. GETCO / STU shall provide the relevant data with regards to the transmission capacity utilization on its existing network.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GEDA registration procedures, GETCO/STU evacuation permissions, GERC/CERC connectivity regulations, CEA safety regulations, ABT Compliant metering standards

CROSS-REFERENCES: Hybrid project definitions in Section 5 (Definitions 24-25), Metering provisions in Section 21, Grid connectivity in Section 20, Forecasting and scheduling in Section 23

## CHUNK 15: HYBRID TYPE-B PROJECTS – NEW WIND-SOLAR HYBRID FRAMEWORK

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 11 – Wind-Solar Hybrid / Section 11.3.3

PROVISION: Section 11.3.3

TITLE: Type-B Hybrid Projects – New Wind-Solar Hybrid Projects and Integration Requirements

SUMMARY:
Section 11.3.3 defines Type-B hybrid projects as new wind-solar hybrid power generation projects that are not registered with GEDA and have not received evacuation permission from GETCO/STU as of policy issuance date. In the absence of common RPO and tariff, only AC integration is permitted. Integration of wind and solar components occurs at plant end or pooling/sending station per DISCOM/GETCO feasibility, following all applicable GERC/CERC connectivity regulations, CEA safety regulations, and codes. ABT Compliant metering systems (main and backup) must be installed at each WTG's generating terminal/output with communication facility to GEDA/GETCO System. Energy accounting is undertaken accordingly with suitable control equipment deployed to manage hybrid project power output. DC integration is contingent on future availability of DC metering standards. RE developers must lay a dedicated evacuation line from pooling/sending-end to GETCO/STU receiving-end per system study. Energy injection is apportioned at receiving-end substation based on meter readings of individual wind and solar meters. These detailed technical requirements ensure grid stability and accurate energy accounting in hybrid projects.

KEY TOPICS: Type-B Hybrid, New Hybrid Projects, AC Integration, DC Integration (Contingent), Metering Systems, Energy Apportionment, Dedicated Lines, Grid Stability, GEDA Registration, GETCO Coordination

TEXT:

11.3.3. Type-B Projects: This includes new wind-solar hybrid power generation projects that are not registered with GEDA or for which evacuation permission has not been granted by GETCO/ STU until the date of issuance of this policy. Further, in the absence of a common RPO and tariff, only AC integration will be allowed. The integration of wind and solar components of a wind-solar hybrid project shall be allowed at the plant end or at the pooling / sending station depending upon the feasibility issued by DISCOM / GETCO in accordance with the connectivity regulations of GERC / CERC, Safety regulations issued by CEA and all other applicable regulations / standards / code. Provided further that a separate set of main and back up ABT Compliant metering systems for the purpose of apportioning of energy shall be installed at the generating terminal/turbine output of each WTG with necessary communication facility to the GEDA / GETCO System and the energy accounting shall be undertaken accordingly. Further suitable control equipment shall be deployed for controlling the power output of the hybrid project. 

DC integration shall be contingent on the availability of DC metering standards, which may evolve over time. 

Under all circumstances, the RE developer shall lay a dedicated line for the evacuation of power from the pooling/sending-end sub-station of the hybrid project to the receiving-end sub-station of GETCO I STU as per the system study undertaken by GETCO/ STU. Energy injection from wind and solar capacity at the receiving end of the GETCO / STU sub-station shall be worked out separately on the basis of the meter reading of the common meter installed at the receiving end of the sub-station and appropriately apportioned as per the respective meter readings of the wind and solar meters.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GEDA registration procedures, GETCO/STU evacuation permission procedures, GERC/CERC connectivity regulations, CEA safety regulations, ABT metering standards (current and DC standards as they evolve)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Type-A projects in Section 11.3.1-11.3.2, Grid connectivity requirements in Section 20, Metering standards in Section 21, Forecasting and scheduling in Section 23
