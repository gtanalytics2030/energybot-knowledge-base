## CHUNK 17: RENEWABLE ENERGY PARKS – ESTABLISHMENT AND GOVERNANCE

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 12 – Renewable Energy Parks

PROVISION: Section 12 (Subsections 12.1, 12.2, 12.3)

TITLE: Renewable Energy Parks Framework – Development, Capacity, and Designation

SUMMARY:
Section 12 establishes a framework for Renewable Energy Parks to minimize common infrastructure costs and optimize evacuation infrastructure. RE parks can be solar parks, wind parks, or hybrid parks (solar-wind). Minimum RE park capacity is 50 MW, with maximum capacity determined by MNRE guidelines as issued from time to time. The Government of Gujarat may designate RE Park developers on a nomination basis, which may be Gujarat Power Corporation Limited or other state government agencies. Central Government guidelines for solar park development apply to all park developers. This framework centralizes infrastructure development, enabling economies of scale while maintaining central government standards and state government oversight through nominated developers.

KEY TOPICS: Renewable Energy Parks, Solar Parks, Wind Parks, Hybrid Parks, Common Infrastructure, Evacuation Infrastructure, Minimum Capacity, Developer Designation, MNRE Guidelines, GPCL

TEXT:

12. Renewable Energy Parks 

12.1 In order to minimize the cost of common infrastructure and optimize the evacuation infrastructure along with fulfilling the objectives of this Policy, it is also desirable to promote the development of RE parks, which include solar parks, wind parks, and hybrid parks (i.e., solar-wind). 

12.2 Park Size: The minimum capacity of RE park shall be 50 MW, and the maximum park capacity shall be in accordance with the guidelines or schemes of MNRE as issued from time to time. 

12.3 The Government of Gujarat may designate the Renewable Energy Park developer on a nomination basis, which may be Gujarat Power Corporation Limited or any other state government agency. The Guidelines issued by Central Government from time to time for development of Solar Parks shall be applicable to all Park Developers.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: MNRE Guidelines and Schemes, Central Government Solar Park Guidelines, Government of Gujarat designation authority

CROSS-REFERENCES: Land allocation provisions in Section 28, Grid connectivity and evacuation in Section 20, Operation and maintenance in Section 24

## CHUNK 18: PROJECTS UNDER REC MECHANISM

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 13 – Projects under REC Mechanism

PROVISION: Section 13 (Subsections 13.1, 13.2)

TITLE: Renewable Energy Certificate (REC) Mechanism Framework

SUMMARY:
Section 13 establishes that RE projects may be set up under the REC mechanism for captive use or third-party sale in accordance with CERC regulations as amended from time to time. Wheeling of power under REC mechanism is allowed upon payment of charges per Clause 15 (Grid Charges). Energy settlement for REC projects follows Clause 14 (Energy Settlement and Banking). The REC mechanism provides a market-based alternative to Power Purchase Agreements, allowing generators and consumers to trade renewable energy attributes separately from physical power, enabling flexible procurement and compliance pathways.

KEY TOPICS: REC Mechanism, Renewable Energy Certificates, Captive Use, Third-Party Sale, CERC Regulations, Wheeling, Energy Settlement, Market-Based Framework, Renewable Attributes

TEXT:

13. Projects under REC mechanism 

13.1 RE projects may be setup under the REC mechanism for captive use / third party sale in accordancewith the CERC regulations as amended from time to time. 

13.2 Wheeling of power under REC mechanism shall be allowed on payment of charges as per Clause No. 15 and energy settlement will be as per Clause No: 14 of this policy.

## CHUNK 19: ENERGY SETTLEMENT AND BANKING – GENERAL FRAMEWORK

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 14 – Energy Settlement and Banking

PROVISION: Section 14.1 and 14.2

TITLE: Energy Accounting, Banking, and Settlement Mechanisms

SUMMARY:
Section 14.1 and 14.2 establish the framework for energy accounting and banking for all renewable energy projects, including rooftop projects, in accordance with regulations framed by GERC per the Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022. For consumers availing banking facility, settlement of renewable energy against consumption occurs on a billing cycle basis upon payment of applicable banking charges determined by GERC. No banking charges apply to solar power consumed by residential consumers. For consumers not availing banking facility and consuming from REC-registered projects, energy settlement occurs on 15-minute time block basis with no banking charges applicable. This dual framework provides flexibility: consumers can bank excess generation across billing cycles (paying charges) or settle energy on 15-minute blocks without banking charges for real-time utilization.

KEY TOPICS: Energy Accounting, Banking Facility, Settlement Period, Billing Cycle, Rooftop Projects, REC Mechanism, Banking Charges, Residential Consumers, 15-Minute Settlement, GERC Regulations

TEXT:

14. Energy Settlement and Banking 

14.1 Energy accounting and banking for all renewable energy projects, including rooftop projects, shall be as per the regulations framed by GERC from time to time in accordance with the Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 notified by the Ministry of Power, Govt of India. In case of the consumers availing energy banking facility, the settlement of renewable energy against consumer's consumption shall be carried out on billing cycle basis upon payment of applicable banking charges as determined by GERC from time to time. No banking charges shall be applicable on solar power consumed by Residential CO flS urn e rs. 

14.2 In case the consumer is not availing energy banking facility and consumption is from RE projects registered under REC mechanism, the energy settlement shall be carried out on 15 minute time block basis and no banking charges will be applicable.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GERC Regulations (issued from time to time), Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 (Ministry of Power), Clause 14.3 (Banking charge determination), Clause 14.4 (Energy storage obligations)

## CHUNK 20: ENERGY SETTLEMENT – BANKING CHARGES AND CONDITIONS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 14 – Energy Settlement and Banking

PROVISION: Section 14.3, 14.4, 14.5, 14.6, 14.7

TITLE: Banking Facility Terms, Energy Storage Obligations, Net Import Tariffs, and Peak Hour Charges

SUMMARY:
Section 14.3 establishes that energy banking on billing cycle basis requires payment of applicable banking charges determined by GERC in a cost-reflective manner, accounting for DISCOM cost implications. Banking is allowed to the extent of grid capacity to absorb RE power, with RE projects having no right to claim compensation from utilities. SLDC/Distribution/Transmission companies make efforts to enhance load management capacity through infrastructure upgrades. Banking charges are determined on monthly/quarterly basis per prior month/quarter details. Section 14.4 incorporates MoP guidelines on energy storage obligations for captive and third-party consumers. Section 14.5 specifies that for net import (energy consumption after RE set-off), DISCOMs charge applicable tariff including fixed/demand charges, energy charges, peak hour charges, and other applicable charges. Section 14.6 mandates that power generated from RE projects must be utilized within applicable settlement periods; unutilized energy is treated as inadvertent flow with no DISCOM payment. Section 14.7 applies peak hour charges per GERC tariff schedule to entire peak hour energy consumption as recorded in the consumer meter, including banked energy. These provisions balance consumer interests, utility financial sustainability, and grid operations.

KEY TOPICS: Banking Charges, Cost-Reflective Tariff, Grid Absorption Capacity, Energy Storage Obligations, Net Import, Tariff Components, Inadvertent Flow, Peak Hour Charges, Settlement Period, Grid Management

TEXT:

14.3 Energy banking facility on billing cycle basis shall be allowed upon payment of applicable banking charges, which shall be determined by GERC from time to time in a cost reflective manner taking in to account the cost implications for DISCOMs in providing the banking facility. Banking facility will be allowed to the extent of capacity to absorb RE power in the state's grid and RE Project will not have any right to claim any compensation from utilities. However the SLDC / Distribution companies / Transmission companies will make efforts for enhancing the load management capacity by upgrading! modernization of their infrastructure. The banking charges shall be determined on monthly/quarterly basis as per the details / information of the previous month/quarter. 

14.4 MoP, Gol guidelines related to energy storage obligations as amended from time to time will be applicable to the captive and third party consumers. 

14.5 For net import of power from DISCOMs i.e. energy consumption after providing set off of renewable energy, DISCOM will charge the tariff applicable to respective category of the consumer which shall include fixed or demand charges, energy charges, peak hour charges, other charges or penalties, etc. as applicable to other consumers. 

14.6 Power generated from the RE project shall be utilized within applicable energy settlement period. Any energy that remains unutilized at the end of settlement period shall be treated as inadvertent flow and no payment shall be made by the DISCOM for such energy. 

14.7 Peak hour charges as per the GERC tariff schedule shall be levied on the entire peak hours energy consumption as recorded in the consumer meter i.e. including banked energy.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GERC Tariff Regulations (updated periodically), MoP Energy Storage Guidelines, Electricity Act 2003 Section 86, Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022

CROSS-REFERENCES: Banking definitions in Section 5 (Definition 47 – Settlement Period), Grid charges application in Section 15, Net import provisions in Section 5 (Definition 33)

## CHUNK 21: GRID CHARGES – TRANSMISSION, WHEELING, AND OPEN ACCESS

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 15 – Grid Charges

PROVISION: Section 15.1 and 15.2

TITLE: Transmission and Wheeling Charges Framework

SUMMARY:
Section 15.1 and 15.2 establish the grid charge framework for wheeling renewable power. For utilization of state transmission/distribution network for wheeling power from RE projects (whether located within the state or outside) to consumer end, transmission and wheeling charges and losses determined by GERC are levied as applicable to normal open access consumers, depending on RE plant location and consumption point location. For utilization of state transmission network for power transmission from RE generation plant to consumption end, RE generators or consumers must secure open access sanction/allocation of transmission capacity from State Transmission Utility per extant regulations and procedures. This framework ensures cost-reflective pricing while leveraging existing transmission infrastructure, with capacity allocation mechanisms ensuring grid stability and efficient utilization.

KEY TOPICS: Transmission Charges, Wheeling Charges, Open Access, Transmission Capacity, Transmission Losses, State Transmission Utility, Generator-Consumer Model, Grid Access Procedures

TEXT:

15. Grid Charges 

15.1 For utilization of State transmission / distribution network for wheeling of power from RE projects located within the state or from outside the state to consumer end, transmission and wheeling charges and losses as determined by GERC shall be levied as applicable to normal open access consumer depending on the location of the RE plant and the point of consumption. 

15.2 For utilization of State transmission network for transmission of power from RE generation plant to consumption end, the open access Jsanction I allocation of transmission capacity shall be secured by RE generator or consumer for the quantum of power (MW) to be transferred by making requisite application before State Transmission Utility in accordance with the extant regulations / procedures.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GERC Tariff Regulations (open access charges), State Transmission Utility procedures, Electricity Act 2003 Section 42, CERC Open Access Regulations

CROSS-REFERENCES: Wheeling applicability in Sections 9.1.3, 10.4, 11.4, Transmission capacity provisions in Section 15.3-15.5, Cross-subsidy surcharge in Section 15.8, Additional surcharge in Section 15.8

## CHUNK 22: GRID CHARGES – HYBRID PROJECTS AND TRANSMISSION CAPACITY

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 15 – Grid Charges

PROVISION: Section 15.3 and 15.4

TITLE: Transmission Capacity Requirements for Hybrid Projects

SUMMARY:
Section 15.3 and 15.4 establish transmission capacity requirements specific to hybrid projects. For hybrid projects set up for captive use or third-party sale, RE developers/consumers must seek sanction/allocation of transmission capacity at minimum for the installed capacity of whichever resource (wind or solar) is higher. Transmission losses apply on energy feed-in basis as applicable to standalone wind or solar projects. RE developers may seek higher transmission capacity sanction if required. When solar and wind capacity of hybrid projects is not connected at a single location, the required transmission capacity sanction equals total installed capacity of wind and solar at both locations, with transmission charges levied accordingly. This framework appropriately scales transmission requirements to actual project configurations while optimizing capacity allocation.

KEY TOPICS: Hybrid Projects, Transmission Capacity, Wind and Solar Capacity, Transmission Losses, Multiple Locations, Transmission Charges, Capacity Sanction, Feed-In Basis

TEXT:

15.3 In case of Hybrid projects set up for Captive use or third party sale, the RE Developer/consumer shall be required to seek sanction / allocation of transmission capacity at least for the installed capacity of the wind or solar capacity, whichever is higher. The transmission losses shall be applicable on energy feed-in basis as applicable to any other wind or solar project. However RE developer / consumer may seek higher sanction / allocation of transmission capacity if required. 

15.4 In case, the Solar and Wind capacity of wind-solar hybrid project is not connected at single location, the required sanction I allocation of transmission capacity shall be equivalent to total installed capacity of wind and solar at both the locations and transmission charges shall be levied accordingly.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Transmission capacity allocation procedures (STU/GETCO), GERC connectivity regulations, Hybrid project definitions in Section 5 (Definitions 24-25)

## CHUNK 23: GRID CHARGES – INADVERTENT FLOW, REACTIVE POWER, MULTI-LOCATION WHEELING

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 15 – Grid Charges

PROVISION: Section 15.5, 15.6, 15.7

TITLE: Inadvertent Flow, Reactive Power Charges, and Multi-Location Wheeling Framework

SUMMARY:
Section 15.5 specifies that injection of RE power exceeding sanctioned/allocated transmission capacity in 15-minute time blocks is treated as inadvertent flow with no payment or set-off by GETCO/Distribution Licensees. Section 15.6 establishes that reactive power drawl pricing is determined by GERC in GETCO/STU tariff orders from time to time. Section 15.7 allows wheeling of energy for captive use or third-party sale at multiple locations from a single project upon payment of 5 paisa per unit of energy fed into the grid (as measured at receiving end substation of GETCO/STU) to the concerned DISCOM in whose area power is consumed, in addition to transmission charges and losses. This framework prevents gaming of capacity allocation while establishing mechanisms for distributed consumption scenarios and compensating distribution utilities for managing multiple injection/off-take points.

KEY TOPICS: Inadvertent Flow, Transmission Capacity, 15-Minute Time Block, Reactive Power, Reactive Charges, Multi-Location Wheeling, Paise Per Unit, Compensation to DISCOMs

TEXT:

15.5 Injection of RE power in excess of sanctioned / allocated transmission capacity in 15 minutes time block shall be considered as inadvertent flow of power and no payment / set off will be given by GETCO/ Distribution Licensees. 

15.6 The Pricing for drawl of Reactive Power shall be as decided by GERC in the GETCO / STU Tariff Orders from time to time. 

15.7 Wheeling of energy for Captive use /Third party sale at more than one location from one project shall be allowed upon payment of 5 paise per unit of energy fed in the grid as measured at receiving end sub- station of GETCO/STU to the concerned DISCOM in whose area power is consumed in addition to above mentioned transmission charges and losses, as applicable.

## CHUNK 24: GRID CHARGES – CROSS-SUBSIDY SURCHARGE, ADDITIONAL SURCHARGE, AND RPO ACCOUNTING

DOCUMENT: Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy-2023

DOCUMENT TYPE: Policy

HIERARCHY: Section 15 – Grid Charges

PROVISION: Section 15.8 and 15.9

TITLE: Cross-Subsidy Surcharge, Additional Surcharge, and RPO Attribute Accounting

SUMMARY:
Section 15.8 specifies that cross-subsidy surcharge and additional surcharge shall not apply to consumption from RE projects meeting captive power project criteria per Electricity Rules 2005. RE projects not meeting captive criteria are considered third-party sale projects and cross-subsidy surcharge and additional surcharge apply per GERC determinations. Section 15.9 provides that if RE projects set up for captive use or third-party sale are not utilizing the RE attributes for meeting their own RPO or are not registered under REC mechanism, the RE attributes shall be accounted for in DISCOM's RPO. This framework balances consumer incentives (exempting qualifying captive projects from surcharges) while ensuring renewable energy attributes flow to DISCOMs for RPO compliance when not otherwise utilized, maintaining overall renewable energy market integrity.

KEY TOPICS: Cross-Subsidy Surcharge, Additional Surcharge, Captive Power Projects, Third-Party Sale, Electricity Rules 2005, RPO Attributes, DISCOM RPO Compliance, Renewable Energy Attributes

TEXT:

15.8 Cross-subsidy surcharge and additional surcharge shall not be applicable for consumption from RE projects fulfilling the criteria of captive power projects as per Electricity Rules 2005. The RE projects not fulfilling the criteria of captive power projects shall be considered as third party sale projects and Cross-subsidy surcharge and Additional surcharge shall be levied as determined by GERC from time to time for consumption from third party RE projects. 

15.9 If the RE projects are setup for captive use or third-party sale and if they are not utilizing the RE attribute of the project for meeting their own RPO or are not registered under the REC mechanism, the RE attribute of such projects shall be accounted for in DISCOM's RPO.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Electricity Rules 2005 (captive plant definitions), GERC Tariff Determinations, Electricity Act 2003 Section 86, REC Mechanism (CERC Regulations)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Captive generating plant definition in Section 5 (Definition 6), RPO obligations in Section 19 (Green Power Supply), Energy settlement in Section 14, REC mechanism in Section 13
