**PROVISION:** Section 8.3 - Distributed Solar for Agricultural Consumers: Supply Framework

**TITLE:** Distributed Solar to Power Agricultural Demand: Feeder-Level Electrification Strategy

**SUMMARY:** Section 8.3 establishes three key mechanisms for solar support to agricultural consumers: (1) Section 8.3.1: Distribution Licensees shall leverage the PM KUSUM scheme to identify priorities and notify substation-wise lists for solarization of segregated agricultural feeders through grid-connected distributed solar plants in accordance with MNRE guidelines; (2) Section 8.3.2: Distribution/Transmission Licensees shall undertake required sub-station and network augmentation to integrate RE projects with or without BESS, with authority to incentivize co-located distributed storage (BESS) to support power availability during non-solar hours and reduce peak-hour procurement; (3) Section 8.3.3: The State shall support Agriphotovoltaics (AgriPV) and other innovative dual-use models on suitable land parcels to expand land availability for distributed solar, avoiding land-food-energy conflicts. Together, these provisions create a comprehensive agricultural renewable energy transition program balancing feeder electrification, storage integration, and land-efficient technologies.

**TEXT:**

**Section 8.3.1** The Distribution Licensees shall leverage PM KUSUM scheme and identify priorities and notify a substation-wise list for Solarisation of segregated agricultural feeders through grid-connected distributed solar plants in accordance with guidelines issued by MNRE from time to time.

**HIERARCHY:** Section 8 / Subsection 8.4 (Floating / Canal-based Solar)

**PROVISION:** Section 8.4.1 - Floating and Canal-Based Solar: Resource Potential and Benefits

**TITLE:** Floating and Canal-Based Solar: Resource Assessment and Deployment Framework

**SUMMARY:** Section 8.4.1 establishes the foundation for floating solar deployment by identifying Gujarat's significant water resource potential. The State has several water bodies including reservoirs, lakes, ponds, and canals suitable for floating and canal-based solar projects. These projects offer multiple benefits: (1) avoiding land requirements (critical for agricultural states); (2) lower evaporation from water bodies due to panel shading; (3) higher energy output from PV cells due to lower operating temperatures from water cooling; (4) ease of cleaning; and (5) overall improved system efficiency. This resource-based approach enables rapid capacity scaling while preserving agricultural land—a key sustainability advantage for Gujarat.

**TEXT:**

The State has several water bodies such as reservoirs, lakes, ponds and canals which can be used to set up floating solar and canal based projects. Such projects offer multiple benefits, like avoiding land requirements, lower evaporation from water bodies, producing higher energy due to lower operating temperatures of the PV cells, ease of cleaning, etc.
