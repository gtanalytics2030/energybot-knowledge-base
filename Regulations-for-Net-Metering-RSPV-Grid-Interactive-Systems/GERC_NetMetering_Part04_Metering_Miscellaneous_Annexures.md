## CHUNK 30

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Section 13 (Metering arrangement)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 13 (Metering arrangement)

**TITLE:** Metering System Requirements and Meter Specifications

**SUMMARY:**
Metering systems must comply with Central Electricity Authority (Installation & Operation of Meters) Regulations 2006. A bidirectional meter or two separate meters (one for import, one for export) of same accuracy class as consumer's existing meter shall be installed in place of the existing meter. Meters may be provided by distribution licensee or consumer; if provided by licensee, cost is recovered from consumer. Industrial, commercial, and other consumers utilizing both energy and renewable attributes must use ABT (Availability Based Tariff)-compliant meters. Meters must be Meter Reading Instrument (MRI) or wireless equipment-compliant for recording readings. If bills are prepared based on MRI downloads or remote meter reading, consumer must be allowed to retain a record (Regulation 13.4). Solar meters (for generation tracking) must be 0.2s accuracy class with MRI/wireless capability; check meters are mandatory for systems >20 kW, optional for ≤20 kW installations (Regulation 13.5). For time-of-day tariff consumers, time-recording-compliant meters must be installed. All meters are tested, inspected, and sealed by distribution licensee in consumer's presence. Meter readings by licensee form the basis of commercial settlement.

**KEY TOPICS:** Bidirectional Meter, Import-Export Meters, Meter Accuracy Class, ABT Meter, Solar Meter (0.2s), Check Meter (>20 kW), MRI (Meter Reading Instrument), Wireless Meter Reading, Meter Testing and Sealing, CEA Meter Regulations

**TEXT:**
13.1 The metering system shall be as per the Central Electricity Authority (Installation & Operation of Meters) Regulations, 2006as amended from time to time.

13.2 Bi-directional meter or alternately two separate meters for import and export of the same accuracy class as the consumer's meter existing before the commissioning of the Rooftop Solar PV System shall be installed in replacement of existing meter. Such meters may be provided by the distribution licensee or consumer. If the meter is installed by the distribution licensee in that case, cost of the same shall be recovered from the consumer.

13.3 Industrial, Commercial and Other consumers utilizing both 'energy attribute' as well as 'renewable attribute' of the generated solar energy shall have to use ABT compliant meter.

13.4 These meters shall be Meter Reading instrument (MRI) or wireless equipment compliant for recording meter readings.

Provided, if bills are prepared on the basis of MRI downloads or if meter reading is taken on the basis of remote meter-reading and the consumer wishes to have a record of the reading taken, he shall be allowed so by the licensee.

13.5 The main Solar Meters shall be of 0.2s class accuracy and with facility for recording meter readings using Meter Reading Instrument (MRI) or wireless equipment. Check meters shall be mandatory for Rooftop Solar PV Systems having capacity more than 20 kW. For installations size of less than or equal to 20 kW, the Solar Check meters would be optional.

Provided that the cost of new/additional meter (s) shall be borne by the Eligible Consumer such meter shall be tested and installed by the distribution licensee.

The meters installed shall be inspected, verified for the accuracy and sealed by the distribution licensee in the presence of the representative of the consumer.

Provided that in case the Eligible Consumer is under the ambit of time of day tariff, meters compliant of recording time of day consumption/generation shall be installed.

13.6 The meter reading taken by the distribution licensee shall form the basis of commercial settlement.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Central Electricity Authority (Installation & Operation of Meters) Regulations 2006, Regulation 9 (Energy Settlement - meter readings are input data), Regulation 13.3 (ABT meter requirement for RPO consumers), Annexure-VI (Meter Specifications by consumer category and system capacity)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Bidirectional meter enables net metering by recording both import (grid to consumer) and export (consumer to grid) simultaneously; 0.2s accuracy (±0.2%) is higher accuracy than standard utility meters (typically 1.0s); check meter provides redundancy for large installations and dispute resolution; MRI downloads enable time-series data for power quality assessment and tariff audits; meter cost recovery from consumer aligns with user-pays principle for metering infrastructure; ABT meters required for time-block settlement (Regulation 9.4, 9.5)

## CHUNK 31

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Section 14-16 (Miscellaneous Provisions)

**PROVISION:** Regulation 14 (Sharing of CDM benefits), Regulation 15 (Demand Cut), Regulation 16 (Penalty or Compensation)

**TITLE:** CDM Benefits, Demand Cut Exemptions, and Penalty Framework

**SUMMARY:**
Sharing of CDM (Clean Development Mechanism) benefits: 100% of gross proceeds from CDM credit trading are retained by the rooftop solar PV system owner. Distribution licensee makes no claim on CDM revenue. Demand Cut (peak demand management surcharge): No demand cut is applicable for residential and government consumers. Industrial, commercial, and other consumers are exempted from demand cut up to 50% of installed solar capacity, wherever technically feasible. This provision recognizes that solar generation can partially offset demand charges (which are typically 50-100% of energy charges for industrial consumers). Penalty for Compensation (for net metering system failures): Penalties or compensation follow GERC (Standard of Performance of Distribution Licensee) Regulations 2005, creating a standard framework for failure remediation rather than ad hoc negotiation. Examples of failures include: meter malfunction, prolonged settlement delays, unauthorized disconnection, or system down time affecting consumer's solar export capability.

**KEY TOPICS:** CDM (Clean Development Mechanism), CDM Credits, CDM Revenue, Demand Cut, Peak Demand Surcharge, 50% Exemption, Industrial/Commercial Consumer, Standard of Performance, Penalty Framework

**TEXT:**
14 Sharing of CDM benefits

100% of the gross proceeds on account of CDM benefit to be retained by the Rooftop Solar PV System owner.

15 Demand Cut

No demand cut shall be applicable for the Residential and Government consumers. However, Industrial, Commercial and Other consumers shall be exempted from demand cut up to 50% of the installed solar capacity, wherever technically feasible.

16 Penalty or Compensation

In case of failure of net metering system, penalty or compensation shall be payable as per the provisions of the GERC (Standard of Performance of Distribution Licensee)Regulations, 2005 as amended from time to time.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** GERC Standard of Performance Regulations 2005, United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (CDM procedures), Regulation 9 (Energy Settlement framework), Regulation 7 (System commissioning and capacity approval)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** CDM benefit retention incentivizes solar project economics for investors; demand cut exemption recognizes solar's contribution to peak load reduction and makes solar more attractive for industrial consumers; Standard of Performance framework provides objective criteria for compensation (typically ₹10-500 per day depending on outage duration); applies to distribution licensee failures, not consumer-side equipment failures

## CHUNK 33

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation

**HIERARCHY:** Annexure-III

**PROVISION:** Annexure-III (Connectivity Charges)

**TITLE:** Standardized Connectivity Charges by System Capacity

**SUMMARY:**
Connectivity charges are standardized rates payable by solar rooftop consumer/project owner to the respective distribution licensee. Charge structure is capacity-based: (1) 1-6 kW: ₹1,500 per connection (applicable for most residential and small commercial systems), (2) >6-100 kW: ₹10,000 per connection (applicable for medium commercial and small industrial systems), (3) >100 kW-1 MW: ₹50,000 per connection (applicable for large industrial and captive power plants). Charges cover licensee's cost for: interconnection design assessment, protection device installation, meter integration, testing, and commissioning coordination. Fixed charge structure (not per-kW) incentivizes higher capacities within the 1 MW cap. Charges are payable during Stage 6-7 of application procedure (post-TFR approval, pre-project-start authorization). Non-payment of connectivity charges delays project authorization and eventual commissioning. Connectivity charges are separate from system strengthening costs (borne by consumer if required per Regulation 5) and meter cost (recovered from consumer if licensee installs per Regulation 13.2).

**KEY TOPICS:** Connectivity Charges, System Capacity Tier, ₹1,500 (1-6 kW), ₹10,000 (6-100 kW), ₹50,000 (100 kW-1 MW), Distribution Licensee Fee, Cost Recovery

**TEXT:**
Connectivity Charges applicable for Rooftop Solar PV System shall be as under:

| System Size | Applicable fees per connection |
|-------------|-------------------------------|
| From 1 kW up to 6 kW | INR 1,500 |
| More than 6 kW up to 100 kW | INR 10,000 |
| More than 100 kW up to 1 MW | INR 50,000 |

The Connectivity Charges for Rooftop Solar PV System shall be payable to the respective Distribution Licensee.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Regulation 5 (1 kW minimum, 1 MW maximum), Regulation 6.2 (50% of sanctioned load limit, 1 kW-1 MW absolute limits), Regulation 7 (Stage 6: In-Principle Consent and Connectivity Charges specification)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Charges are fixed per-connection regardless of voltage level or distance to grid connection point; three-tier structure encourages solar penetration at residential scale while maintaining cost recovery for larger industrial systems; charges have remained unchanged since 2016 (no inflation adjustment mechanism in Regulations - updated by GERC amendments/orders); charged once per system for new connections; transferred with property if ownership changes (governed by transfer agreement with licensee)

Clearances and Approvals (Section 3): Consumer obtains necessary statutory environmental and grid connection approvals before connecting. Access and Disconnection (Section 4): Licensee has 24/7 access to metering/disconnection equipment (automatic and manual); licensee may disconnect in emergencies without access to manual switches. Liabilities (Section 5): Consumer indemnifies licensee for negligence/misconduct; licensee not liable for fiscal/government incentives non-realization; licensee may count consumer's solar generation toward RPO compliance.

## CHUNK 36

**DOCUMENT:** GERC (Net Metering Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems) Regulations, 2016

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Regulation/Technical Standard

**HIERARCHY:** Annexure-V

**PROVISION:** Annexure-V (Inverter Standards) – Efficiency, Environmental Testing, Grid Supervision, Harmonics, and Technical Parameters

**TITLE:** Inverter and Power Quality Standards – Comprehensive Technical Requirements

**SUMMARY:**
Annexure-V specifies comprehensive technical standards for inverters and power conditioning units. Inverter Efficiency and Environmental Compliance: Must comply with IEC61683/IS61683 for efficiency measurements and with IEC 60068-2 environmental testing standards (temperature/humidity/vibration per test procedures 1,2,14,30). Grid Condition Supervision: Inverter must continuously monitor grid voltage and frequency; on grid failure, under-voltage, or over-voltage, the system must auto-disconnect via circuit breaker/auto-switch and comply with Regulation 8 requirements.
