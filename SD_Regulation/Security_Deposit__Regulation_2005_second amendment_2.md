DOCUMENT: GERC (Security Deposit) (Second Amendment) Regulations, 2015
DOCUMENT TYPE: GERC Amendment Regulation - Substantive Changes
NOTIFICATION: Notification No. 3 of 2015
AMENDS: GERC (Security Deposit) Regulations, 2005 (Principal Regulations, Notification No. 8 of 2005)
HIERARCHY: Amendment / Sections 2-5
PROVISION: Amendment to Clauses 4.3, 4.6, 4.9, and New Clause 4.15
TITLE: Substantive Amendments - Payment Options, Review Frequency, Deposit Adjustment, and Disclosure

SUMMARY:
The Second Amendment introduces four significant substantive changes to the Principal Regulations: (1) Clause 4.3 now permits consumers with deposits exceeding Rs. 25 Lacs to furnish irrevocable bank guarantees (2-year validity) as alternative security, with consumer responsibility for renewal; (2) Clause 4.6 differentiates review frequency - annual for HT consumers, biennial for LT consumers, based on 12-month consumption averages; (3) Clause 4.9 refines deposit adjustment procedures, streamlining excess refunds through next-bill adjustment and 30-day cheque/DD option; (4) New Clause 4.15 mandates licensees disclose existing deposit amounts on all consumer electricity bills. These amendments enhance payment flexibility, consumer protection, transparency, and reduce administrative burden.

KEY TOPICS:
- Bank guarantee as security deposit form
- Large-deposit consumer options (Rs. 25 Lacs threshold)
- Irrevocable bank guarantee requirements
- Nationalized and scheduled commercial banks
- Bank guarantee renewal responsibility
- HT consumer review frequency (annual)
- LT consumer review frequency (biennial)
- 12-month consumption average basis
- Deposit adjustment procedures
- Excess refund mechanism (next-bill adjustment)
- Cheque/DD refund for balance amount
- 30-day refund timeline
- Deposit disclosure on electricity bills
- Consumer transparency requirements

**SECTION 2: AMENDMENT TO CLAUSE 4.3 - PAYMENT FORM & BANK GUARANTEE OPTION**

**Original Clause 4.3 (Principal Regulations 2005):**

"The amount payable towards security shall be in the form of cash or demand draft (DD) drawn in favour of the licensee."

**Amended Clause 4.3 (Second Amendment 2015):**

"The amount payable towards security shall be in the form of cash or demand draft (DD) drawn in favour of the licensee. The Consumer whose total amount of Security Deposit exceeds Rs. 25 Lacs may, at his option, furnish the security deposit in the form of irrevocable bank guarantee initially valid for a period of two years. Such bank guarantee shall be from a nationalized or scheduled commercial bank. It shall be the responsibility of the consumer to keep the bank guarantee valid at all times and to renew the bank guarantee at least 2 months prior to its expiry."

---

**COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS - CLAUSE 4.3:**

| Aspect | Original (2005) | Amended (2015) |
|--------|---|---|
| **Permitted Forms** | Cash, DD | Cash, DD, Bank Guarantee (if deposit > Rs. 25 L) |
| **Bank Guarantee Option** | Not available | Available |
| **Eligibility for BG** | N/A | Deposit exceeds Rs. 25 Lacs |
| **Consumer Discretion** | N/A | At consumer's option |
| **BG Validity Period** | N/A | 2 years initially |
| **Eligible Banks** | N/A | Nationalized or scheduled commercial banks |
| **Renewal Responsibility** | N/A | Consumer's responsibility |
| **Renewal Timing** | N/A | At least 2 months prior to expiry |
| **Continuous Validity** | N/A | Consumer must maintain validity at all times |

---

**KEY PROVISIONS - CLAUSE 4.3 AMENDMENT:**

**1. Threshold for Bank Guarantee Option:**
- **Condition:** "whose total amount of Security Deposit exceeds Rs. 25 Lacs"
- **Meaning:** Any single consumer whose total deposit obligation > Rs. 25,00,000
- **Application:** 
  - Large HT consumers (multi-MW contracts)
  - Large industrial consumers
  - Government/institutional consumers with substantial loads

**2. Bank Guarantee Specifications:**

**Type:** Irrevocable Bank Guarantee
- "Irrevocable" = Cannot be cancelled or modified without licensee's consent
- Binding on issuing bank
- Valid and enforceable if conditions met

**Validity:** "initially valid for a period of two years"
- First issuance: Valid for 24 months from date of issue
- After expiry: Must be renewed before expiry date
- Gap-free requirement: Continuous coverage required

**Issuing Bank:** "nationalized or scheduled commercial bank"
- **Nationalized Banks:** SBI and its associates, other banks nationalized post-1969
- **Scheduled Commercial Banks:** Banks listed in RBI's Schedule of the Banking Regulation Act 1949
- **Not Eligible:** Cooperative banks, small finance banks, payment banks, private non-scheduled banks

**Examples of Eligible Banks:**
- State Bank of India
- Bank of Baroda
- Bank of India
- Canara Bank
- Union Bank of India
- ICICI Bank
- HDFC Bank
- Axis Bank
- IndusInd Bank
- Kotak Mahindra Bank

**3. Consumer Renewal Obligations:**

**Timing Requirement:** "renew the bank guarantee at least 2 months prior to its expiry"
- Calculate expiry date of existing BG
- Subtract 2 months
- Submit renewal request to bank
- Bank processes and issues new BG
- Allow buffer for processing delays

**Continuous Validity Requirement:** "keep the bank guarantee valid at all times"
- No gap between expiry of old and issuance of new
- Licensee may disconnect for lapsed guarantee
- Consumer's sole responsibility (licensee not obligated to remind)

**Non-Compliance Consequences:**
- BG expires without renewal: Deposit non-payment situation arises
- Licensee may treat as non-provision of security
- Supply may be disconnected per Regulation 3.2
- Or converted to cash/DD requirement

**SECTION 3: AMENDMENT TO CLAUSE 4.6 - REVIEW FREQUENCY**

**Original Clause 4.6 (Principal Regulations 2005):**

"Subject to the restrictions of the periods of three months or two months or one and a half months as specified in Clauses 4.1 and 4.2, the adequacy of the amount of Security Deposit calculation in respect of consumers may be reviewed by the licensee usually once in every year (preferably after revision of tariff for the respective year) based on the average unit consumption for the period representing 12 (twelve) months from April to March of the previous year."

**Amended Clause 4.6 (Second Amendment 2015):**

"Subject to the restrictions of the periods of three months or two months or one and a half months as specified in Clauses 4.1 and 4.2, the adequacy of the amount of Security Deposit in respect of consumers may be reviewed by the licensee every year for HT Consumers and once in every two years for LT Consumers based on the average units consumption for the past 12 (twelve) months."

---

**COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS - CLAUSE 4.6:**

| Aspect | Original (2005) | Amended (2015) |
|---|---|---|
| **HT Review Frequency** | "Usually once every year" | "Every year" (mandatory) |
| **LT Review Frequency** | "Usually once every year" | "Once in every two years" (extended) |
| **Timing Flexibility** | "Usually" (discretionary) | Fixed (for HT yearly, LT biennial) |
| **Tariff Revision Timing** | "Preferably after tariff revision" | Removed |
| **Measurement Period** | "12 months from April to March" | "Past 12 months" (more flexible) |
| **Basis for Review** | "Average unit consumption" | "Average units consumption" |

---

**KEY CHANGES - CLAUSE 4.6 AMENDMENT:**

**Change 1: HT Consumer Review - Mandatory Annual**

**Original:** "usually once in every year" (discretionary language)
**Amended:** "every year for HT Consumers" (mandatory language)

**Effect:**
- HT consumer reviews are now mandatory (not optional)
- Frequency: Once per calendar/financial year (minimum)
- Licensee cannot skip HT reviews
- Typically aligned with tariff revision (but not mandatory per this clause)

**Rationale:**
- HT consumers are large, with significant consumption variations
- Annual monitoring better reflects changing business conditions
- Protects licensee's interest with frequent verification

**Implementation:**
- Mark calendar date for each HT consumer's review
- Conduct review without consumer prompting
- Document review and any deposit adjustments
- Communicate changes to consumer per Regulation 4.9 procedures

---

**Change 2: LT Consumer Review - Biennial (Every 2 Years)**

**Original:** "usually once in every year" (annual)
**Amended:** "once in every two years for LT Consumers" (biennial)

**Effect:**
- LT consumer reviews are now biennial (every 2 years)
- Frequency: Once per 24-month period (reduced from annual)
- Licensee must conduct minimum biennial review (may conduct annually if desired)
- Reduces administrative burden on both licensee and consumer

**Rationale:**
- LT consumers are typically residential/small commercial
- Consumption patterns relatively stable
- Excessive annual reviews create administrative burden
- Biennial review still provides adequate monitoring

**Implementation:**
- Maintain 24-month review cycle for each LT consumer
- Stagger reviews to distribute administrative load
- Conduct review without consumer prompting
- Document review and communicate any changes

**Example Timeline for LT Consumer:**

```
Year 1: Review conducted (April-May)
Year 2: No review required (standard cycle)
Year 3: Next review due (April-May) - 24 months later
Year 4: No review required
Year 5: Next review due (April-May) - 24 months later
```

---

**Change 3: Removal of "Preferably after tariff revision" Reference**

**Original:** "preferably after revision of tariff for the respective year"
**Amended:** [Removed from amended text]

**Effect:**
- Removes preference for post-tariff-revision timing
- Allows licensees flexibility in review scheduling
- Reviews can occur at any time during the review year/period
- Decouples deposit reviews from tariff revision cycle

**Rationale:**
- Tariff revisions may not align with deposit review requirements
- Licensees need operational flexibility
- Consumption changes are continuous, not tied to tariff dates
- Simplified administration

---

**Change 4: Clarification of Measurement Period - "Past 12 months"**

**Original:** "for the period representing 12 (twelve) months from April to March of the previous year"
**Amended:** "for the past 12 (twelve) months"

**Effect:**
- More flexible measurement period
- Not rigid April-March cycle
- "Past 12 months" = preceding 12 months from review date
- Allows rolling 12-month window

**Example Flexibility:**
- Review on 15 June 2016: Use 15 June 2015 - 15 June 2016 consumption
- Review on 20 September 2016: Use 20 September 2015 - 20 September 2016 consumption
- Not limited to fiscal year (April-March) data

---

**REVIEW FREQUENCY SUMMARY TABLE:**

| Consumer Type | Original | Amended | Mandatory? | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HT (High Tension)** | ~Annual (discretionary) | Annual | Yes | 12-month consumption |
| **LT (Low Tension)** | ~Annual (discretionary) | Biennial | Yes (min. every 2 years) | 12-month consumption |

**SECTION 4: AMENDMENT TO CLAUSE 4.9 - DEPOSIT ADJUSTMENT & REFUND PROCEDURE**

**Original Clause 4.9 (Principal Regulations 2005):**

"Based on such review, the licensee may either seek increase in amount of Security Deposit or reduce its amount as the case may be. Provided that if the Security Deposit payable by the consumer is short by not more than 10% of the existing Security Deposit, no demand will be made for payment of further Security Deposit. If the existing Security Deposit of a consumer is found to be in excess by more than 10% of the required Security Deposit, refund of the excess Security Deposit shall be made by adjustment in future bills. Where the consumer is required to pay additional amount by way of Security Deposit, a notice for payment within 30 days shall be issued."

**Amended Clause 4.9 (Second Amendment 2015):**

"Based on such review, the licensee may either seek increase in the amount of Security Deposit or reduce its amount as the case may be. Provided that if the Security Deposit payable by the consumer is short by not more than 10% of the existing Security Deposit, no demand will be made for payment of further Security Deposit. If the existing Security Deposit of a consumer is found to be in excess by more than 10% of the required Security Deposit, refund of the excess Security Deposit shall be made by adjustment in next electricity bill. In case the full amount of refund cannot be adjusted in the next bill, balance amount shall be refunded by way of single cheque/DD within a period of 30 days from the date of adjustment in electricity bill. Where the consumer is required to pay additional amount by way of Security Deposit, he shall pay the same within 30 days from the date notice issued by the Licensee."

---

**COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS - CLAUSE 4.9:**

| Aspect | Original (2005) | Amended (2015) |
|---|---|---|
| **Shortfall ≤ 10%** | No demand for additional | No demand for additional [UNCHANGED] |
| **Excess > 10%** | "Adjustment in future bills" | "Adjustment in next electricity bill" (specific) |
| **Partial Refund Handling** | Not addressed | "If full amount not adjusted in next bill, balance refunded" |
| **Partial Refund Form** | N/A | "Single cheque/DD" |
| **Partial Refund Timeline** | N/A | "30 days from date of adjustment in bill" |
| **Additional Payment Deadline** | "Within 30 days from date of notice" | "Within 30 days from date notice issued" [CLARIFIED] |

---

**KEY CHANGES - CLAUSE 4.9 AMENDMENT:**

**Change 1: Specific Reference to "Next" Electricity Bill**

**Original:** "adjustment in future bills" (vague - could be any future bill)
**Amended:** "adjustment in next electricity bill" (specific - immediately next bill)

**Effect:**
- Clarity: Refund must be adjusted in the very next bill after review decision
- Timeline: Shortens refund period significantly
- Consumer benefit: Faster recovery of excess deposits
- Licensee obligation: Cannot delay refund to subsequent bills

**Implementation:**
- Review completion date: Identify the next billing date
- Adjust full excess in that next bill (if possible)
- If partial adjustment possible, proceed to cheque/DD option

---

**Change 2: Addition of Partial Refund Mechanism**

**Original:** Doesn't address situation where full excess cannot be adjusted in one bill
**Amended:** "In case the full amount of refund cannot be adjusted in the next bill, balance amount shall be refunded by way of single cheque/DD within a period of 30 days from the date of adjustment in electricity bill."

**Scenarios Where Partial Adjustment Applies:**

**Scenario 1: Excess exceeds one month's bill**
- Consumer deposit excess: Rs. 10,000
- Next month's bill: Rs. 2,500
- First bill adjustment: Rs. 2,500
- Balance for cheque/DD: Rs. 7,500

**Scenario 2: Negative bill balance**
- Current month bill (due): Rs. 3,000
- Excess refund: Rs. 5,000
- Adjustment: Offset bill (bill becomes zero), credit deposit
- Balance for cheque/DD: Rs. 2,000 (or may adjust over multiple bills)

---

**New Procedure for Partial Refund:**

**Step 1: Determine Excess Amount**
- Required deposit (after review): Rs. 5,000
- Existing deposit: Rs. 15,000
- Excess: Rs. 10,000

**Step 2: Attempt Full Adjustment in Next Bill**
- Next bill amount: Rs. 3,000
- Excess available: Rs. 10,000
- Can fully adjust? Yes
- Next bill: Rs. 0 (paid by excess credit)
- Remaining excess: Rs. 7,000 carried to step 3

**Alternative - If full adjustment not possible in next bill:**
- Next bill amount: Rs. 15,000 (very high usage)
- Excess available: Rs. 10,000
- Can fully adjust? No (bill exceeds excess)
- Adjust by: Rs. 10,000 (full excess)
- Next bill becomes: Rs. 5,000 (due from customer)
- Remaining excess: Rs. 0 (fully adjusted)

**Step 3: Issue Cheque/DD for Balance**

**Conditions:**
- Only if balance remains after next-bill adjustment
- Form: Single cheque or DD (consumer choice or licensee default)
- Payee: Consumer name
- Timeline: Within 30 days from date of adjustment in electricity bill
- Timing calculation: 30 days from the date the adjustment appears on the bill

**30-Day Timeline Example:**
- Bill date (adjustment shown): 15 August 2015
- Deadline for cheque/DD: 14 September 2015 (30 days later)
- Licensee must issue cheque/DD by this date

---

**Change 3: Clarification of Additional Payment Deadline**

**Original:** "a notice for payment within 30 days shall be issued"
**Amended:** "he shall pay the same within 30 days from the date notice issued by the Licensee"

**Effect:**
- Clarifies payment obligation (consumer must pay)
- Clarifies timeline start point (from date of notice)
- Reinforces notice requirement
- Aligns with Regulation 4.13 payment procedures

**Implementation:**
- Licensee issues written notice on specific date
- Consumer payment deadline: 30 days from that notice date
- Notice should clearly state deadline date
- Non-payment: Subject to surcharge per Regulation 4.13

---

**COMBINED CLAUSE 4.9 REFUND FLOWCHART:**

```

    ↓
Adjust full excess in next electricity bill
(if possible)

    ├─→ Full excess adjusted? 
    │   ├─ YES → End process
    │   │         (consumer receives full refund via bill adjustment)
    │   │
```

**SECTION 5: NEW CLAUSE 4.15 - DEPOSIT DISCLOSURE ON ELECTRICITY BILLS**

**New Clause 4.15 (Added by Second Amendment 2015):**

"Distribution Licensee shall specify the amount of existing Security Deposit on Electricity Bill of Consumers of all categories."

**Status:** Entirely New Provision (not in Principal Regulations 2005)

---

**KEY PROVISIONS - CLAUSE 4.15:**

**Mandatory Requirement:**
- **"shall specify"** (mandatory, not optional)
- Applies to: Distribution Licensee
- Scope: All consumers of all categories
- Timing: On every electricity bill

**What Must Be Disclosed:**
- **"amount of existing Security Deposit"**
- Current balance of security deposit held by licensee
- Must reflect latest deposit status

**Where to Display:**
- **"on Electricity Bill"**
- Specific location not prescribed (licensee discretion)
- Likely locations:
  - Summary section of bill
  - Separate line item in charges table
  - Consumer account summary
  - Bill footer or reference section

**Applicability:**
- **"Consumers of all categories"**
- Applies uniformly to: LT, HT, EHT, residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural, temporary, etc.
- No category exemption

---

**CONSUMER BENEFIT - TRANSPARENCY & TRACKING:**

**Information Consumer Receives:**
- Current deposit amount on file with licensee
- Updated after each deposit review
- Updated after each deposit adjustment
- Reference for verification and disputes
- Helps consumer track deposit status over time

**Example Bill Disclosure:**

```
CONSUMER ACCOUNT SUMMARY
─────────────────────────
Consumer ID: 123456789
Connected Load: 5 kW
Billing Period: 01-Aug-2015 to 31-Aug-2015

CHARGES:
Energy Charges: Rs. 1,200
Fixed Charges: Rs. 150
Electricity Duty: Rs. 80
Total Due: Rs. 1,430

─────────────────────────
SECURITY DEPOSIT INFORMATION:
Existing Security Deposit: Rs. 5,000
─────────────────────────
```

---

**LICENSEE OBLIGATION & IMPLEMENTATION:**

**When to Update Bill Disclosure:**

1. **After Review (Regulation 4.6):**
   - Review completed: Deposit amount may change
   - Next bill: New amount must appear

2. **After Adjustment (Regulation 4.9):**
   - Excess refunded: Deposit amount decreases
   - Next bill: Decreased amount must appear

3. **After Additional Payment (Regulation 4.13):**
   - Consumer pays additional deposit: Amount increases
   - Next bill: Increased amount must appear

4. **Normal Monthly Billing:**
   - No review/adjustment: Amount remains same
   - Disclosure shows unchanged amount

**System Integration Requirements:**

- Update billing system to include deposit field
- Link deposit tracking to consumer account
- Automate disclosure on bill generation
- Ensure accuracy and currency of data

---

**DISPUTE RESOLUTION & VERIFICATION:**

**Consumer Can Verify:**
- Bill shows deposit amount
- Consumer can cross-check against:
  - Deposit receipt (if paid in cash)
  - DD confirmation (if paid by DD)
  - Bank guarantee reference (if under bank guarantee)
  - Previous bills (for trend verification)

**Consumer Challenges Deposit Amount:**
- References bill disclosure
- Requests review if discrepancy suspected
- Licensee must justify deposit amount
- Can file grievance with CGRF if unresolved
