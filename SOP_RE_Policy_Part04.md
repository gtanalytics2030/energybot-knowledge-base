HIERARCHY: Section 7 / Wind-Solar Hybrid Projects

PROVISION: Section 7.1

TITLE: Wind-Solar Hybrid Power Projects

SUMMARY:
Section 7.1 covers the procedural framework for development of wind-solar hybrid renewable energy projects combining both wind and solar generation on the same or adjacent sites. Hybrid projects benefit from optimized land use and complementary generation patterns. The section specifies registration, development permission, transfer permission, commissioning, and operational requirements with specific considerations for managing multiple technologies.

KEY TOPICS: Hybrid Projects, Wind-Solar Hybrid, Combined Generation, Land Optimization, Complementary Generation, Hybrid Registration, Hybrid Commissioning

TEXT:
Section 7.1 - Wind-Solar Hybrid Power Projects

Wind-solar hybrid projects combine wind turbine generators and solar photovoltaic systems on the same or adjacent sites to leverage complementary generation characteristics and optimize land utilization. Hybrid projects enjoy cost benefits and improved capacity utilization factors.

A. REGISTRATION AND GRID CONNECTIVITY (7.1.1)

Registration Process:
(1) Provisional registration covering both wind and solar components
(2) Single or separate applications allowed (at developer's choice)
(3) Land area reduced due to dual technology (approximately 1.5-2 hectares per MW)
(4) Single grid connection point with combined metering

B. ADDITION OF WIND OR SOLAR COMPONENT (7.1.2)

Expansion Options:
(1) Existing wind project can add solar component:
    - Fresh development permission for solar component
    - Shared grid infrastructure
    - Additional processing fees for solar component
    - Combined metering at interconnection point
(2) Existing solar project can add wind component:
    - Fresh development permission for wind component
    - May require expanded grid connectivity
    - Combined metering arrangement
(3) Aggregation of existing projects:
    - Operational wind and solar projects can be aggregated
    - Both projects must have valid registrations
    - Single SCADA system for combined monitoring

C. COORDINATES VERIFICATION (7.1.3)

Requirements:
(1) Separate verification for wind and solar components if on different sites
(2) Single verification for integrated site
(3) Minimum distance between wind and solar equipment:
    - WTG to solar field boundary: 100 meters
    - To prevent shadowing and wind turbulence effects

D. DEVELOPMENT PERMISSION (7.1.4)

Application Requirements:
(1) Separate DPRs for each component or integrated DPR
(2) Combined site layout with both technologies marked
(3) Micro-siting report for wind component
(4) Solar layout plan and equipment specifications
(5) Combined energy forecast and generation profile

Processing Fees (7.1.4.2):
(1) Hybrid project fees (exclusive of GST):
    - Less than 1 MW: Rs. 12,000/-
    - 1 MW to 10 MW: Rs. 18,000/- per MW
    - Above 10 MW: Rs. 25,000/- per MW
(2) Represents single fee for combined project

Development Permission Validity (7.1.4.3):
(1) Single development permission for entire project
(2) Valid for 24 months
(3) Extensible for 12 months
(4) Revision allowed for technology or capacity changes within 20 days

E. TRANSFER PERMISSION (7.1.5)

Transfer Process:
(1) Single transfer application for entire hybrid project
(2) Both wind and solar components must be transferred together
(3) Cannot split hybrid project into separate technologies
(4) Transfer fees: 50% of development permission fees

F. COMMISSIONING (7.1.6)

Phased Commissioning Allowed:
(1) Wind and solar components can be commissioned separately
(2) Each component requires CEI inspection and approval
(3) Grid operator synchronization for each component
(4) But final registration issued only when entire project commissioned
(5) Within 6 months of first component commissioning, second component must be commissioned

G. ENERGY ACCOUNTING (7.1.7)

Combined Accounting:
(1) Separate energy metering for wind and solar components
(2) Combined export/import metering at grid interconnection
(3) Energy accounting and settlement:
    - Separate deviation settlement for each technology
    - Separate forecasting and scheduling requirements
    - Combined monthly statement
(4) Compliance with forecasting regulations for both technologies

H. NAME CHANGE AND OWNERSHIP CHANGE (7.1.8)

Post-Commissioning Changes:
(1) Name change of project allowed
(2) Ownership change allowed with:
    - Transfer permission (if before commissioning)
    - Fresh ownership certificate (if after commissioning)
(3) Technology change not allowed post-commissioning
(4) Capacity increase allowed (separate approval needed)

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GIREP-2025, Section 7, Hybrid Projects, Wind-Solar Hybrid, Combined Generation, Registration, Transfer

CROSS-REFERENCES: Wind Projects (Section 5), Solar Projects (Section 6), Development Permission, Commissioning, Transfer Permission

HIERARCHY: Section 8 / Standalone BESS Projects

PROVISION: Section 8.1-8.8

TITLE: Standalone Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) Projects

SUMMARY:
Section 8 covers the complete framework for development and operation of standalone Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) projects. BESS projects store electrical energy in batteries for later dispatch to the grid, supporting grid balancing and renewable energy integration. The section specifies registration, grid connectivity, BESS components, technical specifications, safety requirements, and commissioning procedures specific to energy storage systems.

KEY TOPICS: BESS, Battery Storage, Energy Storage, Grid Support, Grid Balancing, Battery Management System, PCS, Energy Accounting, Storage Safety

TEXT:
Section 8 - Standalone BESS Projects

Standalone BESS projects are dedicated energy storage systems that charge from the grid (or connected RE generators) and discharge to support grid operations, renewable integration, and demand management. These projects do not directly generate electricity but store and deliver electricity.

A. REGISTRATION AND GRID CONNECTIVITY (8.1, 8.1.1)

Registration Requirements:
(1) Online provisional registration on RE Portal
(2) Documentation:
    - Building/site ownership proof
    - BESS technology specifications
    - Battery capacity (in MWh)
    - Power rating (MW for charge/discharge)
    - Battery chemistry and manufacturer
(3) Grid connectivity NOC from DISCOM/GETCO
(4) Provisional registration fee: Rs. 5,000/- plus GST

Grid Connectivity:
(1) BESS can charge from:
    - Grid (parasitic charging)
    - Co-located RE generator
    - Multiple RE generators (aggregated)
(2) BESS must discharge to grid or connected consumer
(3) Grid code compliance mandatory
(4) Real-time monitoring through SCADA required

B. FIRST-TIME CHARGING OF BESS (8.2)

Initial Charging Procedure:
(1) Before commercial operation, BESS must be charged and tested
(2) Procedure:
    - Battery isolation from grid
    - Cell-by-cell testing
    - BMS (Battery Management System) calibration
    - Temperature and voltage monitoring during initial charge cycles
    - No load discharge test cycles
(3) Minimum 5 full charge-discharge cycles before grid connection
(4) Testing report submitted to grid operator
(5) Safety clearance issued before synchronization

C. BESS COMPONENTS (8.3)

Major Components:

(1) Battery Cells and Modules:
    - Unit cells, battery modules, battery racks, battery containers
    - Lithium-ion, Lead-acid, or other approved chemistry
    - Module voltage and capacity rating
    - Container environmental control (temperature, humidity)

(2) Battery Management System (BMS):
    - Cell-level monitoring (voltage, current, temperature)
    - Pack-level control and balancing
    - State of charge (SOC) and state of health (SOH) estimation
    - Alarm and protection functions
    - Communication interface with PCS and SCADA

(3) Power Conversion System (PCS):
    - Bidirectional inverter (AC to DC and DC to AC)
    - Power rating: MW for continuous operation
    - Efficiency >95% (round-trip)
    - Operating voltage and frequency range
    - Protection and safety features

D. TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS (8.4)

Performance Requirements:
(1) Energy Capacity:
    - Measured at 25°C ambient temperature
    - Defined at C/10 discharge rate (for batteries with rate-dependent capacity)
    - Minimum 80% capacity retention over 10-year period

(2) Power Rating:
    - Maximum continuous charge/discharge power in MW
    - Peak power capability (for 15-30 minutes)
    - Ramping capability (MW per minute)

(3) Round-Trip Efficiency:
    - Minimum 85% for lithium-ion systems
    - Minimum 80% for other chemistries
    - Measured under standard conditions

(4) Response Time:
    - Frequency response: <200 milliseconds
    - Power ramp rate: Programmable (minimum 5% per minute)
    - State of charge range: 10-90% (usable capacity)

E. SAFETY REQUIREMENTS (8.5, 8.6)

Safety Systems:
(1) Fire Suppression:
    - Automatic fire detection system
    - Fire suppression (CO2, foam, or specialty agents)
    - Thermal management system
(2) Ventilation:
    - Adequate air flow for cooling
    - Exhaust for gaseous emissions (if applicable)
(3) Containment:
    - Spill containment for electrolyte
    - Insulating barriers between cell groups
(4) Electrical Safety:
    - Isolation switches
    - Ground fault protection
    - Over-current and over-voltage protection
    - Qualified personnel trained in BESS operation

(5) Emergency Procedures:
    - Emergency shutdown system
    - Evacuation procedures
    - First aid for chemical exposure
    - Regular safety drills (quarterly minimum)

F. COMMISSIONING (8.7)

Pre-Commissioning:
(1) All components installed and tested
(2) BMS and PCS integration tested
(3) Safety systems operational
(4) Grid code compliance testing
(5) Insurance and indemnity in place

Commissioning:
(1) Grid operator supervised synchronization
(2) Initial charging test
(3) Frequency response testing
(4) Ramp rate testing
(5) Protection device testing

G. METERING AND MONITORING (8.8)

Metering Requirements:
(1) AC side energy meter:
    - Separate import and export metering
    - Revenue meter for billing
(2) DC side monitoring:
    - Battery voltage and current
    - Temperature monitoring
    - SOC and SOH estimation
(3) Real-time data logging:
    - 5-minute interval minimum
    - Historical data retention (minimum 1 year)
(4) SCADA integration:
    - Real-time communication with grid operator
    - Remote monitoring and control capability

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GIREP-2025, Section 8, BESS, Battery Storage, Energy Storage, Standalone Storage, Battery Management, Power Conversion, Energy Accounting

CROSS-REFERENCES: Grid Connectivity (Section 4.5), Technical Standards (Section 4.7), Grid Code Compliance, Energy Accounting

HIERARCHY: Section 9 / RE Parks

PROVISION: Section 9.1

TITLE: RE Parks Development and Management

SUMMARY:
Section 9 covers the development and management of Renewable Energy Parks - dedicated zones where multiple RE generators operate with shared grid infrastructure and centralized management. RE Parks promote economies of scale, infrastructure sharing, and simplified administration. The section specifies park development models, developer responsibilities, and procedures for park establishment.

KEY TOPICS: RE Parks, Renewable Energy Parks, Shared Infrastructure, Aggregated Projects, Park Development, Developer Responsibilities, Infrastructure Sharing

TEXT:
Section 9 - RE Parks

RE Parks are designated areas where multiple renewable energy projects (wind, solar, hybrid) are developed with shared infrastructure and centralized administration to promote efficient use of land and grid resources.

A. RE PARK DEVELOPMENT MODELS (9.1)

Development Options:

(1) Developer-Owned Parks:
    - Single developer owns and operates entire park
    - Park developer manages all infrastructure
    - Sub-allocation to other generators allowed (with park developer's permission)
    - All generators follow common grid connectivity rules

(2) Government-Promoted Parks:
    - State or Central Government establishes RE Park infrastructure
    - Government allocates plots/sites to private developers
    - Developers own their projects; park infrastructure maintained by Government
    - Government recovers costs through user fees/land lease

(3) Cooperative/Community Parks:
    - Multiple generators jointly develop park
    - Shared infrastructure cost and management
    - Common decision-making body
    - Equal voting rights for all members

(4) Aggregated Parks:
    - Existing standalone projects registered as park
    - Retrofitted with shared infrastructure
    - Gradual expansion possible
    - More than 5 MW minimum aggregate capacity required

B. RESPONSIBILITIES OF RE PARK DEVELOPER (9.1.1)

Park Establishment:

(1) Infrastructure Development:
    - Shared substation and grid interconnection
    - Internal transmission network (if >10 MW)
    - Common SCADA system
    - Central energy accounting
    - Common control center (for large parks)

(2) Land Management:
    - Acquisition of park land
    - Lease/allocation to individual generators
    - Environmental management (greening, water management)
    - Waste management and recycling

(3) Compliance and Administration:
    - Ensure all generators comply with regulations
    - Unified forecasting and scheduling
    - Central deviation settlement management
    - Safety and security management
    - Land and title documentation

(4) Operations:
    - Maintain shared infrastructure
    - Manage grid interconnection
    - Conduct regular audits and inspections
    - Coordinate with grid operator
    - Handle emergency situations

Benefits of RE Parks:
- Reduced land requirement per MW (15-30% savings)
- Lower grid connectivity costs (shared infrastructure)
- Improved power quality and stability
- Centralized monitoring and maintenance
- Better environmental management
- Economies of scale in equipment procurement

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GIREP-2025, Section 9, RE Parks, Park Development, Infrastructure Sharing, Aggregated Projects, Park Management

CROSS-REFERENCES: Wind Projects (Section 5), Solar Projects (Section 6), Grid Connectivity (Section 4.5), Technical Standards (Section 4.7)

HIERARCHY: Section 10 / Off-Grid RE Projects

PROVISION: Section 10

TITLE: Off-Grid Renewable Energy Projects (Solar/Wind/Hybrid with or without Storage)

SUMMARY:
Section 10 covers the development framework for off-grid renewable energy projects not connected to any electrical grid. Off-grid projects serve remote areas, villages, and isolated consumers where grid connectivity is not feasible or economic. The section specifies registration, development requirements, and operational procedures for standalone off-grid systems combining generation and storage.

KEY TOPICS: Off-grid RE, Off-grid Solar, Off-grid Wind, Off-grid Hybrid, Standalone RE Systems, Rural Electrification, Energy Storage, Microgrid, Remote Areas

TEXT:
Section 10 - Off-Grid Renewable Energy Projects

Off-grid RE projects are renewable energy systems that operate independently without connection to any state or central grid. These projects serve consumers in remote or isolated areas where grid connectivity is not available or economical.

A. SCOPE AND APPLICABILITY

Covered Projects:
(1) Off-grid solar systems (rooftop or ground-mounted)
(2) Off-grid wind projects
(3) Off-grid wind-solar hybrid projects
(4) All with or without battery storage systems (BESS)

Typical End Uses:
(1) Rural electrification and household supply
(2) Agricultural water pumping
(3) Telecommunications tower power
(4) Remote industrial applications
(5) Village microgrids
(6) Captive power plants for industrial consumption

B. REGISTRATION REQUIREMENTS

Online Registration:
(1) RE Portal registration with off-grid project classification
(2) Project description:
    - Location and beneficiary details
    - Technology type (solar/wind/hybrid)
    - Capacity (kW or MW)
    - End use and beneficiary base
    - Storage capacity (if applicable)

(3) Documentation:
    - Land ownership/lease proof
    - Beneficiary list (for community projects)
    - Single-line diagram of off-grid system
    - Load calculation and design specifications
    - BESS specifications (if applicable)
    - Equipment list and certifications

(4) NOC from DISCOM/GETCO confirming:
    - Project is not connected to any grid
    - Project will not be connected in future

C. DEVELOPMENT PERMISSION

Application Process:
(1) Detailed Project Report (DPR) including:
    - Site assessment and resource availability
    - Technology selection and sizing
    - Component specifications
    - Installation methodology
    - O&M plan
    - Financial analysis
    - Risk assessment

(2) Processing timeline: 30 days for complete application

(3) Development permission validity: 24 months

D. INSTALLATION AND COMMISSIONING

Installation Requirements:
(1) Equipment from approved manufacturers
(2) Installation by qualified technicians
(3) Compliance with safety standards (CEI/CEA)
(4) Proper grounding and protection systems
(5) Storage system safety (if BESS included)

Commissioning Checklist:
(1) All equipment installed per design
(2) Safety systems operational
(3) Initial generation test and BESS charging cycles
(4) Load test with actual consumers
(5) O&M training for operators
(6) Documentation and manuals provided

E. OPERATIONAL REQUIREMENTS

Ongoing Compliance:
(1) Regular maintenance (quarterly minimum)
(2) Performance monitoring and reporting
(3) Immediate notification to GEDA of any major issues
(4) Annual inspection and certification
(5) Insurance cover (recommended)

Non-Connection Undertaking:
(1) Developer must sign binding undertaking that project will NOT be connected to any grid
(2) Undertaking valid throughout project life
(3) Violation may result in penalties and de-registration

F. FINANCIAL AND GOVERNMENT SUPPORT

Subsidy Availability:
(1) MNRE (Ministry of New and Renewable Energy) schemes
(2) State government renewable energy subsidy programs
(3) Rural electrification programs
(4) SAUBHAGYA scheme funding
(5) Other government and NGO support

Support Documentation:
(1) Subsidy application and approval
(2) Subsidy disbursement schedule
(3) Audit and compliance verification
(4) Final completion and payment release

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: GIREP-2025, Section 10, Off-Grid Projects, Off-Grid Solar, Off-Grid Wind, Rural Electrification, Standalone Systems, Microgrids, Energy Storage

CROSS-REFERENCES: Wind Projects (Section 5), Solar Projects (Section 6), Hybrid Projects (Section 7), BESS Projects (Section 8), Development Permission, Technical Standards (Section 4.7)
