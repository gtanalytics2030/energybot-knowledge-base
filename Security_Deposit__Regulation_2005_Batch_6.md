TITLE: Non-Payment Consequences, Interest Accrual, Default Review, Payment Timeline, and Refund Procedures

SUMMARY:
Regulations 4.10-4.14 establish enforcement and settlement procedures. Non-payment of initial deposit allows refusal of supply; non-payment of review-demanded deposit triggers disconnection under Electricity Act Sections 47(3) and 56. Licensees must pay interest on deposits at RBI Bank Rate (or higher rate fixed by Commission), credited annually through bill adjustments. Defaults in energy bill payment trigger deposit review. Additional deposits must be paid within 30 days; delays incur 0.25% weekly surcharge. Upon supply termination, security deposits are refunded after adjustment of dues within 30 days.

**4.10 Non-Payment of Security Deposit - Supply Refusal and Disconnection:**

If the applicant does not make payment of required Security Deposit as demanded by the licensee, as mentioned in clause 4.4, the licensee can refuse to release supply. In case where the security deposit requirement is reviewed by licensee in terms of clause 4.8, and accordingly, the consumer is required to pay further amount as Security Deposit and the consumer does not make payment, the supply of consumer shall be liable for disconnection as per sections 47(3) and 56 of The Electricity Act, 2003.

**SCENARIO 1: Non-Payment of Initial Deposit (New Applicants)**

**Regulation Reference:** Clause 4.4
**Trigger:** Applicant fails to pay initial security deposit demand
**Consequence:** Licensee may refuse to release supply
**Scope:** Complete refusal; no supply is provided
**Legal Basis:** Section 47 of Electricity Act 2003 (authority to require security and refuse supply)
**Duration:** Refusal continues until security is deposited

**Process:**
1. Licensee demands initial deposit per Regulation 4.4 (in amount appropriate to consumer category)
2. Applicant fails to make payment
3. Licensee refuses to provide/activate electricity supply
4. No electricity connection released until deposit received
5. Supply refusal is discretionary (licensee may refuse, but not required to)

---

**SCENARIO 2: Non-Payment of Additional Deposit (Reviewed/Revised Deposits)**

**Regulation References:** Clauses 4.6, 4.7, 4.8, 4.9
**Trigger:** Consumer fails to pay additional deposit demanded after review
**Consequence:** Supply liability for disconnection
**Legal Basis:** Sections 47(3) and 56 of Electricity Act 2003
**Status:** "Liable for disconnection" (discretionary; licensee may disconnect)

**Background:**
- Licensee reviews deposit following procedures in 4.6, 4.7, or 4.8
- Deposit shortfall exceeds 10% threshold (per Regulation 4.9)
- Licensee issues notice demanding additional payment (per Regulation 4.9, 4.13)
- Consumer receives 30-day payment period (per Regulation 4.13)
- Consumer fails to pay within 30 days

**Disconnection Process:**
- Electricity Act Section 47(3) empowers disconnection for non-payment of demanded security
- Electricity Act Section 56 provides disconnection procedure for non-payment
- Disconnection is discretionary (consumer is "liable for disconnection")
- Notice and other procedural requirements per Electricity Act apply

---

**DISTINCTION:**
- **Initial deposit non-payment:** "Refusal to release supply" (pre-connection stage)
- **Additional deposit non-payment:** "Liable for disconnection" (post-connection stage, legal consequences per EA 2003)

**STATUTORY REFERENCES:**

Section 47(3) of Electricity Act 2003 - Deals with disconnection for non-payment
Section 56 of Electricity Act 2003 - Disconnection procedure for non-payment

**4.11 Interest on Security Deposit - Accrual and Crediting:**

The licensee shall pay interest on Security Deposit of consumer for the electricity supplied, at the Bank Rate (as on 1st April of every year) notified by Reserve Bank of India or such higher rate as may be fixed by the Commission from time to time. The interest accrued to the credit of the consumer during the previous year shall be adjusted in electricity bills of May of every year for consumers covered under the monthly billing cycle and in electricity bill of May or June for those covered under two monthly billing cycles.

**INTEREST CALCULATION:**

**Rate Basis:**
- **Default Rate:** Bank Rate as notified by RBI on 1st April of each year
- **Alternative Rate:** Higher rate as fixed by GERC (if higher than RBI Bank Rate)
- **Rate Selection:** Licensee uses the higher of two rates
- **Effective From:** Applies from 1st April of each financial year

**Bank Rate Definition:**
- Notified by Reserve Bank of India
- Typically the rate at which RBI is prepared to lend to scheduled banks
- Published annually by RBI, usually on 1st April
- Public information, accessible from RBI website

**GERC Authority:**
- Commission may fix higher interest rate than Bank Rate
- Rate fixed by GERC supersedes RBI Bank Rate if higher
- Commission authority exercisable from time to time (rate can be revised)
- No specific cap on Commission authority mentioned

**APPLICATION PERIOD:**
- Interest accrued during financial year (1st April to 31st March)
- Previous financial year's accrual credited in current year's bills
- Annual crediting cycle (not monthly or quarterly)

**CREDITING MECHANISM:**

**For Monthly Billing Cycle Consumers:**
- Interest accrued during previous financial year (Apr 20XX - Mar 20XX+1)
- Credited in **May** electricity bill of next year
- May bill shows credit for 12 months of accrued interest
- Amount adjusted directly against consumption charges

**For Bi-Monthly (Two-Monthly) Billing Cycle Consumers:**
- Interest accrued during previous financial year (Apr 20XX - Mar 20XX+1)
- Credited in **May or June** electricity bill of next year
- Licensee has discretion to credit in May or June bill
- Amount adjusted directly against consumption charges

**EXAMPLE CALCULATION:**

Scenario: LT Consumer with monthly billing, deposit of Rs. 1,000

| Financial Year | Bank Rate (on 1 Apr) | Interest Rate | Interest Accrued | Billing Month Credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-24 | 6.5% | 6.5% | Rs. 65 | May 2024 |
| 2024-25 | 6.75% | 6.75% | Rs. 67.50 | May 2025 |
| 2025-26 | 6.5% | 6.5% | Rs. 65 | May 2026 |

**Effective Interest Yield:**
- Annual yield to consumer = Bank Rate % on deposit amount
- Compounds only when deposit increases
- Resets or recalculates if deposit reviewed/adjusted

**4.12 Review of Deposit Upon Consumer Default:**

In case a consumer commits default in making payment towards the energy bill, the amount of Security Deposit paid earlier shall be subjected to review by the Distribution Licensee. The said deposit amount shall be worked out based on the prevailing norms in accordance with these Regulations.

**Trigger:**
- Consumer default in payment towards energy bill (electricity consumption charges)
- Default means non-payment within due date (typically 15-30 days per tariff order)
- Any default event triggers deposit review (no threshold mentioned)

**Licensee Action:**
- Automatic review of deposit required upon notification/discovery of default
- Licensee recalculates required deposit using prevailing calculation norms
- Deposit amount worked out per Regulation 4.4 methodology

**Purpose:**
- Risk mitigation: Higher deposit for defaulters
- Reflects payment behavior risk
- May protect licensee in case of subsequent disconnection

**Recalculation Basis:**
- Current prevailing tariff rates
- Current prevailing load/demand (unchanged unless altered)
- Application of Regulation 4.4 consumer category rules

**Outcome:**
- If recalculated deposit > existing: Demand for additional deposit (per Regulation 4.13)
- If recalculated deposit < existing: Likely subject to refund (per Regulation 4.9)
- If recalculated deposit = existing: No change required

**4.13 Payment Timeline and Surcharge for Delayed Payment:**

The consumers shall pay the further Security Deposit within thirty days from the date of service of the demand notice issued by the licensee. If there is any delay in payment, the consumer shall pay surcharge thereon at 0.25% per week or at such rate as may be fixed by the Commission from time to time.

**PAYMENT DEADLINE:**

**Duration:** 30 days from date of service
**Service:** Licensee issues formal demand notice
**Effective Date:** Counting begins from date of actual service to consumer
**Time-Barred:** Any payment after 30 days is subject to surcharge

**SURCHARGE MECHANISM:**

**Default Rate:** 0.25% per week (calculated on amount due, not deposited)
**Calculation Method:** Compound or simple (not specified; likely simple interest on outstanding)

**Weekly Surcharge Calculation:**

| Days Late | Weeks Late | Surcharge Rate | Surcharge on Rs. 100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-7 days | ~1 week | 0.25% | Rs. 0.25 |
| 8-14 days | ~2 weeks | 0.50% | Rs. 0.50 |
| 15-21 days | ~3 weeks | 0.75% | Rs. 0.75 |
| 22-28 days | ~4 weeks | 1.00% | Rs. 1.00 |
| 29+ days | ~5+ weeks | 1.25%+ | Rs. 1.25+ |

**Commission Authority:**
- Commission may fix surcharge rate different from default 0.25%/week
- Authority exercisable from time to time
- Commission may increase or decrease rate by order
- Any revised rate replaces the default 0.25% rate

**Example Scenario:**

- Notice dated: 1st March 2024
- Payment due: 31st March 2024 (30 days)
- Demand deposit: Rs. 1,000
- Payment made: 28th April 2024 (28 days late = 4 weeks approximately)
- Surcharge: 0.25% × 4 = 1.0% × Rs. 1,000 = Rs. 10
- Total payable: Rs. 1,010

**Effect on Deposit Status:**
- Surcharge is collected separately or added to deposit
- Core deposit amount remains Rs. 1,000 (principal)
- Surcharge represents penalty for delay
- Payment of surcharge doesn't reduce deposit obligation

**4.14 Refund of Security Deposit Upon Termination:**

The Licensee, on termination of the agreement by either party shall refund Security Deposit after adjustment of dues, if any within 30 days.

**TERMINATION EVENTS:**

**By Consumer:**
- Voluntary disconnection request
- Change of residence/business
- Downgrade or merger

**By Licensee:**
- Non-payment disconnection
- Violation of supply terms
- End of temporary service period
- Technical reasons

**REFUND PROCESS:**

**Step 1: Calculation of Net Refund**
- Deposit Amount (principal): [All deposits maintained/paid]
- Less: Outstanding dues (electricity charges, taxes, penalties)
- Less: Any non-refundable fees or charges
- Net Refund = Deposit - Total Dues

**Step 2: Outstanding Dues Adjustment:**

Outstanding dues may include:
- Unpaid electricity bills
- Meter rent (if applicable)
- Energy meter charges
- Connection charges (if not yet paid)
- Any penalties or surcharges
- Amounts due under disconnection notices

**Step 3: Interest Accrual Until Termination:**
- Interest continues to accrue until final settlement date
- Final interest credited in termination settlement
- May be included in deposit amount or credited separately

**REFUND TIMELINE:**

- **Trigger:** Termination of supply agreement (by either party)
- **Calculation period:** Time taken to determine net refund amount
- **Refund deadline:** Within 30 days of termination
- **Method:** Cash, check, or account transfer (not specified in Regulation; per licensee procedure)

**EXAMPLE REFUND CALCULATION:**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Original security deposit | Rs. 5,000 |
| Additional deposits (over time) | Rs. 1,000 |
| Interest accrued (3 years × 6.5%) | Rs. 1,170 |
| **Total in Deposit Account** | **Rs. 7,170** |
| Less: Final electricity bill (unpaid) | (Rs. 2,500) |
| Less: Meter rent arrears | (Rs. 300) |
| Less: Penalty for late payment | (Rs. 200) |
| **Net Refund Payable** | **Rs. 4,170** |

**Non-Refundable Items:**

Items NOT adjusted against deposit (typically):
- Connection charges (if separately billed and paid)
- Meter purchase cost (if separately paid)
- Installation charges (if separately paid)
- Penalties for theft/meter tampering (subject to claims process)

**DISPUTE RESOLUTION:**

If consumer disputes:
- Outstanding dues amount
- Non-payment claim
- Refund calculation

Consumer may:
- Approach Distribution Licensee for clarification
- File complaint with CGRF (Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum)
- Approach GERC for regulatory intervention
- Pursue legal remedies under EA 2003
