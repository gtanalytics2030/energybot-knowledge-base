**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order

**PROVISION:** Section 1.1

**TITLE:** Background and Legal Authority

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission issues this Order under powers conferred by Sections 3(1), 61(h), 62(1)(a), and 86(1)(b) & (e) of the Electricity Act 2003, National Electricity Policy 2005, and Tariff Policy 2016. The Order determines generic tariff and conditions for MSW-to-Energy project power procurement by distribution licensees for projects commissioned post-June 5, 2022. The Commission previously issued Order No. 04 of 2016 with control period through March 31, 2019, later extended to March 31, 2021 via Suo Motu order dated October 23, 2017.

**KEY TOPICS:** MSW-to-Energy Projects, Waste to Energy, Generic Tariff, Distribution Licensees, Electricity Act 2003, GERC Authority, Control Period, Power Procurement, Gujarat Waste to Energy Policy 2022

**TEXT:**
SECTION 1.1 - BACKGROUND

In exercise of the powers conferred under Sections 3 (1), 61 (h), 62 (1) (a) and 86 (1) (b) & (e) of the Electricity Act, 2003, National Electricity Policy, 2005, and Tariff Policy, 2016 and all other powers enabling it in this behalf, the Gujarat Electricity Regulatory Commission (GERC or Commission) presents this Order for 'Determination of Generic Tariff and other terms and conditions for Procurement of Power by Distribution licensees from Municipal Solid Waste to Energy Projects in the State of Gujarat'. The provisions of this Order shall be applicable for the MSW based Power Projects commissioned post 5th June 2022 up to the control period specified in this Order.

The Commission previously had issued Order No. 04 of 2016 dated 10th November 2016 "Determination of Tariff and other terms & conditions for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees from Municipal Solid Waste to Energy Projects in the State of Gujarat." The control period of the said order was up to March 31, 2019. The Commission vide Order dated 23rd October 2017 in Suo Motu Petition No. 1654 of 2017 had extended the control period of the Order No. 04 of 2016 upto 31st March 2021.

The Commission while framing the discussion paper considered the Policy & Regulatory provisions outlined in the Electricity Act, 2003, and Rules framed under the Act including the 'Gujarat Waste to Energy Policy, 2022' notified by Government of Gujarat.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Electricity Act 2003 Section 3(1), 61(h), 62(1)(a), 86(1)(b), 86(1)(e); National Electricity Policy 2005; Tariff Policy 2016; GERC Order No. 04 of 2016 dated November 10, 2016; GERC Suo Motu Order dated October 23, 2017 in Petition No. 1654 of 2017; Gujarat Waste to Energy Policy 2022

**PROVISION:** Section 1.5

**TITLE:** Tariff Policy 2016 - Principles and Guidelines

**SUMMARY:**
The Tariff Policy 2016 from the Ministry of Power establishes guidelines for tariff determination by CERC and State Commissions. For renewable energy, the policy mandates competitive bidding for tariff determination; allows generic tariff as ceiling where bid tariff exceeds it; encourages central generating stations and renewable generators to participate in power markets; and permits renewable generators to sell excess power through market mechanisms, directly informing the tariff framework for MSW-to-Energy projects.

**KEY TOPICS:** Tariff Policy 2016, Renewable Energy Tariff, Competitive Bidding, Generic Tariff, RPO - Renewable Purchase Obligation, Power Market Mechanism, Tariff Determination Principles, Renewable Energy Generation

**TEXT:**
SECTION 1.5 - TARIFF POLICY 2016

The Tariff Policy, 2016 issued by the Ministry of Power provides specific guidelines and principles for tariff determination by the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC) and State Electricity Regulatory Commissions (SERCs/SRCs). The policy establishes norms and guidelines for tariff determination for conventional and renewable energy sources. 

With regard to Renewable Energy Based Generation, the Tariff Policy, 2016 mandates that:

a) Tariff for renewable energy generation facilities shall be determined through competitive bidding process;

b) In case tariff determined through competitive bidding is higher than the generic tariff, the difference shall be met by the Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO) mechanism;

c) The Central Generating Stations and facilities generating renewable energy should be encouraged to participate in the power market;

d) Renewable energy generators should be allowed to sell excess power generated during high wind/solar availability periods through market mechanism.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Tariff Policy 2016 (Ministry of Power); Renewable Energy Based Generation provisions

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Section 1.4 (National Electricity Policy 2005), Section 1.6 (Gujarat Waste to Energy Policy 2022), Section 2.30 (Generic Tariff Computation), Section 2.31 (Financial Assistance and Subsidy Treatment)
