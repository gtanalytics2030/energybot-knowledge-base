HIERARCHY: Section 4 / 4.1 Benchmark Capital Cost

PROVISION: Section 4.1

TITLE: Benchmark Capital Cost — Fixed at Rs. 3.05 Crores/MW

SUMMARY:
Section 4 begins the Commission's determination of generic tariff for Solar Power Projects below the threshold limit of eligibility for competitive bidding (below 5 MW), for a representative 1 MW capacity project, exercised under Section 62 of the Electricity Act, 2003 read with the GERC (MYT) Regulations, 2016. For Capital Cost, the discussion paper had proposed a normative benchmark of Rs. 3.45 Crores/MW (inclusive of evacuation cost up to the GETCO Sub-Station). No stakeholder comments were received. The Commission, observing that GUVNL's recent competitive bidding had discovered tariffs in the range of Rs 2.30/kWh to Rs 2.66/kWh and that Solar Module prices were declining in Q1 of FY 2024-25, decided to fix the benchmark normative capital cost at the lower figure of Rs. 3.05 Crores/MW for the control period of this order. This capital cost is inclusive of all capital works including plant and machinery, civil work, erection and commissioning, financing and interest during construction, and evacuation infrastructure up to the inter-connection point.

KEY TOPICS: Benchmark Capital Cost, Normative Capital Cost, Solar Power Projects Below Threshold, Representative 1 MW Project, GUVNL Competitive Bidding, Declining Solar Module Prices, Evacuation Infrastructure, Section 62 Electricity Act 2003, GERC MYT Regulations 2016

4.1 Benchmark Capital Cost

4.1.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Commission in the discussion paper have proposed a normative benchmark capital cost of Rs. 3.45 Crores/MW for tariff determination purpose. The above cost is including evacuation cost up to the GETCO Sub-Station.

4.1.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

4.1.3 Commission's Analysis and Ruling

The Commission observed that the tariff discovered in competitive bidding conducted by GUVNL in recent past is in the range of Rs 2.30 / kWh to Rs 2.66 /kWh. The Commission also noted that the Solar Module prices during Q1 of FY 2024-25 are in declining mode. In view of this, the Commission decides to fix the benchmark normative capital cost of Rs 3.05 Crores/MW for determination of tariff for Solar power projects below threshold limit of eligibility to participate in bidding process for the control period of this order.

The capital cost is inclusive of the of all capital work including plant and machinery, civil work, erection and commissioning, financing and interest during construction, and evacuation infrastructure up to inter-connection point.

PROVISION: Section 4.8

TITLE: Return on Equity — Revised to 15.5% per GERC MYT Regulations 2024

SUMMARY:
GERC (Multi Year Tariff) Regulations, 2016 provided for Return on Equity at the rate of 14% plus applicable tax payment, which the Commission follows as the RoE norm for conventional and renewable generation tariff determination. In the discussion paper, the Commission accordingly proposed RoE of 14% with MAT at 17.47% per annum for the first 10 years and corporate tax at 34.94% (Income Tax rate 30% plus 12% surcharge plus 4% cess) per annum for the next 15 years. No stakeholder comments were received. In its ruling, the Commission noted that the GERC MYT Regulations 2024 (the newly notified regulations superseding the 2016 regulations) provide for RoE of 15.5% for generating projects, and accordingly decided to consider 15.5% RoE for Solar Power Projects below the threshold limit, with the tax payment of MAT at 17.47% for the first 10 years and Corporate Tax at 34.94% for the next 15 years considered separately.

KEY TOPICS: Return on Equity, RoE 15.5 Percent, GERC MYT Regulations 2024, Minimum Alternative Tax 17.47 Percent, Corporate Tax 34.94 Percent, Tax Payment Computation

TEXT:
4.8 Return on Equity

4.8.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

GERC (Multi Year Tariff) Regulations, 2016 provides for Return of Equity at the rate 14% plus the applicable tax payment. The Commission follow the RoE norms as specified in MYT Regulations while determining generation tariff for conventional and renewable power projects. Accordingly, in the discussion paper, the Commission proposed to consider the RoE of 14% and the tax payment of MAT @ 17.47 % per annum for first 10 years and corporate tax @ 34.94% (IT Rate 30% + 12% Surcharge + 4% Cess) per annum for the next 15 years for tariff determination purpose for the new control period.

4.8.2 Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

4.8.3 Commission's Analysis and Ruling

The Commission notes that the GERC MYT Regulations 2024 notified by the Commission provides for RoE of 15.5% for determination of tariff for generating projects. Accordingly, the Commission decide to consider 15.5 % RoE for determination of tariff for Solar Power Projects below threshold limit. Further, the tax payment of MAT @ 17.47% per annum for first 10 years and Corporate Tax @ 34.94% per annum for the next 15 years has been considered separately for tariff determination purpose.

PROVISION: Section 4.10

TITLE: Accelerated Depreciation — 60% Income Tax Depreciation Benefit in First Year

SUMMARY:
For Accelerated Depreciation, the discussion paper proposed a methodology for ascertaining the Income Tax benefit on account of accelerated/additional depreciation: the benefit assessment is based on normative Capital Cost, the accelerated/additional depreciation rate per the relevant Income Tax Act provisions, and the Corporate Income Tax rate; capitalisation of Solar Power Projects is assumed for the full financial year; and per-unit benefit is derived on a levelized basis at a discounting factor equivalent to the post-tax weighted average cost of capital. As per Income Tax Act provisions, 60% depreciation is available in the first year of commissioning, which the Commission considered while calculating the per-unit AD benefit. No stakeholder comments were received. The Commission decided to ascertain the Income Tax benefit on account of accelerated/additional depreciation exactly as proposed in the discussion paper.

KEY TOPICS: Accelerated Depreciation, AD Benefit, Income Tax Act, 60 Percent First Year Depreciation, Post-Tax WACC, Levelized Per-Unit Benefit

TEXT:
4.10 Accelerated Depreciation

4.10.1 Proposed in Discussion Paper

The Commission in the discussion paper for ascertaining the Income Tax benefit on account of accelerated or additional depreciation has proposed following methodology:

i. The assessment of benefit shall be based on normative Capital Cost, accelerated/ additional depreciation rate as per the relevant provisions of the Income Tax Act and the Corporate Income Tax rate.
ii. Capitalisation of Solar Power Projects for the full financial year.
iii. Per-unit benefit shall be derived on levelized basis at a discounting factor equivalent to the post-tax weighted average cost of capital.
