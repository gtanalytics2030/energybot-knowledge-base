## CHUNK 1: Clause 3.1 – Tariff Framework (Projects Above Threshold Limit)

---
DOCUMENT: GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

DOCUMENT TYPE: Tariff Order

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / Clause 2.1 / Sub-Clause 2.1.3

PROVISION: Clause 3.1 – Tariff Framework for Projects Above Threshold Limit

TITLE: Commission's Decision on Tariff Framework for Wind Power Projects Above 10 MW Threshold

SUMMARY:
For wind power projects above the 10 MW threshold limit (as per revised Ministry of Power Competitive Bidding Guidelines of 26.07.2023), distribution licensees shall continue to procure electricity through competitive bidding under Section 63 of the Electricity Act, 2003, or by following the competitive bidding process of SECI/MNRE. Distribution licensees may approach the Commission for adoption of tariff discovered through such competitive bidding process. The Commission's decision aligns with Clause 6.4(2) of the National Tariff Policy and the revised threshold of 10 MW specified in MNRE guidelines.

KEY TOPICS: Tariff determination, Competitive Bidding, Wind Power Projects, Section 63 of Electricity Act 2003, SECI/MNRE, Distribution Licensees, Threshold limit (10 MW), Tariff Discovery, Commission Adoption

TEXT:
**2.1.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling**

The Commission has carefully gone through the submissions made by the stakeholders and provisions outlined under the Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023. The Commission noted that the threshold limit of Wind Power Project to participate in competitive bidding has been specified as 10 MW in the Tariff based competitive bidding guidelines for Wind Energy projects notified by MNRE on 26.07.2023. The Commission also has to keep in mind the tariff related provisions specified in the Electricity Act 2003 and the Tariff Policy 2016. After considering all the relevant aspects, the Commission decided to specify following tariff framework for Procurement of Power from Wind Energy Projects above/ below the threshold limit specified in Competitive Bidding Guidelines published by the Ministry of Power, Government of India.

**Projects above threshold limit**

As recommended in Clause 6.4 (2) of the Tariff Policy, the distribution licensees shall continue to procure electricity from the eligible Wind Power Projects through competitive bidding under Section 63 of the Act or by following Competitive Bidding Process followed by SECI/MNRE etc. Further, the Distribution Licensees may approach the Commission for adoption of the tariff discovered through such Competitive Bidding Process.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 63 of Electricity Act 2003, Clause 6.4(2) of Tariff Policy 2016, MNRE Tariff Based Competitive Bidding Guidelines dated 26.07.2023, Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023, SECI procurement guidelines

CROSS-REFERENCES: Related to Clause 3.1(b) for projects below threshold limit, Section 62 of Electricity Act 2003 (generic tariff determination), CERC Regulations on competitive bidding, Distribution Code provisions on power procurement

## CHUNK 2: Clause 3.1 – Tariff Framework (Projects Below Threshold Limit)

---
DOCUMENT: GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

DOCUMENT TYPE: Tariff Order

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / Clause 2.1 / Sub-Clause 2.1.3

PROVISION: Clause 3.1 – Tariff Framework for Projects Below 10 MW Threshold

TITLE: Commission's Decision on Generic Tariff for Small Wind Power Projects (Below 10 MW)

SUMMARY:
For wind power projects below the 10 MW threshold limit of eligibility for competitive bidding, the Commission determined generic tariff under Section 62 of the Electricity Act, 2003, as discussed in Chapter 4 of the Order. The Commission noted that Section 62 empowers the Commission to determine tariff for supply of electricity by a generating company to the distribution licensee, guided by the terms and conditions specified for tariff determination under Section 61 of the Act. This approach deemed more appropriate than relying on discovered tariff mechanisms, due to the specific challenges of tariffing small projects.

KEY TOPICS: Generic Tariff, Section 62 of Electricity Act 2003, Section 61 conditions, Small Wind Projects, Below-threshold capacity, Commission determination, Tariff methodology, Distribution Licensee procurement

TEXT:
**Projects below threshold limit**

The Commission noted that as per the revised Competitive Bidding Guidelines issued by the Ministry of Power, Govt. of India, the threshold limit for Wind Power Projects to participate in competitive bidding process is 10 MW. The Section 62 of the Electricity Act empower the Commission to determine the tariff for supply of electricity by a generating company to the distribution licensee. While doing so the Commission is guided by the terms and conditions specified by the Commission for determination of tariff under Section 61 of the Act. For Wind Power Projects below the threshold limit of 10 MW, the Commission deemed it more appropriate to determine the tariff under Section 62 of the Act.

Accordingly, the Commission has determined the generic tariff for such projects as discussed in the Chapter 4 of this Order.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 62 of Electricity Act 2003, Section 61 of Electricity Act 2003, MNRE Competitive Bidding Guidelines (26.07.2023), Chapter 4 of GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 (Generic Tariff Determination), Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023

CROSS-REFERENCES: Clause 3.1(a) for above-threshold projects, Chapter 4 of this Order (detailed generic tariff calculations), GERC Tariff Regulations, Distribution Code Section on power procurement agreements

## CHUNK 3: Clause 3.2(a) – Control Period – Commission's Decision

---
DOCUMENT: GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

DOCUMENT TYPE: Tariff Order

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / Clause 2.2 / Sub-Clause 2.2.3

PROVISION: Clause 3.2(a) – Control Period

TITLE: Control Period for Wind Power Tariff Framework: 06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027

SUMMARY:
The Commission established the control period of this wind power tariff framework from 06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027. This control period aligns with the effective date of Ministry of Power's Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022 (notified on 06.06.2022). The Commission noted that there was no tariff order in effect as of 06.06.2022 following expiry of the previous Wind Tariff Order dated 30.04.2020 (which had control period up to 31.03.2022). The previous order's control period is deemed extended to 05.06.2022 to bridge the gap. Wind Power Projects commissioned during the control period shall be governed by MoP's Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022, read with GERC Green Energy Open Access Regulations, 2024, and this Order.

KEY TOPICS: Control Period, Tariff Framework, Wind Power Projects, Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022, GERC Open Access Regulations 2024, Commissioning Date, Banking Facility, Energy Accounting, Open Access charges

TEXT:
**2.2.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling**

The Commission has gone through the suggestions received from the stakeholders. The Commission also noted that the Order No. 02 of 2020 dated 30.04.2020 for Wind Power Project having control period up to 31.03.2022 and thereafter, no extension in control period of said order is granted.

The Commission has noted that the Ministry of Power, Government of India has notified the Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy Through Green Energy Open Access) Rules, 2022 on 6th June 2022 outlining the policy and regulatory provisions for promotion of renewable sources of energy through Open Access. Further, the MoP Rules are effective from the date of its Notification, i.e. from 06.06.2022. The Commission also notes that the Wind Tariff dated 30.04.2020 was having control period only up to 31.03.2022 and there was no tariff order of the Commission as on 06.06.2022 and also specified in the discussion paper that the new Order shall be effective from 06.06.2022. Accordingly, the control period of previous tariff order dated 30.04.2020 deemed to be extended up to 05.06.2022.

Considering above aspects and to give effect to the provisions of Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 notified by Ministry of Power, Govt. of India under the Electricity Act, 2003 and specifically when there was no tariff order of the Commission as on 06.06.2022 with regard to Wind Power Project tariff framework, the Commission found it more appropriate to adopt the policy and regulatory provisions outlined in MoP' Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022 and defined the Control Period of new tariff order to be made effective from 06.06.2022 in the discussion paper also. Accordingly, the Commission decides to define the Control Period of this tariff framework effective from 06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027. The projects which are commissioned during aforesaid Control Period shall be governed by the provisions MoP's Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022, read with provisions of GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024 and this Order of the Commission and eligible for the benefits stipulated in this Order. Further, Wind Power Projects commissioned during the Control Period of this Order shall be liable to pay applicable Open Access charges and provisions related to banking facility, energy accounting etc. and shall be governed by this Order read with GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Order No. 02 of 2020 (Previous Wind Tariff Order dated 30.04.2020), MoP Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 (notified 06.06.2022), Electricity Act 2003, GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations 2024, Chapter 3 of this Order (Detailed Framework Provisions)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Clause 3.2(e) on Eligibility Criteria, Clause 3.7 on Banking Facility, Clause 3.8 on Security Deposit, Section 2 of this Order (Discussion Paper proposing same dates), GERC Open Access Regulations 2024 (applicable provisions during control period)

## CHUNK 4: Clause 3.2(e) – Eligibility Criteria – Commission's Decision

---
DOCUMENT: GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

DOCUMENT TYPE: Tariff Order

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / Clause 2.3 / Sub-Clause 2.3.3

PROVISION: Clause 3.2(e) – Eligibility Criteria for Wind Power Projects

TITLE: Eligibility Criteria for Wind Power Projects: PPA Signing and Commissioning Requirements

SUMMARY:
Wind Power Projects using new plant and machinery for which PPAs are signed during the control period (06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027) and commissioned as per PPA terms and conditions shall be eligible to sell power to distribution licensees of Gujarat at the tariff framework decided by the Commission in this Order. Similarly, Wind Power Projects set up for captive use or third-party sale and commissioned during the control period using new plant and machinery shall be liable to pay applicable Open Access charges and provisions related to banking facility and energy accounting, and shall be governed by this Order read with GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024. The Commission clarified that the PPA signing date (rather than commissioning date) determines eligibility, to provide practical clarity on project timelines.

KEY TOPICS: Eligibility, Wind Power Projects, PPA (Power Purchase Agreement), Commissioning, New Plant and Machinery, Distribution Licensees, Tariff Framework, Captive Use, Third-party Sale, Open Access, Banking Facility, Energy Accounting

TEXT:
**2.3.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling**

The Commission finds merit in the submission of GUVNL in regard to project commissioning timelines for the Wind Power Projects supplying power to Distribution Licensee under the PPA.

The Commission after examining the practical difficulties pointed out by GUVNL has decided to modify the eligibility criteria as given below:

"the Wind Power Projects using new plant and machineries for which PPA is sign during the control period of this tariff order and commissioned as per PPA terms and conditions shall be considered as eligible to sell power to distribution licensees of Gujarat at the tariff framework decide by the Commission in this Order. Similarly, the Wind Power Projects set up for captive use / third party sale and commissioned during the control period of this Order using new plant and machineries, shall be liable to pay applicable Open Access charges and provisions related to banking facility, energy accounting etc. and shall be governed by this Order read with GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024.

The Commission has already deliberated on the aspects of tariff framework for Wind projects below /above threshold limit of participation in bidding process in Clause 2.1 above.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: PPA (Power Purchase Agreement) terms and conditions, GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024, Distribution Licensee Supply Code, Clause 3.1 (Tariff Framework), Section 63 and 62 of Electricity Act 2003

CROSS-REFERENCES: Clause 3.2(a) on Control Period, Clause 3.1 on Tariff Framework (above/below threshold), Clause 3.3 on Wheeling charges, Clause 3.7 on Banking Facility, Clause 3.8 on Security Deposit requirements, GERC Open Access Regulations 2024

## CHUNK 5: Clause 3.2(e) – Forecasting and Scheduling for Wind Power – Commission's Decision

---
DOCUMENT: GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

DOCUMENT TYPE: Tariff Order

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / Clause 2.4 / Sub-Clause 2.4.3

PROVISION: Clause 3.2(e) – Forecasting and Scheduling for Wind Power Projects

TITLE: Forecasting, Scheduling, and Deviation Settlement for Wind Power Projects

SUMMARY:
For ISTS-connected Wind Projects (including inter-state RE projects in Gujarat directly connected to ISTS and supplying power within or outside Gujarat), energy accounting for deviation shall follow CERC Regulations. For Gujarat-based wind projects connected to the state grid through ISTS and selling power outside/inside the state, deviation charges shall apply at the higher of Reference Rate or Normal Rate of Charges for shortfall, as per GERC Green Energy Open Access Regulations, 2024. For wind power projects set up for captive use or third-party sale, consumers shall not change the quantum of power consumed through green energy open access for more than 12 time-blocks to avoid variations in demand to be met by the distribution licensee. The Commission did not modify these provisions, as they align with MoP's Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy through Green Energy Open Access) Rules, 2022, and are adequately covered in GERC's Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement Regulations, 2019.

KEY TOPICS: Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement, Energy Accounting, ISTS-connected projects, State grid, Green Energy Open Access, Time-blocks (12), Reference Rate, Normal Rate of Charges, CERC Regulations, GERC Open Access Regulations 2024, Captive consumption, Third-party sale

TEXT:
**2.4.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling**

The Commission has gone through the submission of the stakeholders. The Commission is of the opinion that the GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024 and GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019 has provisions with regard to energy accounting and deviation settlement in respect of Wind/ Solar Power Projects.

The provision of the minimum number of time blocks, which shall not be more than 12 time-blocks, for which the consumer shall not change the quantum of power consumed through Green Energy Open Access are incorporated as per the provisions of MoP' Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy through Green Energy Open Access) Rules, 2022. Therefore, no change is required in this provision.

The Commission has also noted that SLDC has filed Petition No. 2061 of 2022 seeking amendment of the GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations, 2019. The Commission will look into the matter and take appropriate decision in this regard.

In view of above, the Commission decided to keep the Forecasting and Scheduling related provisions for Wind Power Projects as proposed in the discussion paper.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: CERC Regulations (for ISTS-connected projects), GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations 2024, GERC (Forecasting, Scheduling, Deviation Settlement and Related Matters of Solar and Wind Generation Sources) Regulations 2019, MoP Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy through Green Energy Open Access) Rules 2022, Petition No. 2061/2022

CROSS-REFERENCES: Clause 3.3 on transmission and wheeling charges, Clause 3.7 on banking facility and energy accounting, GERC Grid Code provisions on scheduling, Electricity Act 2003 Section 66 (scheduling and dispatch), Distribution Code provisions on energy accounting

## CHUNK 6: Clause 3.2(f) – Merit Order Dispatch – Commission's Decision

---
DOCUMENT: GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

DOCUMENT TYPE: Tariff Order

HIERARCHY: Section 2 / Clause 2.5 / Sub-Clause 2.5.3

PROVISION: Clause 3.2(f) – Applicability of Merit Order Dispatch Principle

TITLE: MUST RUN Status for Wind Power Plants and Compensation Mechanism for Reduced Off-take

SUMMARY:
Wind power plants, irrespective of plant capacity, shall be treated as MUST RUN power plants and shall not be subjected to Merit Order Dispatch (MOD) principles. This status is provided under the Indian Electricity Grid Code 2023 and State Grid Code, which extend MUST RUN status to all renewable energy plants. The Commission noted that MoP's Competitive Bidding Guidelines (dated 28.07.2023) provide a compensation mechanism for renewable energy generators in case of reduced off-take, non-despatch of power due to grid-related exigencies. For wind power projects procuring power to distribution licensees through competitive bidding, compensation provisions shall be governed by approved bid documents/PPA, while MUST RUN status applies irrespective of PPA terms.

KEY TOPICS: MUST RUN Status, Merit Order Dispatch, Renewable Energy, Wind Power Plants, Indian Electricity Grid Code 2023, State Grid Code, Compensation mechanism, Reduced off-take, Grid exigencies, Power despatch, PPA terms

TEXT:
**2.5.3 Analysis and Commission's Ruling**

The Indian Electricity Grid Code 2023 as well as State Grid Code provides 'Must Run' Status to Renewables, irrespective of the plant capacity and hence, RE plant shall not be subjected to Merit Order Despatch Principle.

The Commission noted that the MoP's Competitive Bidding Guideline dated 28.07.2023 provides compensation mechanism for RE Generator in case of reduced off-take, non-despatch of power due to grid related exigencies. Therefore, in case of Wind Power Projects set up under Competitive bidding route for supply of power to Distribution Licensees, the provisions related to compensation mechanism for non/reduced off-take of power shall be governed by the approved bid documents/PPA.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Indian Electricity Grid Code 2023, State Grid Code (GERC Grid Code), MoP Competitive Bidding Guidelines (28.07.2023), Electricity Act 2003 Section 21 (Grid operation and scheduling), Section 66 (scheduling and dispatch), GERC Distribution Code, CERC Wind Energy Regulations

CROSS-REFERENCES: Clause 3.3 on transmission charges (applies irrespective of dispatch), Clause 3.1 on tariff framework for competitively bid projects (where PPA compensation terms apply), Distribution Code provisions on generator dispatch, Electricity Act 2003 Section 21 (grid code implementation)
