**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 3 / Section 3.1

**PROVISION:** Section 3.1

**TITLE:** Tariff Framework for Wind Power Projects - Bifurcation by Project Capacity Threshold

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission establishes a bifurcated tariff framework for wind power projects based on their capacity relative to the 10 MW threshold. Projects of 10 MW or above are eligible to participate in competitive bidding under Section 63 of the Electricity Act and will have their tariff discovered through competitive process, with the Commission authorized to adopt such tariff. Projects below 10 MW are not eligible for competitive bidding and will receive generic tariff determined by the Commission under Section 62 of the Act. This framework applies to projects commissioned during the control period of 06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027.

**KEY TOPICS:** Wind Power Tariff Framework, Competitive Bidding, Section 62 Tariff, Section 63 Tariff, Threshold Limit (10 MW), Generic Tariff, Project Capacity, Eligible Projects, Distribution Licensees, SECI

**TEXT:**

Having considered all the comments from the stakeholders, the Commission hereby issues the final Tariff Framework for Wind Power Projects effective from 06.06.2022, as under:

**Projects above threshold limit**

As recommended in clause 6.4 (2) of the Tariff Policy, the distribution licensees shall continue to procure electricity from the eligible Wind Power Projects through competitive bidding under Section 63 of the Act or by following competitive bidding process followed by SECI/MNRE etc. Further, the distribution licensees may approach the Commission for adoption of the tariff discovered through such competitive bidding process.

**Projects below threshold limit**

For Wind Power projects below the threshold limit of 10 MW for eligibility in participation in Competitive Bidding, the Commission has determined generic tariff for procurement of power by Distribution Licensee under Section 62 of the Act, as discussed in the later part of this Order.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Tariff Policy clause 6.4(2), Section 62 of Electricity Act 2003, Section 63 of Electricity Act 2003, SECI Competitive Bidding Guidelines, MNRE Guidelines

**PROVISION:** Section 3.2(a)

**TITLE:** Control Period for Wind Power Tariff Framework

**SUMMARY:**
The control period for the wind power tariff framework is established from 06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027. This aligns with the Ministry of Power's notification of the Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy Through Green Energy Open Access) Rules, 2022, which became effective on 06.06.2022. All wind power projects commissioned during this control period are governed by these tariff framework provisions, including the provisions of MoP Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022, GERC Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access Regulations 2024, and this Order. Wind projects commissioned during this control period remain liable for applicable Open Access charges and are subject to banking facility and energy accounting provisions.

**KEY TOPICS:** Control Period, Effective Date (06.06.2022), Commissioning, Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022, Banking Facility, Energy Accounting, Regulatory Compliance, GERC Regulations 2024

**TEXT:**

The Commission has noted that the Ministry of Power notified the Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy Through Green Energy Open Access) Rules, 2022 on 6th June 2022 outlining the policy and regulatory provisions for promotion of renewable sources of energy through Open Access. Further, the MoP Rules is effective from the date of its Notification, i.e., from 06.06.2022. The Commission also notes that the Wind Tariff Order dated 30.04.2020 read with amendments made in it, was having control period upto 31.03.2022 and there was no tariff order of the Commission as on 06.06.2022 and also specified in the discussion paper that the new Order shall be effective from 06.06.2022 i.e. from the effective date of MoP' Rules.

Considering above aspects and to give effect to the provisions of Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 notified by Ministry of Power, Govt. of India under the Electricity Act, 2003, the Commission found it more appropriate to adopt the policy and regulatory provisions outlined in MoP' Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022 and defined the Control Period of new tariff order to be made effective from 06.06.2022 in the discussion paper also. Accordingly, the Commission decides to define the Control Period of this tariff framework effective from 06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027. The Wind Power Projects which are commissioned during aforesaid Control Period shall be governed by the provisions MoP' Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022, read with provisions of GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024 and this Order of the Commission and eligible for the benefits stipulated in this Order. Further, Wind Power Projects commissioned during the Control Period of this Order shall be liable to pay applicable Open Access charges and provisions related to banking facility, energy accounting etc. and shall be governed by this Order read with GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Electricity (Promoting Renewable Energy Through Green Energy Open Access) Rules 2022 (Ministry of Power), Electricity Act 2003, GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations 2024, Wind Tariff Order dated 30.04.2020

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Banking facility provisions in Section 3.7, Energy accounting in Section 3.7, Open Access charges structure in Section 3.3, Applicability date 06.06.2022 referenced in Section 4.13

**PROVISION:** Section 3.2(f)

**TITLE:** Applicability of Merit Order Dispatch Principle - Wind Power Plants Designated as MUST RUN

**SUMMARY:**
All wind power plants, irrespective of their capacity, are classified as "MUST RUN" power plants and are exempted from Merit Order Dispatch (MOD) principles. This means that wind generating units cannot be constrained or decommitted based on merit order ranking or short-term operational economic considerations. Wind power generation must be accepted and scheduled by the system operator in preference to economic merit-order principles, ensuring priority dispatch for all wind power generation regardless of plant size.

**KEY TOPICS:** Merit Order Dispatch, MUST RUN Status, Wind Power Plants, Plant Capacity Irrelevant, Economic Dispatch, Priority Dispatch, System Operations, Grid Stability, Renewable Energy Promotion

**TEXT:**

f) **Applicability of Merit Order Dispatch Principle**

The Wind power plants irrespective of plant capacity shall be considered as 'MUST RUN' power plants and shall not be subjected to 'Merit Order Dispatch' principles.

**TITLE:** Transmission and Wheeling Charges Framework - Third-Party Sale and Captive Use Configurations

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission establishes a comprehensive framework for transmission and wheeling charges applicable to wind power projects undertaking third-party sales or captive consumption through open access. Charges vary based on voltage levels at injection and drawl points: (a) 66 KV and above at both points: transmission charges and losses on energy feed basis; (b) 66 KV and above injection with below 66 KV drawl: transmission, transmission losses, wheeling charges, and wheeling losses; (c) Both points below 66 KV within same DISCOM: wheeling charges and losses only. Wind generators engaging in third-party sale/consumption receive 25% concession in Cross-Subsidy Surcharge and Additional Surcharge if RE attributes are not claimed; 100% surcharge applies if RE attributes are utilized or REC mechanism is employed. Captive mode largely mirrors third-party frameworks with identical charge structures. A flat 5 paise/unit charge applies for wheeling to multiple locations. Cross-subsidy surcharge and additional surcharge are governed by MoP Rules and GERC Green Energy Open Access Regulations 2024.

**KEY TOPICS:** Transmission Charges, Wheeling Charges, Third-Party Sale, Captive Use, Voltage Levels (66 KV), Cross-Subsidy Surcharge, Additional Surcharge, RE Attributes, REC Mechanism, Energy Feed Basis, GETCO Sub-Station, Multiple Locations

**TEXT:**

**Wheeling of power for Third Party Sale**

a) In case of injection of the electricity at 66 KV level or above and drawl of electricity at 66 KV level or above, the transmission of energy from the injection point to drawl place shall be allowed by paying applicable transmission charges and losses on energy feed basis as determined by the Commission from time to time, as applicable to Green Energy Open Access Transaction as per GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024 and amendments in it read with tariff Regulations of the Commission as applicable from time to time.

b) In case of injection of electricity at 66 KV or above and drawl of electricity below 66 KV voltage level, wheeling of electricity generated from the Wind power Project to desired location(s) within the State shall be allowed on payment of applicable transmission charges & transmission losses on energy feed basis and wheeling charges & wheeling losses on the energy fed into grid as measured at receiving Sub-Station of GETCO, as determined by the Commission from time to time and applicable to green energy open access transaction as per GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024 and amendments in it read with Tariff Regulations of the Commission as applicable from time to time.

c) In case point of injection and drawl at a voltage level below 66 KV, lies within area of same DISCOM, the wheeling of energy from Wind Project shall be allowed upon payment of Wheeling Charges and Wheeling Losses of energy fed to grid, as applicable to green energy open access transaction as per GERC (Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access) Regulations, 2024 and amendments in it read with Tariff Regulations of the Commission as applicable from time to time.

**PROVISION:** Section 3.5

**TITLE:** Pricing of Reactive Power for Wind Power Projects

**SUMMARY:**
Wind power projects require reactive power from the grid during initial start-up and for continuous transformer operation, necessitating grid stability measures through reactive power limitation devices. To restrain wind power projects from consuming excessive reactive power and encourage installation of suitable reactive power compensation devices, the Commission establishes levies on reactive power charges. The pricing of reactive power for all prospective wind power projects is determined consistently as per GETCO's Tariff Order applicable from time to time, read with GERC Grid Code specifications. This ensures that all wind generators operate under identical reactive power pricing mechanisms and incentivizes power factor correction through compensation devices.

**KEY TOPICS:** Reactive Power, Grid Stability, Start-up, Station Transformers, Compensation Devices, Reactive Power Consumption, Reactive Power Charges, Power Factor, GETCO Tariff Order, GERC Grid Code, Grid Support

**TEXT:**

**Pricing of Reactive Power**

Reactive Power is required by the Wind generators during initial start-up and station transformers also continuously require reactive power from the grid. Hence, in order to maintain grid stability, it is necessary to limit such reactive power consumption from the grid by installation of suitable compensation devices. In order to restrain the Wind power projects from consuming more reactive power from the grid and to encourage them to install suitable compensation devices to limit such reactive power consumption, the levy of reactive charges is necessary.

The pricing of reactive power for all prospective Wind power projects shall be same as decided by the Commission in the GETCO's Tariff Order from time to time read with GERC's Grid Code.
