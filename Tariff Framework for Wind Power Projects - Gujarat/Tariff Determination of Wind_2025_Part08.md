## Chunk 18

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by the Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.1

**PROVISION:** Section 4.1 (Threshold Limit and Section 4.1.3 - Commission's Final Decision)

**TITLE:** Threshold Limit for Competitive Bidding Eligibility - 10 MW Determination

**SUMMARY:**
For wind power projects, the threshold limit for eligibility to participate in competitive bidding is fixed at 10 MW capacity. This aligns with Ministry of Power Guidelines dated 26.07.2023 for Tariff-Based Competitive Bidding and the Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023. The threshold was revised from 25 MW (originally proposed in discussion paper) to 10 MW based on stakeholder submissions noting that MoP guidelines establish 10 MW for intra-state transmission system projects and 50 MW for inter-state transmission system projects. Projects of 10 MW and above are eligible for competitive bidding under Section 63 of the Electricity Act 2003. Projects below 10 MW receive generic pre-fixed levelized tariff determined under Section 62 of the Act. This bifurcation ensures alignment with national policy and allows flexibility in renewable energy procurement.

**KEY TOPICS:** Threshold Limit (10 MW), Competitive Bidding Eligibility, Section 62 Tariff, Section 63 Tariff, MoP Guidelines (26.07.2023), Intra-State Transmission System, Inter-State Transmission System, Generic Tariff, Pre-Fixed Levelized Tariff, Gujarat RE Policy 2023

**TEXT:**

**4.1. Threshold limit for participating in Competitive Bidding Process**

**4.1.1. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

The Commission in the discussion paper has proposed a generic levelled tariff for procurement of electricity from the Wind power projects below the threshold limit of eligibility of 25 MW to participate in competitive bidding process. The proposed tariff was determined by the Commission as per section 62 of the Act for representative 1 MW capacity Wind Power Project based on benchmark financial and operational parameters and provisions under GERC MYT Regulations 2016.

**4.1.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

GUVNL submitted that the MoP guidelines dated 26.07.2023 for competitive bidding process, sets threshold limit for (a) bid capacity of 10 MW and above for project connected with intra-state system and (b) bid capacity of 50 MW and above for project connected to inter-state system. Further, Gujarat RE Policy, 2023 allows the DISCOM to procure the power from small size wind project upto 10 MW capacity at pre-fixed levelized tariff. Therefore, in line with Gujarat RE Policy, 2023 and MoP Guidelines dated 26.07.2023, it is suggested to keep threshold capacity below 10 MW instead of 25 MW to eligible for pre-fixed levelized tariff.

**4.1.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission observed that as per the revised Guidelines for Tariff Based Competitive Bidding Process for Procurement Power from Grid Connected Wind Power Projects notified by the Ministry of Power dated 26.07.2023 , the threshold limit of Wind power projects eligible to participate in competitive bidding has been defined as , (a) bid capacity of 10 MW and above for projects connected to Intra-State transmission system; and (b) bid capacity of 50 MW and above for projects connected to Inter-State Transmission System. Hence the Commission decided to determine the generic pre-fixed levelized tariff under Section 62 for procurement of electricity from the Wind power projects below 10 MW capacity.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Electricity Act 2003 (Section 62, Section 63), MoP Guidelines for Tariff Based Competitive Bidding dated 26.07.2023, Gujarat Renewable Energy Policy 2023, GERC MYT Regulations 2016

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Tariff framework bifurcation in Section 3.1 (Projects above 10 MW use competitive bidding; projects below 10 MW use generic tariff); applicable to all wind projects commissioned during control period per Section 4.13; tariff computation details in Sections 4.2-4.12

## Chunk 19

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by the Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.2

**PROVISION:** Section 4.2 (Benchmark Capital Cost - Commission's Final Decision)

**TITLE:** Benchmark Capital Cost for Wind Power Projects Below 10 MW - Rs. 7.00 Crore/MW

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission has determined the normative benchmark capital cost for wind power projects below the 10 MW threshold at Rs. 7.00 Crore per MW for tariff determination during the control period (06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027). This cost was revised upward from the initially proposed Rs. 6.40 Crore/MW based on consideration of current market trends showing preference for multi-MW class WTGs with higher hub heights for accessing superior wind resources at elevated heights. The Commission reviewed a recent quotation for 4 MW wind project supply, erection, and commissioning that amounted to Rs. 6.75 Crore/MW, supporting the Rs. 7.00 Crore/MW determination. The capital cost is inclusive of all capital works: plant and machinery, civil works, land cost, erection and commissioning, financing and interest during construction, and evacuation infrastructure up to inter-connection point. This single benchmark is applied uniformly to all projects below 10 MW capacity to ensure transparency and predictability.

**KEY TOPICS:** Benchmark Capital Cost (Rs. 7.00 Crore/MW), Capital Cost Components (Plant, Civil Works, Land, Erection, Commissioning, Financing, Evacuation), Market Trends, WTG Technology (Higher Hub Heights), Single Benchmark Determination, Tariff Calculation Basis

**TEXT:**

**4.2. Benchmark Capital Cost**

**4.2.1. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

The Commission in the discussion paper has proposed a normative benchmark capital cost of Rs 6.40 Crores/MW for tariff determination purpose. The above cost is including evacuation cost up to the GETCO Sub-Station.

**4.2.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

Indian Wind Energy Association (InWEA) submitted that the proposed Benchmark Capital Cost of Rs. 640 Lakh / MW is on the lower side and not justified. It is further submitted that the capital cost of wind projects is closely linked to market variations and thus is prone to change over time. Seeing the past trend, the cost components have always shown an increasing trend. InWEA requested to consider the benchmark capital cost of Rs 7.90 Cr/MW as per Explanatory Memorandum on Draft CERC (Terms and Conditions for Tariff Determination from Renewable Energy Sources) Regulations, 2024 for tariff determination purpose during the control period.

**4.2.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission is of the opinion that capital cost of Wind Power Project depends on various factor such as location of the plant, size of the plant, technology etc. The Commission observed that the sample Wind Power Projects of which capital cost is covered in the explanatory Memorandum referred by the objector is too small (16 numbers of project) for considering it as representative benchmark capital cost of wind power project. The Commission noted that the objector had not submitted any documentary evidence to substantiate the claim for the proposed benchmark capital cost of Wind Power project.

The Commission observed that as per the present market trend, for harnessing wind potential at higher height, the Wind developers /generators prefer MW class WTG with higher hub height. The Commission also come across the recent quotation for supply, erection and commissioning of 4 MW Wind Power Project, wherein quoted price works out to Rs. 6.75 Crore / MW. Therefore, the Commission deems that the benchmark capital cost of Rs. 7.00 Crore/MW for Wind Power Project is adequate and reasonable considering the market trend. In view of above, the Commission has decided to fix normative benchmark capital cost of Rs. 7.00 Crore/MW for determination of tariff for Wind Power Projects below threshold limit of eligibility to participate in bidding process for the control period of this order.

The capital cost is inclusive of the of all capital work including plant and machinery, civil work, cost of land, erection and commissioning, financing and interest during construction, and evacuation infrastructure up to inter-connection point.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** CERC (Terms and Conditions for Tariff Determination from Renewable Energy Sources) Regulations 2024 (Explanatory Memorandum), Market quotation analysis (4 MW WTG project), GERC MYT Regulations 2016 (Capital cost methodology)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Capital cost directly impacts depreciation calculation in Section 4.7; affects tariff computation in Section 4.12; used in working capital calculation in Section 4.8 (maintenance spares at 1% of capital cost); ties to project planning under Section 3.8 (Security Deposit) which references transmission capacity allotment

## Chunk 20

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by the Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.3

**PROVISION:** Section 4.3 (Operation & Maintenance Cost - Commission's Final Decision)

**TITLE:** Operation and Maintenance Cost for Wind Power Projects - Rs. 7.00 Lakh/MW with 3.84% Annual Escalation

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission has determined the O&M cost for wind power projects below 10 MW threshold at Rs. 7.00 Lakh per MW for the first year of operation with an annual escalation of 3.84% per annum over the 25-year project life. This was revised from the originally proposed Rs. 7.50 Lakh/MW based on comparative analysis of SERCs which typically specify O&M expenses in the range of Rs. 5.75 Lakh/MW to Rs. 12.55 Lakh/MW. The Commission rejected the Indian Wind Energy Association's proposal for Rs. 13.82 Lakh/MW escalating at 5.89%, reasoning that: (1) Comparison with 2016 tariff order is inappropriate given the evolution of competitive bidding frameworks; (2) Tariffs discovered under competitive bidding during 2016 control period (Rs. 2.43-2.95/kWh) were significantly lower than generic tariff of Rs. 4.19/kWh; (3) Technological advancement and economy of scale have substantially reduced wind project capital costs; (4) O&M expenses are significantly linked to capital cost. Rs. 7.00 Lakh/MW with 3.84% escalation reasonably covers legitimate O&M costs given technological improvements, economy of scale, and market conditions.

**KEY TOPICS:** O&M Cost (Rs. 7.00 Lakh/MW), Annual Escalation (3.84%), First-Year Cost, Economic Scale, Technology Advancement, Cost Linkage to Capital Cost, SERCs Comparison (Rs. 5.75-12.55 Lakh/MW), Competitive Bidding Impact, Market Conditions

**TEXT:**

**4.3. Operation & Maintenance Cost**

**4.9.3. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

The Commission in the discussion paper proposed the O&M cost as Rs 7.50 Lakh/MW for first year of operation with an annual escalation of 3.84% per annum for tariff determination purpose.

**4.9.4. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

InWEA submitted that Commission in its Wind Tariff Order 2016, have considered the O&M Expenses as Rs. 9.9 Lakh / MW for first year with annual escalation of 5.72%. InWEA submitted that as per CERC Explanatory Memorandum on Draft RE Tariff Regulations 2024, the growth rate recorded in WPI and CPI during FY 2018-19 to FY 22-23 is around 5.89%. Considering the same, InWEA requested for fixing the O&M cost of Rs 13.82 lakh/MW for the first year with annual escalation at 5.89% per annum.

**4.9.5. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission observed that most of the SERCs have specified Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Expenses for Wind Power Projects in RE Tariff Regulations / Tariff Orders in the range of Rs. 5.75 Lakh/MW to Rs. 12.55Lakh/MW. The O&M cost is depending on the location and size of the projects as well as prevalent market conditions.

The Commission feel that comparison of instant case with the regulatory regime / tariff order prevalent during the year 2016 is not appropriate. The Wind tariff order refer above was notified by the Commission in August 2016 prior to the notification of Competitive bidding guidelines by the GoI. It may be noted that the tariff discovered under the competitive bidding conducted by GUVNL / SECI during the control period of Wind Tariff order 2016 (between 2017 to 2019) was in the range of Rs. 2.43 /kWh to Rs. 2.95/kWh as against tariff of Rs. 4.19/kWh determined by the Commission under the Wind tariff order notified in August 2016. Further, due to advancement of technology and economy of scale, the capital cost of Wind Power Project has been reduced substantially which is also evident from the tariff discovered through competitive bidding. Significant part of O & M expenses linked with capital cost of the project.

Considering above aspects, the Commission feel that the O&M cost of Rs. 7.00 Lacs per MW with O&M escalation rate of 3.84% is reasonable and covers the legitimate cost of O&M.

In view of above, the Commission decides to fix the O&M expense as Rs 7.00 lakh/MW during the first year of the control period with annual escalation at 3.84%.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** GERC Wind Tariff Order 2016 (August 2016 - for comparison context only), CERC Explanatory Memorandum on Draft RE Tariff Regulations 2024, SERCs Tariff Orders comparison, Competitive Bidding Guidelines (MoP), GERC MYT Regulations 2016

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** O&M cost forms basis for working capital calculation in Section 4.8 (one month O&M expenses); used in tariff computation in Section 4.12; linked to depreciation patterns under Section 4.7; affects long-term cost recovery and tariff affordability; benchmarked against competitive bidding outcomes referencing Section 3.1 tariff discovery mechanism

## Chunk 21

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by the Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.4

**PROVISION:** Section 4.4 (Capacity Utilization Factor - Commission's Final Decision)

**TITLE:** Capacity Utilization Factor for Wind Power Projects - 38% Fixed for 25-Year Project Life

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission has determined the normative Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF) for wind power projects below 10 MW at 38% fixed for the entire 25-year project life. This was revised upward from the initially proposed 35% based on analysis of wind resource distribution and technological advancement. The National Institute of Wind Energy (NIWE) Wind Resource Atlas at 120 meters above ground level (magl) shows that most unexplored potential areas in Gujarat fall within CUF ranges of 32-35% and greater than 35%. The Commission observed that most wind projects commissioned in the prior control period utilized higher hub heights with multi-MW class WTGs, which have achieved superior CUF performance. The higher capital cost allowed by the Commission (Rs. 7.00 Crore/MW per Section 4.2) is justified by the technological advancement yielding higher CUF from elevated hub heights. The 38% CUF is applied uniformly as a normative assumption for tariff calculation, providing standardized revenue expectations over the 25-year tariff period regardless of individual project performance variations.

**KEY TOPICS:** Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF), 38% (Fixed), 25-Year Project Life, Wind Resource Atlas (NIWE, 120 magl), Hub Height Technology, Multi-MW Class WTGs, CUF Range (32-35%+), Tariff Normalization, Revenue Assumption, Grid Stability

**TEXT:**

**4.4. Capacity Utilization Factor**

**4.4.1. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

The Commission in the discussion paper has proposed normative Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF) of 35% for tariff determination purpose during the control period of this order.

**4.4.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

**4.4.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission observed that the Wind Power Density at preferred hub height will be a better indicator of CUF. The Wind Resource Atlas at 120 magl prepared by National Institute of Wind Energy (NIWE), MNRE shows most of unexplored potential areas in Gujrat falls under the CUF range of 32 to 35% and greater than 35%. The Commission noted that most of the wind projects commissioned in last control period have higher hub height with MW scale WTGs. The Commission believe that advancement in technology yield higher CUF due to higher hub height for which higher capital cost is allowed for determination of tariff.

In view of this, the Commission decides to fix the normative Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF) of 38% fixed for 25 years of project life for tariff determination purpose during the control period of this order.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** National Institute of Wind Energy (NIWE) Wind Resource Atlas (120 magl), Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) wind resource assessments, IEC (International Electrotechnical Commission) standards on wind turbine performance

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** CUF directly impacts annual energy generation calculation used in tariff computation in Section 4.12; linked to technological advancements discussed in Section 4.2 (higher hub heights justify higher capital cost); affects revenue adequacy over 25-year tariff period; tied to Section 3.2(b) 25-year useful life of plant; fundamental parameter for levelized tariff calculation in Section 4.10

**PROVISION:** Section 4.5 (Debt-Equity Ratio)

**TITLE:** Debt-Equity Ratio for Wind Power Project Financing - 70:30

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission has determined the normative debt-equity ratio for wind power projects below the 10 MW threshold at 70:30 (70% debt, 30% equity) for tariff determination purposes during the control period. This ratio is aligned with provisions under the National Tariff Policy formulated by the Ministry of Power and GERC Multi-Year Tariff (MYT) Regulations 2016. The 70:30 ratio reflects market-standard financing patterns where debt instruments (bank loans, financial institution credit) comprise 70% of capital structure and equity investments comprise 30%. No stakeholder objections were received. The uniform application of this ratio ensures consistent treatment of project financing costs in tariff determination and aligns with regulatory precedent in prior tariff orders.

**KEY TOPICS:** Debt-Equity Ratio (70:30), Debt (70%), Equity (30%), Project Financing, Tariff Policy (Ministry of Power), GERC MYT Regulations 2016, Normative Ratio, Capital Structure, Financing Cost

**4.5.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

**4.5.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission decides to consider the debt-equity ratio of 70:30 for tariff determination purpose for the control period of this order.

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.6

**PROVISION:** Section 4.6 (Interest on Term Loan and Loan Tenure)

**TITLE:** Interest Rate on Term Loan and Loan Repayment Period - 10.15% for 15 Years

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission has determined the normative interest rate on term loan at 10.15%, computed as SBI MCLR (Marginal Cost of Lending Rate) plus 200 basis points. This rate reflects current banking market conditions for renewable energy project financing. The loan repayment period is fixed at 15 years, representing a standard amortization schedule for wind power projects. No moratorium period is allowed (0 years), meaning repayment begins immediately upon project commissioning. The 10.15% interest rate and 15-year tenure are applied uniformly to all projects below 10 MW during the control period for consistent tariff treatment. No stakeholder objections were received to these parameters.

**KEY TOPICS:** Interest Rate on Term Loan (10.15%), SBI MCLR Plus 200 Basis Points, Loan Repayment Period (15 Years), Moratorium Period (0 Years), Amortization Schedule, Term Loan Principal, Debt Servicing, Project Financing Cost, Renewable Energy Financing

**TEXT:**

**4.6. Interest on term loan and Loan tenure**

**4.6.1. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

The Commission in the discussion paper has proposed the normative interest rate on term loan as SBI MCLR rate plus 200 basis points which works out to 10.15 % for computation of interest on term loan and loan repayment period as 15 years for tariff determination purpose.

**4.6.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

**4.6.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission decides to consider the normative interest rate equal to 10.15 % for computation of interest on term loan. The Commission decides to fix the loan repayment period as 15 years for tariff determination purpose for the control period of this tariff order.
