**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.7

**PROVISION:** Section 4.7 (Depreciation - Two-Tier Structure)

**TITLE:** Depreciation Schedule for Wind Power Projects - 4.67% (15 Years) then 2.00% (Years 16-25)

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission has determined a two-tier straight-line depreciation schedule for wind power projects below 10 MW: 4.67% per annum for the first 15 years, and 2.00% per annum from year 16 to year 25 (end of useful life). This structure aligns with GERC Multi-Year Tariff (MYT) Regulations 2016 and reflects asset recovery priorities during the initial debt repayment period (which concludes at year 15 per Section 4.6). The higher depreciation rate in years 1-15 enables faster capital cost recovery while debt obligations are being serviced, supporting tariff adequacy. The reduced rate in years 16-25 reflects the asset's residual recovery beyond the debt servicing period. No salvage value is assumed (100% asset depreciation over 25 years); however, a 10% non-depreciable amount (residual value) is retained as per GERC policy, with 90% of capital cost being depreciable. This two-tier approach is standard in GERC tariff determination for generating stations with multi-decade useful lives.

**KEY TOPICS:** Depreciation, Straight-Line Method, 4.67% Per Annum (Years 1-15), 2.00% Per Annum (Years 16-25), Non-Depreciable Amount (10%), Depreciable Amount (90%), Asset Recovery, Debt Servicing Period, GERC MYT Regulations 2016

**TEXT:**

**4.7. Depreciation**

**4.7.1. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

The Commission in line with GERC Multi Year Tariff (MYT) Regulations, 2016 has proposed to consider depreciation at the rate of 4.67% per annum for the first 15 years, and 2% from 16th year to 25th year for tariff determination purpose for the new control period.

**4.7.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

The Commission has not received any comments/suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

**4.7.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission decides to fix the depreciation at the rate of 4.67% per annum for the first 15 years, and 2% from 16th year to 25th year for tariff determination purpose for the control period this Order.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** GERC Multi Year Tariff (MYT) Regulations 2016, Electricity Act 2003 (Section 22 on capital cost and depreciation), Income Tax Act 1961 (accelerated depreciation basis), Accounting Standards (AS-6 on Depreciation)

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Depreciation calculation directly impacts working capital requirements in Section 4.8; used in tariff computation in Section 4.12 with specific depreciation values for each year 1-25; affects Return on Equity calculation in Section 4.9 (equity return applied to undepreciated asset value); interacts with accelerated depreciation benefit in Section 4.11; connects to 25-year project life in Section 3.2(b) and 15-year loan tenure in Section 4.6

## Chunk 25

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by the Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.8

**PROVISION:** Section 4.8 (Working Capital and Interest on Working Capital)

**TITLE:** Working Capital Requirement and Interest Rate - Components and 10.65% Interest Rate

**SUMMARY:**
Working capital for wind power projects comprises three components: (1) O&M expenses for one month (operating liquidity); (2) Receivables of one month charges for sale of electricity (revenue cycle working capital); (3) Maintenance spares at 1% of capital cost escalated at 5% per annum (inventory/emergency reserves). The normative interest on working capital is fixed at 10.65%, calculated as SBI MCLR plus 250 basis points, per GERC MYT Regulations 2016. No stakeholder objections were received. The working capital allowance ensures the project developer has adequate liquidity for month-to-month O&M, recovery of receivables, and spare parts inventory management, particularly critical given wind power's dependence on maintenance and rapid billing cycles. The escalation of maintenance spares at 5% annually reflects inflation in spare parts costs over the 25-year project life.

**KEY TOPICS:** Working Capital, O&M Expenses (One Month), Receivables (One Month), Maintenance Spares (1% of Capital Cost), Spares Escalation (5% p.a.), Interest on Working Capital (10.65%), SBI MCLR Plus 250 Basis Points, Liquidity Management, Revenue Cycle, Inventory Management

**TEXT:**

**4.8. Working Capital and Interest on Working Capital**

**4.8.1. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

The Commission in the discussion paper has proposed to consider following component of working capital for determination of working capital requirement for Wind Power Project.

• O&M expenses for one month.
• Receivables of one month charges for sale of electricity.
• Maintenance spares at 1% of the capital cost escalated at 5% per annum

As per provisions in the GERC MYT Regulations, it is proposed to consider the interest on working capital equal to SBI MCLR plus 250 basis points, which works out as 10.65 %.

**4.8.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

**4.8.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission decides to fix following component of working capital for determination of working capital requirement for Wind Power Project.

• O&M expenses for one month.
• Receivables of one month charges for sale of electricity.
• Maintenance spares at 1% of the capital cost escalated at 5% per annum

The Commission decides to fix normative interest on working capital equal to 10.65% for tariff determination purpose for the control period this order.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** GERC Multi Year Tariff (MYT) Regulations 2016, SBI MCLR (Marginal Cost of Lending Rate), GERC Supply Code provisions on billing and receivables cycles

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Working capital calculation uses O&M cost from Section 4.3 (one month provision); capital cost from Section 4.2 (for maintenance spares at 1%); calculated annually and incorporated in tariff computation in Section 4.12; interest on working capital is separate cost element from interest on term loan (Section 4.6); escalation rate of 5% on spares differs from O&M escalation rate of 3.84% (Section 4.3)

## Chunk 26

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by the Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.9

**PROVISION:** Section 4.9 (Return on Equity with Tax Adjustment)

**TITLE:** Return on Equity and Tax Treatment - 15.5% RoE with MAT and Corporate Tax

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission has determined the Return on Equity (RoE) for wind power projects at 15.5% for the entire project life (years 1-25), aligned with GERC MYT Regulations 2024 (notified more recently than the 2016 version). This is applicable to the 30% equity component determined in Section 4.5. Tax treatment reflects two regimes: Minimum Alternative Tax (MAT) at 17.47% for the first 10 years of project operation, and Corporate Tax at 34.94% (comprising 30% Income Tax + 12% Surcharge + 4% Cess) for years 11-25. The dual tax regime reflects India's tax code which subjects profitable entities to whichever is higher - MAT (minimum threshold) or standard corporate tax. The uniform 15.5% RoE is applied across all years despite changing tax rates, ensuring equitable returns to equity investors throughout the project's operational life. Tax adjustments are calculated separately rather than grossing-up RoE.

**KEY TOPICS:** Return on Equity (15.5%), Equity Component (30% of Capital), RoE Applied (Years 1-25), Minimum Alternative Tax (MAT, 17.47%, Years 1-10), Corporate Tax (34.94%, Years 11-25), Tax Rate Components (30% IT + 12% Surcharge + 4% Cess), GERC MYT Regulations 2024

**TEXT:**

**4.9. Return on Equity**

**4.9.1. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

GERC (Multi Year Tariff) Regulations, 2016 provides for Return of Equity at the rate 14% plus the applicable tax payment. The Commission follow the RoE norms as specified in MYT Regulations while determining generic tariff for conventional and renewable power projects. Accordingly, the Commission proposed to consider the RoE of 14% and applicability of MAT @ 17.47 % per annum for first 10 years and corporate tax @ 34.94% (IT Rate 30% +12% Surcharge + 4% Cess) per annum for the next 15 years for tariff determination purpose in the discussion paper.

**4.9.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard

**4.9.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission notes that the GERC MYT Regulations 2024 notified by the Commission provides for RoE of 15.5% for determination of tariff for generating projects. Accordingly, the Commission decide to consider 15.5 % RoE for determination of tariff for Wind Power Projects below threshold limit. Further, the tax payment of MAT @ 17.47% per annum for first 10 years and Corporate Tax @ 34.94% per annum for the next 15 years has been considered separately for tariff determination purpose.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** GERC Multi Year Tariff (MYT) Regulations 2024, GERC MYT Regulations 2016 (superseded for current order but precedential), Income Tax Act 1961 (tax rates), Section 115JB of Income Tax Act (MAT provisions), Electricity Act 2003

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Equity value determined from debt-equity ratio in Section 4.5 (30% of Rs. 700 Lakh capital cost = Rs. 210 Lakh); RoE component is applied to undepreciated equity value year-by-year in tariff computation in Section 4.12; separate from Interest on Term Loan (Section 4.6); combined with depreciation and interest costs to determine total annual cost in tariff calculation; tax treatment interacts with accelerated depreciation benefit in Section 4.11

## Chunk 27

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by the Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.10

**PROVISION:** Section 4.10 (Discount Rate for Levelized Tariff Calculation)

**TITLE:** Discount Rate (WACC) for Levelized Tariff - 9.77%

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission has determined the discount rate for levelized tariff calculations at 9.77%, which represents the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) derived from the normative financing parameters determined in prior sections. The WACC is calculated as the blended cost of 70% debt at 10.15% interest (Section 4.6) and 30% equity at 15.5% return (Section 4.9), resulting in WACC of 9.77%. This discount rate converts the 25-year stream of annual costs and revenues into a single levelized tariff figure (Rs./kWh) that remains constant in real terms over the project life. The discount rate of 9.77% reflects the weighted cost of capital to finance the project and is used to discount all future cash flows (costs and revenues) to present value for levelized tariff determination. No stakeholder objections were received.

**KEY TOPICS:** Discount Rate (9.77%), WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital), Cost of Debt (10.15%), Cost of Equity (15.5%), Debt Weight (70%), Equity Weight (30%), Levelized Tariff Calculation, Present Value Discounting, NPV Analysis, Financial Viability

**TEXT:**

**4.10. Discount Rate**

**4.10.1. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

The Commission in the discussion paper proposed the discount rate equal to weighted average cost of capital (WACC). Considering the normative interest rate and normative Return of Equity, the discount rate of 9.32 % was proposed for levelized tariff calculations.

**4.10.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

**4.10.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission decides to consider the discount rate of 9.77% corresponding to the rate of Interest on Term Loan and RoE for levelized tariff calculations of Wind Power Project for the control period of this Order.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** GERC MYT Regulations 2016 and 2024 (WACC methodology), Finance textbooks and standards on cost of capital calculation, NPV and levelized cost methodologies

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Discount rate directly used in Section 4.12 tariff computation table to convert 25-year annual costs into single levelized tariff; incorporates interest rate from Section 4.6 (10.15%) and RoE from Section 4.9 (15.5%); affects sensitivity of tariff to changes in financing costs; lower than some SERC standards reflecting current market interest rates and equity return expectations; essential for determining financial viability threshold of the project

## Chunk 28

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by the Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.11

**PROVISION:** Section 4.11 (Accelerated Depreciation Benefit on Income Tax)

**TITLE:** Accelerated Depreciation Tax Benefit Calculation and Application

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission determines the income tax benefit arising from accelerated depreciation under Section 32(1)(ii) of the Income Tax Act 1961. Wind power projects are eligible for 60% depreciation in the first year of commissioning under Income Tax provisions, compared to normative straight-line depreciation used for tariff purposes (4.67% year 1 per Section 4.7). The tax benefit is calculated using: (1) Normative capital cost as base for calculation; (2) Accelerated depreciation rate (60% first year) per Income Tax Act; (3) Corporate income tax rate applicable year-by-year; (4) Per-unit benefit derived on levelized basis using discount factor equivalent to post-tax WACC. The benefit is calculated annually over the project life and levelized across the 25-year period. The accelerated depreciation benefit acts as a cost reduction to be subtracted from the levelized tariff, resulting in a net tariff benefit to consumers while providing tax incentives to project developers.

**KEY TOPICS:** Accelerated Depreciation, 60% First-Year Depreciation, Income Tax Act 1961 (Section 32), Tax Benefit Calculation, Corporate Income Tax Rate, Levelized Benefit, Per-Unit Benefit, Post-Tax WACC, Cost Reduction, Tariff Impact

**TEXT:**

**4.11. Accelerated Depreciation**

**4.11.1. Proposed in Discussion Paper**

The Commission in the discussion paper for ascertaining the Income Tax benefit on account of accelerated or additional depreciation has proposed following methodology:

i. The assessment of benefit shall be based on normative Capital Cost, accelerated/ additional depreciation rate as per the relevant provisions of the Income Tax Act and the Corporate Income Tax rate;

ii. Capitalisation of Wind Power Projects for the full financial year;

iii. Per-unit benefit shall be derived on levelized basis at a discounting factor equivalent to the post-tax weighted average cost of capital.

As per provisions in the Income Tax act, the Wind Project Developer can avail 60% depreciation in the first year of commissioning. The Commission has considered above depreciation rate while calculating per unit AD benefit.

**4.11.2. Suggestions/Objections of the Stakeholders**

The Commission has not received any comments / suggestions from the stakeholders in this regard.

**4.11.3. Commission's Analysis and Ruling**

The Commission decides to ascertain the Income Tax benefit on account of accelerated or additional depreciation as proposed in the discussion paper.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Income Tax Act 1961 (Section 32(1)(ii) - accelerated depreciation), Income Tax Rules 1962, Ministry of Finance notifications on wind power tax benefits, Corporate Tax rates applicable during project life

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Accelerated depreciation rate (60% first year) contrasts with normative straight-line depreciation in Section 4.7 (4.67% year 1); AD benefit is calculated separately and applied as reduction to computed tariff in Section 4.12; results in "Net tariff" after AD benefit (displayed as 0.33 Rs./kWh in tariff table); tax benefit incentivizes renewable energy development; benefits consumers through reduced tariff while supporting developer project economics

**TITLE:** Generic Levelized Tariff for Wind Power Projects Below 10 MW - Rs. 2.84/kWh (Net of Accelerated Depreciation)

**SUMMARY:**
The Commission has determined the generic levelized tariff for wind power projects below 10 MW capacity commissioned during the control period (06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027) at Rs. 2.84 per kWh (net of accelerated depreciation benefit). The tariff comprises: (1) Levelized tariff (gross): Rs. 3.17/kWh calculated using comprehensive financial and operational parameters including Rs. 7.00 Cr/MW capital cost, Rs. 7.00 Lakh/MW O&M cost with 3.84% escalation, 38% CUF, 70:30 debt-equity ratio, 10.15% interest on term loan, 15-year loan repayment, 4.67%/2.0% two-tier depreciation, 15.5% RoE, 10.65% interest on working capital, MAT and corporate tax, and 9.77% discount rate. (3) Net tariff: Rs. 2.84/kWh after subtracting AD benefit. The single generic tariff applies uniformly to all eligible projects regardless of location or capacity variation within the below-10-MW category. Projects may be eligible for central government financial assistance/subsidies; Commission will re-determine tariff for projects availing such benefits by factoring in the assistance amount. The tariff is applicable only to projects commissioned on or after 06.06.2022 per applicability provisions in Section 4.13. This tariff structure balances revenue adequacy for project developers with cost efficiency for distribution licensees and consumers.

**DOCUMENT:** GERC Order No. 05 of 2024 - Tariff Framework for Procurement of Power by the Distribution Licensees and Others from Wind Power Projects for the State of Gujarat

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** Tariff Order / Regulatory Order

**HIERARCHY:** Section 4 / Section 4.13

**PROVISION:** Section 4.13 (Applicability of the Order - Effective Date, Control Period, and Transmission Agreement Transition)

**TITLE:** Applicability Framework - Effective Date 06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027; Transmission Agreement Alignment

**SUMMARY:**
The tariff framework specified in this Order becomes effective from 06.06.2022 and applies only to wind power projects commissioned on or after this date. The control period runs from 06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027 (5-year period). All wind power projects commissioned after 06.06.2022 are governed by this tariff framework and the underlying regulatory provisions (MoP Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022, GERC Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access Regulations 2024, and this Order). Wind projects with Transmission and/or Wheeling Agreements executed after 05.06.2022 but under the previous Tariff Order No. 02 of 2020 (dated 30.04.2020) read with Order No. 04 of 2020 (dated 26.05.2020) are required to modify and align such agreements with the provisions of this new Order (Order No. 05 of 2024). Projects commissioned prior to 06.06.2022 continue to be governed by the previous tariff orders (No. 02 of 2020 read with No. 04 of 2020) and are not affected by this new Order. This transitional provision ensures all projects have clarity on which tariff framework applies based on their commissioning date and agreement execution timing.

**KEY TOPICS:** Effective Date (06.06.2022), Control Period (06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027), Commissioned On or After 06.06.2022, Applicability Framework, Transmission and/or Wheeling Agreement, Previous Orders (No. 02 and 04 of 2020), Agreement Modification, Regulatory Transition, Retroactive Application (None)

**TEXT:**

**4.13. Applicability of the Order**

The effective date of this Order is from 06.06.2022 to 31.03.2027. Accordingly, the tariff framework specified in this Order shall be applicable to Wind Power Projects commissioned on or after 06.06.2022. If any Transmission and/or Wheeling Agreement for the Wind Power Project executed after 05.06.2022 as per the provisions of previous Tariff Order No. 02 of 2020 dated 30.04.2020 read with Order No. 04 of 2020 dated 26.05.2020, the same shall be modified and aligned with the provisions of this Order.

**REFERENCED PROVISIONS:** Tariff Order No. 02 of 2020 (dated 30.04.2020) - superseded for new projects, Tariff Order No. 04 of 2020 (dated 26.05.2020) - superseded for new projects, MoP Green Energy Open Access Rules 2022 (effective 06.06.2022), GERC Terms and Conditions for Green Energy Open Access Regulations 2024, Electricity Act 2003

**CROSS-REFERENCES:** Effective date 06.06.2022 aligns with MoP Green Energy Open Access Rules notification date referenced in Section 3.2(a) (Control Period); commissioning date determines tariff applicability (projects before 06.06.2022 use previous orders; on/after 06.06.2022 use this order); applies to generic tariff in Section 4.12 (Rs. 2.84/kWh applies only to projects commissioned 06.06.2022 onward); transmission/wheeling agreement modification requirement ensures all projects operate under unified regulatory framework; creates clear demarcation for regulatory compliance and tariff application
