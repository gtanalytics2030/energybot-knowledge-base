PROVISION: Regulation 19

TITLE: Compliance with Laws and Regulatory Requirements

SUMMARY:
Regulation 19 establishes comprehensive compliance obligations for Transmission Licensees. Licensees must comply with all applicable laws, the Electricity Act, the Grid Code, Transmission Operating Standards, Transmission Planning and Safety Standards, rules, regulations, orders, and directions issued by the Commission. Licensees must duly comply with orders and directions from the National Load Despatch Centre (NLDC), Regional Load Despatch Centre (RLDC), State Load Despatch Centre (SLDC), and other statutory authorities in their discharge of functions. Licensees must coordinate with Regional Electricity Boards/Power Committees, Regional and State Load Despatch Centres, Central and State Transmission Utilities, other licensees, and generating companies on all transmission-related activities. Licensees must pay license fees as specified and maintain up-to-date customer records and transaction records, providing them to the Commission as required. Licensees must establish adequate communication facilities (telephone, fax, computer, internet) before undertaking transmission. Licensees must render full assistance to persons authorized by the Commission to regulate or assist in carrying out transmission duties. If a licensee fails to undertake transmission activity for four consecutive quarters, the license is subject to revocation. Licensees must not enter agreements leading to abuse of dominant position or combinations adversely affecting competition. These provisions ensure responsible, coordinated, and competitive transmission operations.

19. Compliance

(a) The Transmission Licensee shall comply with the requirements of laws in force and, in particular, the Acts, the Grid Code, Transmission Operating Standards, Transmission Planning and Safety Standards, rules, regulations, orders and directions issued by the Commission from time to time;

PROVISION: Regulation 20

TITLE: Activities of the Licensee

SUMMARY:
Regulation 20 defines the primary activities that a Transmission Licensee must undertake. First, the Transmission Licensee shall duly discharge the duties specified under Section 40 of the Electricity Act, 2003, which include maintaining efficient transmission systems. Second, the licensee shall provide non-discriminatory open access to the transmission system for use by other licensees, generating companies (including captive generating plants), and consumers in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and regulations. This open access is subject to: (1) availability of transmission capacity in the transmission lines; and (2) for supply of electricity to consumers, subject to payment of surcharge to meet the current level of cross-subsidy as specified in Section 40 read with Section 42(2) of the Electricity Act, 2003. This provision mandates non-discriminatory access to transmission infrastructure while preserving the Commission's ability to determine appropriate cross-subsidy mechanisms.

KEY TOPICS: Transmission Licensee Duties, Section 40 Electricity Act, Open Access, Non-Discriminatory Access, Transmission Capacity, Wheeling, Cross-Subsidy Surcharge, Generating Companies, Consumers, Section 42 Electricity Act

20. Activities of the Licensee

(a) The Transmission Licensee shall duly discharge the duties of the Transmission Licensee specified under Section 40 of the Electricity Act, 2003.

(b) The Transmission Licensee shall provide non-discriminatory open access to the transmission system for use of Licensees and generating companies including captive generating plants and consumers in accordance with the laws, rules and regulations for the time being in force, subject to availability of transmission capacity in the transmission lines and, in the case of use of such transmission system for supply of electricity to consumers, subject to payment of surcharge to meet the current level of cross subsidy as envisaged in Section 40 read with sub-section (2) of Section 42 of the Electricity Act, 2003.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Section 40 Electricity Act 2003, Section 42(2) Electricity Act 2003

DOCUMENT TYPE: Regulation

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Transmission License / Regulation 21

PROVISION: Regulation 21

TITLE: Prohibited Activities

SUMMARY:
Regulation 21 establishes strict prohibitions on certain business activities for Transmission Licensees to prevent conflicts of interest and anti-competitive conduct. Without prior Commission approval: (1) licensees cannot acquire, purchase, take over, or otherwise acquire the utility of any other licensee; (2) licensees cannot acquire, retain ownership or beneficial interest in a Distribution Licensee, Trading Licensee, generating company, or generating set selling electricity into the licensed area; (3) licensees cannot merge their utility with another licensee's utility; or (4) licensees cannot assign or transfer the transmission license by sale, lease, exchange, or otherwise. However, licensees may use the license as collateral for ordinary course financing from banks and financial institutions, provided they: (1) inform the Commission at least 15 days prior to the agreement effective date; (2) act prudently and reasonably; and (3) retain operational control of transmission assets. Additionally, licensees shall not directly or through subsidiaries engage in electricity trading. Licensees shall not use transmission assets for purposes other than transmission without prior Commission approval under Regulation 23. When engaging in other authorized business, a specified proportion of revenue shall reduce transmission and wheeling charges, and separate accounting shall ensure the licensed business is not encumbered or subsidized. These restrictions prevent conflicts of interest while allowing legitimate asset financing.

KEY TOPICS: Prohibited Activities, Acquisition, Merger, Assignment, Transfer, Distribution Licensee, Trading Licensee, Generating Company, Dominant Position, Financing, Collateral, Operational Control, Other Business, Asset Use Restrictions

TEXT:

21. Prohibited Activities

(a) The Transmission Licensee shall not, without prior approval of the Commission under Regulation 20 below:

i) Acquire by purchase or take over or otherwise acquire the utility of any other Licensee; or

ii) Acquire or retain any ownership or beneficial interest in a Distribution Licensee, Trading Licensee, generating company or generating set engaged in selling electricity into the Licensed Area; or

iii) Merge its utility with the utility of any other Licensee; 

iv) Assign or transfer its Transmission Licence to any person, by sale, lease, exchange or otherwise.

Provided, however, that the Transmission Licensee will be entitled to utilise the Transmission Licence as a means of facilitating funding or financing the Licensed Business in the ordinary course of business including loans and credit facilities from banks and financial institutions, subject to the conditions:

(1) that the Transmission Licensee shall inform the Commission about such arrangements at least 15 days prior to the effective date of the relevant agreements; 

(2) the Transmission Licensee shall act in a prudent and reasonable manner in such utilisation of the Transmission License; and

(3) the Transmission Licensee shall retain operational control over assets in the transmission system.

(b) The Transmission Licensee shall not, either directly or through its subsidiary, engage in the business of trading of electricity.

(c) The Transmission Licensee shall not make use of the assets of the system for a purpose other than transmission of electricity, except with the prior approval of the Commission as provided for in Regulation 23. 

Provided that where the Transmission Licensee engages in any such business, such proportion of the revenue as may be specified by the Commission shall be utilised for reducing charges for transmission and wheeling and separate accounts shall be maintained to ensure that such business does not in any manner encumber the transmission assets or be subsidised by the Licensed Business.

(d) Wherever prior approval of the Commission is required, the Transmission Licensee shall submit an appropriate application before the Commission.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Regulation 22 (Transfer of Assets), Regulation 23 (Other Business), Regulation 20 (Open Access), Section 40 Electricity Act 2003

PROVISION: Regulation 22

TITLE: Transfer of Assets

SUMMARY:
Regulation 22 establishes comprehensive procedures and conditions for Transmission Licensees to transfer their utility or any part thereof through sale, lease, exchange, or otherwise. The licensee must provide the Commission prior written notice of intent to transfer, disclosing all relevant facts. The Commission may, within thirty days of receiving notice, seek further supporting information, and must generally respond within thirty days of receiving such information, or within sixty days if no further information is sought, by allowing the transfer subject to terms and conditions, or by rejection through reasoned order. The licensee may proceed with transfer if: (1) the Commission confirms written consent subject to specified conditions; or (2) the Commission does not respond within the specified timeframe. Additionally, transfers are permitted when: (1) the Commission has issued general directions allowing specified transfer types subject to conditions; (2) the transfer is required by law; or (3) the asset was acquired for and used in authorized other business and does not constitute land interests or part of the transmission system. Notwithstanding these restrictions, licensees may use assets as collateral for ordinary course financing from banks and financial institutions, provided they: (1) inform the Commission at least 15 days prior to the agreement effective date; (2) act prudently and reasonably; and (3) retain operational control over transmission assets. These provisions protect transmission system integrity while allowing legitimate asset transfers and financing arrangements.

KEY TOPICS: Transfer of Assets, Sale, Lease, Exchange, Prior Notice, Commission Consent, Thirty-Day Timeline, Reasoned Order, Conditions, Asset Financing, Collateral, Operational Control, Other Business Assets

TEXT:

22. Transfer of Assets

(a) The Transmission Licensee shall not, in a single transaction or a set of related transactions, transfer its utility or any part thereof by sale, lease, exchange or otherwise, without complying with the conditions stipulated in this Regulation 22.

(b) The Transmission Licensee shall give to the Commission prior written notice of its intention to transfer its utility and disclose all relevant facts. The Commission may, within thirty (30) days of the receipt of the notice, seek further information in support of the transaction and shall, generally within thirty (30) days of such further information being submitted by the Transmission Licensee, and where no such further information is sought by the Commission as above, within sixty (60) days of the filing of the application, allow the transfer arrangement subject to such terms and conditions or modifications as may be considered appropriate or reject the same, by means of a reasoned order.

(c) The Transmission Licensee may transfer its utility as is specified in any notice given under clause (b) if:

i) the Commission confirms in writing that it consents to such transfer, subject to such conditions as the Commission may impose; or

ii) the Commission does not, within the time specified under Clause (b) for the decision of the Commission, give any response to the notice of the Transmission Licensee that it intends to deal with the application of the Transmission Licensee.

(d) The Transmission Licensee may transfer its utility where:

i) the Commission has issued directions for the purposes of this Regulation 22 containing a general consent (whether or not subject to conditions) to transactions of a specified description, and/or the transfer of a specified description, and the transfer is in accordance with any conditions to which the consent is subject; or

ii) the transfer in question is required by or under the mandate of any law; or

iii) the asset in question was acquired and used by the Transmission Licensee exclusively or primarily in connection with any Other Business that it has been authorised to carry on pursuant to Regulation 14 and does not constitute a legal or beneficial interest in land, or otherwise form part of the transmission system or is not otherwise an asset required for the Licensed Business.

i) that the Transmission Licensee will inform the Commission about such arrangements at least fifteen (15) days prior to the effective date of the relevant agreements; 

ii) the Transmission Licensee acts in a prudent and reasonable manner in such utilisation of assets; and

iii) the Transmission Licensee retains Operational Control over assets in the transmission system.

DOCUMENT TYPE: Regulation

HIERARCHY: Chapter 4 / General Conditions of Transmission License / Regulation 23

PROVISION: Regulation 23

TITLE: Other Business

SUMMARY:
Regulation 23 permits Transmission Licensees to engage in other business activities (beyond licensed transmission) for optimum utilization of assets, subject to prior intimation to the Commission. When engaging in other business, licensees must ensure: (1) the licensed transmission business is not prejudiced or adversely affected; (2) transmission assets are not encumbered to support other business; (3) the licensed business does not subsidize other business; (4) if no additional investment is required in other business, a maximum of 25% of profits (after reasonable expenses including corporate tax) may be retained in the other business, with the balance reducing transmission and wheeling charges; (5) if additional investment is made for other business, the licensee may charge the other business a fee no less than 75% of what an independent agency would charge for similar assets/services, with collected fees reducing transmission and wheeling charges; (6) separate accounting records must be kept for other business as if it were a separate company, separately identifying revenues, costs, assets, liabilities, reserves, and provisions; and (7) assets used in the transmission system for other business purposes cannot be transferred without prior Commission approval. These conditions ensure other business activities do not adversely affect the core transmission business and that transmission customers benefit from other business revenues.

KEY TOPICS: Other Business, Optimum Asset Utilization, Prior Intimation, Profit Retention (25%), Additional Investment, Separate Accounting, Fee Collection, Transmission Charges Reduction, Asset Encumbrance Prevention, Asset Transfer Restrictions

TEXT:

23. Other Business

The Transmission Licensee may, with prior intimation to the Commission, engage in any Other Business for the optimum utilization of its assets. Where the Transmission Licensee engages in such other business, it shall ensure that:

(a) the Licensed Business is not prejudiced and/or adversely affected in any manner by the carrying on of the Other Business; 

(b) the transmission assets of the Transmission Licensee are not encumbered to support the Other Business; 

(c) the Licensed Business does not subsidise the Other Business; 

(d) If there are no investments involved in the Other Business, a maximum of 25% of the profit earned by the Transmission Licensee from such new business, after meeting all reasonable expenses including corporate tax towards this income, may be retained by the Transmission Licensee in that business, and the balance shall be used towards reducing its charges for transmission and wheeling to its customers;

(e) If the Transmission Licensee makes an additional investment for the purpose of the Other Business, the Transmission Licensee may charge the new business a fee for making use of the transmission assets, which shall be not less than 75% of the fee which such new business would have paid for similar assets/services from an independent agency/company, and the fee so collected may be used towards reducing its charges for transmission and wheeling to its customers;

(f) separate accounting records are kept in respect of such activities as if they were carried on by a separate company, so that the revenues, costs, assets, liabilities, reserves and provisions of, or reasonably attributable to each such Other Business are separately identifiable from those of the Licensed Business; and

(g) the assets used in the transmission system for the purpose of the Other Business are not transferred without the prior approval of the Commission.

REFERENCED PROVISIONS: Regulation 21(c) (Prohibited activities - other business use of assets requires Commission approval), Regulation 24 (Accounts and Audit)

CROSS-REFERENCES: Other business revenues reduce charges per Regulation 24; separate accounting required for regulatory verification; related to asset-use efficiency principles

TITLE: Accounts and Audit

SUMMARY:
Regulation 24 establishes comprehensive accounting and audit requirements for Transmission Licensees. Auditors must provide reports stating whether statements have been properly prepared and give true and fair views of revenues, costs, assets, liabilities, and reserves reasonably attributable to each business. Accounting statements and auditor's reports must be submitted to the Commission within six months after year-end. Persons authorized by the Commission may inspect and verify accounts; licensees must render necessary assistance. Licensees cannot change the basis of charge, apportionment, or allocation from the previous financial year without Commission approval; if changed during a year, the Commission may require statements prepared on both old and new bases. For other business carried on, licensees must deliver: accounting statements, interim profit and loss accounts/cash flow statements/balance sheets for the first six months, and auditor's reports annually, with interim accounts submitted within three months after the period and annual statements and auditor's reports within six months after year-end. Accounting statements and auditor's reports must be publicized as the Commission directs and made available to requesting parties at reasonable cost or prices the Commission specifies. These requirements ensure transparency, accurate cost allocation, and effective Commission oversight.

PROVISION: Regulation 27

TITLE: Major Incident

SUMMARY:
Regulation 27 establishes procedures for reporting and investigating major incidents affecting transmission systems. A Transmission Licensee must notify the Commission of any major incident affecting the transmission or distribution system, including generator interconnection facilities. A major incident is defined (in Regulation 2.1) as an incident resulting in significant service interruption, substantial equipment damage, loss of life, or significant human injury, or as otherwise directed by the Commission. Upon occurrence, the licensee must submit a detailed report giving full incident facts and causes. The Commission may, at its discretion, require submission of a report prepared by an independent person at the licensee's expense. The licensee must provide copies of the report to the Commission and to all parties involved in the major incident. The Commission's determination of what constitutes a major incident is final. Beyond violation proceedings, if the Commission reasonably believes a major incident has occurred but has not been reported by the licensee, the Commission may require the licensee to file a major incident report in accordance with these procedures. These requirements ensure transparency in serious transmission events and enable the Commission to identify systematic issues requiring corrective action.

KEY TOPICS: Major Incident, Notification, Report Submission, Independent Investigation, Incident Facts and Causes, Distribution System, Generator Interconnection, Significant Interruption, Equipment Damage, Loss of Life, Injury, Commission Determination

TEXT:

27. Major Incident

(a) The Transmission Licensee shall notify the Commission of any Major Incident affecting any part of the transmission or distribution system, including generator interconnection facilities, which has occurred and shall, 

i) submit a report giving full details of the facts of the incident and its cause. The Commission at its own discretion may require the submission of a report to be prepared by an independent person at the expense of the Transmission Licensee; and

ii) give copies of the report to the Commission and to all parties involved in the Major Incident.

(b) The decision of the Commission as to what is a Major Incident shall be final.

(c) Besides taking action for violation of the Regulations, the Commission shall be entitled to require the Transmission Licensee to file a report of any Major Incident in accordance with clause (a) above in any situation where the Commission reasonably believes a Major Incident has occurred but has not been reported by the Transmission Licensee.

Regulation 32 establishes the comprehensive grounds and procedures for revocation of a Transmission Licence. The license shall not be revoked except after inquiry by an adjudicating officer appointed by the Commission under Section 143 of the Act, in the manner prescribed by the State Government. The Commission must provide the licensee not less than three months' written notice stating grounds for proposed revocation and must consider any cause shown by the licensee within that notice period before revoking. When revocation is decided, the Commission serves notice of revocation on the licensee fixing the effective revocation date. If the Commission has given revocation notice, without prejudice to any penalty or prosecution under the Acts, the licensee may, with prior Commission approval, sell the utility/business to a person found eligible by the Commission for a transmission license.
